# 立つ (たつ) — stand; stand up

## Meaning

立つ means standing or getting to one's feet. In this use it is intransitive: the person who stands is the subject.
立っている (たっている) commonly describes being in a standing position.

- ここに立ってください。 (ここにたってください。) — Please stand here.
- 先生は黒板の前に立っています。 (せんせいはこくばんのまえにたっています。) — The teacher is standing in front of the blackboard.

## How the kanji combine

This is a single native verb. [立](../kanji/立.md) supplies た, and つ is okurigana.
It is a godan verb: 立ちます (たちます), 立って (たって), 立った (たった), 立たない (たたない).

## Synonyms and antonyms

- 座る (すわる), "sit down," is the practical posture contrast.
- 立てる (たてる) means setting something upright. It describes what someone does to an object, rather than that object's standing.
- 建つ (たつ) means being built or erected. It has the same reading but a different spelling and meaning.

## Distinctive meaning

Use 立つ for a person's standing and learn the many extended senses through their phrases.
Do not replace [建てる](建てる.md) (たてる, build) with 立つ just because a finished building stands upright.
The matching transitive/intransitive pairs are useful: 立てる / 立つ for setting upright, 建てる / 建つ for construction.
