# 単に (たんに) - merely; simply

## Meaning

単に is an adverb meaning "merely," "simply," or "solely." It narrows an explanation or description: there is no additional reason, significance, or activity beyond what the speaker names.

- 怒っているのではなく、単に疲れているんです。 (おこっているのではなく、たんにつかれているんです。) - I'm not angry; I'm simply tired.
- 単に読むだけでなく、声に出して練習しましょう。 (たんによむだけでなく、こえにだしてれんしゅうしましょう。) - Let's not merely read it, but practice saying it aloud.

単に～だけでなく means "not merely... but also..." The second sentence uses the expression to expand beyond a limited activity.

## How the kanji combine

[単](../kanji/単.md) contributes singleness or simplicity, read タン. The following に forms the adverbial expression. There is no multi-kanji relationship, and に is part of this fixed adverb.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[ただ](ただ.md) overlaps in "just; merely." [たった](たった.md) often emphasizes a small amount or number. [簡単](簡単.md) (かんたん) describes ease or simplicity: 簡単に説明する means explaining simply, while 単に説明する means merely explaining.

## Distinctive meaning

The English word "simply" can mean either "merely" or "in an easy way." 単に teaches the first meaning here. It can minimize the significance of something, so 単に冗談です (たんにじょうだんです), "It is merely a joke," may dismiss another person's concern depending on context.
