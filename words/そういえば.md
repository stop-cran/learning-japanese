# そういえば - now that you mention it; come to think of it

## Meaning

そういえば introduces something that comes to mind through the current
conversation or situation. It can mean "now that you mention it,"
"come to think of it," or "that reminds me."
It commonly starts the next remark.

- A: 明日は田中さんの誕生日ですね。 (あしたはたなかさんのたんじょうびですね。) - Tomorrow is Tanaka's birthday, isn't it?
- B: そういえば、まだプレゼントを買っていません。 (そういえば、まだプレゼントをかっていません。) - Now that you mention it, I haven't bought a present yet.

そういえば、駅で山田さんに会いました。
(そういえば、えきでやまださんにあいました。)
means "Come to think of it, I saw Yamada at the station."
The link to the preceding conversation may be understood rather than repeated.

## How the kanji combine

Kana is usual. The recognized spelling そう言えば uses
[言](../kanji/言.md), saying; 言えば (いえば) is a conditional form
of 言う. Learn the whole conversational expression rather than treating
each use as a literal instruction to say something.
The kana-primary card has no written kanji in its metadata.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[ところで](ところで.md), "by the way," also introduces a remark,
but can change the subject without the same recollection or connection.
[思い出す](思い出す.md) (おもいだす) is the verb "remember; recall,"
not an interchangeable sentence-opening expression.

## Distinctive meaning

そういえば does not require that someone explicitly asked you to remember.
Something you notice can prompt the thought. Nor does the expression by
itself mean "therefore": it introduces an association, not necessarily
a logical conclusion.
