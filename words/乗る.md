# 乗る (のる) — board; ride

## Meaning

乗る describes getting on or taking a vehicle. For this use, mark the vehicle with に:
電車に乗る (でんしゃにのる), "take a train." The rider is the subject.
Although English says "ride a bus," Japanese does not copy that direct-object pattern here.

- 毎朝、駅で電車に乗ります。 (まいあさ、えきででんしゃにのります。) — Every morning I board a train at the station.
- 昨日はバスに乗りました。 (きのうはバスにのりました。) — Yesterday I took a bus.

乗る is a godan verb: its plain past is 乗った (のった), and its て-form is 乗って (のって).

## How the kanji combine

[乗](../kanji/乗.md) carries the riding meaning, with the native reading の and the ending る.
This is a single verb, not a compound. Compare the on reading ジョウ in 乗車 (じょうしゃ), "boarding a vehicle."

## Synonyms and antonyms

降りる (おりる), "get off," provides the practical opposite: 電車を降りる (でんしゃをおりる).
Notice the change from vehicle に when boarding to vehicle を when getting off.
乗せる (のせる) is different again: it means putting someone aboard or giving them a ride.

## Distinctive meaning

乗る presents the travel from the rider's side. 友達を車に乗せる (ともだちをくるまにのせる),
"give a friend a ride in the car," instead makes the friend the object of 乗せる.
Choose the verb according to whose action the sentence describes.
