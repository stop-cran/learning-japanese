# 鴻鵠 (こうこく) — large birds; great person

## Meaning

鴻鵠 means **large birds** and, figuratively, **a person of great stature**.
Learn it for literary and proverbial recognition, not as the ordinary name
of any bird or a casual way to praise someone.

- 鴻鵠の志 (こうこくのこころざし) — lofty aspirations, likened to those of a great bird

This is an attested phrase from a classical proverb: small birds stand for
small-minded people who cannot understand the great person's aspirations.
The image concerns large birds, not a claim that the word names a phoenix.

## How the kanji combine

[鴻](../kanji/鴻.md) is **コウ** here, a large-bird character; 鵠 is **コク**,
associated with the swan. Dictionaries explain くぐい as an old swan name.
The compound pairs related bird names and extends their large scale
metaphorically to a great person. It does not identify one modern bird species.

## Synonyms and antonyms

- [鳥](鳥.md) (とり), "bird," is the ordinary broader category.
  [鴻](鴻.md) (ひしくい), "bean goose," is a specific native bird noun,
  not the reading of this two-character word.
- [偉大](偉大.md) (いだい), "great; outstanding," is a な-adjective for
  exceptional greatness. 鴻鵠 is a noun and preserves a literary bird metaphor.
- [鷹揚](鷹揚.md) (おうよう), "magnanimous; composed," describes a manner.
  It can overlap in large-heartedness, but it is not this great-person metaphor.

There is no useful general antonym. The proverb contrasts great and small
aspirations; that contrast is not a neutral species classification.

## Distinctive meaning

**こうこく** has a long vowel in コウ, followed by コク.
Do not read the compound by substituting 鴻's native ひしくい.
Nor does the metaphor imply that every great person must be wealthy, famous
or generous in every situation.

Use the phrase above to recognize the literary meaning. For an ordinary
bird sighting, use the established bird name rather than inventing a casual
鴻鵠 expression.
