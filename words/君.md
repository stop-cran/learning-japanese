# 君 (きみ) — you in familiar address

## Meaning

君 is a familiar way to address someone as "you." JMdict describes it as referring to someone of equal or lower status
and notes that it is sometimes considered male language. It is not a neutral pronoun for every listener.

- 君はどう思う？ (きみはどうおもう？) — What do you think?

This example illustrates a familiar conversation in which that address is acceptable.
It is not a recommended opening to an unfamiliar person or a superior.

## How the kanji combine

[君](../kanji/君.md) writes the whole native word きみ; there is no multi-kanji combination.
After a person's name, the same character can instead be read くん, a name suffix.
Do not read every 君 as きみ merely because that is this article's headword.

## Synonyms and antonyms

あなた also means "you," but is not an automatic polite substitute: its interpersonal effect depends on context,
and the dictionary notes that it can sound rude or distant.
For a learner, using a known name with an appropriate title, or leaving the listener implicit when clear,
can avoid an unwanted relationship claim. There is no ordinary antonym of this address form.

## Distinctive meaning

君 can communicate closeness, but it can also sound presumptuous if the listener does not accept that familiarity or hierarchy.
Adding です or ます elsewhere in the sentence does not automatically neutralize the address.
The dictionary's gender note describes a usage association, not a grammatical ban on other speakers.
