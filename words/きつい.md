# きつい (きつい) - tight; tough; intense

## Meaning

きつい is an i-adjective for something constricting, demanding, harsh, or intense.
Clothing can be physically tight; work can be tough; a smell or sunlight can be strong.
It directly modifies a noun: きつい靴 (きついくつ), "tight shoes."

- この靴は少しきついです。 (このくつはすこしきついです。) - These shoes are a little tight.
- 今日の仕事はきつかったです。 (きょうのしごとはきつかったです。) - Today's work was demanding.
- 香水のにおいがきつい。 (こうすいのにおいがきつい。) - The perfume has a strong smell.

The negative is きつくない; the past is きつかった. Use きつく as the adverbial form.

## How the kanji combine

The assigned form is kana-only. The ending い belongs to the adjective's inflection:
it changes to く in きつくない. Katakana キツイ is also a recorded spelling.
There is no kanji compound to unpack.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[厳しい](厳しい.md) (きびしい) overlaps with severity or demanding conditions.
[強い](強い.md) (つよい) overlaps with intensity.
緩い (ゆるい), "loose," contrasts with tight clothing but is not the automatic opposite in every context.

## Distinctive meaning

not being emotionally close to a friend.
きつい言い方 (きついいいかた) is a harsh way of speaking; it does not mean that the sentence is physically tight.
