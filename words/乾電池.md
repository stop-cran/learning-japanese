# 乾電池 (かんでんち) - dry-cell battery

## Meaning

乾電池 is a dry-cell battery, such as a battery used in a remote control.
It names a kind of battery, not electricity itself.

- リモコンの乾電池を交換しました。
  (リモコンのかんでんちをこうかんしました。) - I replaced the batteries in the remote control.
- 予備の乾電池を二本持っていきます。
  (よびのかんでんちをにほんもっていきます。) - I will take two spare batteries.

乾電池を交換する means replacing the batteries.
The second example uses 本 (ほん), as in 二本 (にほん), for
the cylindrical batteries intended in that sentence.

## How the kanji combine

[乾](../kanji/乾.md) contributes dry.
[電](../kanji/電.md) and [池](../kanji/池.md) form 電池 (でんち),
"battery"; 池 is part of the established electrical term, not a literal pond
inside the device. 乾 specifies the type.
The on readings are カン + デン + チ.

## Synonyms and antonyms

電池 (でんち) is the broader battery word.
湿電池 (しつでんち), "wet cell," gives the dictionary's contrasting type.
Not every battery is described as a 乾電池 merely because it is portable.

## Distinctive meaning

"Dry" does not mean an absence of liquid chemistry.
Dictionary explanations describe electrolyte retained in absorbent
material, paste, or gel rather than freely flowing liquid.
The word alone is not a safe instruction about whether a particular
product can be recharged; follow that product's labeling.
