# いえ (いえ) - no; not at all

## Meaning

いえ is an interjection answering "no" or rejecting an assumption. It is also a response to thanks,
meaning "not at all" or "don't mention it." It does not conjugate; a following sentence explains the response.

- 「学生ですか。」「いえ、会社員です。」 (がくせいですか。いえ、かいしゃいんです。) - "Are you a student?" "No, I work for a company."
- 「ありがとうございました。」「いえ、どういたしまして。」 - "Thank you very much." "Not at all; you're welcome."

The second exchange politely downplays the favor rather than contradicting the fact that the speaker was thanked.

## How the kanji combine

This response is written in kana. JMdict lists いえ and [いいえ](いいえ.md) under the same interjection entry.
Do not write it as [家](家.md): 家 (いえ), "house," is a different word with the same pronunciation as the shorter response.

## Synonyms and antonyms

いいえ supplies the same basic negative response with a longer vowel sequence.
[はい](はい.md) can give an affirmative response. These responses must still be interpreted against the question,
especially when the question itself is negative.

## Distinctive meaning

Shorter spelling does not by itself decide politeness. Tone, context, and the explanation that follows matter.
いえ、結構です (いえ、けっこうです), "No, thank you," declines an offer; it is not the same conversational act as accepting thanks.
Learn the response as part of an exchange, not as a replacement for every English "no."
