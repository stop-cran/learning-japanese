# 蒸す (むす) - steam food; be hot and humid

## Meaning

蒸す is a godan verb with two useful uses. Transitive 蒸す means heating
something with steam, including food or a towel. The thing steamed takes
を. Intransitive 蒸す describes hot, humid, sultry conditions; it does not
need a thing being cooked.

- 野菜を蒸します。 (やさいをむします。) - I steam the vegetables.
- 今日は蒸しますね。 (きょうはむしますね。) - It is hot and humid today, isn't it?

Forms include 蒸して (むして), 蒸した (むした) and 蒸さない (むさない).
The weather sentence is not a passive meaning "is steamed."

## How the kanji combine

[蒸](../kanji/蒸.md) writes む and す completes the native verb. There is
no multi-kanji compound here. The kanji's steam and sultry-heat meanings
help connect the two uses, but do not turn every word containing 蒸 into
this verb.

## Synonyms and antonyms

蒸らす (むらす) also concerns cooking with steam; 蒸れる (むれる) can
mean being steamed properly or becoming stuffy. Their endings and
grammatical roles are not interchangeable with every use of 蒸す.

[暑い](暑い.md) (あつい) describes hot surroundings more generally.
[じめじめ](じめじめ.md) and [じとじと](じとじと.md) overlap in unpleasant
humid or clammy conditions without necessarily specifying the same
degree of heat. These overlaps need direct quiz safeguards even though
the words are not freely substitutable in every construction.

Some additional quiz exclusions protect English labels rather than
Japanese synonyms. English "sticky" and "close" can describe sultry
weather, but [べたべた](べたべた.md) and the words labelled "close" here
teach stickiness, closeness or closing, not a shared Japanese weather
sense.

## Distinctive meaning

Steam-cooking is a particular method, not a general translation of
"cook." Conversely, the weather use is not a claim that rain is falling.
[ほかほか](ほかほか.md) describes comfortable warmth, including
steaming-hot food, rather than the action of steaming it or oppressive
humid weather. [熱い](熱い.md) (あつい), hot to the touch, is a different
starting point from a statement about sultry surroundings.
