# もったいない - wasteful; too good for someone

## Meaning

もったいない judges something valuable as being wasted or not properly appreciated.
It is an i-adjective: もったいない使い方 (もったいないつかいかた),
"a wasteful way of using something"; もったいなくない, "not wasteful."

- まだ食べられるのに、捨てるのはもったいないです。 (まだたべられるのに、すてるのはもったいないです。) - It is a waste to throw it away when it is still edible.
- 私にはもったいないほどのお褒めの言葉です。 (わたしにはもったいないほどのおほめのことばです。) - That is more praise than I deserve.

The second use is a modest judgment that something is too good for oneself,
not a complaint that the praise should have been thrown away.
に identifies who is considered unworthy of it.

## How the kanji combine

Kana is usual. The dictionary also records 勿体ない and 勿体無い.
The written characters do not provide a literal rule for every modern use.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[無駄](無駄.md) (むだ) overlaps with waste or wasted effort.
[惜しい](惜しい.md) (おしい) can describe something too good to discard.
[有難い](有難い.md) (ありがたい) expresses gratitude or appreciation;
it need not imply that the recipient feels undeserving.

## Distinctive meaning

The word can evaluate wasting food, money, time, or an opportunity.
It is not simply a price adjective meaning "expensive."
The dictionary also records impious or sacrilegious uses, but those do not
make an ordinary complaint about wasting food a religious statement.
