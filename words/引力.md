# 引力 (いんりょく) - attraction; gravitational pull

## Meaning

引力 is an attractive force, including gravitational attraction.
It can also describe an appealing or magnetic quality figuratively.
It is a noun, not the verb "pull."

- 地球の引力が物を引きつけます。 (ちきゅうのいんりょくがものをひきつけます。) - Earth's gravitational pull attracts objects.
- 彼の話には不思議な引力があります。 (かれのはなしにはふしぎないんりょくがあります。) - His stories have a mysterious magnetic appeal.

The first example concerns physical attraction; the second does not
claim that a story exerts measurable gravity.

## How the kanji combine

[引](../kanji/引.md) contributes pulling, and [力](../kanji/力.md)
means force or power. The relation is a force that pulls.
The on readings are イン + リョク, not the separate native readings
ひく + ちから.

## Synonyms and antonyms

重力 (じゅうりょく) is gravity. 引力 can describe attraction more
broadly, including magnetic attraction.
[魅力](魅力.md) (みりょく), "charm; appeal," overlaps with the
figurative use. 斥力 (せきりょく), "repulsive force," supplies a
physical contrast.

## Distinctive meaning

Do not read every 引力 as gravity alone. The context can concern other
attractive forces or figurative appeal. [引く](引く.md) (ひく) is the
action verb "pull"; 引力 names the force or quality, so the sentence
grammar differs even when English uses the word "pull."
