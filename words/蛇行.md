# 蛇行 (だこう) — meandering; snaking; zigzagging

## Meaning

蛇行 describes a course that bends from side to side, like a snake's
movement. It is a noun and an intransitive する verb: a river, road or
moving subject can 蛇行する.

- この川は蛇行しています。 (このかわはだこうしています。) — This river meanders.
- この道は蛇行しています。 (このみちはだこうしています。) — This road winds from side to side.

The subject has or follows the winding course; the sentences do not mean
that someone bends the river or road. Forms include 蛇行します
(だこうします) and 蛇行した (だこうした).

**じゃこう** is another documented reading of the same word. This article
and its word-reading question use **だこう**; じゃこう is not invalid.

## How the kanji combine

[蛇](../kanji/蛇.md), snake, qualifies [行](../kanji/行.md), going:
a snake-like course. The selected whole reading uses on-readings
ダ + コウ. The word need not describe an actual snake.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[曲がる](曲る.md) まがる, bend or turn, is broader: it can describe a
single turn at a corner. [カーブ](カーブ.md) names a curve or bend and
also has an intransitive カーブする use. 蛇行 highlights a winding,
side-to-side course, not every isolated turn.

まっすぐ進む (まっすぐすすむ), go straight, provides a practical contrast.
[うろうろ](うろうろ.md) describes restless or aimless wandering;
that behavioral emphasis is different from a river's meandering shape.

## Distinctive meaning

The English "meandering" can describe an unfocused conversation, but do
not assume that every English figurative use translates as 蛇行.
The river and road examples concern the actual course.

蛇行運転 (だこううんてん) means driving in a snaking or zigzagging course.
It describes movement, not its cause: the expression alone does not
establish intoxication, intention or a medical condition.
