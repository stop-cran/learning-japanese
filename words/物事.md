# 物事 (ものごと) - things; matters generally

## Meaning

物事 refers broadly to things, matters, or affairs under consideration.
It is useful when talking about how someone thinks, judges, or deals with
what happens.

- 物事をよく考えてから決めてください。 (ものごとをよくかんがえてからきめてください。) - Please think things through before deciding.
- 物事には順序があります。 (ものごとにはじゅんじょがあります。) - There is an order to doing things.

It is a noun, so the following verb determines its particle: 物事を考える,
"think about things"; 物事が進む (ものごとがすすむ), "things move forward."

## How the kanji combine

[物](../kanji/物.md) contributes things, and [事](../kanji/事.md) contributes
events or matters. This pair brings concrete things and affairs together
into a broad expression. Both readings are native: もの + こと, with こと
voiced to ごと in the compound.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[物](物.md) (もの) can name a physical object.
事 (こと) can name a matter or event. 物事 draws them together when speaking
generally, rather than distinguishing individual objects one by one.
There is no useful single antonym for this broad category.

## Distinctive meaning

English "everything" is sometimes a natural translation, but 物事 does not
automatically quantify every object in existence. It refers to the matters
relevant to the statement. For concrete belongings placed in a bag, name
the belongings or use 物 rather than assuming 物事 is simply its plural.
