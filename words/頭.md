# 頭 (あたま) — head

## Meaning

頭 (あたま) names the head. In this article the focus is the physical body part:
the whole head, rather than only its face, hair, or neck.

- 頭が痛いです。 (あたまがいたいです。) — My head hurts.
- 頭を下げました。 (あたまをさげました。) — I lowered my head.

In the first sentence, が marks the body part that hurts. In the second, を marks what is lowered.
You do not need to add 私の (わたしの), "my," when the owner is already clear.

## How the kanji combine

The single kanji [頭](../kanji/頭.md) writes the native noun あたま.
Other words select different readings: 頭痛 (ずつう), "headache," uses ズ,
and 先頭 (せんとう), "front; head of a line," uses トウ.
Do not replace あたま with one of those readings just because the spelling is the same character.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[顔](顔.md) (かお) is the face, and [首](首.md) (くび) is the neck.
They are neighboring body parts, not synonyms of the whole head.
頭痛 names a headache; 頭 itself does not imply pain.

## Distinctive meaning

The body-part meaning is a useful starting point, but compounds can extend the idea of a head to a leading position.
先頭 is a good example: the front of a line is not a body part.
Read the whole word before deciding that every occurrence of 頭 means someone's physical head.
