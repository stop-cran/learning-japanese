# 単なる (たんなる) - mere; nothing more than

## Meaning

単なる means "mere" or "nothing more than." It directly modifies a following
noun: **単なる + noun**. It is a pre-noun expression, not an い-adjective
or an ordinary な-adjective.

- それは単なるうわさです。 (それはたんなるうわさです。) - That is merely a rumor.
- この本は単なる旅行案内ではありません。 (このほんはたんなるりょこうあんないではありません。) - This book is more than just a travel guide.

The second sentence negates the limited description, suggesting that the book
offers something beyond it.

## How the kanji combine

[単](../kanji/単.md) contributes "single; simple." The Sino-Japanese reading
たん combines with the ending なる to make this established modifier.
This なる is not telling you that something "becomes single" in the example
sentences.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[ただ](ただ.md) in ただのうわさ, "just a rumor," overlaps with 単なる.
[簡単](簡単.md) (かんたん) means easy or uncomplicated, which is different:
a 単なる間違い (たんなるまちがい), "mere mistake," need not be an easy
mistake to understand. There is no single direct antonym.

## Distinctive meaning

単なる limits the importance or category being claimed. Calling something
単なるうわさ rejects treating it as established fact.
Keep the noun: the first example ends with うわさです, not 単なるです.
Likewise, do not add な after 単なる. Negation belongs to the larger statement,
as in 単なるNではない, rather than an invented adjective ending.
