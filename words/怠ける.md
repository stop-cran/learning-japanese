# 怠ける (なまける) - be lazy; neglect

## Meaning

怠ける is an ichidan verb with two related patterns.
Used intransitively, it means to be lazy, idle, or slack.
Used transitively, **work or duty + を怠ける** means to neglect or skip it.

- 怠けてばかりいないで、少し手伝ってください。 (なまけてばかりいないで、すこしてつだってください。) - Please stop just lazing around and help a little.
- 最近、勉強を怠けていました。 (さいきん、べんきょうをなまけていました。) - Lately, I had been neglecting my studies.

Conjugations include 怠けます, 怠けない, 怠けた, and 怠けて.
The first example judges the person's behavior; the second names the neglected activity with を.

## How the kanji combine

怠 contributes laziness or neglect; ける completes the native verb.
Learn the whole reading なまける and the ichidan pattern, dropping final る before ます or ない.
It is not conjugated as a godan verb merely because it ends in る.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[休む](休む.md) (やすむ) means to rest or take time off and does not inherently accuse someone of neglect.
サボる is a colloquial overlap for shirking or skipping.
[働く](働く.md) (はたらく), "work," contrasts in some contexts but is not a universal opposite.

## Distinctive meaning

怠ける carries a negative judgment about effort or responsibility.
Time away from work for illness, rest, or another legitimate reason is not automatically 怠ける.
Be careful when describing someone else: the word can sound accusatory rather than neutrally reporting absence.
For your own behavior, it can acknowledge that a duty was neglected.
