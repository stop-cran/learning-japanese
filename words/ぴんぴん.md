# ぴんぴん — lively; full of life

## Meaning

ぴんぴん describes being energetic and vigorous, including someone who seems surprisingly well. It also depicts lively jumping or flopping; a separate sound use describes a sharp, penetrating sound.

- 祖父は九十歳でもぴんぴんしている。 — My grandfather is still full of life at ninety. (祖父: そふ; 九十歳: きゅうじゅっさい.)
- 釣り上げた魚がバケツの中でぴんぴん跳ねている。 — The fish we caught is flopping vigorously in the bucket. (釣り上げる: つりあげる; 跳ねる: はねる.)

Use ぴんぴんする or ぴんぴんしている for vitality; ぴんぴん（と）跳ねる describes vigorous movement.

## How the kanji combine

The repeated kana form includes ん in each unit. Its state, movement, and sharp-sound senses are recorded under the same dictionary entry; they do not require a kanji decomposition.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[元気](元気.md) (げんき) is broader and more neutral for health or energy. [ぐったり](ぐったり.md) and [へとへと](へとへと.md) can contrast when someone is drained or exhausted.

## Distinctive meaning

ぴんぴんしている often has a colloquial tone of surprise or emphasis. It is not a medical guarantee of perfect health. When someone was expected to be ill, saying they are ぴんぴんしている may sound teasing or skeptical.
