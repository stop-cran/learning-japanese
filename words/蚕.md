# 蚕 (かいこ) — silkworm

## Meaning

蚕 means **silkworm**. In ordinary descriptions of raising silkworms, it is the larva of the silkworm moth, kept for the silk obtained from its cocoon.

- 蚕は桑の葉を食べます。 (かいこはくわのはをたべます。) — Silkworms eat mulberry leaves.
- 蚕を飼っています。 (かいこをかっています。) — We are raising silkworms.

In the first sentence, は introduces what is being described and を marks the leaves being eaten. In the second, を marks the animal being kept; [飼う](飼う.md) かう is the animal-keeping verb, not [買う](買う.md) かう, buy.

## How the kanji combine

The single character [蚕](../kanji/蚕.md) writes the native noun かいこ. There is no compound to split or kana ending to add. **かいこ** and **カイコ** are also spellings of the animal name; katakana alone does not identify it as a loanword.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[虫](虫.md) むし, insect/bug, is a broader category. [繭](繭.md) まゆ is the cocoon made by the larva, and [絹](絹.md) きぬ is silk as a material. Keep the insect, its covering and the material separate.

[桑](桑.md) くわ names the mulberry tree; 桑の葉 (くわのは) specifies its leaves. There is no useful direct antonym for a silkworm.

## Distinctive meaning

This article practices the **larva in the rearing context**. Dictionaries can also use かいこ for the moth more broadly. **カイコガ** (蚕蛾, かいこが), silkworm moth, makes the moth explicit; **さなぎ**, pupa, names a developmental stage, not the cocoon covering.

Use **かいこ** for this standalone word. 養蚕 (ようさん), silkworm raising or sericulture, has a different whole-word reading and names the activity. The character's other readings do not make さん or てん interchangeable answers for this article's かいこ.
