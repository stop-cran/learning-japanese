# ざあざあ — heavy rushing rain or water

## Meaning

A strong, sustained rushing sound, especially heavy rain or plentiful flowing water. It can also describe static or white noise.

- 外では雨がざあざあと降っている。 — Outside, the rain is pouring down noisily.
- 蛇口（じゃぐち）から水がざあざあ流れていた。 — Water was rushing from the tap.
- ラジオからざあざあという雑音（ざつおん）が聞こえる。 — I can hear a rushing static noise from the radio.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[しとしと](しとしと.md) is soft, gentle rain. [ぱらぱら](ぱらぱら.md) and [ぽつぽつ](ぽつぽつ.md) suggest more separated or scattered drops. [ぐつぐつ](ぐつぐつ.md) concerns bubbling cooking liquid rather than rushing flow.

## Distinctive meaning

ざあざあ降る, ざあざあと流れる, and ざあざあという音 are useful. It evokes an acoustic impression, not a measured rainfall rate. In the radio example there is no water: the similarity is the broad rushing noise. Do not assume that every long-vowel mimetic must use a long-vowel mark in hiragana.
