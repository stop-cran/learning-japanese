# 突然 (とつぜん) — suddenly; abrupt

## Meaning

突然 describes something happening abruptly or unexpectedly.
It can go directly before an event as an adverb, without adding に.
A useful pattern before a noun is 突然の, as in 突然の知らせ (とつぜんのしらせ), "unexpected news."

- 突然、雨が降り始めました。 (とつぜん、あめがふりはじめました。) — It suddenly started raining.
- 突然の電話に驚きました。 (とつぜんのでんわにおどろきました。) — I was surprised by the unexpected phone call.

JMdict also records な-adjective use; 突然の is a useful pattern to learn, not the word's only possible attachment.

## How the kanji combine

[突](../kanji/突.md) contributes abruptness here, rather than the physical action of thrusting.
[然](../kanji/然.md) helps form the expression of a state or manner.
Learn the complete on-reading compound トツ + ゼン; it does not mean "a natural collision."

## Synonyms and antonyms

[急に](急.md) (きゅうに) can also mean "suddenly." 急 has additional senses such as urgent or steep,
so not every 急 expression can be replaced with 突然.

[偶然](偶然.md) ぐうぜん means chance or coincidence.
Meeting a friend 偶然 emphasizes that the meeting happened by chance;
an event described as 突然 emphasizes its abrupt or unexpected occurrence.
The two ideas can coincide but are not the same.

## Distinctive meaning

突然 concerns how an event happens or strikes the observer, not how quickly someone performs every part of an action.
In the rain example, the onset is sudden; the word does not measure the speed of each raindrop.
Nor does suddenness by itself explain the event's cause.
