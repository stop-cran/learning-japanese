# 享受 (きょうじゅ) - enjoyment of benefits or rights

## Meaning

享受 means receiving something as one's own or receiving and enjoying it, especially benefits, freedom or rights. It is a noun and forms the transitive verb **享受する**. What is received or enjoyed takes を: **自由を享受する** (じゆうをきょうじゅする), "enjoy freedom."

- 私たちは自由を享受しています。 (わたしたちはじゆうをきょうじゅしています。) - We enjoy freedom.
- 私たちは科学技術の恩恵を享受しています。 (わたしたちはかがくぎじゅつのおんけいをきょうじゅしています。) - We enjoy the benefits of science and technology.

Useful forms include 享受します (きょうじゅします), 享受して (きょうじゅして), and 享受した (きょうじゅした). The word does not require the benefit to be money or a gift deliberately handed over by someone.

## How the kanji combine

[享](../kanji/享.md) contributes receiving and enjoying; [受](../kanji/受.md) contributes receiving. Their related meanings reinforce taking something in and enjoying its benefits. The on readings are キョウ + ジュ.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[楽しむ](楽しむ.md) (たのしむ) is a broader verb for enjoying an activity or experience. [受ける](受ける.md) (うける) covers receiving or undergoing, including unwelcome experiences. Neither is freely interchangeable with 享受する in every sentence.

[恩恵](恩恵.md) (おんけい) names a benefit or blessing; 享受 names receiving or enjoying it. [利益](利益.md) (りえき) can name profit or a broader benefit. Losing a right is a contextual contrast, not a single antonym for every use of 享受.

## Distinctive meaning

The focus is having or enjoying something available to a recipient, rather than merely playing or feeling happy. For ordinary recreational enjoyment, learn the relevant activity with 楽しむ rather than treating 享受する as a replacement for every English "enjoy."

Do not confuse it with [教授](教授.md), also きょうじゅ: that word means "professor" or "instruction." The shared pronunciation does not make the words related in meaning.
