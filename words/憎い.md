# 憎い (にくい) - hateful; ironically admirable

## Meaning

憎い is an i-adjective meaning hateful, detestable or provoking.
It describes the target of the speaker's hostile feeling.

- あの人が憎いです。 (あのひとがにくいです。) - I hate that person.
- 憎い演出ですね。 (にくいえんしゅつですね。) - That is a clever touch!

The second example assumes an approving tone. 憎い can also express admiration:
something is so clever or well done that the speaker almost resents it.
It is not invariably an accusation of hatred.

## How the kanji combine

[憎](../kanji/憎.md) writes the native stem にく-, followed by い.
The negative is 憎くない (にくくない), and the past is 憎かった
(にくかった). The related verb [憎む](憎む.md) (にくむ) means "hate; detest."

## Synonyms and antonyms

[憎らしい](憎らしい.md) (にくらしい) overlaps strongly, including ironic uses;
do not invent a fixed intensity ranking between them.
[嫌い](嫌い.md) (きらい) expresses dislike with different grammar.
[すばらしい](すばらしい.md), "wonderful," gives a more directly positive evaluation.

## Distinctive meaning

Tone and the surrounding words determine whether the speaker condemns or admires.
Do not use 憎い as a universally neutral compliment.
The separate ending -にくい after a verb stem means "hard to do," as in
読みにくい (よみにくい), "hard to read." That ending is not a claim
that reading is hateful.
