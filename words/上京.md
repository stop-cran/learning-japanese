# 上京 (じょうきょう) - going to Tokyo

## Meaning

上京 means going to the capital; in contemporary Japanese contexts, it refers to going to Tokyo.
It is a noun and an intransitive する verb.
It can describe a move for work or study, but does not itself require a permanent move.

- 大学に入るために上京しました。 (だいがくにはいるためにじょうきょうしました。) - I went to Tokyo to attend university.
- 来週、仕事で上京します。 (らいしゅう、しごとでじょうきょうします。) - I will go to Tokyo on business next week.

The first example gives a purpose with ために. The second gives the reason for travel with で.
上京する already identifies the destination, so a separate 東京へ is unnecessary in these sentences.

## How the kanji combine

[上](../kanji/上.md) contributes "go up," and [京](../kanji/京.md) means capital.
The compound presents travel to the capital as going up to it.
This is not a claim about a northward route or higher elevation.
The on readings are ジョウ + キョウ.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[行く](行く.md) (いく) is the general verb "go"; 東京へ行く is a straightforward alternative.
[旅行](旅行.md) (りょこう) means travel and does not select Tokyo as the destination.
Returning home may contrast with 上京 in a story, but it is not the word's universal opposite.

## Distinctive meaning

上京 frames the trip as coming to Tokyo from elsewhere, not every small journey made within Tokyo.
Do not translate 京 as Kyoto automatically in this compound.
When reading historical material, the capital context may differ from contemporary use.
