# かるた (かるた) - karuta; Japanese playing cards

## Meaning

かるた names traditional Japanese playing cards and the game played with them. Familiar examples include いろはかるた and 百人一首のかるた (ひゃくにんいっしゅのかるた).

- 正月に家族でかるたをしました。 (しょうがつにかぞくでかるたをしました。) - My family played karuta at New Year.
- 読まれた言葉を聞いて、かるたの札を取りました。 (よまれたことばをきいて、かるたのふだをとりました。) - I listened to the words being read and took a karuta card.

The second example illustrates a reading-and-taking game; it is not a complete rulebook for every kind of karuta.

## How the kanji combine

The word comes from Portuguese *carta*. This loanword is written in hiragana here; loanword origin does not require every Japanese spelling to be katakana. カルタ is another recorded kana form.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[カード](カード.md) is broader "card," including non-game cards. [トランプ](トランプ.md) usually names Western-style playing cards. A simple English "playing cards" label can hide that distinction, so learn the kind of cards and game.

## Distinctive meaning

The source's generic English gloss is broader than this practical Japanese-karuta lesson. Do not infer that かるた means any credit card, or impose the reading-and-taking example on all historical card games. The dictionary's traditional-card sense is the starting point here.
