# どこか (どこか) - somewhere; in some way

## Meaning

どこか refers to an unspecified place: "somewhere," or often "anywhere" in an
English question. Add the particle needed by the sentence, such as に for a
destination or で for the place of an action.

- 週末はどこかに行きたいです。 (しゅうまつはどこかにいきたいです。) - I want to go somewhere at the weekend.
- どこかで会ったことがありますか。 (どこかであったことがありますか。) - Have we met somewhere before?

It can also mean "in some way": この絵はどこか変です
(このえはどこかへんです), "There is something odd about this picture."

## How the kanji combine

The question word [どこ](どこ.md), "where," combines with か to form an
indefinite expression. It does not become a question merely because it
contains か. The dictionary also lists the rare spelling 何処か.

## Synonyms and antonyms

どこ asks which place; どこか leaves the place unspecified.
どこにも + negative means "nowhere/not anywhere," a different construction
from どこか. It is not made simply by negating an arbitrary どこか sentence.

## Distinctive meaning

どこかに行きませんか means "Would you like to go somewhere?" despite its
negative verb ending. The invitation pattern determines that interpretation.
Learn the whole sentence rather than mapping English "anywhere" to one
Japanese form regardless of question, invitation, or negation.
