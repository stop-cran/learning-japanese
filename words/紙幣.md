# 紙幣 (しへい) - paper money; banknotes

## Meaning

紙幣 is paper money: banknotes, bills, or notes, as distinct from coins. It is a noun.

- 紙幣を財布に入れました。
  (しへいをさいふにいれました。) - I put the banknotes in my wallet.
- この機械では、紙幣も硬貨も使えます。
  (このきかいでは、しへいもこうかもつかえます。) - This machine accepts both banknotes and coins.

Individual banknotes can be counted with 枚 (まい): 紙幣三枚 (しへいさんまい), "three banknotes." That count does not by itself give their monetary value.

## How the kanji combine

[紙](../kanji/紙.md) contributes paper and 幣 money or currency. This is a modifier + head compound: currency in paper-note form. The on readings are シ + ヘイ, しへい; keep the final い in the spelling.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[札](札.md) (さつ) is also a banknote, especially useful with a denomination such as 千円札 (せんえんさつ), "a 1,000-yen note."

[お金](お金.md) (おかね) is money more broadly. [現金](現金.md) (げんきん), "cash," includes coins as well as banknotes. [硬貨](硬貨.md) (こうか), "coins," contrasts by physical form; it is not a synonym for having no money.

## Distinctive meaning

English "notes" here does not mean a [ノート](ノート.md), notebook, or written reminders. "Bills" does not mean invoices or payment requests. Nor is any valuable sheet of paper automatically 紙幣: tickets, certificates, and banknotes serve different functions.
