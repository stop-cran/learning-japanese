# ごくろうさま - thank you for your hard work

## Meaning

ごくろうさま acknowledges someone's effort or work.
It is usually said to someone junior or subordinate, not used as
a general-purpose polite thank-you to anyone.

- 上司が部下に「ごくろうさま」と言いました。
  (じょうしがぶかに「ごくろうさま」といいました。) - The supervisor said to the subordinate, "Thank you for your hard work."
- 配達、ごくろうさまです。
  (はいたつ、ごくろうさまです。) - Thank you for making the delivery.

ごくろうさまでした acknowledges completed work. Adding です or
でした makes the ending polite, but does not erase the relationship
implied by the expression.

## How the kanji combine

The dictionary also records ご苦労様 and ご苦労さま.
The polite prefix ご precedes [苦労](苦労.md) (くろう), effort or
hardship, and the respectful ending 様 (さま).
The kango category here follows the Sino-Japanese base 苦労;
the entire expression is not a sequence of on readings.
Its kana spelling has no written-kanji metadata.

## Synonyms and antonyms

お疲れ様 (おつかれさま) also acknowledges work and is the usual
workplace choice with colleagues and superiors. [ありがとう](ありがとう.md)
expresses thanks more generally. [感謝](感謝.md) (かんしゃ) and
[お礼](御礼.md) (おれい) name gratitude rather than this particular greeting.

## Distinctive meaning

Do not choose ごくろうさま for a superior merely because an English
list says "Thank you very much." Its respectful-looking pieces do
not make it an honorific formula for every relationship.
The usual junior-directed pattern is a usage tendency, not a claim
that every real interaction follows one hierarchy rule.
