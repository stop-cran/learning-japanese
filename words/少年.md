# 少年 (しょうねん) - boy; juvenile

## Meaning

少年 is a noun meaning "boy." It can identify one boy or boys as a group;
Japanese does not require a plural ending here.
In legal contexts, it also has a broader "juvenile" or "child" sense.

- 一人の少年が公園で本を読んでいました。 (ひとりのしょうねんがこうえんでほんをよんでいました。) - A boy was reading a book in the park.
- 彼は少年のころから野球が好きでした。 (かれはしょうねんのころからやきゅうがすきでした。) - He had liked baseball since he was a boy.

少年のころ means "when one was a boy." It locates a period of life rather than giving an exact age.

## How the kanji combine

[少](../kanji/少.md) contributes youth or fewness, and [年](../kanji/年.md) contributes years.
Together they suggest someone of young years. The on readings are ショウ + ネン.
The first syllable is long: しょうねん, not しょねん.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[少女](少女.md) (しょうじょ), "girl," is the everyday gender contrast.
[子供](子供.md) (こども), "child," is broader.
[青年](青年.md) (せいねん) concerns a young person at a different life stage, but these everyday
categories do not themselves supply precise legal age boundaries.

## Distinctive meaning

Keep everyday and technical contexts separate. The ordinary "boy" gloss is useful in stories
and descriptions, but it does not prove that every legal occurrence excludes girls.
When a legal definition matters, consult the relevant law and context rather than inferring an
age range or gender restriction from the everyday gloss.
