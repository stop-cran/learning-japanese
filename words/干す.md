# 干す (ほす) - hang out to dry; air out

## Meaning

干す means to put something out to dry or air it. It is transitive:
the item being dried or aired takes を.

- 洗濯物を干した。 (せんたくものをほした。) - I hung out the laundry to dry.
- 布団を干した。 (ふとんをほした。) - I aired the futon.

The action does not itself guarantee that the item has already become dry.

## How the kanji combine

[干](../kanji/干.md) supplies ほ, and す completes the native verb. It is godan:
干します (ほします), 干して (ほして), 干した (ほした).
The written ending does not by itself identify the whole conjugation base.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[乾かす](乾かす.md) (かわかす), dry something, describes causing dryness more generally.
[乾く](乾く.md) (かわく), become dry, is intransitive: 洗濯物が乾いた
(せんたくものがかわいた), "the laundry dried." Contrast the drying thing marked
with が there and the object marked with を in 洗濯物を干す.

## Distinctive meaning

In the household use taught here, 干す focuses on putting laundry or bedding
out for drying or airing. It is not simply a spelling variant of 乾く or
a statement that an object is dry. 乾す is another dictionary spelling of ほす.

Recognize three further uses without losing the household distinction:
draining a pond or well, drinking a cup completely, and depriving someone of
work or a role. 杯を干す (さかずきをほす) is to drain one's cup, not to dry
the vessel with a towel. The work/role use is usually passive:
仕事を干された (しごとをほされた), "I was frozen out of work."
It is not simply the ordinary verb for losing any possession.
