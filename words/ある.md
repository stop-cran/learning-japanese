# ある (ある) — exist; have

## Meaning

ある expresses **existence or possession**, usually for things rather than people or animals. Use place + に + thing + が + ある for location. In possession statements, the thing possessed still normally takes が, not を.

- 机の上に本があります。 (つくえのうえにほんがあります。) — There is a book on the desk.
- 今日は時間があります。 (きょうはじかんがあります。) — I have time today.

This is an irregular godan verb: polite あります, past あった, and て-form あって. Its ordinary plain negative is **ない**, not あらない; the polite negative is ありません.

## How the kanji combine

Kana is usual. The spellings [有](../kanji/有.md)る and [在](../kanji/在.md)る also exist, but the basic patterns do not require choosing between them.

## Synonyms and antonyms

いる describes people and animals being somewhere. 持っている (もっている) emphasizes possessing or holding something; it is not a substitute for every existential ある.

## Distinctive meaning

ある can also say an event takes place: 明日、試験があります (あした、しけんがあります), “There is an exam tomorrow.” English “have” therefore does not imply that Japanese uses a transitive verb here.
