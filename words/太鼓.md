# 太鼓 (たいこ) - drum

## Meaning

太鼓 is a drum. The word is particularly familiar for Japanese drums,
but it is not restricted to one particular size, shape, or performance.
太鼓をたたく means to beat or play a drum.

- 祭りで太鼓をたたきました。 (まつりでたいこをたたきました。) - I played a drum at the festival.
- 遠くから太鼓の音が聞こえます。 (とおくからたいこのおとがきこえます。) - I can hear the sound of a drum in the distance.

The instrument takes を with たたく; 太鼓の音 means the sound of the drum.

## How the kanji combine

[太](../kanji/太.md) contributes thickness or stoutness; 鼓 names a drum.
These character associations help recall the instrument, but they are not
a requirement that every 太鼓 be physically large or an etymology of its
invention. The on readings are タイ + コ.

## Synonyms and antonyms

ドラム is a loanword also used for drums, including a drum kit in the right
context. 和太鼓 (わだいこ) specifies Japanese drums. タンバリン is
"tambourine"; learn that specific instrument name rather than treating the
source list's loose English gloss as a rule that every tambourine is called
太鼓 in ordinary conversation.

## Distinctive meaning

太鼓 alone does not specify the instrument's material, playing technique,
or musical tradition. Nor does it mean percussion instruments in general.
The surrounding word or situation supplies those details.
