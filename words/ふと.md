# ふと (ふと) - unexpectedly; without thinking

## Meaning

ふと is an adverb for something happening unexpectedly, casually, or without deliberate intention.
It often describes a thought, realization, or small action that arises spontaneously.
Place it before the verb or clause it modifies; it does not need an added に.

- ふと昔の友人を思い出しました。 (ふとむかしのゆうじんをおもいだしました。) - An old friend suddenly came to mind.
- ふと窓の外を見ると、雪が降っていました。 (ふとまどのそとをみると、ゆきがふっていました。) - I happened to glance out the window and saw that it was snowing.

In the second sentence, ふと describes the unplanned glance, not necessarily a sudden start to the snowfall.

## How the kanji combine

The teaching form is kana **ふと**.
JMdict records 不図 and 不斗 as rare ateji spellings.
The community source used 不図, but that does not make it the preferred learner spelling.
Do not derive the adverb's meaning or history from a literal interpretation of those characters.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[突然](突然.md) (とつぜん) emphasizes abrupt or unexpected occurrence more broadly.
[偶然](偶然.md) (ぐうぜん) emphasizes chance or coincidence.
[わざと](わざと.md), "deliberately," contrasts with the unintentional aspect in suitable contexts.

## Distinctive meaning

ふと does not necessarily describe a dramatic event: an ordinary memory or momentary glance is enough.
Translate the relationship to intention and awareness, rather than mechanically using "suddenly" every time.
It also is not a universal substitute for "accidentally" when describing damage or a serious mishap.
