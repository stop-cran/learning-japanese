# ごめん - sorry; no thanks

## Meaning

ごめん is a short, familiar apology. It also appears in refusals with **noun or nominalized action + はごめんだ**: "I want no part of that" or "No thanks to that."

- 遅れてごめん。 (おくれてごめん。) - Sorry I'm late.
- そんな仕事はごめんだ。 (そんなしごとはごめんだ。) - I want no part of that kind of work.

The second sentence is a rejection, not an apology to the job. It can sound forceful.

## How the kanji combine

御免 (ごめん) is the recognized kanji spelling, but the entry marks the word as usually written in kana. [御](../kanji/御.md) supplies ご, and 免 concerns excusing or releasing. These associations do not make the shortened apology formally respectful in every situation.

The noun 御免 also has permission/authorization and release-from-a-post meanings in official, set, or older language. Those meanings help explain the source's broader glosses; they are not a rule that casual ごめん grants a modern license.

- 名字帯刀が御免になる (みょうじたいとうがごめんになる) - be permitted to use a surname and wear swords, a historical authorization
- お役が御免になる (おやくがごめんになる) - be relieved of one's duties

## Synonyms and antonyms

[ごめんなさい](ごめんなさい.md) is the fuller apology. [すみません](すみません.md) is useful for a polite apology or attracting attention. [謝る](謝る.md) (あやまる) names the action of apologizing; ごめん is what one may say.

[断る](断る.md) (ことわる) reports refusing. ～はごめんだ directly expresses the speaker's unwillingness. [許す](許す.md) (ゆるす), "forgive," can describe the other person's response, not the apology itself.

[お待ちどおさま](お待ちどおさま.md) and [お待たせしました](お待たせしました.md) specifically acknowledge having kept someone waiting; ごめん is a broader familiar apology. [あいにく](あいにく.md), "unfortunately; sorry, but," frames an inconvenient circumstance. It is not a general substitute for apologizing or for the direct rejection ～はごめんだ.

## Distinctive meaning

The dictionary also records ごめん as a visiting or leave-taking greeting. Digital Daijisen's short familiar visiting example is ごめん、お邪魔するよ (ごめん、おじゃまするよ), "Excuse me, I'm coming in." Recognize this use, but learn the fuller [ごめんください](ごめんください.md) for calling at someone's door rather than treating bare ごめん as a universally polite entrance formula.

For an apology to a superior or a serious professional apology, the bare short form can be too casual. Choose the complete expression for the relationship, not from the origin of 御 alone.
