# 冷たい (つめたい) — cold to the touch

## Meaning

冷たい describes coldness experienced through contact, as with water, food, or a hand.
It is an い-adjective: 冷たい水 (つめたいみず) is "cold water," and 水が冷たい says "the water is cold."
It can also describe emotionally cold or unfriendly treatment.

- この水は冷たいです。 (このみずはつめたいです。) — This water is cold.
- 彼は私に冷たいです。 (かれはわたしにつめたいです。) — He is cold toward me.

The person marked with に in the second sentence is the recipient of the cold treatment, not an object being refrigerated.

## How the kanji combine

[冷](../kanji/冷.md) supplies coldness, and たい completes this native adjective.
Do not analyze this ending as the separate ～たい pattern meaning "want to."
The past is 冷たかった (つめたかった), and the negative is 冷たくない (つめたくない).

## Synonyms and antonyms

[寒い](寒い.md) さむい describes cold weather or feeling cold, rather than the temperature of a drink against your mouth.
[熱い](熱い.md) あつい, "hot to the touch," is a useful physical-temperature opposite.
[優しい](優しい.md) やさしい, "kind; gentle," provides a contrast for the emotional sense.

## Distinctive meaning

Choose the expression for the situation: 冷たい水 is cold water, while 今日は寒い
(きょうはさむい), "It is cold today," describes the day's conditions.
The emotional meaning is another established sense of the same adjective, not a claim about body temperature.
