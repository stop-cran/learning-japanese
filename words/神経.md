# 神経 (しんけい) - nerve; sensitivity

## Meaning

神経 is a noun for a nerve in the body. It also refers to nerves or sensitivity in expressions
about attention, strain, and consideration.
Learn the physical and figurative constructions separately.

- 歯の神経を取る治療を受けました。 (はのしんけいをとるちりょうをうけました。) - I had treatment to remove the nerve tissue in a tooth.
- 人混みでの運転は神経を使います。 (ひとごみでのうんてんはしんけいをつかいます。) - Driving through crowds requires a lot of care and concentration.

神経を使う literally has "use nerves" as its structure, but idiomatically concerns mental
attention or strain, not physically using a particular named nerve.

## How the kanji combine

[神](../kanji/神.md) has spirit or mind associations, and [経](../kanji/経.md) has thread or pathway associations.
These give a way to remember the established anatomical compound; its modern meaning is not
simply "a god's scripture." The readings are シン + ケイ.
This component guide is not a historical account of how the term was coined.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[心配](心配.md) (しんぱい), "worry," can accompany 神経を使う, but careful concentration does not
necessarily mean anxiety. [気持ち](気持ち.md) (きもち), "feeling," is broader and is not a name
for anatomical nerves.

## Distinctive meaning

The noun alone does not translate every English use of "nerve."
In particular, English "have the nerve to do something" needs its own contextual Japanese expression.
In 神経を使う, the issue may be attentive care for others, difficult conditions, or mental strain;
the surrounding situation determines the translation.
