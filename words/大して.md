# 大して (たいして) - not very; not particularly

## Meaning

In ordinary modern usage, 大して accompanies a negative predicate:
"not very," "not much," or "not particularly." It modifies the degree of
the following description or action.

- この問題は大して難しくありません。 (このもんだいはたいしてむずかしくありません。) - This problem is not very difficult.
- 昨日は大して歩きませんでした。 (きのうはたいしてあるきませんでした。) - I did not walk very much yesterday.

The first negative is 難しくありません; the second is 歩きませんでした.
Do not drop the negative ending while keeping the same English meaning.
大して changes neither the adjective nor the verb's conjugation.

## How the kanji combine

[大](../kanji/大.md) contributes degree or magnitude and is read タイ here.
Learn the whole established adverb 大して; the ending is not a productive
instruction to turn arbitrary kanji into adverbs with して.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[あまり](あまり.md) and [あんまり](あんまり.md) overlap in negative
constructions. [それほど](それほど.md) and [そんなに](そんなに.md) can
mean "to that extent," with the predicate determining polarity.
[別に](別に.md) (べつに) also occurs with negatives for "not particularly."

## Distinctive meaning

大してない does not mean absolutely none: 大して歩かなかった can still
describe walking a little. The modern negative-construction rule here is
practical guidance, not a claim that affirmative historical 大して never
existed. Do not confuse it with [大層](大層.md) (たいそう), an ordinary
degree word meaning "very."
