# なんとも - really; not at all

## Meaning

なんとも has two important adverbial uses. Before an evaluation it can mean
"really; very; extremely." With a negative predicate it can mean
"not anything; not at all." Read the whole expression rather than giving
every occurrence the same English translation.

- なんとも美しい景色ですね。 (なんともうつくしいけしきですね。) - What a truly beautiful view!
- そんなことはなんとも思いません。 (そんなことはなんともおもいません。) - I do not care about that at all.

なんともない can mean that nothing is wrong or that something has had no effect.
なんとも言えない (なんともいえない) can mean "I cannot say/judge" or
"it is hard to describe"; context determines what cannot be said.

## How the kanji combine

The spelling 何とも uses [何](../kanji/何.md), read なん here, followed by とも.
The kana spelling is also useful to recognize. The adverb itself does not conjugate.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[とても](とても.md) and [非常に](非常に.md) (ひじょうに) overlap with the degree
use. [全然](全然.md) (ぜんぜん) and [ちっとも](ちっとも.md) overlap with the
negative "not at all" use, but they do not replace every fixed expression.

## Distinctive meaning

The negative in なんとも思わない belongs to 思わない: なんとも alone does
not invariably mean "nothing." Likewise, なんとも言えない does not necessarily
mean "extremely good"; the speaker may genuinely lack enough information to judge.
Do not confuse this word with [なんとなく](なんとなく.md), which leaves a
reason or purpose unclear.
