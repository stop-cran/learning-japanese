# しみじみ (しみじみ) - deeply felt; quietly reflective

## Meaning

しみじみ is an adverb for feeling or appreciating something deeply and keenly. It often presents a heartfelt realization rather than merely a high degree of something. It can modify a verb with or without と.

- 家族の大切さをしみじみと感じました。
  (かぞくのたいせつさをしみじみとかんじました。) - I felt deeply how important my family is.
- 久しぶりに会って、友達としみじみ話しました。
  (ひさしぶりにあって、ともだちとしみじみはなしました。) - Meeting again after a long time, I had a quiet, heartfelt talk with my friend.

The second example illustrates calmly or intimately facing a person or subject. The dictionary also records fixed, intent looking: **しみじみと眺める** (しみじみとながめる), "gaze intently." The following verb determines which aspect is foregrounded.

## How the kanji combine

This article uses kana, the usual spelling in JMdict. It also records the rarely used written forms 沁み沁み, 染み染み, and 沁沁. Their recognition does not require spelling the ordinary examples in kanji or constructing a literal account of emotional depth from the characters.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[しんみり](しんみり.md) overlaps in quiet, heartfelt reflection and can suggest a subdued or sentimental mood. しみじみ especially foregrounds deep appreciation or feeling; neither word requires that the feeling be sad.

[じっと](じっと.md), "intently; still," overlaps in sustained looking. [深い](深い.md) (ふかい) supplies the broader idea of depth, but the adverb しみじみ is not an adjective to put directly before a noun.

## Distinctive meaning

しみじみと感じる and しみじみ感じる are both recorded attachment patterns. An attested noun-modifying construction is **しみじみとした気持ち** (しみじみとしたきもち), "a deeply felt, reflective feeling." Learn this construction separately: the principal entry's adverb label is not a blanket ban on forms containing する, nor a license to add する to every use.

It is not an interchangeable degree intensifier for every "very," "completely," or "often." A heartfelt realization, an intimate conversation, and fixed gazing share an attentive quality, but the gloss appropriate to one does not automatically fit the others.
