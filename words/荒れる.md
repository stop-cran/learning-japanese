# 荒れる (あれる) - become rough; fall into disorder

## Meaning

荒れる describes becoming rough, stormy, neglected or disordered.
It is an intransitive ichidan verb: the thing whose condition changes
is marked with が. The specific English translation depends on that thing.

- 海が荒れています。 (うみがあれています。) - The sea is rough.
- 冬は肌が荒れやすいです。 (ふゆははだがあれやすいです。) - My skin tends to get rough in winter.
- 会議が荒れました。 (かいぎがあれました。) - The meeting became unruly.

The dictionary also records neglected or dilapidated places and an
unsettled life. Not every use describes weather.

Forms include 荒れます (あれます), 荒れて (あれて) and
荒れない (あれない). Remove the final る to obtain the stem 荒れ.

## How the kanji combine

[荒](../kanji/荒.md) supplies あ and the rough/disordered association;
れる is okurigana in this word. This is a single native verb,
not a compound or a passive form derived from 荒らす.

## Synonyms and antonyms

荒らす (あらす) is transitive: something is damaged or laid waste,
as in 畑を荒らす (はたけをあらす), "damage a field."
The contrast in participant structure does not make every sense a
freely substitutable verb pair.

[暴れる](暴れる.md) (あばれる) describes violent or wild behavior.
[混乱](混乱.md) (こんらん) concerns confusion or disorder;
[狂う](狂う.md) (くるう) can describe going awry or out of order.
Their meanings overlap in relevant settings without covering all of
荒れる's weather, skin and neglect uses.

[かさかさ](かさかさ.md) overlaps specifically with dry, rough skin.
[ざらざら](ざらざら.md) emphasizes a coarse or gritty texture;
its tactile roughness is another overlapping quiz label.
Neither word's sound use should be equated with
荒れる's weather or unruly-behavior senses.

[混雑](混雑.md) (こんざつ) principally means crowding or congestion
and also has a disorder sense. The exclusion protects that overlap
with the broad disorder label, not a rule that every crowded place
is unruly.

## Distinctive meaning

荒れている can present the resulting rough condition, as in the sea
example; it need not mean that the change is occurring at that instant.
Choose the whole construction and subject. The skin example does not
describe the skin behaving violently, and a noisy gathering is not
automatically a storm at sea.
