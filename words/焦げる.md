# 焦げる (こげる) - get scorched; burn; char

## Meaning

焦げる describes something becoming scorched, singed, or charred.
It is an intransitive ichidan verb: the affected thing takes が.
Forms include 焦げます, 焦げた, 焦げて, and 焦げない.

- パンが少し焦げました。
  (パンがすこしこげました。) - The bread got a little burnt.
- 鍋の底が焦げてしまいました。
  (なべのそこがこげてしまいました。) - The bottom of the pot got scorched.

焦げている describes a scorched condition. English "be burned" here
does not make the Japanese verb a passive conjugation.

## How the kanji combine

焦 contributes scorching or charring. It writes こ here, followed
by the okurigana げる. This is a native verb, not an on-reading compound.
The related transitive verb is [焦がす](焦がす.md) (こがす).

## Synonyms and antonyms

[焼ける](焼ける.md) (やける) also concerns burning or becoming cooked,
but パンが焼けた can describe properly baked bread.
[燃える](燃える.md) (もえる) concerns burning or being on fire;
a scorched surface need not be ablaze.
[焼く](焼く.md) (やく) is transitive and can describe baking or burning.

## Distinctive meaning

Compare パンを焦がした (パンをこがした), "I scorched the bread,"
with パンが焦げた, "The bread got scorched."
The transitive/intransitive distinction changes the construction,
not just the English translation. 焦げる does not itself identify
who caused the scorching or whether it was intentional.
