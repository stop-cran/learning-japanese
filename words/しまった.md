# しまった (しまった) - oops; oh no

## Meaning

しまった is an exclamation on realizing a mistake, missed opportunity, or unwelcome fact. Depending on the situation, English "Oops!", "Oh no!", or "Darn!" can fit. It need not be an obscenity.

- しまった、鍵を家に忘れた。 (しまった、かぎをいえにわすれた。) - Oh no, I left my keys at home.
- 「しまった、名前を書き忘れた。」「まだ間に合うよ。」 (「しまった、なまえをかきわすれた。」「まだまにあうよ。」) - "Oops, I forgot to write my name." "There's still time."

As an interjection it stands outside the following clause; it does not take an object or require a separate verb.

## How the kanji combine

The usual spelling is kana. The source list uses 仕舞った, a dictionary-recorded rare kanji spelling. The form is related to the past form of しまう, but this card teaches the standalone exclamation.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[失敗](失敗.md) (しっぱい) names a mistake or failure rather than the sudden reaction to noticing it.

[残念](残念.md) (ざんねん), "unfortunate; regrettable," can express disappointment without implying that the speaker has just discovered their own mistake.

## Distinctive meaning

しまった is not automatically an apology to another person. A speaker may exclaim しまった to themselves, then separately apologize if they caused trouble. Choose the English strength from the event and tone rather than treating the community list's "Damn it!" as mandatory.
