# 見送る (みおくる) - see off; let pass; postpone

## Meaning

見送る can mean to accompany someone to their departure point or to watch someone or something go away.

- 駅で友達を見送りました。 (えきでともだちをみおくりました。) - I saw my friend off at the station.
- 船が見えなくなるまで見送りました。 (ふねがみえなくなるまでみおくりました。) - I watched the ship until it disappeared from view.

It also means to let something pass or to defer a decision or action.

- 混んでいたので、バスを一本見送りました。 (こんでいたので、バスをいっぽんみおくりました。) - It was crowded, so I let one bus go by.
- 新しい計画の実施を見送りました。 (あたらしいけいかくのじっしをみおくりました。) - We deferred putting the new plan into effect.

Further uses concern caring for someone until death or attending their funeral, and holding off buying or selling while watching the market. These are context-dependent uses, not the meaning of every departure sentence.

It is a transitive godan verb: 見送ります, 見送って, 見送らない.

## How the kanji combine

[見](../kanji/見.md), "see," combines with [送る](送る.md) (おくる), "send; escort," written with [送](../kanji/送.md). Both parts use native readings: み + おくる.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[見送り](見送り.md) (みおくり) is the corresponding noun, including a send-off or postponement. [延期](延期.md) (えんき) explicitly names postponement. [中止](中止.md) (ちゅうし) is cancellation or discontinuation; it is not an automatic translation of every 見送る.

## Distinctive meaning

The object and setting matter. 人を見送る can be a farewell; バスを見送る can be deliberately not boarding; 計画を見送る can be shelving a plan. Shelving does not by itself promise a new date, nor always mean permanent cancellation.
