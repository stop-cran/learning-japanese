# 草履 (ぞうり) - zori; traditional Japanese sandals

## Meaning

草履 are traditional Japanese thonged sandals, with a relatively flat
sole and a thong held between the toes.
Use 草履を履く (ぞうりをはく) for putting them on or wearing them.

- 着物に合わせて草履を選びました。 (きものにあわせてぞうりをえらびました。) - I chose zori to go with my kimono.
- 草履を履いて庭に出ました。 (ぞうりをはいてにわにでました。) - I put on zori and went out into the garden.

The noun does not change for one sandal or a pair.
The footwear counter 足 (そく) counts pairs:
草履一足 (ぞうりいっそく), one pair of zori.

## How the kanji combine

[草](../kanji/草.md) contributes a grass association, and
履 footwear or wearing it.
Learn the complete reading ぞうり rather than reading 草 as くさ here.
The spelling does not require every modern pair to be made of grass.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[サンダル](サンダル.md) means sandals more broadly.
[下駄](下駄.md) (げた) are traditional wooden sandals, typically raised
on supports; 草履 are not another spelling of 下駄.
[履く](履く.md) (はく) is the verb for putting this footwear on.

## Distinctive meaning

Traditional and modern materials vary: straw and other plant materials
as well as synthetic materials are recorded.
The flat-soled form is a more useful distinction from typical geta than
an absolute "grass versus wood" material rule.
Use the current primary reading ぞうり; dictionary じょうり is outdated.
