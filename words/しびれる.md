# しびれる (しびれる) - become numb; be enthralled

## Meaning

しびれる is to become numb or lose normal sensation, as when a limb "goes to sleep." It is an intransitive ichidan verb: **足がしびれる** (あしがしびれる), "one's foot/leg becomes numb."

- 長く正座していたので、足がしびれました。
  (ながくせいざしていたので、あしがしびれました。) - My legs went numb because I had been sitting in seiza for a long time.
- 指先がしびれて、ボタンを留めにくいです。
  (ゆびさきがしびれて、ボタンをとめにくいです。) - My fingertips are numb, making it hard to fasten buttons.

正座 (せいざ) is the upright kneeling sitting position. The affected body part takes が; this verb does not mean making someone else's limb numb. Its forms include しびれます, しびれて, and しびれない.

The word also describes tingling from an electric shock. Figuratively, **演奏にしびれる** (えんそうにしびれる) means "be enthralled by a performance," not that the performance caused a medical loss of sensation.

## How the kanji combine

Kana is the usual spelling in this dictionary entry. 痺れる is also recorded, with 痺 representing しび and れる written in kana. It is one native verb, not a compound of separate character meanings.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[じんじん](じんじん.md) and [びりびり](びりびり.md) can describe tingling sensations; びりびり also has an electric-shock use. しびれる is a verb naming the condition; those mimetics describe how the sensation is experienced.

[感動](感動.md) (かんどう), "being deeply moved," overlaps with the figurative emotional use.

## Distinctive meaning

English says that a leg "goes to sleep," but [寝る](寝る.md) (ねる) and [眠る](眠る.md) (ねむる) ordinarily describe sleeping, not this bodily sensation. Numbness and [痛い](痛い.md) (いたい), "painful," can occur together, but one does not simply mean the other.
