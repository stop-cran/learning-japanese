# 歌舞伎 (かぶき) — kabuki

## Meaning

歌舞伎 is kabuki, a traditional Japanese stage form of drama and music. JMdict describes it as “performed by male actors wearing makeup mainly in white and red”. Digital Daijisen calls it a theater unique to Japan that arose at the start of the early modern period and grew up in Edo-period culture; it gathered dance, music, spoken drama and other earlier arts into one popular theater that continues today.

- 週末に歌舞伎を見に行きました。
  (しゅうまつにかぶきをみにいきました。)
  "I went to see kabuki on the weekend."
- 初めて歌舞伎を見て、とても感動しました。
  (はじめてかぶきをみて、とてもかんどうしました。)
  "I saw kabuki for the first time and was very moved."

These are original usage examples, not quotations from a dictionary or other source.

## How the kanji combine

[歌](../kanji/歌.md) (カ) contributes singing; [舞](../kanji/舞.md) (ブ) contributes dancing; [伎](../kanji/伎.md) (キ) contributes a performer or entertainer. The three characters together describe singing, dancing and performers; the first two also form 歌舞 かぶ, “singing and dancing”. These meanings describe the written form, not where the word かぶき itself comes from, and not the historical origin of any character.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[能](能.md) (のう), "talent; noh theatre," can name noh, a different stage art; it is not 歌舞伎. [芝居](芝居.md) (しばい), "play; drama," and [演劇](演劇.md) (えんげき), "theatre; drama," are broader: they can describe any play, while 歌舞伎 is one particular form of theater.

There is no single fixed antonym. A different kind of show is simply another art form, not an opposite word.

## Distinctive meaning

歌舞伎 names one particular stage form, not any play or show. Keep it apart from 能 and from general words for drama such as 芝居 and 演劇.

JMdict also lists the spelling 歌舞妓 and marks it as a rarely used kanji form.
