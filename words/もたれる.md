# もたれる - lean against; feel heavy on the stomach

## Meaning

もたれる is an intransitive ichidan verb. It means leaning or reclining
against a person or thing that supports one's body. It also describes
food lying heavily on the stomach or the resulting heavy stomach feeling.

- 壁にもたれて休みました。 (かべにもたれてやすみました。) - I rested leaning against the wall.
- 脂っこい物を食べると、胃がもたれます。 (あぶらっこいものをたべると、いがもたれます。) - When I eat greasy food, my stomach feels heavy.

The support takes に in the first example. In the second, 胃 is the
subject; it is not something the speaker is physically leaning against.
Forms include もたれます, もたれて, and もたれない.

## How the kanji combine

Kana is usual. 凭れる and 靠れる are rare recognized spellings.
Learn the whole native reading もたれる; do not invent readings for
pieces of the characters.

## Synonyms and antonyms

寄りかかる (よりかかる) overlaps in the physical leaning sense.
[寄る](寄る.md) (よる) also has a leaning/reclining sense beyond its
familiar approach and stop-by uses.
[消化](消化.md) (しょうか) is digestion, not the heavy feeling itself.

## Distinctive meaning

Leaning against a support is not just tilting in any direction.
The Japanese dictionary also records a clinging or dependent use,
甘えて頼る (あまえてたよる), "depend on someone indulgently."
Recognize that additional use without treating もたれる as a general
replacement for [頼る](頼る.md) (たよる) in every request for help.
