# 鮎 (あゆ) — sweetfish; ayu

## Meaning

鮎 names **ayu**, also called **sweetfish**. It is a particular fish, not a general name for small river fish.

- 川に鮎がいます。 (かわにあゆがいます。) — There are ayu in the river.
- 鮎を焼いて食べました。 (あゆをやいてたべました。) — I grilled and ate ayu.

In the first sentence, に marks the location and が identifies what is there. In the second, を marks the food; [焼く](焼く.md) やく appears as 焼いて, linking the cooking action to [食べる](食べる.md) たべる, eat.

## How the kanji combine

The single character [鮎](../kanji/鮎.md) writes the whole native noun あゆ, with no compound to split or kana ending to add. **あゆ** and **アユ** are also spellings; JMdict labels this animal name usually written in kana.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[魚](魚.md) さかな, fish, is the broader category. [鯉](鯉.md) こい, carp, is a different animal, not a synonym.

[川](川.md) かわ, river, names a habitat rather than the fish. There is no useful direct antonym for ayu.

## Distinctive meaning

Use **あゆ** for this Japanese fish noun. The character dictionary's なまず reading concerns a catfish sense, not another pronunciation of the ayu animal name. Catfish is normally なまず・ナマズ・鯰 in modern Japanese.

**鮎釣り** (あゆつり・あゆづり) means ayu fishing. Both readings belong to that complete compound; they do not alter this article's primary reading あゆ.
