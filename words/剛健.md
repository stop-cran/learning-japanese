# 剛健 (ごうけん) — robust; vigorous

## Meaning

剛健 describes being robust and strong in body or spirit. It is a noun and a na-adjective: **剛健な + noun**, or **剛健です** as a polite predicate.

Digital Daijisen gives the phrase 勤勉で剛健な気風 (きんべんでごうけんなきふう), "a hardworking and robust spirit." Here 気風 means a characteristic spirit or ethos, and な connects 剛健 to that noun.

The expression 質実剛健 (しつじつごうけん) combines being plain and sincere with fortitude and vigor. It does not simply mean being cheerful or busy.

## How the kanji combine

[剛](../kanji/剛.md) contributes strength or sturdiness, and 健 contributes health or strength. The related meanings reinforce the idea of robust vigor. The on readings here are ゴウ + ケン. This is a meaning aid, not a reconstruction of the word's history.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[丈夫](丈夫.md) じょうぶ can describe a robust constitution and also durable objects. [健康](健康.md) けんこう concerns health; [元気](元気.md) げんき can emphasize being well or energetic. These meanings overlap, but 剛健 emphasizes robust strength rather than just a momentary lively mood.

[強い](強い.md) つよい is a broader adjective for strength or intensity. [弱い](弱い.md) よわい, "weak," gives a contextual contrast, not a judgment of a person's worth. There is no single opposite covering every bodily and mental use.

## Distinctive meaning

Japanese dictionaries describe a traditional masculine association in 剛健. That association is part of its connotation, not a grammatical restriction to male subjects or a rule about who can be strong.

Learn 剛健 as an adjective/noun, not as an i-adjective or an automatic する verb. It describes a robust quality; it is not the general word for every vigorous activity or every English use of "strong."
