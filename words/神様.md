# 神様 (かみさま) - god; revered master

## Meaning

神様 is a respectful way to refer to a god or deity. The spelling 神さま has the same reading. Depending on the religious context, English may use "God" or "a god"; the Japanese word does not by itself require belief in only one deity.

- 神様に感謝しています。 (かみさまにかんしゃしています。) - I am grateful to the deity.
- 彼は料理の神様と呼ばれています。 (かれはりょうりのかみさまとよばれています。) - He is called a god of cooking.

The second example figuratively praises outstanding skill. It does not assert that the cook is literally divine.

## How the kanji combine

[神](../kanji/神.md) supplies the native noun かみ, "god," and [様](../kanji/様.md), read さま here, adds respect. Do not replace さま with the character's よう reading in this word.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[神](神.md) is the unextended deity noun. [名人](名人.md) (めいじん), "master; expert," overlaps with the skill-praising use, without the divine metaphor. [博士](博士.md) (はかせ) can praise expertise, but 神様 does not establish an academic degree.

## Distinctive meaning

Use the surrounding subject to distinguish religious reference from exceptional-person praise. Adding 様 is not a rule for converting any noun into a religious title, and the figurative use is stronger than simply saying that someone can do an activity.
