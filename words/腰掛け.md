# 腰掛け (こしかけ) - seat; bench

## Meaning

腰掛け is a seat, such as a stool, chair, or bench: something
provided or used for sitting. It is a noun.

- 庭に木の腰掛けを置きました。
  (にわにきのこしかけをおきました。) - We put a wooden seat in the garden.
- その腰掛けには二人座れます。
  (そのこしかけにはふたりすわれます。) - Two people can sit on that bench.

A separate colloquial use describes a job treated as temporary
while waiting for something else: 腰掛けの仕事 (こしかけのしごと),
"a stopgap job." Context supplies that figurative reading.

## How the kanji combine

[腰](../kanji/腰.md) (こし), waist or lower back, combines with
[掛](../kanji/掛.md)け (かけ), connected with placing or resting.
The related verb [腰掛ける](腰掛ける.md) means to sit down.
The object named here supports a seated person; it is not a device
for hanging someone's waist.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[いす](いす.md) means a chair; [ベンチ](ベンチ.md) specifies a bench.
[席](席.md) (せき) and [座席](座席.md) (ざせき) can refer to
a place provided for someone to sit, not necessarily separate furniture.
These meanings overlap without naming exactly the same object in every setting.

## Distinctive meaning

腰掛け is not the verb "sit down": compare 腰掛けを置く,
"put out a seat," with 椅子に腰掛ける (いすにこしかける),
"sit on a chair." The temporary-job sense does not imply that
every seat or every short-term employee is a stopgap.
