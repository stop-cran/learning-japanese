# 雷 (かみなり) - thunder; lightning

## Meaning

雷 is a noun covering thunder and lightning. English often separates the
sound from the electrical flash or strike; Japanese can use 雷 for the
storm phenomenon and let the verb specify what happens.

- 遠くで雷が鳴っています。 (とおくでかみなりがなっています。) - Thunder is rumbling in the distance.
- 近くの木に雷が落ちました。 (ちかくのきにかみなりがおちました。) - Lightning struck a nearby tree.

雷が鳴る describes the sound, while 雷が落ちる describes a strike.
In the second pattern, に marks what is struck. Translating both examples
with English "thunder" would lose this distinction.

## How the kanji combine

The single character 雷 represents the whole weather word, read
かみなり here. There are no separate written words to combine.
The visible rain element is a useful weather association, not a
character-by-character derivation of the spoken word.

## Synonyms and antonyms

稲妻 (いなずま) focuses on lightning, especially the flash.
[雨](雨.md) (あめ), "rain," is a related weather word, not a synonym:
雷 names a different phenomenon. Clear weather is a situational contrast,
not a lexical opposite.

## Distinctive meaning

Learn 雷 together with its verb. 鳴る, 光る (ひかる, "flash"), and
落ちる draw attention to different aspects of the same storm phenomenon.
The noun alone need not distinguish what English calls thunder from
what it calls lightning.
