# 丞相 (じょうしょう) — grand chancellor (ancient China)

## Meaning

丞相 is a **historical title**: a grand chancellor or chief councillor in ancient China,
the highest-ranking minister assisting the ruler with affairs of state.
In historical Japanese usage, it can also be a Chinese-style title for a minister,
so its interpretation depends on the setting.

- 丞相は君主を助けて国の政治を行った。 (じょうしょうはくんしゅをたすけてくにのせいじをおこなった。) — The grand chancellor assisted the ruler in governing the country.

This example describes the historical role; it is not a quotation about a particular officeholder.
The older reading **しょうじょう** is also recorded. Learn **じょうしょう** as this article's primary reading;
the alternative is for recognition, not a rule for swapping the readings of the two characters.

## How the kanji combine

[丞](../kanji/丞.md) and [相](../kanji/相.md) both contribute the idea of **assisting** here.
This is a combination of related meanings, naming a high office that assists the ruler.
Do not interpret 相 as "mutual" in this word merely because that is another meaning of the character.
The primary compound reading is **じょう + しょう**.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[大臣](大臣.md) だいじん, "cabinet minister," and [首相](首相.md) しゅしょう, "prime minister,"
are useful modern-office comparisons, not unrestricted substitutes for this historical title.
[総理大臣](総理大臣.md) そうりだいじん is another modern prime-minister title.
An English translation such as "chancellor" must fit the place and period; it does not make these offices identical.
There is no simple lexical antonym.

## Distinctive meaning

Recognize 丞相 in historical writing rather than using it as a neutral present-day job title.
The assistance meaning of its components does not make the complete word mean any helper or assistant.
Neither the historical title nor the kanji's N1 study label establishes a vocabulary JLPT level.
