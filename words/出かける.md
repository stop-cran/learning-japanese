# 出かける (でかける) — go out; set off

## Meaning

出かける means to go out or set off, often from home for an errand, visit, or outing. It is intransitive. A destination can take に or へ.

- 午後、買い物に出かけます。  
  ごご、かいものにでかけます。  
  "I'll go out shopping this afternoon."
- 八時に出かけました。  
  はちじにでかけました。  
  "I went out at eight."

In 買い物に, に marks the purpose of the outing.

## How the kanji combine

[出](../kanji/出.md) contributes going out and is read で. 出掛ける is another standard spelling;
[掛](../kanji/掛.md), read か in that spelling, is part of the established verb, not an instruction
to translate this as hanging something. The whole verb is ichidan: 出かけます (でかけます),
出かけて (でかけて), 出かけない (でかけない).

## Synonyms and antonyms

出る (でる), "leave; come out", has a broader range. 行く (いく), "go", does not itself emphasize setting out. 帰る (かえる), "return home", is a useful outing-related contrast.

## Distinctive meaning

出かけています (でかけています) can mean that someone is out at present, not that they are in the act of stepping through the door. The outing and resulting absence matter.

In a suitable context, 出かける can also mean being about to leave:
今、出かけるところです (いま、でかけるところです), "I am just about to go out."
Here ところです makes the immediate stage explicit. Do not confuse this impending departure
with the resulting absence in 出かけています.

ところ is a general expression for a stage or situation, not a special ending of 出かける.
The dictionary also illustrates the departure-starting use with 出かけたところに客が来ました
(でかけたところにきゃくがきました), "A visitor arrived just as I was going out."
In that context, 出かけた presents the departure as just being started, not a completed outing;
ところに places the visitor's arrival at that moment.
