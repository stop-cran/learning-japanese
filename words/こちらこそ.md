# こちらこそ - it is I who should say so

## Meaning

こちらこそ returns a sentiment to the other person with the emphasis
"It is I who should say that" or "The same goes for me."
What is being returned comes from the conversation.

- A: 今日はありがとうございました。
  (きょうはありがとうございました。) - Thank you for today.
- B: こちらこそ、ありがとうございました。 - It is I who should thank you.

It also works with a mutual greeting:

- A: よろしくお願いします。
  (よろしくおねがいします。) - I look forward to working with you.
- B: こちらこそ、よろしくお願いします。
  (こちらこそ、よろしくおねがいします。) - Likewise, I look forward to working with you.

The longer response states what こちらこそ alone leaves understood.

## How the kanji combine

This is the kana expression [こちら](こちら.md), "this side" or
"our side," followed by emphatic こそ.
It emphasizes the speaker's side in the exchange, rather than giving
directions toward a place. The dictionary's 此方こそ is a search-only
spelling, not a spelling recommended for this lesson.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[ありがとう](ありがとう.md) directly expresses thanks.
こちらこそ can return that thanks, but also returns sentiments
other than gratitude. The appropriate English response may be
"Likewise" or "No, thank you," depending on the preceding words.
There is no single useful antonym.

## Distinctive meaning

Do not translate it mechanically as "here especially," or use
"the same to you" as a universal reply regardless of context.
The phrase highlights a sentiment that reasonably applies to the
speaker's side too. It does not merely repeat any statement the
other person happens to make.
