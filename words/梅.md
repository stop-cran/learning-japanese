# 梅 (うめ) - ume; Japanese apricot

## Meaning

梅 names the ume tree and its fruit, *Prunus mume*, also called Japanese apricot or Japanese plum. It does not mean every fruit called a plum in English.

- 庭の梅が咲きました。 (にわのうめがさきました。) - The ume tree in the garden has blossomed.
- 梅の実でジャムを作りました。 (うめのみでジャムをつくりました。) - I made jam from ume fruit.

梅の花 (うめのはな) specifies the blossom, while 梅の実 (うめのみ) specifies the fruit.

## How the kanji combine

梅 writes the whole native noun うめ; there is no multi-kanji compound. The same character appears in 梅干し (うめぼし), pickled ume. Recognizing the character helps connect the tree, its blossoms, and foods made from its fruit.

## Synonyms and antonyms

すもも refers to a different plum, so the two names should not be treated as interchangeable plant labels. [松](松.md) (まつ), "pine," and 竹 (たけ), "bamboo," are neither synonyms nor botanical opposites of 梅.

## Distinctive meaning

松, 竹, and 梅 can instead label three grades of a product or service. In that ranking, 梅 is the lowest tier. On a menu listing 松・竹・梅, 梅のコース (うめのコース) can therefore mean the basic course, not a meal made from ume. Context separates this supported ranking sense from the plant sense.
