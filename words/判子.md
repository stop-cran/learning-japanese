# 判子 (はんこ) - personal seal; stamp

## Meaning

判子 is a seal or stamp, including a personal seal used on documents.
The word is usually written はんこ or ハンコ; 判子 is a dictionary
spelling retained here from the community source.

- この欄にはんこを押してください。 (このらんにはんこをおしてください。) - Please stamp your seal in this space.
- はんこを家に忘れてきました。 (はんこをいえにわすれてきました。) - I left my seal at home.

はんこを押す (はんこをおす) means "stamp a seal."
The noun is not itself a verb meaning "sign."

## How the kanji combine

[判](../kanji/判.md) and [子](../kanji/子.md) are used as
**ateji** here: the spelling must not be explained as a literal
"judgment child" compound.
Digital Daijisen traces はんこ to はんこう（版行）.
The `kango` grouping follows that Sino-Japanese lineage; it does not
claim that the modern spelling is an ordinary all-on-reading compound.

## Synonyms and antonyms

印鑑 (いんかん) is an overlapping word used for a seal in everyday
contexts. 署名 (しょめい) is a handwritten signature, a different
way of identifying or authorizing.
[切手](切手.md) (きって) is a postage stamp, not this stamping implement.
English "stamp" therefore needs context.

## Distinctive meaning

A personal seal may be used where English would mention signing, but
not every signature or authorization requires a はんこ.
Do not turn this vocabulary example into a universal rule about Japanese
paperwork. Also distinguish 印 read いん in seal vocabulary from the
separate native reading しるし, "mark; sign."
