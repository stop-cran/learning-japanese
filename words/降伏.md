# 降伏 (こうふく) — surrender; capitulation

## Meaning

降伏 is surrender: admitting that you have lost a fight and giving in to the other side. JMdict glosses it “surrender; capitulation; submission”, and it can be used as a verb, 降伏する.

- 敵の軍は、ついに降伏しました。
  (てきのぐんは、ついにこうふくしました。)
  "The enemy army finally surrendered."
- 彼らは「降伏しない」と言いました。
  (かれらは「こうふくしない」といいました。)
  "They said, 'We will not surrender.'"

These are original usage examples, not quotations from a dictionary or other source.

## How the kanji combine

[降](../kanji/降.md) (コウ) contributes losing and then obeying the winner; [伏](../kanji/伏.md) (フク) contributes submitting and following. This is a relation of two characters with similar meanings side by side: both describe giving in to someone. It explains the compound's meaning, not the historical origin of either character.

## Synonyms and antonyms

降参 こうさん, “surrender; submission; capitulation”, is very close, and Digital Daijisen gives it as a synonym; JMdict also glosses 降参 “being defeated (e.g. by a problem); giving up; giving in”. [負ける](負ける.md) (まける), "lose a contest," only says that someone lost; 降伏 adds giving in to the other side. [諦める](諦める.md) (あきらめる), "give up; abandon hope," is about giving up a hope or plan, not about yielding to an opponent. [従う](従う.md) (したがう), "obey; follow; comply," is broader and need not follow a defeat.

There is no single fixed antonym. Continuing to fight is a situation, not an opposite word.

## Distinctive meaning

The same spelling read ごうぶく is a different word. Digital Daijisen defines ごうぶく as holding back demons or enemies by the power of gods and buddhas or by the power of Buddhist teaching.

JMdict also gives 降伏 a technical sense in engineering, “yield”. Both dictionaries also list the spelling 降服; JMdict marks it as a rarely used form.
