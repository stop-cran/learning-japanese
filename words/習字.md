# 習字 (しゅうじ) - penmanship; calligraphy

## Meaning

習字 is practicing how to write characters correctly and beautifully: penmanship or calligraphy. It can involve a brush or a pen; the word is not restricted to brush writing.

- 子供のころ、習字を習っていました。
  (こどものころ、しゅうじをならっていました。) - I took calligraphy lessons as a child.
- 毎週、習字の教室に通っています。
  (まいしゅう、しゅうじのきょうしつにかよっています。) - I attend a penmanship class every week.

習字をする means doing penmanship practice. It is an activity, not the name of a particular written character.

## How the kanji combine

[習](../kanji/習.md) contributes learning or practice, and [字](../kanji/字.md) supplies written characters. Together they identify learning to write characters well. The readings are シュウ + ジ; there is no extra long vowel after じ.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[練習](練習.md) (れんしゅう) is practice more broadly. [字](字.md) (じ) can mean a character or someone's handwriting; 習字 emphasizes the writing practice. 書道 (しょどう) names calligraphy as a discipline or art, with overlap rather than a rigid pen-versus-brush division.

## Distinctive meaning

Being able to recognize or read a character is not the same skill as writing it neatly. Likewise, practicing writing does not imply that every 習字 lesson produces an exhibition piece. The activity and the finished writing should not be confused.
