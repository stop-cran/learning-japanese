# 拍手 (はくしゅ) - applause; clapping

## Meaning

拍手 is clapping or applause. 拍手する means to clap, and
**person or performance に拍手する** identifies what receives the applause.
The noun also works with を: 拍手を送る (はくしゅをおくる), "give applause."

- 演奏が終わると、みんなが拍手しました。 (えんそうがおわると、みんながはくしゅしました。) - When the performance ended, everyone applauded.
- 選手たちに大きな拍手を送りました。 (せんしゅたちにおおきなはくしゅをおくりました。) - We gave the players a big round of applause.

In the second example, に marks the recipients, not the hands doing the clapping.

## How the kanji combine

拍 contributes beating or clapping, and [手](../kanji/手.md) means hands.
The action and the body part combine to name clapping one's hands.
The on readings are はく + しゅ, not the standalone hand reading て.

## Synonyms and antonyms

手をたたく (てをたたく), "clap one's hands," describes the action directly.
[歓迎](歓迎.md) (かんげい), "welcome," describes a reception that may include
applause but does not require it. Silence contrasts with audible applause,
but silence does not by itself prove disapproval.

## Distinctive meaning

拍手 can express praise, encouragement, or a welcome in the examples above.
The dictionary also records hand-clapping in shrine prayer; context separates
that ritual action from applause for a performer. Do not infer the audience's
exact feelings merely from the mechanical act of clapping, or treat every
hand sound as praise.
