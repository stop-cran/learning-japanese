# ごめんください - may I come in?

## Meaning

ごめんください is a polite call when visiting someone's home or another place where you need the occupant's attention: "Hello, may I come in?" It announces your presence and asks to be received, not that you have already been invited inside.

- A: ごめんください。 - Hello, may I come in?
- B: はい、どうぞお入りください。 (はい、どうぞおはいりください。) - Yes, please come in.

It can also express a polite request for forgiveness. That additional apology use does not make it the default replacement for every everyday "sorry."

## How the kanji combine

The recognized spelling 御免下さい (ごめんください) combines [御](../kanji/御.md)免, pardon or permission, with 下さい, a request form associated with [下](../kanji/下.md). The entry marks the expression as usually written in kana.

Its Sino-Japanese ごめん base is followed by native ください: this is a mixed conventional phrase, not an entirely Sino-Japanese compound or a literal order to "give permission."

## Synonyms and antonyms

[おじゃまします](おじゃまします.md), "excuse the intrusion; I'm coming in," acknowledges entering the host's space. ごめんください can instead call for the host before that invitation.

[ごめんなさい](ごめんなさい.md) apologizes, while [すみません](すみません.md) can apologize or attract someone's attention more broadly. The visiting formula here is not the universal attention-getter for a server or passer-by.

## Distinctive meaning

The standard door-calling use is polite, unlike bare familiar [ごめん](ごめん.md). Public dictionaries also record leave-taking and apology uses; the exchange above deliberately teaches arriving and asking to be received.
