# 生き物 (いきもの) - living thing; creature

## Meaning

生き物 is a noun meaning "living thing" or "living creature." It is useful when talking about
life around us without naming an exact species. Context may focus on animals, but the word is
not a taxonomic label for animals alone.

- 池にはいろいろな生き物がいます。 (いけにはいろいろないきものがいます。) - There are various creatures in the pond.
- 植物も生き物です。 (しょくぶつもいきものです。) - Plants are living things too.

The first example uses いる for the creatures being described. Do not take the noun itself as
a rule that every referent requires いる: Japanese normally describes the existence of plants with ある.

## How the kanji combine

[生](../kanji/生.md) appears in [生きる](生きる.md) (いきる), "to live"; 生き- is its connecting stem.
[物](../kanji/物.md) is もの, "thing." Together they form a native compound, "a living thing."
The written き makes the reading easier to identify than a bare two-kanji compound.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[動物](動物.md) (どうぶつ), "animal," and [植物](植物.md) (しょくぶつ), "plant," name narrower categories.
[生物](生物.md) (せいぶつ), "organism; living thing," overlaps and is useful in biological discussion.
A stone is a contrasting nonliving thing, not an antonym word built into 生き物.

## Distinctive meaning

Choose 生き物 when the fact that something is alive matters, as in observing pond creatures
or caring for a pet. It does not mean a person's lifestyle: [生活](生活.md) (せいかつ) concerns
daily life and living conditions instead.
