# 蕎麦 (そば) - buckwheat; soba noodles

## Meaning

蕎麦 names buckwheat, the crop, and soba noodles made with buckwheat flour.
In a restaurant, そば usually identifies the noodles or a dish made with them.
Both kanji 蕎麦 and kana そば are useful to recognize.

- 昼ご飯に冷たいそばを食べました。 (ひるごはんにつめたいそばをたべました。) - I ate cold soba noodles for lunch.
- この畑では蕎麦を育てています。 (このはたけではそばをそだてています。) - They grow buckwheat in this field.

The first example is food; the second is the plant.
The word does not promise that the noodles contain only buckwheat flour.

## How the kanji combine

蕎 identifies buckwheat, while [麦](../kanji/麦.md) is associated with grains
such as wheat and barley. Learn **蕎麦 = そば as a whole-word reading**:
do not divide it into an invented 蕎 = そ and 麦 = ば.
Here `jukujikun` records that whole-word reading relationship.
It does not make buckwheat the same plant as wheat.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[うどん](うどん.md) names thick wheat noodles, not another name for every soba dish.
麺 (めん) is the broader word for noodles.
[側](側.md) (そば), beside/nearby, is an unrelated word despite the identical reading.
There is no ordinary antonym for this food or crop.

## Distinctive meaning

そば also occurs in names for Chinese-style wheat noodles, such as
中華そば (ちゅうかそば), and in 焼きそば (やきそば).
Do not infer buckwheat ingredients from every food name containing そば.
The dictionary's older plant readings そばむぎ and そまむぎ are not this card's
production reading, and its noodle senses are tied to そば.
The additional plant/noodle-name coverage is not a separate JLPT assignment.
