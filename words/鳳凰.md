# 鳳凰 (ほうおう) — Chinese phoenix; fenghuang

## Meaning

鳳凰 names the **Chinese phoenix**, or *fenghuang*: a mythical bird, not a living
bird species. The word also identifies the bird as represented in a picture,
decorative pattern or crest.

- 鳳凰の絵を見ました。 (ほうおうのえをみました。) — I saw a picture of a Chinese phoenix.
- この布には鳳凰の模様があります。 (このぬのにはほうおうのもようがあります。) — This cloth has a Chinese-phoenix pattern.

These examples concern a representation of the bird, not a claimed sighting of a real animal.

## How the kanji combine

[鳳](../kanji/鳳.md) is read **ホウ** and 凰 **オウ** here. Traditionally, 鳳 names the
male bird and 凰 the female. The compound pairs those related names to designate
the fenghuang; it does not require each use to describe two separate birds.

## Synonyms and antonyms

- フェニックス (ふぇにっくす) and 不死鳥 (ふしちょう) name the phoenix associated
  with death and rebirth in the Egyptian/Western tradition. They are related
  translation terms, not a reason to import that story into every use of 鳳凰.
- [鳥](鳥.md) (とり), "bird," is the broad category, not the specific mythical name.
  [鶴](鶴.md) (つる), "crane," and [鷹](鷹.md) (たか), "hawk; falcon," name real birds.

There is no useful antonym.

## Distinctive meaning

Use 鳳凰 to recognize this mythical bird and its motifs. "Chinese phoenix" helps
distinguish it from the other phoenix tradition; **rebirth from ashes is not
implied just by the word 鳳凰**. Fiction and translations can associate the
traditions, so the surrounding work still matters.

The reading is ほうおう, with a long vowel in each part. The separately documented
鳳 (おおとり) is not the reading of this two-character word.
