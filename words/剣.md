# 剣 (つるぎ) — sword; double-edged blade

## Meaning

剣 is a noun for a sword, especially a double-edged one. This article teaches **つるぎ**. Dictionaries also recognize broader sword usage, so the double-edged association is not an absolute restriction.

- 博物館で古い剣を見ました。 (はくぶつかんでふるいつるぎをみました。) — I saw an old sword at the museum.
- これは剣の写真です。 (これはつるぎのしゃしんです。) — This is a photograph of a sword.

In 剣を見る (つるぎをみる), "look at a sword," を marks the object. 剣の写真 uses の to connect the sword with its photograph.

## How the kanji combine

[剣](../kanji/剣.md) represents the whole native noun つるぎ here. There is no compound to split. The same spelling also has the word reading けん; this is another valid pronunciation, not a correction of つるぎ.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[刀](刀.md) かたな especially suggests a Japanese single-edged sword, while 剣 つるぎ especially suggests a double-edged one. These associations do not partition every broader use of "sword." [日本刀](日本刀.md) にほんとう identifies the Japanese sword tradition.

[刃](刃.md) は, "blade; cutting edge," focuses on a cutting part. [ナイフ](ナイフ.md), "knife," and [包丁](包丁.md) ほうちょう, "kitchen knife," name different kinds of tools, not general translations of つるぎ. There is no natural lexical antonym.

## Distinctive meaning

[真剣](真剣.md) しんけん has a literal noun sense "real sword," contrasting with a wooden practice weapon, as well as its familiar "serious; earnest" adjective sense. Do not infer the meaning of the whole compound just by translating 剣.

JMdict restricts its separate bayonet, swordsmanship and zoological senses to the reading けん. Those are not additional senses to memorize for this つるぎ article.
