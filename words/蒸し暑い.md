# 蒸し暑い (むしあつい) -- muggy; sultry

## Meaning

蒸し暑い describes uncomfortable heat combined with humidity: muggy
or sultry conditions, not moisture alone. むし暑い and 蒸暑い are
recorded spellings of the same むしあつい.

- 今日は蒸し暑いですね。 (きょうはむしあついですね。) -- It is muggy today, isn't it?
- 蒸し暑い夜は寝苦しいです。 (むしあついよるはねぐるしいです。) -- It is hard to sleep on a sultry night.

It is an i-adjective: 蒸し暑くない (むしあつくない),
蒸し暑かった (むしあつかった) and 蒸し暑くなる (むしあつくなる).

## How the kanji combine

[蒸](../kanji/蒸.md) supplies む in 蒸し, connecting with sultry heat;
暑 writes あつ in 暑い, hot weather. The native compound combines
むし + あつい. It does not mean the weather is literally cooking
someone with steam.

## Synonyms and antonyms

- [蒸す](蒸す.md) (むす) has a weather use meaning hot and humid, as well as a separate transitive steam-cooking use.
- [暑い](暑い.md) (あつい) describes hot surroundings without itself specifying high humidity.
- [じめじめ](じめじめ.md) and [じとじと](じとじと.md) overlap in unpleasant damp or clammy conditions; they need not specify the same heat.
- [湿気](湿気.md) (しっけ) is moisture or humidity; [湿度](湿度.md) (しつど) is humidity as a measure. Neither alone guarantees oppressive heat.

## Distinctive meaning

Keep the combination of heat and humidity. Rain need not be falling,
and a cold damp day is not automatically 蒸し暑い. English "sticky"
and "close" can describe this weather, but the Japanese words teaching
adhesiveness, closeness or closing do not thereby acquire a shared
weather sense. Their quiz safeguards concern ambiguous English labels.
