# ひやひや — nervous about what might go wrong

## Meaning

ひやひや describes nervous unease about something that might go wrong: an accident,
a failure, or a secret being discovered. The concern can be for yourself or someone else.
It does not mean that the feared outcome actually happens.

Use **ひやひやする** for feeling this apprehension and **ひやひやしている** for an ongoing state.

- 事故が起きるのではないかと、ひやひやした。（じこがおきるのではないかと、ひやひやした。）
  — I was nervous that an accident might happen.
- 秘密がばれるのではないかと、ひやひやしていた。（ひみつがばれるのではないかと、ひやひやしていた。）
  — I was on edge, worried that the secret might get out.

In both examples, **のではないかと** introduces the worry, roughly “what if ...?”
ばれる means “be found out; come to light.”

## Synonyms and antonyms

[はらはら](はらはら.md) also describes anxious suspense, often while watching an uncertain
event unfold. ひやひや highlights concern that something bad might happen or be discovered.
Watching a child climb a tall tree, you could use either word. The difference is one
of emphasis, not a rule about who is watching and who is in danger.

[どきどき](どきどき.md) focuses on a pounding heart. A person can feel both どきどき and
ひやひや, but ひやひや does not itself say that the heart is racing. Nor does it require
actual shivering or a cold sweat.

Feeling relieved afterward can be expressed with [ほっと](ほっと.md)する, but relief is not a required
next stage: the worry may continue.

## Distinctive meaning

**冷や冷や** is read ひやひや. The word is usually written in kana, as ひやひや or
ヒヤヒヤ; the choice of spelling does not separate its meanings.

Dictionaries also list a literal sense: **feeling cold against the skin**, for example
from chilly air. That is separate from the apprehension taught here, not a bodily symptom
required by the two examples. Compare [ひんやり](ひんやり.md) for felt coolness; context,
not the spelling 冷や冷や, tells you whether physical cold is meant.
