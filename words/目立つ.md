# 目立つ (めだつ) - stand out; be conspicuous

## Meaning

目立つ is an intransitive godan つ verb: stand out, be conspicuous, or be readily noticeable.
The thing that stands out normally takes が.

- 白い壁では黒い汚れが目立ちます。 (しろいかべではくろいよごれがめだちます。) - Black dirt stands out on a white wall.
- 彼は背が高いので、よく目立ちます。 (かれはせがたかいので、よくめだちます。) - He stands out because he is tall.
- 目立たない色の服を選びました。 (めだたないいろのふくをえらびました。) - I chose clothes in an inconspicuous color.

目立つ can describe something welcome or unwelcome. It does not inherently mean "excellent."

## How the kanji combine

[目](../kanji/目.md) contributes the eyes and [立](../kanji/立.md) contributes standing.
"Stand before the eyes" is a memory aid for being noticeable, not a historical origin.
The native reading is めだつ, with a voiced だ in the second part.
Forms include 目立ちます (めだちます), 目立って (めだって),
目立った (めだった), and 目立たない (めだたない).

## Synonyms and antonyms

[派手](派手.md) (はで), "showy; flashy," can explain why something stands out,
but a plain black stain can also 目立つ.
[見える](見える.md) (みえる) means be visible or come into view in relevant uses;
something can be visible without being conspicuous.
目立たない, "not stand out," gives the straightforward negative contrast.

## Distinctive meaning

目立つ describes the noticeable person or thing, not the observer's act of noticing.
[気付く](気付く.md) (きづく) describes noticing or becoming aware.
With 目立つ, an observer may be understood from context without being named.
Do not change the person who notices into the grammatical subject of a transitive "notice" verb.
