# 突っ込む (つっこむ) - thrust into; plunge into

## Meaning

突っ込む can describe **putting something forcefully inside** or **plunging into something**.
The grammar changes with the sense: an object takes を in the first pattern; the moving subject takes が in the second.

- ポケットに手を突っ込みました。
  (ポケットにてをつっこみました。) - I thrust my hand into my pocket.
- 車が塀に突っ込みました。
  (くるまがへいにつっこみました。) - The car crashed into the wall.
- もう少し突っ込んで調べましょう。
  (もうすこしつっこんでしらべましょう。) - Let's investigate a little more deeply.

It also describes sharp questioning, interfering in someone else's business, or making a pointed retort.
突っ込んだ質問 (つっこんだしつもん) is a probing question; a conversational 突っ込み need not be a physical collision.

## How the kanji combine

[突](../kanji/突.md) contributes thrusting; [込](../kanji/込.md) contributes going inward or deeply.
Learn the whole form つっこむ, with small っ. It is godan: 突っ込みます, 突っ込んだ, 突っ込んで.
Not every sense has the same transitivity: shoving something in is transitive, while a car plunging in is intransitive.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[入れる](入れる.md) (いれる) is the broader act of putting something in; [入る](入る.md) (はいる) is entering.
[突き当たる](突き当たる.md) (つきあたる) overlaps in collision, but also means reaching a street end.
[質問](質問.md) (しつもん) is a question generally; 突っ込む can add the idea of probing or challenging an answer.

引き出す (ひきだす), "pull out," can reverse putting something inside. It does not reverse every figurative use.

## Distinctive meaning

The verb often adds force, depth or intervention, but it does not require an injury or an accident.
Putting a hand into a pocket, crashing into a wall, and delving into a subject are different constructions, not one literal scene.
