# 日用品 (にちようひん) - daily necessities; everyday items

## Meaning

日用品 names objects used in ordinary daily life: "daily necessities" or
"everyday items." Soap, towels and toothbrushes are useful examples.
The category is not restricted to things used up after one use.

- スーパーで日用品を買いました。 (スーパーでにちようひんをかいました。) - I bought everyday necessities at the supermarket.
- この店は日用品を売っています。 (このみせはにちようひんをうっています。) - This shop sells everyday household items.

Japanese 日用品 need not be marked as plural when referring to several items.

## How the kanji combine

[日](../kanji/日.md) contributes the daily setting,
[用](../kanji/用.md) contributes use, and [品](../kanji/品.md) contributes
articles or goods. 日用 modifies 品: articles for everyday use.
The on readings are ニチ + ヨウ + ヒン.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[品物](品物.md) (しなもの) is a broader word for items or goods.
[商品](商品.md) (しょうひん) emphasizes goods offered for sale.
[道具](道具.md) (どうぐ) emphasizes a tool or implement.
An object can belong to more than one of these categories.

## Distinctive meaning

日用品 describes an item's everyday use, not an instruction to purchase it
every day or a guarantee that every person needs it.
A durable towel can be 日用品 just as soap can; do not equate the word
with consumables alone. Conversely, not every 商品 is an everyday necessity.
