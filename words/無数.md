# 無数 (むすう) -- countless; innumerable

## Meaning

無数 means countless or innumerable: a number too great to count in
the situation being described. Use 無数の before a noun; dictionaries
also record na-adjectival use with 無数な.

- 空には無数の星が輝いていました。 (そらにはむすうのほしがかがやいていました。) -- Countless stars were shining in the sky.
- この問題には無数の例があります。 (このもんだいにはむすうのれいがあります。) -- There are innumerable examples of this problem.

These sentences emphasize abundance, not a completed calculation of
the number of stars or examples.

## How the kanji combine

無 contributes absence and [数](../kanji/数.md) number or counting.
Together they suggest being beyond counting. The on-readings are
ム + スウ; do not substitute the reading ゲン from 無限.

## Synonyms and antonyms

- [無限](無限.md) (むげん) means infinite or limitless. 無数 can describe a huge finite collection without asserting mathematical infinity.
- [多い](多い.md) (おおい) and [たくさん](たくさん.md) mean many or a lot, without necessarily making the stronger beyond-counting emphasis.
- [大勢](大勢.md) (おおぜい) concerns many people; 無数 can modify things other than people.
- [少ない](少ない.md) (すくない), few or little, contrasts with abundance.

## Distinctive meaning

Do not read the source gloss "infinite number" as a mathematical
guarantee. Equally, do not impose a finite-only rule: ordinary uses of
無数 and 無限 can overlap in expansive descriptions. The useful
starting point is uncountable abundance, not a strict theorem about
whether the collection is finite.
