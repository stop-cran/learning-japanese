# 助教授 (じょきょうじゅ) - associate professor (former Japanese title)

## Meaning

助教授 names a former Japanese university or college rank below 教授,
professor. The Japanese rank was renamed 准教授 (じゅんきょうじゅ),
associate professor, in April 2007. Read 助教授 in its historical or
institutional context.

- 彼は当時、その大学の助教授でした。 (かれはとうじ、そのだいがくのじょきょうじゅでした。) - At that time, he held the former associate-professor rank at that university.
- 古い資料には「助教授」と書かれています。 (ふるいしりょうには「じょきょうじゅ」とかかれています。) - The old document uses the title jokyoju.

当時 means "at that time." The examples deliberately concern an earlier
title, not a claim about a current appointment.

## How the kanji combine

[助](../kanji/助.md) contributes assisting; [教](../kanji/教.md) and 授
contribute teaching and imparting knowledge. 助 qualifies [教授](教授.md)
(きょうじゅ). The established job title has its own institutional history:
it is not just any helper who assists a professor.
The on readings are ジョ + キョウ + ジュ.

## Synonyms and antonyms

准教授 is the successor Japanese title. 助教 (じょきょう), often translated
assistant professor, is a separate rank. [助手](助手.md) (じょしゅ) means
assistant and has its own academic uses; it is not another spelling of 助教授.

## Distinctive meaning

The dictionary records both assistant professor and associate professor as
English glosses. Do not automatically equate the former Japanese rank with
the current US rank called assistant professor. The reform also distinguished
助教 from 助手; changing 助教授 to 准教授 did not merely give all these
roles one interchangeable name.
