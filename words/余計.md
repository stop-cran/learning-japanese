# 余計 (よけい) - extra; unnecessary; all the more

## Meaning

余計 can describe something extra, excessive or unnecessary.
Before a noun it takes な. As an adverb, 余計に or bare 余計 can
mean "more" or "all the more." For example, 前より余計痛くなった
(まえよりよけいいたくなった) means "It became even more painful than before."

- 余計なことを言ってしまいました。 (よけいなことをいってしまいました。) - I ended up saying something unnecessary.
- 心配すると、余計に眠れなくなります。 (しんぱいすると、よけいにねむれなくなります。) - Worrying makes it even harder to sleep.
- 念のため、一つ余計に買いました。 (ねんのため、ひとつよけいにかいました。) - I bought one extra just in case.

The third example is extra provision, not necessarily criticism.
Do not impose an always-negative meaning on every use.

## How the kanji combine

[余](../kanji/余.md) contributes surplus, and [計](../kanji/計.md)
counting or reckoning. The combination is associated with an amount
beyond what is needed or expected. The on readings are ヨ + ケイ.
This association is a meaning aid, not a word-origin claim.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[余分](余分.md) (よぶん) overlaps with extra or surplus.
[無駄](無駄.md) (むだ) emphasizes waste or pointlessness.
[一層](一層.md) (いっそう) and [もっと](もっと.md) are comparisons
for increased degree. [不足](不足.md) (ふそく), a shortage, contrasts with
excess quantity but not every "unnecessary" or "all the more" use.

## Distinctive meaning

余計なお世話 (よけいなおせわ) calls someone's help or concern
unwanted interference. The adjective can therefore evaluate whether
something was called for, not merely count surplus objects.
In 余計に眠れない, however, the word strengthens a comparison;
it does not describe an extra physical item.
