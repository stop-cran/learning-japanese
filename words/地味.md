# 地味 (じみ) - plain; unshowy

## Meaning

地味 describes something plain, subdued, or unshowy, rather than bright or attention-grabbing. It is a な-adjective and noun.

- 仕事には地味な色の服を着ています。
  (しごとにはじみないろのふくをきています。) - I wear clothes in subdued colors to work.
- この建物の外観は地味です。
  (このたてもののがいかんはじみです。) - The exterior of this building is unshowy.

Before a noun, use **地味な**; adverbially, use **地味に**. The word also describes a modest, reserved, or unobtrusive manner of living or behaving. 地味に暮らす (じみにくらす) means "live modestly/quietly," not necessarily live in complete silence.

## How the kanji combine

[地](../kanji/地.md) can contribute a base or underlying nature, and [味](../kanji/味.md) a quality or flavor. Their combination can serve as a memory aid for an unembellished quality, but it is not a verified historical derivation. Learn 地味 as a whole word rather than translating it as "earth taste."

The on readings here are ジ + ミ: じみ. The separate reading ちみ concerns the quality or fertility of soil, not the clothing adjective taught here.

## Synonyms and antonyms

派手 (はで), "showy; flashy," is the dictionary's direct antonym. 控えめ (ひかえめ), "restrained; modest," overlaps, and [おとなしい](おとなしい.md) can describe quiet colors or patterns.

[簡単](簡単.md) (かんたん), "simple; easy," concerns ease or lack of complication. A 地味な design can still be difficult to make; "plain" here does not itself mean easy.

## Distinctive meaning

地味 need not be criticism: understated clothes or patient, unobtrusive work can be appropriate or valued. It does not guarantee poor quality or an unattractive appearance.

JMdict additionally records colloquial 地味に meaning "quite; a bit." For example, 地味に痛い (じみにいたい) can mean "it hurts more than you might think," not "it hurts in a plain-looking way." This colloquial degree use is additional to the source's plain/simple gloss.
