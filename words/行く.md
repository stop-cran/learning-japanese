# 行く (いく) — to go

## Meaning

行く is to go, to move away from the speaker or toward a destination: 学校に行く (go to school), 駅へ行きます, 日本に行ったことがある (I have been to Japan).
It also means "to proceed, to work out": うまく行く (go well), 行ってきます (I'm off; "I'll go and come back").

## How the kanji combine

[行](../kanji/行.md) with the kun reading い plus okurigana く. The form 行く can also be read ゆく (literary, in phrases such as 行く年 ゆくとし, 行方 ゆくえ).
Conjugation: polite 行きます, negative 行かない, て-form 行って, past 行った. The て／た forms are irregular: the regular pattern for く-verbs is いて／いた (書く→書いて), but 行く gives 行って／行った, not 行いて.

The alternative reading ゆく also means "go": 遠くへ行く (とおくへゆく), "go far away."
It is common in literary language and songs; いく is the everyday default. The readings do not name different destinations or directions.

## Synonyms and antonyms

- Antonym: [来る](来る.md) くる (come); 帰る かえる (return home).
- Close: 向かう むかう (head toward), 参る まいる (humble "go"), 伺う うかがう (humble "visit").

## Distinctive meaning

行く moves away from the speaker's viewpoint, while 来る moves toward it. Japanese uses 行く where English sometimes says "come": 今行きます ("I'm coming", I go to where you are).
行く is the main verb in 食べに行く (たべにいく, go in order to eat; purpose に + 行く). It also has auxiliary uses after a て-form: これからも勉強していきます (これからもべんきょうしていきます, I will keep studying from now on) describes continuing an action into the future, not making a literal trip.
