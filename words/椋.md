# 椋 (むく) - muku tree

## Meaning

椋 names the **muku tree**, *Aphananthe aspera*. The longer name is 椋の木 (むくのき), also written 椋木. This deciduous tree loses its leaves seasonally and has rough leaves.

- 庭の椋は大きいです。 (にわのむくはおおきいです。) - The muku tree in the garden is large.
- 椋の葉はざらざらしています。 (むくのははざらざらしています。) - Muku leaves feel rough.

The dictionaries also record 椋 for the tree's fruit and as an abbreviation of 椋鳥 (むくどり), starling. The examples above select the tree sense; the word is not exclusively a tree name in every context.

## How the kanji combine

[椋](../kanji/椋.md) writes むく as a whole. There is no compound to divide. In 椋の木, の connects the name to [木](木.md) (き), tree.

The frozen family permits むく specifically for 椋. Its longer reading むくのき belongs to the listed plant-name spellings; do not assume that every spelling in a dictionary family accepts every listed reading. Plant names are also often written in kana.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[木](木.md) (き), tree or wood, and [植物](植物.md) (しょくぶつ), plant, are broader category words. They do not specify a muku tree. There is no direct antonym.

椋鳥 (むくどり) identifies the grey starling in the bird sense used here. [鳥](鳥.md) (とり) is the broader bird category, not another name for the tree. Dictionaries link the bird's name to its liking for muku fruit; that is a word-name explanation, not the origin of the character 椋.

## Distinctive meaning

Read the surrounding sentence to distinguish the tree, fruit and abbreviated bird uses. A statement about rough leaves points to the tree; a bird description may instead use 椋 as the abbreviation. The character's presence alone does not identify what the speaker means.

The answer label selects the tree sense. Direct quiz guards also account for documented fruit and bird uses and broader plant labels; they do not claim that a tree, fruit and bird are interchangeable referents.
