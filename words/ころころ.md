# ころころ — rolling over and over; changing frequently

## Meaning

ころころ depicts repeated rolling, especially of something relatively small and round. It also describes frequent changes and a light, ringing sound or bright laugh.

- ボールが坂をころころと転がった。 — The ball rolled down the slope. (坂: さか; 転がる: ころがる.)
- 彼は言うことがころころ変わる。 — What he says keeps changing.
- 妹はころころと笑った。 — My younger sister laughed in a bright, ringing voice.

Use it before a verb, optionally with と. ころころした子犬 (こいぬ) describes a round, chubby puppy.

## How the kanji combine

This kana mimetic has a repeated form, not a kanji compound. It represents motion, but can also imitate ringing sounds and some animal calls, including insect chirping and frog croaking. The sound is not required in the rolling sense.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[くるくる](くるくる.md) emphasizes spinning or turning; [ぐるぐる](ぐるぐる.md) often suggests repeated circuits. [ごろごろ](ごろごろ.md) also describes rolling, often with a heavier impression. A rolling ball need not stay in one place spinning. There is no single opposite covering all these senses.

## Distinctive meaning

The frequent-change use can criticize inconsistency; it does not simply mean physical roundness. The dictionary also records コロコロ as a noun for a roller, but that object name is separate from the adverbial grammar above.
