# 刺す (さす) - pierce; prick; sting

## Meaning

刺す means to pierce, prick, or stick a pointed thing into something.
It is transitive: the pointed thing can take を and its destination に.

- 布に針を刺しました。 (ぬのにはりをさしました。) - I stuck a needle into the cloth.
- 蚊に刺されました。 (かにさされました。) - I was bitten by a mosquito.

An insect can be the actor in the stinging or biting sense. In the second example, に marks that actor in a passive sentence.
The forms are 刺します, 刺して, 刺した, and 刺さない; 刺される is passive.

## How the kanji combine

[刺](../kanji/刺.md) contributes piercing and supplies さ; す is the changing ending.
The on reading シ appears in other words, but does not replace さす here.
The search-only spelling 螫す is not needed for writing the ordinary mosquito example.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[刺さる](刺さる.md) (ささる) is intransitive: 布に針が刺さる (ぬのにはりがささる), "a needle sticks into the cloth."
突く (つく) overlaps in poking or thrusting; 刺す foregrounds the point entering or piercing.
[挿す](挿す.md), also さす, concerns insertion, such as putting flowers into a vase.
[噛む](噛む.md) (かむ) covers biting with teeth; the mosquito construction instead uses 刺す.

## Distinctive meaning

There is also a sewing use: 雑巾を刺す (ぞうきんをさす), "stitch a cleaning cloth."
That is not merely another way to say that an insect bites it.
The dictionary additionally records poling a boat, catching with a limed pole, and putting a baseball runner out;
these are construction-specific uses, not meanings to substitute for any "catch" or "put out."

Do not confuse the homophone [指す](指す.md), "point; indicate," with piercing.
The other homophone [差す](差す.md) has umbrella and sunlight uses, as well as a dictionary-recorded boat-poling overlap.
