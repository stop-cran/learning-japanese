# オーバー (オーバー) - exceeding; exaggerated; overcoat

## Meaning

オーバー has several uses with different grammar. As a noun it can mean an overcoat, short for オーバーコート. With する, it means exceed or go beyond a limit. As a na-adjective it means exaggerated or overdone.

- 寒いので、オーバーを着ました。 (さむいので、おーばーをきました。) - It was cold, so I put on an overcoat.
- 費用が予算をオーバーしました。 (ひようがよさんをおーばーしました。) - The cost exceeded the budget.
- それはオーバーな表現です。 (それはおーばーなひょうげんです。) - That is an exaggerated expression.

The budget takes を in the second example. The adjective takes な before 表現 in the third.

## How the kanji combine

This is a katakana word with two long-vowel marks: オー and バー. It has no kanji decomposition. The overcoat use is a shortened form; it should not be explained by forcing every meaning into one Japanese grammatical pattern.

## Synonyms and antonyms

超える (こえる) overlaps with exceeding a limit. 大げさ (おおげさ) overlaps with exaggerated behavior or expression. [コート](コート.md) is a comparison for the garment.

## Distinctive meaning

Do not import all uses of English "over." Japanese オーバー is not a general preposition for "above" or "across," and the examples here do not make it a universal translation of "finished." Identify the sense from the noun, verb, or adjective construction around it.
