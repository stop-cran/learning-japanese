# みっちり - intensively; thoroughly

## Meaning

みっちり describes doing something intensively or thoroughly, without skimping on the work.
It is useful for study, practice, training, or instruction:

- 試験の前に、文法を二時間みっちり復習しました。
  (しけんのまえに、ぶんぽうをにじかんみっちりふくしゅうしました。) - Before the exam, I spent two hours reviewing grammar intensively.
- 先生に発音をみっちりと教わりました。
  (せんせいにはつおんをみっちりとおそわりました。) - My teacher taught me pronunciation thoroughly.

JMdict also lists a second sense, "tightly; compactly; closely."
For a concrete model of packing objects into a container, compare **[ぎっしり](ぎっしり.md)**:

- 箱にはタオルがぎっしり詰まっています。
  (はこにはタオルがぎっしりつまっています。) - The box is tightly packed with towels.

The dictionary glosses overlap; they do not establish that the words are interchangeable in every phrase.
The examples of みっちり here focus on intensive work.

Attach みっちり to the activity verb, directly or with と:
**みっちり練習する／みっちりと練習する**, "practice intensively."
Here **練習（れんしゅう）** means "practice."
Do not use bare みっちりする for "study hard": する in the example belongs to 練習する, "practice."

## Synonyms and antonyms

[しっかり](しっかり.md) is useful for doing something properly or securely; みっちり emphasizes
intensive, thorough work rather than secure execution in general.
[せっせと](せっせと.md) brings steady, industrious activity into focus.
[一生懸命](一生懸命.md) (いっしょうけんめい) emphasizes the person's effort or attitude,
while みっちり emphasizes working through the activity thoroughly and intensively.
The same study session can warrant more than one of these descriptions.

[だらだら](だらだら.md) can contrast with focused training when an activity drags on aimlessly,
but is not an opposite in every sense.

## Distinctive meaning

This is a **mimetic description**, not an imitation of a particular sound.
みっちり often suggests rigorous work or instruction. With **叱る（しかる）**, "scold,"
**みっちり叱られた** can mean "I got a thorough scolding."
Training, teaching, or scolding by someone else can bring a strict nuance into focus;
the word does not mean only punishment.
The duration comes from the sentence: みっちり itself does not mean "for two hours."
