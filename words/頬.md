# 頬 (ほほ) - cheek

## Meaning

頬 is a cheek of the face. Both ほほ and ほお are recognized readings of the
same noun and spelling. This card keeps ほほ as its primary reading, but the
second example demonstrates ほお.

- 冷たい風が頬に当たりました。 (つめたいかぜがほほにあたりました。) - The cold wind hit my cheeks.
- 彼女は頬を赤くしました。 (かのじょはほおをあかくしました。) - Her cheeks flushed.

頬に marks the part reached or touched; 頬を赤くする describes the cheeks
becoming red. Japanese does not need a plural ending to refer to both cheeks.

## How the kanji combine

頬 supplies the whole native noun. There is no multi-kanji compound to divide.
In ほほ, both syllables begin with h; ほお has a long o sound after the initial
h. These are alternative pronunciations, not different body parts.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[顔](顔.md) (かお) is the face as a whole, while 頬 names a part of it.
ほっぺた is a colloquial word for the cheek. Neither comparison provides an antonym:
body-part names do not generally form opposite pairs.

## Distinctive meaning

The dictionary does not require a different physical meaning for ほほ and ほお.
Do not infer that one means just one cheek and the other both cheeks.
Also keep the anatomical sense: the English idiom "having cheek," meaning
impudence, is not automatically expressed with this Japanese noun.
