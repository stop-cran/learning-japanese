# 行き (いき) - outbound trip; bound for

## Meaning

行き, read **いき** or **ゆき**, names the outward journey or the way there.
As a suffix after a destination it means "bound for"; the dictionary notes **ゆき** as the usual reading for that use.

- 行きは電車で、帰りはバスでした。 (いきはでんしゃで、かえりはバスでした。) - I took the train there and the bus back.
- これは東京行きの電車です。 (これはとうきょうゆきのでんしゃです。) - This is a train bound for Tokyo.

In the second pattern, attach 行き directly to the destination: 東京行き, not 東京に行き.
Then add の before the following noun. Both readings belong to this entry; their distribution is a usage preference, not a division into unrelated meanings.

## How the kanji combine

[行](../kanji/行.md) supplies the idea of going, with き completing the noun related to [行く](行く.md) (いく／ゆく).
There is no second kanji to combine with it.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[帰り](帰り.md) (かえり), "the return journey," is the practical counterpart to outbound 行き.
[出発](出発.md) (しゅっぱつ) means departure: it identifies setting off rather than the whole outward leg.

## Distinctive meaning

The point of reference matters: 行き is the journey toward the destination, even if it involves several vehicles.
A destination label such as 大阪行き (おおさかゆき) tells you where a service is headed; it does not identify where the speaker lives.
Read the destination together with the suffix when listening to announcements.
