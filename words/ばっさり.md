# ばっさり — cutting off boldly; removing drastically

## Meaning

ばっさり describes cutting something off boldly or making a drastic cut in what you keep.
With hair, it suggests a substantial cut rather than a little trim. The same image of bold
removal extends to editing a text or slashing a budget.

Use **ばっさり（と）+ verb**: と is optional. Pair it with a verb such as [切る](切る.md)（きる, cut）,
切り落とす（きりおとす, cut off）, [削る](削る.md)（けずる, cut down or delete）, and
切り捨てる（きりすてる, cut off or discard）.

- 髪をばっさり切った。（かみをばっさりきった。）
  — I had my hair cut much shorter.
- 原稿の前半をばっさりと削った。（げんこうのぜんはんをばっさりとけずった。）
  — I cut out the first half of the manuscript.

When lopping off a branch, ばっさり can suggest a single bold stroke. In the hair sentence,
the point is how much came off, not who held the scissors or how many snips it took.
In the manuscript sentence, 削る means deleting text, not physically shaving paper.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[すっぱり](すっぱり.md) also describes a decisive cut or break. In these overlapping uses,
すっぱり foregrounds a clean, complete break, while ばっさり foregrounds bold or drastic removal.
This is a difference in emphasis, not a rule that only one word can fit a given cut.

[きっぱり](きっぱり.md) describes an unambiguous, resolute response or action, such as firmly
refusing a request. It does not itself describe cutting off a large amount.

## Distinctive meaning

ばっさり is not a general replacement for “decisively”: learn it with cutting, deleting,
discarding or similar removal. Figurative use can suggest ruthlessness, but a haircut need
not imply anger or hostility. バッサリ is a recorded katakana spelling of the same word,
not a separate meaning.
