# 番地 (ばんち) - lot number; address

## Meaning

番地 is a number assigned to a piece of land or a house, and can also
refer to an address or residence. Its precise role depends on the address
system; it is not universally a house's sequential position along a street.

- 住所の番地を確認してください。 (じゅうしょのばんちをかくにんしてください。) - Please check the lot or house number in the address.
- 番地を間違えて書きました。 (ばんちをまちがえてかきました。) - I wrote the wrong lot or house number.

As a numbered suffix, 三番地 (さんばんち) identifies lot or address
number three. The surrounding address supplies the location.

## How the kanji combine

[番](../kanji/番.md) contributes a number or position in an order, and
[地](../kanji/地.md) contributes land or place.
Together they associate a place with its identifying number.
Read バン + チ: ばんち, not ばんじ.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[住所](住所.md) (じゅうしょ) broadly means address or residence.
[番号](番号.md) (ばんごう) is an identifying number for many kinds
of things, not just land.
[宛名](宛名.md) (あてな) identifies the recipient or name-and-address
information on correspondence; it does not mean only the numbered plot.
These overlap in an address context without being freely interchangeable.

## Distinctive meaning

In computing, 番地 can also mean an address, such as a memory address.
The numbered-location idea extends beyond physical houses.
Neither use is a count of how many houses or memory locations exist.
Do not infer an entire Japanese address format from the single suffix:
blocks, lots, buildings, and administrative areas need their own context.
