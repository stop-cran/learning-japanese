# つまずく - stumble; trip

## Meaning

つまずく means to stumble or trip, for example when one's foot catches on an obstacle.
The obstacle can take に. It does not necessarily mean falling all the way to the ground.

- 石につまずきましたが、転びませんでした。
  (いしにつまずきましたが、ころびませんでした。) - I tripped over a stone, but did not fall.
- 最初の問題でつまずきました。
  (さいしょのもんだいでつまずきました。) - I ran into difficulty with the very first problem.

The second example is figurative: meeting a difficulty or suffering a setback, not physically tripping over a written question.

## How the kanji combine

JMdict marks the word as usually written in kana. Recognize the spelling 躓く and the reading variant つまづく;
the primary form here is つまずく. There is no 躓 card in the current collection.
This is godan: つまずきます, つまずいた, つまずいて, つまずかない.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[転ぶ](転ぶ.md) (ころぶ) generally describes falling over; a stumble may lead to that, but need not.
滑る (すべる), "slip," involves losing traction rather than necessarily catching one's foot.
[失敗](失敗.md) (しっぱい)する overlaps with the figurative setback or failure use.

踏みとどまる (ふみとどまる), "hold one's ground; stop oneself," can describe avoiding a fall or retreat in context.
There is no one antonym covering both the physical and figurative senses.

## Distinctive meaning

Learn "trip" and "fall" as distinct events even though English answer labels can overlap.
In figurative use, the word identifies a difficulty in one's progress; it does not necessarily say the whole undertaking ultimately failed.
