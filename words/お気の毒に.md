# お気の毒に (おきのどくに) - my sympathies; that's too bad

## Meaning

お気の毒に expresses sympathy on hearing about someone's misfortune.
"I'm sorry to hear that," "that's too bad," or "my condolences" may fit,
depending on the seriousness of the situation.

- A: 大切な写真をなくしてしまいました。 (たいせつなしゃしんをなくしてしまいました。) - I've lost an important photograph.
- B: それはお気の毒に。 (それはおきのどくに。) - I'm sorry to hear that.

The response acknowledges the other person's misfortune; it does not say
that the speaker caused the loss.

## How the kanji combine

The base is [気の毒](気の毒.md) (きのどく).
[気](../kanji/気.md), feeling or mind, and [毒](../kanji/毒.md), harm or poison,
use on readings, with native の between them.
The whole Japanese expression also contains polite お and final に;
the kango grouping does not mean every part is a Chinese-derived reading.
Do not translate the formula literally as poisoned feelings.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[かわいそう](かわいそう.md) expresses pity.
[お見舞い](お見舞い.md) (おみまい) can name a visit, message, or gift
expressing sympathy; お気の毒に is the spoken response itself.
[残念](残念.md) (ざんねん) expresses regret or disappointment more generally.

## Distinctive meaning

This is not a general apology like すみません.
Expressions of pity can sound condescending depending on tone and relationship,
so the presence of お does not guarantee that every listener will welcome it.
The English "condolences" is appropriate to bereavement, not every minor setback.
