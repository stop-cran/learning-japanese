# 指 (ゆび) — finger; toe

## Meaning

指 is a finger or toe. English has separate everyday words for the two, but Japanese can use
手の指 (てのゆび), "finger," and 足の指 (あしのゆび), "toe," when the distinction needs to be explicit.

- 右手の指が痛いです。 (みぎてのゆびがいたいです。) — A finger on my right hand hurts.
- 足の指を動かしてください。 (あしのゆびをうごかしてください。) — Please move your toes.

The noun alone does not mark singular versus plural; the situation determines whether English needs "finger" or "fingers."

## How the kanji combine

This is the single-character native noun [指](../kanji/指.md), read ゆび.
It is not a compound. The same character also writes the verb 指す (さす), "point,"
but that verb needs its ending and has a different reading.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[手](手.md) (て) is the hand, not a synonym for an individual finger.
[足](足.md) (あし) can name the foot or leg; 足の指 specifies its digits.
Body-part names do not require an artificial antonym: the useful distinction here is the part versus the larger body region.

## Distinctive meaning

Do not restrict ゆび to the hand just because the character contains the hand-side component.
In 足の指, the phrase 足の tells you that toes are meant.
Also distinguish the noun from the action: 指で指す (ゆびでさす) means "point with a finger."
