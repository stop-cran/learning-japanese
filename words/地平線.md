# 地平線 (ちへいせん) - land horizon

## Meaning

地平線 is the horizon where land appears to meet the sky. It is a noun describing the apparent distant boundary, not a painted line on the ground.

- 平原の向こうに地平線が見えます。 (へいげんのむこうにちへいせんがみえます。) - The horizon is visible beyond the plain.
- 太陽が地平線の下に沈みました。 (たいようがちへいせんのしたにしずみました。) - The sun sank below the horizon.

地平線の向こう (ちへいせんのむこう) means "beyond the horizon." The particle の links 地平線 to the spatial expression following it.

## How the kanji combine

[地](../kanji/地.md) contributes land or ground, [平](../kanji/平.md) flatness or a level expanse, and 線 a line. 地平 supplies the land-horizon idea; 線 makes the boundary line explicit. The on readings are チ + ヘイ + セン.

## Synonyms and antonyms

水平線 (すいへいせん) is the corresponding word for the horizon over water, and can also mean a horizontal line. The contrast concerns what meets the sky: land or water. It is not a pair of opposites.

## Distinctive meaning

English "horizon" does not require the land/water distinction in its wording, so choose the Japanese term from the scene. A sunset over an open sea calls for 水平線 rather than automatically copying 地平線. Figurative English expressions about expanding one's horizons also need context-specific translations.
