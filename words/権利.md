# 権利 (けんり) — right; entitlement

## Meaning

権利 is a right or entitlement: something a person is entitled to do, have, or receive.
It is a noun. Use 権利がある (けんりがある), "have a right," rather than treating it as a verb.

- 私には説明を求める権利があります。 (わたしにはせつめいをもとめるけんりがあります。) — I have the right to ask for an explanation.
- 自分の権利を守りましょう。 (じぶんのけんりをまもりましょう。) — Let's protect our rights.

A verb phrase can identify the right: 意見を言う権利 (いけんをいうけんり), "the right to express an opinion."

## How the kanji combine

[権](../kanji/権.md) supplies authority or a right, and [利](../kanji/利.md) contributes benefit or advantage.
Together the word concerns an entitlement, not simply any advantage someone happens to have.
The on readings are ケン + リ.

## Synonyms and antonyms

権力 (けんりょく) is power or authority; it is not a synonym for an individual's entitlement.
[義務](義務.md) (ぎむ), "duty; obligation," supplies the useful contrast: what one is obliged to do rather than what one is entitled to do.
Rights and obligations can coexist, so this is not an either-or classification of people.

## Distinctive meaning

Having the practical ability to do something does not by itself establish a 権利 to do it.
Conversely, having a right does not guarantee the power to exercise it.
Which rights exist depends on the relevant rules and context; the word alone does not establish a legal claim.
