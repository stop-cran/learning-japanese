# 新幹線 (しんかんせん) - Shinkansen; bullet train

## Meaning

新幹線 names Japan's high-speed railway system and its trains.
English uses "Shinkansen" or "bullet train." It is a noun.

- 東京から京都まで新幹線で行きました。 (とうきょうからきょうとまでしんかんせんでいきました。) - I went from Tokyo to Kyoto by Shinkansen.
- 新幹線の切符を予約しました。 (しんかんせんのきっぷをよやくしました。) - I booked a Shinkansen ticket.

新幹線で marks the means of travel; 新幹線に乗る (しんかんせんにのる)
means to board or ride a Shinkansen train.

## How the kanji combine

[新](../kanji/新.md), new, qualifies 幹線 (かんせん), a trunk or main line.
幹 contributes a trunk or main-part association, and [線](../kanji/線.md)
contributes a line or railway route. The on readings are シン + カン + セン.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[鉄道](鉄道.md) (てつどう) is railway or rail transport more generally.
[列車](列車.md) (れっしゃ) names a railway train without specifying this
system. [特急](特急.md) (とっきゅう), limited express, is a service label,
not an interchangeable name for the Shinkansen network.

## Distinctive meaning

The established word names a particular Japanese railway system and its
services, not every fast train anywhere in the world. Its component image,
"new trunk line," should not be used to call every newly built railway
a 新幹線. A sentence about 新幹線 can concern the railway or a train on it.
