# 王様 (おうさま) - king

## Meaning

王様 is a respectful noun for a king. It can refer to a ruler in a story or to a king being spoken
about with the respectful suffix 様. It is not a verb or a general word for anyone in authority.

- 王様は城に住んでいました。 (おうさまはしろにすんでいました。) - The king lived in a castle.
- その国の王様は人々の話を聞きました。 (そのくにのおうさまはひとびとのはなしをききました。) - The king of that country listened to the people.

国の王様 identifies the country whose king is meant; 王様の城 means "the king's castle."

## How the kanji combine

[王](../kanji/王.md), "king," is followed by the respectful suffix [様](../kanji/様.md) (さま).
The word mixes the on reading おう with the native suffix reading さま.
The metadata uses the repository's on-first kango category; this is not a two-on-reading compound.
王さま is also a transparent spelling.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[王](王.md) (おう) names the king without the attached 様.
[女王](女王.md) (じょおう) means queen, and [王子](王子.md) (おうじ) means prince:
these are related royal titles, not antonyms.

## Distinctive meaning

The respectful form is compatible with simple storytelling; "honorific" does not mean that a word is
restricted to official documents. 王様 also appears in figurative descriptions of someone treated like
a king. Such a comparison does not grant the person an actual royal title.
