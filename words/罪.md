# 罪 (つみ) — crime; sin

## Meaning

罪 is wrongdoing viewed as a crime, sin, or offense. It can belong to a legal discussion,
but the word also reaches moral or religious wrongdoing. A useful expression is 罪を犯す
(つみをおかす), "commit a crime or sin."

- 彼は自分の罪を認めました。
  (かれはじぶんのつみをみとめました。)
  — He admitted his wrongdoing.

The English "wrongdoing" leaves the legal or moral setting open, just as the sentence does.

罪 can also mean fault or culpability, without naming a specific criminal offense:
子供に罪はありません (こどもにつみはありません), "The child is not to blame."
Here に identifies the person whose responsibility is being discussed.

## How the kanji combine

The single character [罪](../kanji/罪.md) carries the whole word.
つみ is its kun reading, used as a native Japanese noun.
ザイ is the on reading heard in compounds such as 犯罪 (はんざい), "crime."

## Synonyms and antonyms

[犯罪](犯罪.md) (はんざい) focuses on an offense or crime.
[間違い](間違い.md) (まちがい), "mistake," need not imply wrongdoing: an incorrect answer is not automatically a 罪.
有罪 (ゆうざい), "guilt," contrasts with 無罪 (むざい), "innocence; being not guilty."

## Distinctive meaning

罪 carries a judgment of wrong rather than merely saying that an outcome was unsuccessful.
Read the context before choosing "crime" or "sin" in English.
The example's 罪を認める (つみをみとめる) means acknowledging one's wrongdoing, not approving it.
