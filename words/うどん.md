# うどん - udon; thick wheat noodles

## Meaning

うどん names thick Japanese wheat noodles and dishes made with them.
It is a noun, usually written in kana.

- 昼ご飯にうどんを食べました。 (ひるごはんにうどんをたべました。) - I ate udon for lunch.
- 冷たいうどんもありますか。 (つめたいうどんもありますか。) - Do you also have cold udon?

The name does not require a hot soup: udon can be served in different ways.

## How the kanji combine

饂飩 (うどん) is a rare kanji spelling.
The Chinese-derived name has undergone sound changes; the encyclopedic account connects
うんどん and うどん with earlier Chinese-derived noodle/food names.
Here `kango` records that background, not a modern two-character reading exercise.
Learn うどん as a whole word rather than inventing separate modern readings う and どん.
The pinned dictionary marks うんどん as outdated; it is not this article's production reading.

## Synonyms and antonyms

麺 (めん) is the broader word "noodles."
[そば](蕎麦.md) and ラーメン name other noodle types, not interchangeable names for every bowl of udon.
There is no ordinary antonym for this food name.

## Distinctive meaning

English "Japanese noodles" is too broad to distinguish うどん from other noodle dishes.
The wheat-noodle identity matters; the word alone does not specify toppings, portion size or broth.
Rare 饂飩 and usual うどん are spellings of this food, not two different dishes.
