# 伐採 (ばっさい) — felling trees; logging

## Meaning

伐採 means cutting down trees and taking them out of forests and woods. JMdict glosses it “felling timber; cutting down trees; logging; lumbering”, and Digital Daijisen defines it as cutting and bringing out trees from mountain forests and similar places. It is often used as a verb, 伐採する.

- 山の木が伐採されました。
  (やまのきがばっさいされました。)
  "The trees on the mountain were cut down."
- この森では、木の伐採は禁止です。
  (このもりでは、きのばっさいはきんしです。)
  "Cutting down trees is not allowed in this forest."

These are original usage examples, not quotations from a dictionary or other source.

## How the kanji combine

[伐](../kanji/伐.md) (バツ) contributes cutting down trees; in 伐採, ばっ corresponds to バツ (see [compound sound changes](../articles/compound-sound-changes.md)). [採](../kanji/採.md) (サイ) contributes taking. This is a relation of two actions side by side: cutting the trees down and taking them out. It explains the compound's meaning, not the historical origin of either character.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[切る](切る.md) (きる), "cut," is the general verb for cutting anything; 伐採 is only for trees. [刈る](刈る.md) (かる), "mow; clip; reap," is for grass, crops or hair. [木材](木材.md) (もくざい), "lumber; timber," names the wood that can result, not the act of cutting. [森林](森林.md) (しんりん), "forest; woods," names the place.

There is no single fixed antonym. Planting trees, 植林 しょくりん, “afforestation”, is a contrasting activity, not a fixed opposite.

## Distinctive meaning

The thing cut in 伐採 is trees, such as those in mountain forests. Cutting paper, rope or flowers is not 伐採; that is ordinary 切る.
