# 企てる (くわだてる) — plan; attempt

## Meaning

企てる means to devise a plan or attempt to carry something out.
It is a transitive ichidan verb: the planned or attempted activity takes を.
Forms include 企てます (くわだてます), 企てない (くわだてない),
企てた (くわだてた) and 企てている (くわだてている).

- 新しい事業を企てています。 (あたらしいじぎょうをくわだてています。) — We are planning a new business venture.
- 彼らは何かを企てているようです。 (かれらはなにかをくわだてているようです。) — They seem to be plotting something.

The second sentence suggests suspicion about an unspecified plan.
The verb also covers undertaking or attempting an activity, not only
thinking about it. 企てた alone does not establish that the attempt succeeded.

## How the kanji combine

[企](../kanji/企.md) supplies くわだ, the planning or undertaking element
of this native verb; てる is the written ending.
Keep the whole reading くわだてる rather than using the on reading キ.
Learn くわだてる as one ichidan verb meaning "plan; attempt."

## Synonyms and antonyms

[計画](計画.md)する (けいかくする) is a more neutral way to say "plan,"
including ordinary travel arrangements. 企てる can give a plan a more
deliberate or weighty tone and can suggest a plot; it is not restricted
to bad intentions.

[引き受ける](引き受ける.md) (ひきうける) overlaps with "undertake"
when accepting responsibility. Accepting an assigned task and devising
an undertaking emphasize different things, though their English labels overlap.

## Distinctive meaning

企む (たくらむ), usually written たくらむ, is a separate godan verb
often used for scheming. It is not the contracted form of 企てる.
For an ordinary schedule, [予定](予定.md) (よてい) or 計画 is usually
a better starting point than a word that may sound like plotting.

The dictionary also records an archaic tiptoe sense of 企てる.
That label belongs to that sense, not to the planning verb taught here.
The planning use taught here does not describe physically standing on tiptoe.
