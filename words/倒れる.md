# 倒れる (たおれる) — fall over; collapse

## Meaning

倒れる describes something falling over or collapsing.
It is intransitive: the person or thing that falls is the subject, marked with が.
This does not mean there can be no outside cause; it means the verb does not take the fallen thing as a direct object.

- 強い風で木が倒れました。 (つよいかぜできがたおれました。) — A tree fell over in the strong wind.
- 自転車が倒れています。 (じてんしゃがたおれています。) — A bicycle is lying on its side.

The polite form is 倒れます (たおれます), and the plain past is 倒れた (たおれた).

## How the kanji combine

[倒](../kanji/倒.md) expresses toppling or collapse and is read たお here.
The ending れる forms part of this native verb.
Despite that ending, 倒れる is not simply the passive form of 倒す (たおす).

## Synonyms and antonyms

倒す means to knock or bring something down: 木を倒す (きをたおす), "fell a tree."
[落ちる](落ちる.md) (おちる) can describe falling from a higher place, as in a pen falling from a desk.
The distinction is useful for these physical examples, not an absolute division of every figurative "fall."

## Distinctive meaning

A tree can 倒れる by losing its upright position without first dropping from a height.
In the bicycle example, 倒れている describes the resulting position, not necessarily a fall still in progress.
Choose between 倒れる and 倒す according to whether the sentence centers on the thing falling or an action that brings it down.
