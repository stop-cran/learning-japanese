# 五十音 (ごじゅうおん) - Japanese syllabary

## Meaning

五十音 names the conventional arrangement of basic Japanese sounds
represented by kana, beginning あ、い、う、え、お, followed by
か、き、く、け、こ and the subsequent rows.
The arrangement can be shown with either hiragana or katakana.

- 五十音の表を見ながら、平仮名を練習しました。
  (ごじゅうおんのひょうをみながら、ひらがなをれんしゅうしました。) - I practiced hiragana while looking at a kana chart.
- 名簿は五十音順に並んでいます。
  (めいぼはごじゅうおんじゅんにならんでいます。) - The names on the list are arranged in Japanese syllabary order.

五十音順 (ごじゅうおんじゅん) means this conventional ordering,
not English A-to-Z order.

## How the kanji combine

[五](../kanji/五.md) + [十](../kanji/十.md) give fifty;
[音](../kanji/音.md) means sound. The on readings are ゴ + ジュウ + オン.
The name evokes the traditional five-by-ten arrangement.
It does not assert that modern Japanese has exactly fifty distinct
basic kana or exactly fifty possible syllables.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[仮名](仮名.md) (かな) is the broader writing-system term covering
hiragana and [片仮名](片仮名.md) (かたかな).
五十音 focuses on the conventional sound arrangement, rather than
being the name of only one of those two scripts.
漢字 (かんじ) is a different kind of writing, not an antonym.

## Distinctive meaning

The traditional grid has gaps and repeated sound positions, and
modern kana study includes ん. Voiced forms such as が and
combinations such as きゃ are not fifty new rows hidden in the name.
Learn the chart and ordering convention instead of interpreting
五十音 as a complete count of all Japanese pronunciations.
