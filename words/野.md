# 野 (の) - open field; plain

## Meaning

野, read の, is an open field or plain. It names a landscape, not automatically
a cultivated plot. Learn it in phrases such as 野の花 (ののはな), "wildflowers"
or "flowers of the field."

- 野の花が咲いています。 (ののはながさいています。) - Flowers are blooming in the fields.
- 野に出て鳥の声を聞きました。 (のにでてとりのこえをききました。) - I went out into the fields and listened to the birds.

The first の in ののはな is the noun 野; the second is the linking particle の.

## How the kanji combine

[野](../kanji/野.md) supplies the entire native noun の. It also has an on
reading ヤ in compounds, but that is not the pronunciation of this headword.
There are no separate written components to combine into a multi-kanji word.

## Synonyms and antonyms

野原 (のはら) also means an open field.
[畑](畑.md) (はたけ) specifically identifies a cultivated field for crops.
The two can describe neighboring places without being interchangeable.

## Distinctive meaning

野 also acts as の- in names of wild plants and animals, such as 野いちご
(のいちご), "wild strawberry." This prefix use is related to being out in the
wild. Do not read the kanji as the grammatical particle の or assume that
every English "field," including a field of research, takes this native noun.
