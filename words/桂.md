# 桂 (かつら) - katsura tree

## Meaning

桂 names the **katsura tree**, *Cercidiphyllum japonicum*. It is a deciduous tree: it loses its leaves seasonally. This article teaches the plant noun, not a person or place called Katsura.

- 庭に桂の木があります。 (にわにかつらのきがあります。) - There is a katsura tree in the garden.
- 秋には桂の葉が落ちます。 (あきにはかつらのはがおちます。) - Katsura leaves fall in autumn.

The second sentence describes seasonal leaf loss, not a claim that the tree dies when its leaves fall.

## How the kanji combine

The single character [桂](../kanji/桂.md) writes the whole native noun かつら, without okurigana. There is no compound to divide. 桂の木 (かつらのき) connects the plant name to [木](木.md), tree, with の.

桂馬 (けいま), a shogi knight, and 桂皮 (けいひ), cinnamon or cassia bark, use ケイ instead. Those established words do not make けい a replacement pronunciation for かつら in the examples above.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[木](木.md) (き), tree, and [植物](植物.md) (しょくぶつ), plant, are broader categories. [植木](植木.md) (うえき) describes a garden tree or potted plant rather than one species. There is no direct antonym.

月桂樹 (げっけいじゅ), bay laurel, is a different tree despite the shared 桂. The name alone does not establish botanical identity.

## Distinctive meaning

In other dictionary contexts 桂 can concern cinnamon or other aromatic trees, the legendary tree on the moon, or a name. The Japanese katsura plant selected here is not a universal interpretation of the character.

The homophone かつら meaning **wig or hairpiece** is a separate word, often written in kana. A spoken かつら needs context; the tree spelling 桂 does not itself mean a wig.
