# 清書 (せいしょ) - fair copy; neatly rewrite

## Meaning

清書 is a neat or fair copy of writing, and 清書する means to rewrite
it neatly, for example after preparing a draft. Here English "fair"
means a clean, presentable version, not morally fair treatment.

- 手紙の下書きを清書しました。 (てがみのしたがきをせいしょしました。) - I rewrote the draft letter neatly.
- 清書を先生に見せました。 (せいしょをせんせいにみせました。) - I showed the fair copy to the teacher.

The first uses the transitive verb with を; the second treats 清書 as
the copy. The public dictionary also records calligraphic writing
prepared for a teacher's instruction.

## How the kanji combine

[清](../kanji/清.md) contributes clean or clear, and
[書](../kanji/書.md) writing: making the writing presentable.
The on readings are セイ + ショ. The character association is a
memory aid, not a claim that the contents must be morally pure.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[下書き](下書き.md) is a rough or preliminary draft, a useful contrast
in stage and purpose. [コピー](コピー.md) and [写す](写す.md) concern
copying more generally. [書く](書く.md) is the broad verb write.
Not every act of copying or writing makes a fair copy.

## Distinctive meaning

清書 emphasizes a clean, presentable version. It does not by itself
mean proofreading all the facts, publishing a text, or merely pressing
a photocopier's copy button. A draft can be legible already, while a
fair copy may still need substantive correction.
