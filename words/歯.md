# 歯 (は) — tooth; teeth

## Meaning

歯 names a tooth or teeth. The Japanese noun itself does not distinguish singular from plural; the situation supplies that information.
For daily care, learn 歯を磨く (はをみがく), "brush one's teeth."

- 毎朝、歯を磨きます。 (まいあさ、はをみがきます。) — I brush my teeth every morning.
- 奥の歯が痛いです。 (おくのはがいたいです。) — A back tooth hurts.

The first sentence concerns teeth as a group. The second locates the pain without requiring an English-style article in Japanese.

## How the kanji combine

This is a one-kanji noun: [歯](../kanji/歯.md) with its kun reading は.
The on reading シ appears in 歯科 (しか), "dentistry," but standalone 歯 in these examples is は.

## Synonyms and antonyms

口 (くち), "mouth," names the surrounding body part, not the teeth themselves.
歯医者 (はいしゃ), "dentist," names a person who treats teeth; it is related vocabulary, not a synonym for 歯.
There is no useful everyday antonym of "tooth."

## Distinctive meaning

歯磨き (はみがき) can mean the activity of brushing teeth or the toothpaste used for it.
The verb phrase 歯を磨く clearly describes the action.
Keep that phrase separate from the body-part noun so that you can say both what hurts and what you do to care for it.
