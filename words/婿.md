# 婿 (むこ) — son-in-law; groom

## Meaning

A parent can call their daughter's husband 婿. At a wedding it can mean the groom. JMdict also gives the gloss “husband”; Digital Daijisen includes a man marrying into his wife's family. For the neutral word “husband,” use 夫 (おっと). The reading of 婿 is むこ throughout these examples.

- 娘と婿が遊びに来た。 (むすめとむこがあそびにきた。) — My daughter and son-in-law came to visit.
- 写真には花嫁と花婿が写っている。 (しゃしんにははなよめとはなむこがうつっている。) — The photo shows the bride and groom.

## How the kanji combine

[婿](../kanji/婿.md) writes the whole word むこ. The photo example uses the separate compound 花婿 (はなむこ), specifically “bridegroom.” 婿 still supplies むこ.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[夫](夫.md) (おっと) identifies a husband from the standpoint of marriage itself. [嫁](嫁.md) (よめ) can mean a daughter-in-law: the wife of a son.

## Distinctive meaning

If a parent introduces 娘の夫 (むすめのおっと), the person is their 婿. That relationship alone tells you nothing about his surname or where the couple lives.
