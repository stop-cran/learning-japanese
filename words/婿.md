# 婿 (むこ) — son-in-law; groom

## Meaning

A parent can call their daughter's husband 婿. From the perspective of a wedding it can also mean the groom. The same reading, むこ, serves both uses.

- 娘と婿が遊びに来た。 (むすめとむこがあそびにきた。) — My daughter and son-in-law came to visit.
- 写真には花嫁と花婿が写っている。 (しゃしんにははなよめとはなむこがうつっている。) — The photo shows the bride and groom.

## How the kanji combine

[婿](../kanji/婿.md) writes the whole word むこ. In 花婿 (はなむこ) the 花 comes first, and the reading むこ stays the same.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[夫](夫.md) (おっと) identifies a husband from the standpoint of marriage itself. [嫁](嫁.md) (よめ) can mean a daughter-in-law, the corresponding relationship from the other side.

## Distinctive meaning

If a parent introduces 娘の夫 (むすめのおっと), the person is their 婿. That relationship alone tells you nothing about his surname or where the couple lives.
