# 兼任 (けんにん) — holding concurrent posts

## Meaning

兼任 means one person holding two or more posts or duties concurrently.
Use 兼任する as a verb, marking the post or posts with **を**:
an additional post, or two posts joined with **と**.

- 首相が外相を兼任しています。
  (しゅしょうががいしょうをけんにんしています。) — The prime minister also serves as foreign minister.
- 彼女は社長と会長を兼任しています。
  (かのじょはしゃちょうとかいちょうをけんにんしています。) — She holds both the president's and the chairperson's positions.

These are overlapping appointments, not necessarily two tasks performed
at the same instant.

## How the kanji combine

[兼](../kanji/兼.md) contributes combining or serving concurrently;
[任](../kanji/任.md) contributes a post or duty. The modifier 兼 describes
how the posts are held. The on readings are ケン + ニン.
This explains the compound's meaning, not the historical origin of either character.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[兼ねる](兼ねる.md) (かねる) also describes combining posts, but extends
to combined functions, such as a room serving two purposes.
兼務 (けんむ) is a close synonym for carrying out concurrent duties.
専任 (せんにん), dedicated service in a particular post or duty, is the contrast.

[就任](就任.md) (しゅうにん) means taking office. It marks entry into a
post, not the continued holding of two posts. Compare the particles:
社長に就任する (しゃちょうにしゅうにんする), "take office as president,"
but 外相を兼任する (がいしょうをけんにんする), "also serve as foreign minister."

## Distinctive meaning

The point is that **one person has multiple roles**. Two colleagues sharing
one job do not establish 兼任 merely by sharing it. Nor does ordinary employment
by itself imply concurrent posts: [勤める](勤める.md) (つとめる) can simply
mean working for an employer.
