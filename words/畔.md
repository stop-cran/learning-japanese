# 畔 (あぜ) — paddy-field ridge

## Meaning

畔, read **あぜ**, is a raised earth boundary between rice fields. It is often written あぜ or 畦. The ridge can separate fields and help retain their water.

- 田んぼのあぜに草が生えています。（たんぼのあぜにくさがはえています。）— Grass is growing on the ridge between the rice fields.
- あぜを歩いて、隣の田んぼへ行きました。（あぜをあるいて、となりのたんぼへいきました。）— I walked along the paddy ridge to the neighboring rice field.

The second example uses を for the route walked along, not for something being changed. A field path can also be called あぜ道 (あぜみち).

## How the kanji combine

[畔](../kanji/畔.md) writes the whole native noun あぜ here. There are no separately pronounced components to combine.

The same family also records 畔 as くろ, a regional word for a raised field boundary. Learn it as an alternative word reading, not an automatic pronunciation change in every compound. The primary reading of this article remains あぜ.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[畝](畝.md) (うね) is a raised planting row. The useful contrast is the ridge's role: **a boundary between paddies** versus **a row on which crops are planted**. Both may be called "ridge" in English.

[田んぼ](田んぼ.md) (たんぼ) names the field, not just its edge. [道](道.md) (みち), road or path, is much broader than a paddy ridge.

## Distinctive meaning

The same あぜ family also names the dividing ridge between grooves in a sliding-door threshold or upper frame. It can also abbreviate あぜ道. These senses should not be replaced with "rice field" in every context.

The spelling 畔 can separately be read ほとり, side or shore, though that spelling is rare and the word is usually in kana. That is not the reading used in this article's field-ridge examples. [湖畔](湖畔.md) (こはん), lakeside, uses the on reading ハン instead.
