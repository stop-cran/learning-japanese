# 盛る (もる) - dish up; heap up; exaggerate

## Meaning

盛る, read **もる**, means to dish food into a bowl or onto a plate,
or to pile something up.
It is a transitive godan verb: 盛ります, **盛って**, 盛らない.
を marks what is served or piled; に can mark the container.

- 茶碗にご飯を盛りました。 (ちゃわんにごはんをもりました。) - I dished rice into a bowl.
- 皿に果物を山のように盛りました。 (さらにくだものをやまのようにもりました。) - I heaped fruit onto a plate.

盛る also means mixing or administering medicine or poison.
一服盛る (いっぷくもる) can mean giving someone a dose, especially poison.
The source's "prescribe" should not be treated as the unrestricted ordinary
verb for a doctor writing a prescription; 処方する (しょほうする) names prescribing.

As an additional slang use, it means exaggerating a story or applying heavy makeup.

- 彼は話を盛っています。 (かれははなしをもっています。) - He is exaggerating the story.

## How the kanji combine

盛 writes the native verb もる, associated here with filling or heaping.
る is the inflecting ending.
The same character has other words and readings;
do not replace もる with さかる or さかん.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[積む](積む.md) (つむ) and [重ねる](重ねる.md) (かさねる)
overlap with piling or stacking.
[注ぐ](注ぐ.md) includes the separate つぐ reading for pouring or serving,
including serving rice; its そそぐ reading should not be transferred indiscriminately.
[オーバー](オーバー.md) can describe exaggerated expression.

## Distinctive meaning

Serving food, building a heap, and embellishing a story are different uses,
not instructions to translate every occurrence as "serve."
[盛り](盛り.md) read さかり means a peak or prime;
the separate food-serving reading もり appears in 大盛り (おおもり), a large serving.
The medical and slang uses do not make all occurrences either sinister or informal.
