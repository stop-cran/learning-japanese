# 形容詞 (けいようし) - adjective; Japanese i-adjective

## Meaning

形容詞 is a noun naming an adjective.
In traditional Japanese school grammar, it names the inflecting adjective class often called **i-adjectives** by learners.
Such words have a dictionary form ending in い and can describe a noun or serve as a predicate.

- 「高い」は形容詞です。 (「たかい」はけいようしです。) - Takai is an i-adjective.
- 高い山が見えます。 (たかいやまがみえます。) - I can see a high mountain.
- この山は高いです。 (このやまはたかいです。) - This mountain is high.

高い directly precedes 山 in the second example.
Its inflected forms include 高くない (たかくない), "not high," and 高かった (たかかった), "was high."

## How the kanji combine

[形](../kanji/形.md), "form," and [容](../kanji/容.md), with an appearance association, form 形容 (けいよう), describing;
[詞](../kanji/詞.md) supplies the grammatical word-category element.
Learn ケイ + ヨウ + シ as the whole category name, not a test based only on the literal character meanings.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[形容動詞](形容動詞.md) (けいようどうし) names a different class in that school-grammar account, associated with learner "na-adjectives."
English "adjective" can cover both classes in other descriptions.
The study-list gloss "true adjective" should not be read as declaring na-adjectives false or defective.

## Distinctive meaning

The term depends on the language and grammatical framework being discussed.
Do not classify a Japanese word solely because its spelling ends in い: 嫌い (きらい), "disliked; dislike," behaves as a na-adjective.
This article teaches the noun naming the category, not a complete adjective-conjugation course.
