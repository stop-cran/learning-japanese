# 私 (わたし) — I; me

## Meaning

私 is a first-person pronoun: "I" or "me." わたし is a useful choice in polite introductions for speakers of any gender.
The particle shows its role; の makes a phrase meaning "my."

- 私は学生です。 (わたしはがくせいです。) — I am a student.
- これは私の本です。 (これはわたしのほんです。) — This is my book.

The more formal reading わたくし also means "I/me": 私が担当いたします
(わたくしがたんとういたします), "I will be responsible for this." The reading suits formal
self-reference; the polite or humble wording of the rest of the sentence still matters.

## How the kanji combine

This is one native word written with [私](../kanji/私.md), not a multi-kanji compound.
The whole character is read わたし, with no okurigana.
The character's "private" meaning in compounds does not mean that the pronoun itself translates as "private."

## Synonyms and antonyms

- 私 (わたくし) is another first-person word with the same spelling, normally more formal than わたし.
- 僕 (ぼく) is a first-person pronoun commonly associated with male speakers.
- 俺 (おれ) is a masculine-associated, informal and potentially blunt first-person pronoun; it is not a safe replacement in a polite introduction.
- There is no direct antonym. "You" refers to a different participant, not an opposite of "I."

## Distinctive meaning

The choice of first-person pronoun conveys social style as well as reference. In casual conversation わたし
can sound more feminine or more formal, depending on the speaker and context; "women only" would be an incorrect rule.
Japanese also often leaves the pronoun out when the speaker is already clear.

The primary reading on this card is わたし; わたくし is taught as a more formal alternative.
The identical spelling does not make the two readings stylistically interchangeable.
