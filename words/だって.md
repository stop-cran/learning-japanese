# だって (だって) - because; but; even

## Meaning

だって has different jobs depending on its position. At the start of a response,
it can introduce a reason or objection: "because," "after all," or "but."
This conjunction use is conversational and can sound defensive.

- 「どうして行かないの？」「だって、忙しいんだ。」 (「どうしていかないの？」「だって、いそがしいんだ。」) - "Why aren't you going?" "Because I'm busy."
- 「もう帰ろう。」「だって、まだ見たい物があるよ。」 (「もうかえろう。」「だって、まだみたいものがあるよ。」) - "Let's go home." "But there are still things I want to see."

After a noun, the particle can mean "even" or an emphatic "too":
子供だって分かる (こどもだってわかる), "Even a child understands";
私だって行きたい (わたしだっていきたい), "I want to go too."

## How the kanji combine

This expression is written in kana. Learn the position and construction rather
than looking for a kanji decomposition. Sentence-initial だって and noun + だって
are not interchangeable simply because they share their spelling.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[でも](でも.md) can introduce "but." [なぜなら](なぜなら.md) explicitly introduces
a reason and suits a different explanatory structure. Neither replaces all the
particle uses above.

## Distinctive meaning

The reason use responds to what has just been said; it is not the usual neutral
choice for every written explanation. Noun + だって places emphasis on inclusion,
either surprising inclusion ("even") or the speaker's parallel claim ("too").
The context determines which English translation fits.
