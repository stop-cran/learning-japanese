# 呼び出す (よびだす) - summon; call up; invoke

## Meaning

呼び出す means to summon someone, have them come, call them to the
phone, or page them. The person takes を; a destination can take に.

- 先生が私を職員室に呼び出しました。 (せんせいがわたしをしょくいんしつによびだしました。) - The teacher summoned me to the staff room.
- 館内放送でお客様を呼び出しました。 (かんないほうそうでおきゃくさまをよびだしました。) - We paged the customer over the building's public-address system.

In computing it can mean to call or invoke a function, or call up
stored data or a file:
保存したデータを呼び出します (ほぞんしたでーたをよびだします),
"I'll call up the saved data."

It is a transitive godan verb: 呼び出します, 呼び出した,
呼び出さない.

## How the kanji combine

[呼](../kanji/呼.md)び comes from calling, and
[出](../kanji/出.md)す contributes bringing out.
This is a native compound, よび + だす, not two on readings.
The combined meaning can concern bringing someone to a requested
place or bringing information into use; it need not mean going outdoors.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[呼ぶ](呼ぶ.md) (よぶ) is broader calling or summoning.
[呼び掛ける](呼び掛ける.md) (よびかける) includes calling out
and appealing to someone.
[取り出す](取り出す.md) (とりだす) can concern retrieving stored
material, while [開く](開く.md), in its ひらく reading, is a useful
comparison for opening a file.

## Distinctive meaning

The staff-room example requests the person's presence; it does
not merely give the teacher's opinion about them.
The computing sense similarly concerns calling something into use,
not manufacturing the data. Learn the context rather than assuming
that every English "call," "open" or "page" has this Japanese meaning.
A page of a book, for example, is not a summoning action.
