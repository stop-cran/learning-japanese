# 川柳 (せんりゅう) - senryu; humorous short verse

## Meaning

川柳 is senryu, a short Japanese poetic form that often treats human behavior and everyday life with humor or satire. Its basic pattern is 5-7-5 Japanese sound units, or morae. That is not seventeen English syllables; ん, for example, counts as a mora.

- 仕事を題材にした川柳を読みました。 (しごとをだいざいにしたせんりゅうをよみました。) - I read a senryu about work.
- この川柳はおもしろいです。 (このせんりゅうはおもしろいです。) - This senryu is amusing.

Humor and the 5-7-5 pattern are useful starting points, not a claim that every modern work must fit an exceptionless formula or be a joke.

## How the kanji combine

[川](../kanji/川.md) supplies セン and [柳](../kanji/柳.md) supplies リュウ. Although the characters individually suggest river and willow, the verse name is not a modifier-plus-head description of a tree. It comes from the pen name of 柄井川柳 からいせんりゅう, an Edo-period verse selector.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[俳句](俳句.md) はいく is a related poetic form, traditionally associated with a seasonal word. 川柳 does not require that convention and often focuses on human affairs. This is a practical comparison, not a claim that every haiku concerns only nature or that modern traditions never overlap.

[詩](詩.md) し is broader poetry or a poem; [句](句.md) く can name a verse or phrase. There is no useful direct antonym.

## Distinctive meaning

The written form 川柳 also has a different reading, かわやなぎ, for a riverside willow, and an archaic かわやぎ tree reading. JMdict additionally records a kind of coarse tea under かわやなぎ. Those belong to separate dictionary families, not extra meanings pronounced せんりゅう. The article's primary reading and examples concern verse.
