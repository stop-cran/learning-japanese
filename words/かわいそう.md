# かわいそう (かわいそう) - pitiable; unfortunate

## Meaning

かわいそう expresses pity for someone in an unfortunate situation. It behaves as a na-adjective: かわいそうな人, "an unfortunate person," and かわいそうだ, "I feel sorry for them." The speaker is evaluating someone's circumstances.

- 一人で泣いている子がかわいそうです。 (ひとりでないているこがかわいそうです。) - I feel sorry for the child crying alone.
- そんなに責めたら、彼がかわいそうです。 (そんなにせめたら、かれがかわいそうです。) - It is unfair to him to blame him so much.

The second translation conveys the pity expressed in context, not a separate dictionary meaning of "unfair."

## How the kanji combine

Kana is the usual spelling. 可哀想 and 可哀相 are recorded ateji spellings; their characters should not be treated as a literal derivation. Learn かわいそう as a complete word, not as the regular "looks cute" form of かわいい.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[気の毒](気の毒.md) (きのどく) also expresses sympathy for misfortune. [貧しい](貧しい.md) (まずしい) means poor in resources; someone can be かわいそう without lacking money.

## Distinctive meaning

Directly calling someone かわいそう can sound pitying or patronizing, even when kindly intended. It describes how their situation strikes the speaker, rather than simply offering help. The English gloss "pathetic" should not be used to assume that the Japanese word necessarily mocks or insults its subject.
