# 突き当たる (つきあたる) - collide with; reach a street end

## Meaning

突き当たる means to bump or collide with something.
It also means reaching the end of a street, and figuratively running into a difficulty.
It is an intransitive godan verb; what is encountered takes に.

- 暗い廊下で壁に突き当たりました。
  (くらいろうかでかべにつきあたりました。) - I bumped into a wall in the dark hallway.
- この道を進むと、公園の入口に突き当たります。
  (このみちをすすむと、こうえんのいりぐちにつきあたります。) - If you follow this street, you will come to the park entrance at its end.
- 研究は大きな問題に突き当たりました。
  (けんきゅうはおおきなもんだいにつきあたりました。) - The research ran into a major problem.

Forms include 突き当たります, 突き当たった, 突き当たって,
and 突き当たらない. It is not ichidan despite its final る.

## How the kanji combine

[突](../kanji/突.md) contributes thrusting or bumping, and
[当](../kanji/当.md) contributes coming against or hitting.
The native elements つき + あたる combine into the verb.
The noun [突き当たり](突き当たり.md) (つきあたり) identifies the street or hallway end.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[衝突](衝突.md) (しょうとつ) is a collision or clash.
[当たる](当たる.md) (あたる) has broader hitting or coming-against uses.
[着く](着く.md) (つく) means arrive at a destination.
[届く](届く.md) (とどく) also has reach or arrive uses.
Arrival alone does not require reaching the end of a street.

## Distinctive meaning

The directional use does not tell someone to crash into a wall or gate.
It describes coming to the point where the way ahead ends.
Likewise, 問題に突き当たる is encountering a problem, not solving it.
The collision, route-end, and difficulty uses share an encounter image
without requiring the same physical event.
