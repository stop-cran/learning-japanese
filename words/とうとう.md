# とうとう (とうとう) - finally; in the end

## Meaning

とうとう introduces an eventual outcome after time, effort, expectation, or a developing situation. The outcome may be welcome or unwelcome.

- 長い間待って、とうとう会えました。 (ながいあいだまって、とうとうあえました。) - After waiting a long time, I finally got to meet them.
- とうとう電車に間に合いませんでした。 (とうとうでんしゃにまにあいませんでした。) - In the end, I did not make the train.

It is an adverb and does not need に before the predicate.

## How the kanji combine

到頭 is a rare dictionary-listed spelling; everyday Japanese normally writes this word in kana. The source's kanji spelling represents the same word, not a special literary meaning that must be reproduced in conversation.

## Synonyms and antonyms

ついに also emphasizes an eventual result. やっと often highlights effort, difficulty, or relief in achieving something. These meanings overlap; they are not separated by an absolute positive-versus-negative rule.

## Distinctive meaning

The English gloss "after all" here means "in the end." It does not mean the reasoning expression in "After all, it is Sunday." とうとう also differs from the organizing phrase "finally, my last point," which introduces the last item rather than an eventual outcome.
