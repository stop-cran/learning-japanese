# 稲穂 (いなほ) — ear of rice

## Meaning

稲穂 is an **ear or head of rice**: the grain-bearing part of the rice plant, not the entire plant.

- 稲穂が風に揺れています。（いなほがかぜにゆれています。）— The heads of rice are swaying in the wind.
- 稲穂が垂れています。（いなほがたれています。）— The heads of rice are drooping.

English "ear" here describes part of a cereal plant, not an organ for hearing.

## How the kanji combine

[稲](../kanji/稲.md) contributes **いな-**, the rice-plant form used in this compound, and [穂](../kanji/穂.md) contributes **ほ**, an ear or head of grain. It is a modifier-plus-head compound: a head **of rice**.

The independent plant noun [稲](稲.md) is read いね. Learn いなほ as a whole word; do not replace its first part with いね.

## Synonyms and antonyms

穂 (ほ) can name the head of a cereal plant more generally. 稲穂 specifies rice. [稲](稲.md) いね names the rice plant, and [米](米.md) こめ names rice as grain. They are related things, not interchangeable labels for the same part in every sentence.

There is no useful direct antonym for this plant part.

## Distinctive meaning

The key distinction is **part versus whole**. Use 稲穂 when the rice heads are in focus; use 稲 for the plants or crop. Neither word is the ordinary choice for ordering a bowl of cooked rice.
