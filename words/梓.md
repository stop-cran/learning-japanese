# 梓 (あずさ) - azusa tree

## Meaning

梓 is **azusa**, a tree name generally identified in Japanese with Japanese cherry birch, *Betula grossa*. Dictionaries also preserve catalpa and Japanese mallotus identifications. A historical text or a botanical label needs its own context; the spelling alone is not a guarantee of one species.

- これは梓の木です。 (これはあずさのきです。) - This is an azusa tree.
- 梓の木で弓を作りました。 (あずさのきでゆみをつくりました。) - They made a bow from azusa wood.

The second sentence selects the documented wood-for-bows use. It is a language example, not a claim about who made a particular historical bow.

JMdict marks the Japanese-cherry-birch sense as usually written in kana. This is a spelling tendency, not a claim that the pronunciation is rare or that 梓 is invalid.

## How the kanji combine

[梓](../kanji/梓.md) writes あずさ as a whole. There is no compound to divide. In 梓の木 (あずさのき), の connects the name to [木](木.md), tree or wood.

The character also has the on reading シ. 梓 (し) is a printing block, and 上梓 (じょうし) means publication or woodblock printing. The source family does not restrict its printing-block sense to し alone; the Japanese dictionary also records this sense under あずさ. This article's primary reading is あずさ.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[木](木.md) (き), tree or wood, and [植物](植物.md) (しょくぶつ), plant, are broader categories. They do not identify azusa. There is no direct antonym.

梓弓 (あずさゆみ) names a spirit bow, traditionally used to summon spirits; 梓 alone can abbreviate that bow. あずさみこ names a female medium using such a bow, and 梓 can abbreviate that word too in older material. These are context-dependent shortened uses, not synonyms for the tree.

## Distinctive meaning

Do not translate every 梓 as "catalpa." The Japanese tree name and the Chinese tree reference underlying printing vocabulary have different naming histories. This lesson selects the azusa-tree sense without claiming that the other dictionary senses are mistakes.

The printing-block sense names a tool; [印刷](印刷.md) (いんさつ) names printing, and [出版](出版.md) (しゅっぱん) names publication. Neither English activity label by itself describes the tree in the examples.
