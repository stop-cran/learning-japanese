# 憲法 (けんぽう) - constitution

## Meaning

憲法 is a noun meaning a constitution: the fundamental framework of a country's government and law.
日本の憲法 (にほんのけんぽう) means "Japan's constitution."
Use 憲法について for a discussion "about the constitution."

- 授業で日本の憲法について学びました。 (じゅぎょうでにほんのけんぽうについてまなびました。) - We learned about Japan's constitution in class.
- 憲法を変えるべきか、話し合いました。 (けんぽうをかえるべきか、はなしあいました。) - We discussed whether the constitution should be changed.

The second example reports a discussion, not the speaker's position on the proposed change.
憲法を守る (けんぽうをまもる) means "uphold/observe the constitution."

## How the kanji combine

憲 contributes a standard or constitutional rule, and [法](../kanji/法.md) law.
Together they identify constitutional law rather than every individual rule.
The reading taught here is けんぽう, with ぽう for 法 in this compound.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[法律](法律.md) (ほうりつ) means law or legislation more broadly.
[規則](規則.md) (きそく) means rules or regulations, including those of a school or workplace.
These overlap in the idea of rules but are not interchangeable names for a country's constitution.

## Distinctive meaning

English "constitution" also describes someone's physical makeup or health; that is not this vocabulary sense.
A dictionary can list a broader "rules/regulation" use of 憲法, but that does not justify calling an everyday classroom rule a constitution.
Learn the governmental meaning first.
