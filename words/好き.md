# 好き (すき) — fond of; liking

## Meaning

好き describes liking something or being fond of it. Although English often uses the verb "like," Japanese treats 好き as a **な-adjective**:
use 好きです as a polite predicate and 好きな before a noun.
A useful basic pattern is 私は X が好きです, "I like X."

- 私は音楽が好きです。 (わたしはおんがくがすきです。) — I like music.
- これは私の好きな本です。 (これはわたしのすきなほんです。) — This is a book I like.
- 本を読むのが好きです。 (ほんをよむのがすきです。) — I like reading books.

In the last example, verb + の turns the activity into something you can say you like.

## How the kanji combine

There is only one kanji: [好](../kanji/好.md) carries the liking or preference meaning.
The native Japanese word is written 好き, with き in kana; it is not the on reading コウ followed by き.
The related verb 好む (このむ), "like; prefer," has its own pronunciation and grammar.

## Synonyms and antonyms

大好き (だいすき) strengthens the feeling: "like very much; love."
嫌い (きらい), "dislike," is the contrasting な-adjective.
好きではありません (すきではありません), "do not like," is a grammatical negative and need not convey the stronger feeling of hating something.

## Distinctive meaning

Use the adjective pattern rather than adding する to imitate the English verb.
Before a noun, 好きな is essential: 好きな音楽 (すきなおんがく), "music one likes."
When the object of the feeling is a person, context determines whether the liking is romantic; the word alone does not settle that question.
