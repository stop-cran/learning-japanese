# 濡らす (ぬらす) - make wet; moisten

## Meaning

濡らす means making something wet, moistening it or soaking it.
It is transitive: the thing made wet takes を.
It is a godan verb: 濡らします (ぬらします), 濡らした (ぬらした),
濡らして (ぬらして), 濡らさない (ぬらさない).

- タオルを水で濡らしました。
  (タオルをみずでぬらしました。) - I wet the towel with water.
- 本を濡らさないでください。
  (ほんをぬらさないでください。) - Please do not get the book wet.

Water is marked with で in the first example. Making something wet
can be deliberate or accidental; the verb alone does not decide.

## How the kanji combine

濡 supplies ぬ and らす is okurigana in this native verb.
Compare [ぬれる](ぬれる.md), also written 濡れる (ぬれる), "get wet."
The paired verbs differ in whether an agent makes something wet
or the subject becomes wet.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[漬ける](漬ける.md) (つける) includes soaking something in liquid.
濡らす does not require immersion: a splash can wet a book.
[乾かす](乾かす.md) (かわかす), "dry something," is a practical contrast.
湿る (しめる) describes becoming damp rather than the transitive action
of moistening something.

## Distinctive meaning

Compare タオルを濡らした, "I wet the towel," with
タオルがぬれた, "the towel got wet."
Changing を to が changes which participant the sentence presents;
the two verbs are not mechanically interchangeable.
The source gloss "dip" should not be read as dipping anything into anything:
the relevant result here is wetness.
