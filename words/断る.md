# 断る (ことわる) - refuse; decline

## Meaning

断る is a transitive godan verb meaning "refuse" or "turn down."
The request, invitation, or offer takes を. Its polite form is 断ります,
its て-form is 断って, and its negative is 断らない.

- 忙しかったので、食事の誘いを断りました。 (いそがしかったので、しょくじのさそいをことわりました。) - I was busy, so I declined the invitation to a meal.
- 先生に断ってから、教室を出ました。 (せんせいにことわってから、きょうしつをでました。) - I asked the teacher's permission before leaving the classroom.

The second example uses another meaning: informing someone beforehand and
obtaining approval, not refusing the teacher.

## How the kanji combine

[断](../kanji/断.md) conveys cutting off or rejecting in the refusal sense.
The native verb is read ことわる, with る written as its ending.
The character alone does not explain which sense a sentence uses.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[受ける](受ける.md) (うける), "accept," can contrast with turning down a
request. [許す](許す.md) (ゆるす), "permit," describes giving permission,
whereas 先生に断る can describe seeking it or giving prior notice.
These distinctions depend on who performs the action.

## Distinctive meaning

誰にも断らずに (だれにもことわらずに), "without telling/asking anyone
beforehand," belongs to the notice-and-permission use. Do not translate every
instance as "refuse." Conversely, an invitation followed by を断る clearly
supports declining it. Reporting that you declined does not itself specify
whether your actual words were gentle or blunt.
