# 湯気 (ゆげ) - steam rising from hot water

## Meaning

湯気 is steam or vapor rising from hot water, hot food and similar
sources. In everyday observation, the white cloud is tiny condensed
water droplets, not the invisible gaseous water itself.

- 熱いスープから湯気が上がっています。 (あついスープからゆげがあがっています。) - Steam is rising from the hot soup.
- 鍋のふたを開けると、湯気が出ました。 (なべのふたをあけると、ゆげがでました。) - Steam came out when I lifted the pot's lid.

## How the kanji combine

[湯](../kanji/湯.md), hot water, qualifies [気](../kanji/気.md),
air or vapor. Native ゆ comes first; 気 uses its on reading ケ,
voiced here to げ. This is a native-first mixed reading, filed
as wago under this repository's convention, not ゆ + キ.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[蒸気](蒸気.md) (じょうき) is the broader steam/vapor term.
[水蒸気](水蒸気.md) (すいじょうき) is water vapor, especially
water in its gaseous state.
[霧](霧.md) (きり) is atmospheric fog or mist, and
[煙](煙.md) (けむり) is smoke, not another name for 湯気.

## Distinctive meaning

Everyday English "steam" can cover what one sees over a hot cup.
Scientific discussion distinguishes the visible condensed droplets
from invisible water vapor. Do not use that distinction to claim
the ordinary words 湯気 and 蒸気 never overlap.
This card teaches ゆげ, not an unrelated heading read ゆけ.
