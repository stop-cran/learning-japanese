# 意思 (いし) - intention; wish

## Meaning

意思 is a person's intention or wish concerning what they want to do. It is a noun: 意思がある means "have the intention," and 意思を伝える means "communicate one's wishes." A plain nonpast verb can describe the intended action before 意思.

- 参加する意思はありますか。 (さんかするいしはありますか。) - Do you intend to participate?
- 本人の意思を確認しましょう。 (ほんにんのいしをかくにんしましょう。) - Let's check what the person themselves wants.

本人の意思 emphasizes that person's own wishes, rather than what someone else has decided for them.

## How the kanji combine

[意](../kanji/意.md) contributes intention or what is in the mind, and [思](../kanji/思.md) contributes thinking. Their related meanings combine around an intention held in someone's mind. Both use on readings: イ + シ.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[意志](意志.md), also いし, focuses on will or determination. The meanings overlap, but 意思を確認する asks what someone intends, whereas 強い意志 (つよいいし) highlights resolve.

[目的](目的.md) (もくてき) is the goal being pursued. [つもり](つもり.md) is useful for stating an intention: 参加するつもりです, "I intend to participate." There is no simple general antonym.

## Distinctive meaning

意思 does not guarantee action or success. Someone can express a wish without carrying it out. It is useful in discussions of agreement, consent, and personal choice, but is not a substitute for every English use of "mind," such as intelligence or remembering something.
