# いよいよ - at last; increasingly

## Meaning

いよいよ can mark reaching an awaited or decisive stage: "at last" or
"now it is really happening." It also describes an existing condition
becoming stronger: "more and more."

- いよいよ明日、試験です。 (いよいよあした、しけんです。) - At last, the exam is tomorrow.
- 風はいよいよ強くなりました。 (かぜはいよいよつよくなりました。) - The wind grew stronger and stronger.

The exam example anticipates the coming event; it does not say that the
exam has already finished. The wind example concerns degree, not the
final item in a list.

## How the kanji combine

The dictionary marks kana usage as usual. It also lists the rare
spellings 愈, 愈々, and 弥々. Learn the whole adverb いよいよ;
these spellings are not necessary for ordinary production.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[とうとう](とうとう.md) and [遂に](遂に.md) (ついに) describe an
eventual outcome. [やっと](やっと.md) often highlights waiting or effort.
[ますます](ますます.md) overlaps with the increasing-degree use.
None is an unrestricted replacement in every construction.

## Distinctive meaning

いよいよ can look forward to an imminent event as well as describe a
stage reached. It does not inherently guarantee a happy result: context
supplies the speaker's attitude and the direction of change.
Do not use it merely to introduce the last point in an ordinary list.
