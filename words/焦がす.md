# 焦がす (こがす) - scorch; burn; char

## Meaning

焦がす is to scorch, singe, burn, or char something.
It is a transitive godan verb; the affected thing takes を.
Forms include 焦がします, 焦がした, 焦がして, and 焦がさない.

- ご飯を焦がしてしまいました。 (ごはんをこがしてしまいました。) - I accidentally burned the rice.
- トーストを少し焦がしました。 (トーストをすこしこがしました。) - I slightly scorched the toast.

The first example expresses an unwanted result with てしまう.
The verb itself does not require that scorching was accidental.

## How the kanji combine

焦 contributes scorching or charring. The native verb is こがす,
with がす written as okurigana. It is not an on-reading compound.
Compare 焦げる (こげる), the intransitive verb: パンが焦げた
(パンがこげた), "the bread got burnt."

## Synonyms and antonyms

[焼く](焼く.md) (やく) can describe cooking by heat or burning
something, and [焼ける](焼ける.md) (やける) is intransitive.
Their cooking uses need not imply scorching.
[燃える](燃える.md) (もえる) concerns burning or being on fire;
something can be scorched without being described as ablaze.

## Distinctive meaning

Learn the particle contrast: パンを焦がす, "scorch bread," versus
パンが焦げる, "bread gets scorched."
An object marked with を does not make this 焦げる, and the English
word "burn" alone does not establish which Japanese construction fits.
This card focuses on literal scorching, not every figurative use of 焦.
