# 寅 (とら) — Tiger zodiac sign

## Meaning

寅 is the **Tiger sign**, the third of the twelve zodiac signs.
It identifies a place in the cycle, not a living tiger.

- 私の干支は寅です。 (わたしのえとはとらです。) — My zodiac sign is the Tiger.
- 十二支の三番目は寅です。 (じゅうにしのさんばんめはとらです。) — The third of the twelve zodiac signs is the Tiger.

十二支 (じゅうにし) names the twelve-sign set. In the first example,
干支 (えと) has its everyday twelve-year zodiac sense. 干支 can also
mean the sixty-term calendar cycle combining stems and branches. The
reading かんし is documented for that sixty-term sense, not as a substitute
for えと's twelve-year zodiac sense.

## How the kanji combine

The single character [寅](../kanji/寅.md) writes the whole native sign noun
とら, with no okurigana or second character to separate.

Recognize 寅年 (とらどし), **year of the Tiger**: 寅 identifies the sign
and [年](../kanji/年.md) the year. Learn the whole reading とらどし;
the separate noun [年](年.md) has the primary reading とし.
Daijisen also records 寅年 for a person born in that year.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[虎](虎.md) とら is the ordinary animal noun, also written トラ.
It names the animal associated with the sign, but is not an unrestricted
substitute for the sign spelling 寅. The sign has no useful direct antonym.

## Distinctive meaning

Keep the spelling tied to the context: 寅 for the sign, 虎 or トラ for
a living tiger. Both are read とら. 虎 also has a colloquial drunkard
sense; that does not make 寅 mean drunkard.

Older uses of 寅 include an hour around 4 a.m., a traditional direction
labelled east-northeast, and the first lunar month. Dictionaries give both
3–5 a.m. and 4–6 a.m. hour mappings, not one universal modern interval.
The direction is distinct from [丑](丑.md)寅 (うしとら), northeast. These are not
ordinary modern time or direction substitutes, and the lunar month is
not Gregorian January.
