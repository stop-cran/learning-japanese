# ところで (ところで) - by the way; even if

## Meaning

At the beginning of a new topic, ところで means "by the way" or "incidentally."

- ところで、明日は何時に集まりますか。 (ところで、あしたはなんじにあつまりますか。) - By the way, what time are we meeting tomorrow?

There is also a concessive construction, **verb in plain past form + ところで**, meaning "even if." It often presents the action as unlikely to change an unfavorable result.

- 今から走ったところで、電車には間に合いません。 (いまからはしったところで、でんしゃにはまにあいません。) - Even if we run now, we will not make the train.

走った is the required past-form attachment here; the sentence is hypothetical, not a report that we already ran.

## How the kanji combine

This expression is usually written in kana. The dictionary also records 所で, but the kana spelling keeps attention on the whole construction. No loanword origin or kanji decomposition is needed.

## Synonyms and antonyms

そういえば, "come to think of it," can introduce a related recollection rather than simply a new topic. For concession, ～ても also means "even if/even though," but has a broader range of uses than the unfavorable-outcome example above.

## Distinctive meaning

Position and attachment distinguish the two uses. Sentence-initial ところで changes the topic; 走ったところで belongs to a clause and concedes a hypothetical action. Do not substitute "by the way" into the second example or attach the concessive form directly to the dictionary form 走る.
