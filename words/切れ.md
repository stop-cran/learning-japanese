# 切れ (きれ) - piece; slice; sharpness

## Meaning

切れ is a noun for a piece, slice, strip, or scrap. It is also used as a counter for cut pieces: 一切れ (ひときれ), "one slice," and 二切れ (ふたきれ), "two slices." Another noun sense is cutting sharpness.

- パンを一切れ食べました。 (パンをひときれたべました。) - I ate a slice of bread.
- この包丁は切れがいいです。 (このほうちょうはきれがいいです。) - This kitchen knife cuts well.

The second example evaluates the knife's cutting performance; it does not describe a good slice of knife.

## How the kanji combine

[切](../kanji/切.md) supplies the connection with cutting or separation. The complete noun is read きれ. Compare the related verb [切れる](切れる.md) (きれる), but do not assume the noun covers every use of that verb.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[布](布.md) (ぬの) means cloth. 切れ also has a cloth sense, for which the dictionary notes the spellings 布 and 裂. That meaning is separate from the performance meaning in 包丁の切れ.

## Distinctive meaning

Context distinguishes the thing cut off from the ability to cut. A number plus 切れ points toward pieces or slices; 切れがいい evaluates sharpness or effectiveness. English "cut" is therefore not a complete guide to its grammar: the noun does not simply replace a Japanese verb meaning "to cut."
