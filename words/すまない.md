# すまない - sorry; apologetic gratitude

## Meaning

すまない expresses being sorry or feeling that one has imposed on someone.
It can convey apologetic thanks as well as an apology, and can introduce
a request with an excuse-me function.

- 遅れてすまない。 (おくれてすまない。) - I am sorry I am late.
- 手伝ってもらって、すまないね。 (てつだってもらって、すまないね。) - Thanks for helping me; sorry to put you to the trouble.

The dictionary also records the related adjective meanings inexcusable and
remorseful. The second example acknowledges the other person's trouble;
it is not a claim that accepting any help is an offense.

## How the kanji combine

The expression is usually written in kana. The spelling 済まない connects
it with the negative of [済む](済む.md), be finished or settled, and
[済](../kanji/済.md). Learn the social expression as a whole rather than
translating every occurrence as "does not finish."

## Synonyms and antonyms

[すみません](すみません.md) is the familiar polite counterpart.
[ごめんなさい](ごめんなさい.md) directly apologizes, whereas
[ありがとう](ありがとう.md) directly thanks someone.
[恐縮](恐縮.md) also concerns feeling obliged or apologetic.
The functions overlap without making all these expressions interchangeable.

## Distinctive meaning

すまない is plain rather than universally polite business language.
Whether it sounds warm, blunt, or appropriate depends on the relationship
and situation. Use a suitable polite apology with strangers or in formal
settings rather than assuming that this form is always sufficiently respectful.
