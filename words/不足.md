# 不足 (ふそく) - shortage; insufficiency

## Meaning

不足 is a noun for not having enough of something. 不足する is intransitive: what is insufficient takes が. The continuing form 不足している describes a shortage.

- この地域では水が不足しています。 (このちいきではみずがふそくしています。) - Water is in short supply in this area.
- 最近、睡眠不足です。 (さいきん、すいみんぶそくです。) - Recently, I have not been getting enough sleep.

In compounds such as 睡眠不足, 不足 is read ぶそく. The standalone word remains ふそく. A phrase such as 人手不足 (ひとでぶそく) means a shortage of workers.

## How the kanji combine

[不](../kanji/不.md) contributes negation, and [足](../kanji/足.md) contributes sufficiency here, not the body part "foot." Together they express "not sufficient."

## Synonyms and antonyms

[足りる](足りる.md) (たりる) means "be enough"; 足りない closely matches the shortage meaning. [十分](十分.md) (じゅうぶん), "sufficient," provides the contrast. [無し](無し.md) (なし) means none or without, which is stronger than merely too little.

## Distinctive meaning

A shortage is relative to a need. 水が不足している does not say that absolutely no water remains. Specify the missing resource and, when relevant, the amount still needed. Also distinguish 不足する, "be insufficient," from deliberately reducing or wasting something, which requires a different verb.
