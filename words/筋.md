# 筋 (すじ) - sinew; line; thread of a story

## Meaning

筋 is a noun with several connected-looking but context-dependent meanings.
In the body, it can refer to a muscle, tendon, or sinew.
In food it can name a stringy fiber; on a surface it can mean a line or streak.
It also names the plot or thread of a story and the logic of an argument.

- 運動中に足の筋を痛めました。
  (うんどうちゅうにあしのすじをいためました。)
  - I injured a tendon or muscle in my leg while exercising.
- ガラスに白い筋が残っています。
  (ガラスにしろいすじがのこっています。) - White streaks remain on the glass.
- この映画の筋を簡単に説明してください。
  (このえいがのすじをかんたんにせつめいしてください。)
  - Please briefly explain the plot of this film.

セロリの筋を取る (セロリのすじをとる) means "remove the strings from celery."
筋が通る (すじがとおる) means that an argument or account is logical or coherent.

## How the kanji combine

筋 is one kanji representing the whole native word すじ.
Do not substitute the on reading キン used in 筋肉 (きんにく), "muscle."
The character alone does not fix a precise anatomical diagnosis.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[線](線.md) (せん) also means a line, but does not replace 筋 in every body or
story expression. 筋肉 names muscle tissue more specifically than the everyday body-part use of すじ.

## Distinctive meaning

The idea of a running line is a useful memory connection, not a rule for freely
inventing new meanings. A story's 筋 is its connected course, while an argument's
筋 is its coherence. Neither requires a literal string.
