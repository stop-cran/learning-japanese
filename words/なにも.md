# なにも (なにも) - not anything; nothing at all

## Meaning

なにも, also written 何も, expresses "not anything" or "nothing at all" **with a negative predicate**.
The negative ending is part of the construction: なにも食べない (なにもたべない), "not eat anything."
It is not a context-free negative pronoun that makes an affirmative verb negative.

- 朝から何も食べていません。 (あさからなにもたべていません。) - I haven't eaten anything since this morning.
- 箱の中には何もありません。 (はこのなかにはなにもありません。) - There is nothing in the box.

Both sentences visibly contain negation: いません or ありません.

## How the kanji combine

The assigned spelling is kana-only. In 何も, 何 represents なに, "what," and も participates in the negative-polarity expression.
Learn the complete pattern together with its predicate rather than translating the pieces independently.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[何か](何か.md) (なにか), "something," can occur with an affirmative predicate.
[全部](全部.md) (ぜんぶ), "all," is not a simple grammatical opposite of なにも.
The positive or negative predicate remains important in either expression.

## Distinctive meaning

A separate construction uses なにも with an unnecessary-action expression:
何も急ぐことはありません (なにもいそぐことはありません), "There is no particular need to hurry."
Here the point is "not especially/not to that extent," not a missing object meaning "nothing."
The dictionary also records other construction-specific uses; the two patterns taught here should not be generalized to every occurrence of も.
