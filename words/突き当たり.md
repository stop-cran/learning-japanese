# 突き当たり (つきあたり) - end of a street or hallway

## Meaning

突き当たり is the point at which a street or hallway ends in the direction
one is following. It is useful in directions: 突き当たりを右に曲がる
(つきあたりをみぎにまがる), "turn right at the end."

- 廊下の突き当たりに階段があります。
  (ろうかのつきあたりにかいだんがあります。) - There are stairs at the end of the hallway.
- この道の突き当たりを左に曲がってください。
  (このみちのつきあたりをひだりにまがってください。) - Please turn left at the end of this street.

In the first example, に marks the stairs' location.
In the turning instruction, を marks the point at which the turn is made.

## How the kanji combine

[突](../kanji/突.md) contributes thrusting or bumping into, and
[当](../kanji/当.md) contributes hitting or coming against something.
The noun is related to [突き当たる](突き当たる.md) (つきあたる).
The written native elements are つき + あたり, not トツ + トウ.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[端](端.md) (はし), [末](末.md) (すえ), and [先](先.md) (さき)
have broader end or tip uses.
[終点](終点.md) (しゅうてん) identifies an endpoint, especially a route's final stop.
[終わり](終わり.md) (おわり) and [最後](最後.md) (さいご) also cover endings,
but are not specifically directional street-end words.

## Distinctive meaning

Reaching a 突き当たり does not require a physical collision.
The way straight ahead ends, but another way may turn left or right there.
It therefore need not mean there is no possible onward route.
Nor is it a general noun for the end of a story or the conclusion of a meeting.
