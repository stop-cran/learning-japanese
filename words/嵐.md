# 嵐 (あらし) - storm; tempest

## Meaning

嵐 is a noun for a storm: violent, turbulent weather, especially involving strong wind.
It also has a figurative use for a storm of reactions or an uproar.

- 嵐が近づいています。 (あらしがちかづいています。) - A storm is approaching.
- その発言は批判の嵐を招いた。 (そのはつげんはひはんのあらしをまねいた。) - That remark brought a storm of criticism.

Use 嵐が来る (あらしがくる), "a storm comes," or 嵐の中 (あらしのなか), "in a storm."
In the second example, 批判の嵐 names a large, forceful reaction rather than weather.

## How the kanji combine

The single character 嵐 writes the native word あらし. There is no compound reading to assemble.
The visible [山](../kanji/山.md) and [風](../kanji/風.md) can help you recognize the character, but the word does not specifically mean
"a mountain storm." Treat that image as a memory aid, not a restriction on its meaning.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[台風](台風.md) (たいふう) names a typhoon, a particular weather system; 嵐 is not that specific.
[風](風.md) (かぜ) means wind and does not by itself say that conditions are stormy.
Calm weather is a contextual contrast, not a separate sense of 嵐.

## Distinctive meaning

The force and turbulence carry over into figurative expressions. A "storm of criticism" is not
merely one critical comment. Conversely, encountering 嵐 in a weather forecast gives no reason to
translate it figuratively: the surrounding nouns and situation select the sense.
