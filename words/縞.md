# 縞 (しま) - stripe; striped pattern

## Meaning

縞 is a stripe, streak, or striped pattern. It is a noun, also used within compounds and before another noun with の.

- このシャツには青い縞があります。
  (このシャツにはあおいしまがあります。) - This shirt has blue stripes.
- 縞模様の猫が窓のそばにいます。
  (しまもようのねこがまどのそばにいます。) - A striped cat is beside the window.

**縞模様** (しまもよう) means "striped pattern." 縦縞 (たてじま) is vertical striping and 横縞 (よこじま) horizontal striping. Notice the voiced じま in those complete compounds; the standalone noun is しま.

The dictionary also records a woven striped pattern made with two or more colors. Actual stripes need not be woven: the animal example concerns markings.

## How the kanji combine

縞 is one character with the native reading しま. There is no multi-character compound to split in the headword. Its on reading コウ is not an alternative to insert into the examples.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[模様](模様.md) (もよう) is a pattern more broadly; [柄](柄.md), read **がら** in the pattern sense, also overlaps. Stripes are one type of pattern, not every design.

[筋](筋.md) (すじ) and [線](線.md) (せん) include line, streak, or stripe uses. Context distinguishes a stripe in a design from a drawn line or the many nonvisual senses of those words.

[棒](棒.md) (ぼう) can also name a simple drawn line or dash, besides a physical rod. A three-dimensional stick is not itself the striped pattern.

## Distinctive meaning

[島](島.md) (しま), "island," is a separate same-reading word, not the ordinary modern spelling to choose for a striped shirt. JMdict notes former 島/嶋 spellings for the woven-pattern sense; that historical recognition does not turn the geography article into coverage of 縞.

English "bar" in the source entry means a stripe-like mark here, not a drinking establishment or necessarily a three-dimensional rod.
