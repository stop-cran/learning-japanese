# 退屈 (たいくつ) - boredom; tedious

## Meaning

退屈 describes boredom or something tedious. It is a noun and a な-adjective: 退屈な話 means "a boring story or talk." With する, it is an intransitive verb for feeling or becoming bored.

- 会議が長くて、退屈しました。 (かいぎがながくて、たいくつしました。) - The meeting was long, and I got bored.
- 待っている間、とても退屈でした。 (まっているあいだ、とてもたいくつでした。) - I was very bored while waiting.

The subject and context distinguish the person experiencing boredom from the activity judged tedious. 退屈している describes an ongoing bored state.

## How the kanji combine

[退](../kanji/退.md) contributes withdrawing or retreating; 屈 contributes bending or yielding. Their literal component glosses do not transparently define modern boredom. Learn the established compound as たいくつ rather than treating the characters as a historical explanation.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[つまらない](つまらない.md) can mean uninteresting or not worthwhile, extending beyond boredom. [暇](暇.md) (ひま) means free time or being unoccupied; someone with free time need not be bored. [面白い](面白い.md) (おもしろい), "interesting," contrasts with a tedious experience.

## Distinctive meaning

English "tired" can describe either physical fatigue or being tired of something. 退屈 concerns lack of interest, not simply needing sleep or rest. Calling another person's talk 退屈 can sound blunt because it evaluates the experience negatively.
