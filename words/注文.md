# 注文 (ちゅうもん) - order; request

## Meaning

注文 is an order for food or goods, and by extension a request, demand, or condition. It is a noun and a transitive する verb. The item ordered takes を; the shop or supplier can take に.

- コーヒーを二つ注文しました。 (コーヒーをふたつちゅうもんしました。) - I ordered two coffees.
- お客さんから細かい注文がありました。 (おきゃくさんからこまかいちゅうもんがありました。) - The customer had detailed requests.

The second sentence may concern specifications rather than a second purchase. 注文を受ける (ちゅうもんをうける) means "take/receive an order."

## How the kanji combine

[注](../kanji/注.md) contributes noting or specifying, and [文](../kanji/文.md) contributes wording or a written statement. Together they can help you remember a specified request. This component association does not mean that a modern 注文 must be written down.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[頼む](頼む.md) (たのむ) is broader "ask/request" and can also be used to order food. [命令](命令.md) (めいれい) means an authoritative command, not an order for a coffee. The English word "order" covers both, but Japanese distinguishes them.

## Distinctive meaning

ご注文はお決まりですか (ごちゅうもんはおきまりですか) means "Have you decided what to order?" It asks about a customer's choice. 注文が多い can describe someone making many demands, not necessarily buying many items; the surrounding conversation decides which meaning applies.
