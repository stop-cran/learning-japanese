# 或 (ある) - a certain; some

## Meaning

或, read ある, introduces a particular but unnamed person, thing, or occasion: "a certain..." or "some...." It is a pre-noun modifier. Put the noun directly after it, without な or の.

- ある日、昔の友達に会いました。 (あるひ、むかしのともだちにあいました。) - One day, I met an old friend.
- ある学生から質問がありました。 (あるがくせいからしつもんがありました。) - A certain student asked a question.

The speaker may know which student is meant while choosing not to identify them.

## How the kanji combine

This word is usually written in kana as ある. 或 and 或る are recognized kanji spellings of that same word. The single character represents the whole native expression; it is not a compound with separately meaningful parts.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[その](その.md) identifies something through the conversation or situation. ある introduces an unnamed instance instead. There is no useful direct antonym.

## Distinctive meaning

Distinguish this modifier from the verb [ある](ある.md), "exist." Compare ある本 (あるほん), "a certain book," with 本がある (ほんがある), "there is a book." The modifier does not conjugate: to say "a certain student was absent," change the predicate, not ある. Here "some" introduces an unspecified identity, not a measured quantity.
