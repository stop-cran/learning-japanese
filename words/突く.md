# 突く (つく) - poke; thrust; stab

## Meaning

突く means to poke, prod, thrust at, or stab something.
It is a transitive godan verb: the thing or place poked takes を.
It also has pushing, hitting, and striking uses; penetration is not required.

- 友達の肩を指で軽く突きました。
  (ともだちのかたをゆびでかるくつきました。) - I gave my friend's shoulder a light poke with my finger.
- 杖を突いて歩いています。
  (つえをついてあるいています。) - I walk using a cane.

The cane example is an additional use for supporting oneself.
Forms include 突きます, 突いた, 突いて, and 突かない.

## How the kanji combine

[突](../kanji/突.md) supplies つ and the thrusting association;
く is okurigana. The native verb is not read とつく.
Learn the full word rather than treating the kanji's "sudden" association
as part of every translation.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[刺す](刺す.md) (さす) includes piercing or stabbing.
[押す](押す.md) (おす) broadly means push;
[叩く](叩く.md) (たたく) and [打つ](打つ.md) (うつ) include hitting.
[当たる](当たる.md) (あたる) also has hit or make-contact meanings.
These overlaps do not make every push or blow the same kind of movement.

## Distinctive meaning

The spelling is different from [点く](点く.md), "catch fire; come on,"
[付く](付く.md), "be attached," and [着く](着く.md), "arrive,"
even though all have the reading つく.
The related verb つつく can also be written 突く.
It typically describes light, repeated poking or nudging, and also pecking.
Kana or an explicit reading helps distinguish these two verbs sharing a spelling.
Their uses overlap: this does not make つく a forceful-only verb.
There is no useful single antonym covering poking, thrusting, and cane use.
