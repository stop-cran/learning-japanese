# 気の毒 (きのどく) - unfortunate; deserving sympathy

## Meaning

気の毒 describes someone or a situation that evokes sympathy: "unfortunate," "poor," or "a pity." It functions as a noun and a な-adjective. Use 気の毒な人 for "an unfortunate person" and 気の毒に思う for feeling sorry for someone.

- 大切な写真をなくしたそうです。お気の毒に。 (たいせつなしゃしんをなくしたそうです。おきのどくに。) - I hear you lost an important photograph. I'm sorry to hear that.
- 一人で全部片づけたなんて、気の毒です。 (ひとりでぜんぶかたづけたなんて、きのどくです。) - It's a shame they had to tidy everything up alone.

お気の毒に is a sympathetic response, not a claim that the speaker caused the misfortune.

## How the kanji combine

[気](../kanji/気.md) concerns feeling or the mind; 毒 means poison or harm. の connects the two nouns in this fixed expression. Both kanji use on readings, but the whole phrase has an established sympathetic meaning, not a literal medical one.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[かわいそう](かわいそう.md) also expresses pity. [残念](残念.md) (ざんねん) emphasizes regret or disappointment, which need not be directed at another person's suffering. No single opposite fits every sympathetic use.

## Distinctive meaning

Expressing pity can be unwelcome when it sounds as though one is judging someone from above. Consider the relationship and seriousness of the event. 気の毒 describes the sympathetic assessment; it does not tell the listener exactly what practical help will follow.
