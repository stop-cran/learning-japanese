# 現に (げんに) - actually; as a matter of fact

## Meaning

現に is an adverb emphasizing that something is an actual fact, rather
than just a theory, supposition, or imagined possibility.
It can introduce concrete evidence for what the speaker is saying.

- この方法は役に立ちます。現に、私も使っています。 (このほうほうはやくにたちます。げんに、わたしもつかっています。) - This method is useful. In fact, I use it myself.
- 彼が来たことは知っています。現に、この目で見ました。 (かれがきたことはしっています。げんに、このめでみました。) - I know he came. I actually saw him with my own eyes.

The factual emphasis is the point; the events described need not all
be happening at the moment of speaking.

## How the kanji combine

[現](../kanji/現.md) contributes actual or existing.
The on reading ゲン is followed by に to form this adverb.
It is not a compound in which に has its own kanji.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[実際](実際.md) (じっさい) overlaps in "actually; in reality."
[実は](実は.md) (じつは) can introduce a disclosure or correction.
[実に](実に.md) (じつに) often intensifies an evaluation, "truly; indeed."
These adverbs overlap in English without being interchangeable in every sentence.

## Distinctive meaning

Do not use 現に for every English "really." In a sentence meaning
"really delicious," the speaker may simply be intensifying praise;
現に instead directs attention to actuality or evidence.
The English gloss "presently" would also miss uses about a past fact.
