# 原 (はら) - open field; plain

## Meaning

原 read はら names an open field, plain, or stretch of open country. The landscape determines whether English "field," "moor," or another term is suitable. It does not specifically mean a cultivated plot.

- 広い原に花が咲いていました。 (ひろいはらにはながさいていました。) - Flowers were blooming across the open field.
- 原を歩いて、小さな川に着きました。 (はらをあるいて、ちいさなかわにつきました。) - We walked across the plain and reached a small stream.

The first example uses に for the location of the flowers. In the second, を marks the area traversed with 歩く, not something changed by the walking.

## How the kanji combine

[原](../kanji/原.md) is the entire written word and has the native reading はら here. The same character has other readings in compounds; its presence does not make every such compound a synonym for an open field.

## Synonyms and antonyms

野原 (のはら) also means an open field. 平野 (へいや) names a geographical plain. [畑](畑.md) (はたけ) is a cultivated field, especially one used for crops rather than flooded rice cultivation.

## Distinctive meaning

This landscape noun is not the English "field" of study or occupation. It also does not specify one climate or ecosystem: the dictionary's varied English glosses are alternatives selected by context, not a statement that plains, tundra, and moors are identical environments.
