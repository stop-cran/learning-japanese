# 先祖 (せんぞ) - ancestors; forebears

## Meaning

先祖 means ancestors or forebears: people in earlier generations of a family line.
It can also identify the founder of that lineage.
Use Nの先祖 to specify whose ancestors are meant.

- 私の先祖はこの村に住んでいました。 (わたしのせんぞはこのむらにすんでいました。) - My ancestors lived in this village.
- お盆に先祖の墓を訪ねました。 (おぼんにせんぞのはかをたずねました。) - I visited my ancestors' grave during Obon.

Japanese does not require a plural ending here.
Context determines whether English uses one ancestor or several.

## How the kanji combine

[先](../kanji/先.md) contributes preceding generations, and
[祖](../kanji/祖.md) contributes ancestor or founder.
Their related meanings reinforce a link to the earlier family line.
The complete reading is せんぞ: 祖's ソ appears as ぞ here.
Do not substitute the standalone reading さき for 先.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[祖先](祖先.md) (そせん) also means ancestors.
[子孫](子孫.md) (しそん), descendants, points in the other generational direction.
[祖父](祖父.md) (そふ) and [祖母](祖母.md) (そぼ) identify specific grandparent relationships,
rather than the broader ancestry of 先祖.

## Distinctive meaning

先祖 does not identify exactly how many generations back a person lived.
Nor does it mean merely an older colleague:
[先輩](先輩.md) (せんぱい) describes seniority in a shared setting.
The family-line relationship, not age alone, matters in the examples here.
