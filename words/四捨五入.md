# 四捨五入 (ししゃごにゅう) - rounding numbers

## Meaning

四捨五入 is rounding a number to a chosen place. For a nonnegative decimal,
look at the first digit after the last place to keep: 0-4 is discarded,
while 5-9 makes the retained part increase by one unit of that place.

- 小数第一位を四捨五入すると、12.4は12、12.5は13になります。 (しょうすうだいいちいをししゃごにゅうすると、12.4は12、12.5は13になります。) - Rounding at the first decimal place gives 12 from 12.4 and 13 from 12.5.
- 答えを四捨五入して整数にしてください。 (こたえをししゃごにゅうしてせいすうにしてください。) - Round the answer to a whole number.

小数第一位 is the first digit after the decimal point; rounding there
leaves an integer. Specify the place rather than assuming the word always
means rounding to an integer.

## How the kanji combine

[四](../kanji/四.md), four, and [捨](../kanji/捨.md), discard, pair with
[五](../kanji/五.md), five, and [入](../kanji/入.md), put in.
The established expression recalls the 4/5 dividing point.
The on readings are シ + シャ + ゴ + ニュウ.

## Synonyms and antonyms

切り捨て (きりすて), rounding down or discarding the following part,
and 切り上げ (きりあげ), rounding up, are useful comparisons.
四捨五入 is not an instruction to always round upward.

## Distinctive meaning

JMdict specifies the tie rule as **half away from zero**: rounding to
integers gives -2 from -1.5, not -1. Other rounding conventions exist.
The raw community gloss "rounding up" should not replace the 0-4/5-9 rule.

The dictionary also records a figurative use for bringing different views
into agreement. That is an additional sense, not a different arithmetic rule.
