# 障子 (しょうじ) - shoji; paper sliding door

## Meaning

障子 is a shoji: a traditional sliding fitting with a lattice frame covered in translucent paper. It can divide spaces or cover an opening while admitting light.

- 障子を開けると、庭が見えます。 (しょうじをあけると、にわがみえます。) - When you open the shoji, you can see the garden.
- 障子の紙を張り替えました。 (しょうじのかみをはりかえました。) - I replaced the paper on the shoji.

開ける (あける) and 閉める (しめる) describe opening and closing it. The second example concerns replacing the covering, not necessarily the entire frame.

## How the kanji combine

障 has a barrier or partition association. [子](../kanji/子.md) is part of the established object name here; it does not make the compound mean "a barrier's child." Read しょうじ as a whole, keeping the long しょう.

## Synonyms and antonyms

A [ドア](ドア.md) is a door more generally. 襖 (ふすま) is another traditional sliding partition, typically with opaque covered panels; it is not simply another name for the translucent-paper fitting taught here.

## Distinctive meaning

The English gloss "paper door" should not suggest a loose sheet of paper with no supporting frame. This card teaches the modern household fitting. Broader historical partition uses recorded in Japanese dictionaries are not the definition to apply to every modern 障子.
