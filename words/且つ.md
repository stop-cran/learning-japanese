# 且つ (かつ) — and; moreover

## Meaning

かつ adds another fact or joins descriptions that both apply: **and; moreover; as well as**.
It is usually written in kana. **且つ** and **且** are dictionary spellings of the same word.

Two short dictionary examples show useful frames:

- 講演はおもしろく、かつ有意義だった。 (こうえんはおもしろく、かつゆういぎだった。) — The lecture was interesting and also meaningful. (Digital Daijisen)
- 必要かつ十分な条件 (ひつようかつじゅうぶんなじょうけん) — a necessary and sufficient condition (Kanjipedia)

In the first example, the i-adjective おもしろい takes its connecting **おもしろく** form before かつ.
In the second, 必要（ひつよう, necessary） and 十分（じゅうぶん, sufficient） are na-adjective descriptions:
**必要かつ十分な + noun**, with な after the final description, not after 必要.
These are two attested frames, not a complete attachment rule for every predicate.

## How the kanji combine

[且](../kanji/且.md) writes the additive word かつ; **つ** is its following kana (okurigana).
There is no two-kanji compound to divide. The dictionary's usual-kana label concerns spelling, not a rare pronunciation.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[それに](それに.md), [そのうえ](そのうえ.md), [しかも](しかも.md) and [更に](更に.md) (さらに)
also have additive uses. Shared English glosses do not make all their constructions interchangeable.
[また](また.md) can add a point but also means "again"; the かつ examples above do not express repetition.
There is no single lexical antonym of this connector.

## Distinctive meaning

Focus on **both points applying**, not automatically "first this, then that."
The condition example joins two requirements; it does not describe events happening simultaneously.
Conversely, かつ can also present parallel actions, so its use is not limited to adjectives.

Do not insert かつ wherever English uses "and": the examples here connect descriptions, not a simple list of two objects.
The kanji spelling is optional; learning 且つ does not require replacing ordinary かつ in your writing.
