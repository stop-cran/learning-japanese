# えっと (えっと) - um; let me see

## Meaning

えっと fills a thinking pause: "um," "let me see," or hesitant "well."
Speakers use it while recalling information, choosing words or working
out an answer. It is an interjection, not a verb to conjugate.

- えっと、会議は何時からでしたか。 (えっと、かいぎはなんじからでしたか。) - Um, what time was the meeting starting?
- えっと、答えは三つあります。 (えっと、こたえはみっつあります。) - Let me see, there are three answers.

You may also see えと, えーと or えーっと. The spelling ええと
likewise represents the thinking-pause expression; JMdict records it as
a search-only variant. This article uses えっと as its learning form.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[あの](あの.md) can fill a hesitant pause or tentatively get attention.
[さあ](さあ.md) and [まあ](まあ.md) also overlap with some English
"well" uses, but they have additional effects such as encouragement or
qualification. They are not interchangeable signals in every conversation.

## Distinctive meaning

The English "let me see" here does not literally request permission to
look at an object. It signals thinking time. Likewise, do not confuse
this filler with an affirmative [ええ](ええ.md), "yes"; that entry also
records a drawn-out えー hesitation use, so the expressions can overlap
without making every ええ a filler.
