# 歩く (あるく) — to walk

## Meaning

歩く means to walk: it describes moving on foot rather than running or riding.

- 駅まで歩きます。 (えきまで あるきます。) — I will walk to the station.
- 毎朝、この道を歩きます。 (まいあさ、この みちを あるきます。) — I walk along this road every morning.

It is an intransitive godan verb: 歩きます (あるきます), 歩いて (あるいて), 歩いた (あるいた).
In 道を歩く, を marks the route traversed, not an object that receives an action.

## How the kanji combine

This is a native word, not a kanji compound. [歩](../kanji/歩.md) supplies ある, and the okurigana く completes the dictionary form.
The kana ending changes when the verb conjugates; the kanji remains 歩.

## Synonyms and antonyms

- 散歩 (さんぽ), "a stroll," focuses on going for a walk; 歩く itself does not imply a recreational outing.
- [走る](走る.md) (はしる), "to run," contrasts the manner of movement. It is not a strict antonym: both involve moving on foot.
- There is no single direct antonym covering every use of 歩く.

## Distinctive meaning

歩く specifies the way someone travels. [行く](行く.md) (いく) means "go" without telling you whether the person walks, drives, or takes a train.
Use 歩く for walking to a destination as well as walking along a route; it does not mean only "take a stroll."
