# 足 (あし) — foot, leg

## Meaning

足 is the foot and the leg: 足が痛い (my foot hurts), 足が速い あしがはやい (be fast on one's feet), 足を組む (cross one's legs), 足音 あしおと (footsteps).
It is also the means of travel in 足がない (have no transport), and a table's leg is 足 as well.

## How the kanji combine

A single kanji, [足](../kanji/足.md), with the kun reading あし. The on reading ソク appears in 満足 まんぞく, 遠足 えんそく and the shoe counter 一足 いっそく.
In 足りる (たりる, be enough) and 足す (たす, add), 足 contributes た; the following りる or す is okurigana.

## Synonyms and antonyms

- Close: 脚 あし (leg), 足首 あしくび (ankle).
- Related body part: [手](手.md) て (hand), not an antonym.

## Distinctive meaning

足 covers both "foot" and "leg" in everyday speech. 脚 (あし) is also used for a person's legs and the legs of tables or chairs. 脚が長い (あしがながい) means "have long legs"; it does not by itself mean slim. The reading きゃく occurs in counters such as 椅子二脚 (いすにきゃく), "two chairs."
