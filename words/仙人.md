# 仙人 (せんにん) — mountain hermit; Taoist immortal

## Meaning

仙人 is a person who has left ordinary society to live in the mountains, who never grows old or dies, and who is said to have magic powers such as flying. In Taoism, this is an ideal, godlike being. 仙人 can also mean a person who wants nothing for themselves and knows little about worldly affairs.

- 昔話に、山の仙人が出てきます。
  (むかしばなしに、やまのせんにんがでてきます。)
  "A mountain hermit appears in the old tale."
- 祖父は、山の中で仙人のように暮らしています。
  (そふは、やまのなかでせんにんのようにくらしています。)
  "My grandfather lives in the mountains like a hermit."

These are original usage examples, not quotations from a dictionary or other source.

## How the kanji combine

[仙](../kanji/仙.md) (セン) contributes a hermit or immortal who trained in the mountains; [人](../kanji/人.md) (ニン) contributes person. This is a modifier + head relation: a person who is a mountain immortal. It explains the compound's meaning, not the historical origin of either character.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[神](神.md) (かみ), "god; deity," and [神様](神様.md) (かみさま), "god; revered master," name gods; a 仙人 is a person who has gained special powers. [僧侶](僧侶.md) (そうりょ), "Buddhist priest; monk," and [坊さん](坊さん.md) (ぼうさん), "Buddhist priest; monk," are Buddhist monks, not 仙人.

There is no single fixed antonym.

## Distinctive meaning

The second sense, "person not bound by earthly desires," describes a real person who wants little and does not know much about worldly matters; it does not mean that person has magic powers. The character 仙 alone, read せん, can also mean "hermit; wizard."
