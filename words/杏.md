# 杏 (あんず) - apricot tree; apricot

## Meaning

杏 names the apricot tree and its fruit, *Prunus armeniaca*. The surrounding expression tells you whether a tree or fruit is intended.

- 杏の花が咲きました。 (あんずのはながさきました。) - The apricot blossoms have opened.
- 杏でジャムを作りました。 (あんずでジャムをつくりました。) - I made jam from apricots.

杏の木 (あんずのき) specifies the tree, and 杏の実 (あんずのみ) specifies the fruit. In the second example, で marks the ingredient.

## How the kanji combine

The single character [杏](../kanji/杏.md) writes the whole noun あんず. JMdict marks the word as usually written in kana; あんず and アンズ are alternatives to the kanji spelling.

杏子 is another dictionary spelling of あんず. Learn that whole spelling and reading together rather than assigning ず as a freely reusable reading of [子](../kanji/子.md). This page teaches the plant noun, not personal names with the same spelling.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[梅](梅.md) (うめ), ume or Japanese apricot, names *Prunus mume*, a different plant. The English word "apricot" in both labels does not make the Japanese nouns interchangeable.

[李](李.md) (すもも), Japanese plum, and [桃](桃.md) (もも), peach, are other related fruit trees, not synonyms. [果物](果物.md) (くだもの), fruit as food, and [木](木.md) (き), tree, are broader categories. There is no direct antonym.

## Distinctive meaning

杏仁 (きょうにん／あんにん) names an apricot kernel, not the flesh eaten as fruit. 杏仁豆腐 (あんにんどうふ／きょうにんどうふ) is a prepared dessert often translated "almond jelly"; it is not a name for apricot jam or a bowl of ordinary tofu. These compounds do not change the reading of the standalone fruit noun to きょう or あん.

Do not treat a dictionary kernel gloss as permission to eat raw kernels. This is a vocabulary explanation, not preparation or medicinal-use guidance.
