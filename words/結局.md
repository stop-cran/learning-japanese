# 結局 (けっきょく) - in the end; ultimately

## Meaning

結局 is an adverb that introduces the eventual outcome or bottom-line conclusion.
It often follows a period of considering possibilities, trying something, or expecting a
different result. It can be used with either an affirmative or a negative predicate.

- いろいろな店を見ましたが、結局何も買いませんでした。
  (いろいろなみせをみましたが、けっきょくなにもかいませんでした。)
  - I looked around various shops, but in the end I bought nothing.
- 電車が止まっていたので、結局歩いて帰りました。
  (でんしゃがとまっていたので、けっきょくあるいてかえりました。)
  - The trains were not running, so I ended up walking home.

Neither sentence requires that the result be surprising to everyone.

## How the kanji combine

結 contributes tying up or concluding, and [局](../kanji/局.md) can concern an affair
or situation. The compound presents how matters finally settle.
The combined on reading is けっきょく, with a small っ.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[結果](結果.md) (けっか) is a noun for a result.
[最後](最後.md) (さいご) names the end or last part; it need not sum up a course of events.
[やはり](やはり.md) can confirm an expectation, while 結局 emphasizes the eventual outcome.

## Distinctive meaning

English "after all" sometimes introduces a supporting reason: "After all, he is a child."
That use does not automatically translate as 結局. Use 結局 when drawing the conclusion
or describing what ultimately happened, as in the shopping example.
