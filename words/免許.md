# 免許 (めんきょ) - license; official permission

## Meaning

免許 is a license or official permission to do something. In a driving context, it can stand for a driver's license. Useful patterns include 免許を取る (めんきょをとる), "get a license," and 免許を持っている, "have a license."

- 去年、運転免許を取りました。 (きょねん、うんてんめんきょをとりました。) - I got my driver's license last year.
- 免許を見せてください。 (めんきょをみせてください。) - Please show me your license.

The second sentence needs context to establish which license is meant. 免許証 (めんきょしょう) explicitly names the document or card.

## How the kanji combine

免 contributes release or exemption, and [許](../kanji/許.md) contributes permitting. Together they concern granting authorization. This is a guide to the characters' meanings, not a claim about a particular modern licensing law. The readings are メン + キョ.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[許可](許可.md) (きょか) is permission or authorization more broadly. 資格 (しかく) is a qualification or eligibility; not every qualification is a license. 無免許 (むめんきょ), "unlicensed," contrasts with having the required license.

## Distinctive meaning

Taking lessons and receiving a license are different events. Also, English "certificate" can mean many documents that are not 免許. Use the Japanese word for the actual authorization involved, rather than applying it to every piece of paper showing completion or attendance.
