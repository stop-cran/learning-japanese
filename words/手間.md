# 手間 (てま) - time and effort

## Meaning

手間 is the time, labor, or effort involved in doing something.
Learn 手間がかかる (てまがかかる), "take effort/time," and 手間をかける (てまをかける),
"put effort into." The first describes a requirement; the second describes someone's investment of effort.

- この料理は手間がかかります。 (このりょうりはてまがかかります。) - This dish takes a lot of work.
- 手間をかけて、丁寧に作りました。 (てまをかけて、ていねいにつくりました。) - I put time and care into making it.

手間を省く (てまをはぶく) means "save effort" or "eliminate steps."
余計な手間 (よけいなてま) is unnecessary trouble or extra work.

## How the kanji combine

[手](../kanji/手.md) contributes the hand and, by extension here, work.
[間](../kanji/間.md) contributes an interval or time.
Together the native readings て + ま provide a useful image of the labor and time a task demands.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[時間](時間.md) (じかん) is time or duration in general.
手間 focuses on the work involved, so waiting an hour is not automatically the same as spending an hour's labor.
[苦労](苦労.md) (くろう) emphasizes hardship or difficulty.

## Distinctive meaning

手間 need not be wasted effort. It can be valued care, as in the second example, or avoidable inconvenience.
The surrounding phrase determines the evaluation.
手間がかからない means that something requires little effort, not necessarily that it happens in zero seconds.
