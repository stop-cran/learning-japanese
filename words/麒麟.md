# 麒麟 (きりん) — giraffe; qilin

## Meaning

麒麟 has two useful animal meanings:

- **Giraffe**, the living long-necked animal. This sense is usually written
  in kana, especially キリン.
- **Qilin**, an imaginary animal in Chinese tradition, associated with
  auspicious appearances. It is not a living giraffe or simply the Western
  unicorn under another name.

Compare a real-animal sighting with a representation of the mythical creature:

- 動物園でキリンを見ました。 (どうぶつえんでキリンをみました。) — I saw a giraffe at the zoo.
- この絵には麒麟が描かれています。 (このえにはきりんがえがかれています。) — A qilin is depicted in this picture.

The second translation assumes an artwork showing the mythical creature;
the kanji spelling alone does not force that interpretation. Both meanings
are pronounced きりん.

## How the kanji combine

麒 supplies **キ** and [麟](../kanji/麟.md) supplies **リン**.
Both characters are associated with the mythical animal. One traditional
account calls the male 麒 and the female 麟; the paired compound names the
creature, not necessarily two animals. This account does not make every
giraffe written 麒麟 female, or every occurrence a male-and-female pair.
Learn the whole word rather than adding two independent English glosses.

## Synonyms and antonyms

- [動物](動物.md) (どうぶつ), "animal," and [生き物](生き物.md)
  (いきもの), "living thing; creature," are broader category words for
  a living giraffe, not its specific name.
- [鹿](鹿.md) (しか), "deer," names a different animal. Sharing the 鹿
  component does not make 麒麟 an ordinary deer noun.
- [鳳凰](鳳凰.md) (ほうおう), the fenghuang or Chinese phoenix, is a
  different mythical creature, not a synonym for qilin.

There is no useful general antonym.

## Distinctive meaning

The giraffe's usual-kana notation concerns **spelling**, not a rare
pronunciation. The mythical sense is not given that same notation in the
frozen JMdict entry. 麒麟 is retained here as a kanji-recognition spelling.

Digital Daijisen also records **an exceptionally talented person** as a
figurative sense of 麒麟. Recognize it in context rather than translating
every occurrence as an animal. The related 麒麟児 (きりんじ), "child
prodigy," specifically concerns a promising young person; that does not
restrict every gifted-person use of bare 麒麟 to a child.
[才能](才能.md) (さいのう), "talent," names an ability, not that person.

JMdict additionally records the rare spelling 騏驎 (きりん). Its archaic
extraordinary-horse sense is restricted to that spelling in the frozen entry;
do not transfer it to every 麒麟 or キリン.
