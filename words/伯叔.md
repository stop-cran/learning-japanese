# 伯叔 (はくしゅく) — brothers; one's father's brothers

## Meaning

Digital Daijisen gives 伯叔 two senses: (1) older and younger brothers; (2) the older and younger brothers of one's father or mother, that is, 伯父 and 叔父, one's uncles. JMdict glosses it as “brothers; one's father's brothers.” Both examples below use sense 2.

- 祖父の葬儀には、伯叔がそろって集まりました。 (そふのそうぎには、はくしゅくがそろってあつまりました。) — All my uncles gathered for my grandfather's funeral.
- 私の伯叔は皆、父と同じ町で育ちました。 (わたしのはくしゅくはみな、ちちとおなじまちでそだちました。) — My uncles all grew up in the same town as my father.

These are original usage examples, not dictionary quotations.

## How the kanji combine

[伯](../kanji/伯.md) stands for the older and [叔](../kanji/叔.md) for the younger. In Digital Daijisen's first sense, 兄と弟, they are an older and a younger brother; in the second, a parent's older and younger brothers, 伯父 and 叔父. JMdict notes that 伯父 or 叔父 can mean an uncle older or younger than one's parent (entry 1607070).

## Synonyms and antonyms

[兄弟](兄弟.md) (きょうだい) means siblings or brothers. [おじさん](おじさん.md) is an uncle, and [親戚](親戚.md) (しんせき) covers relatives in general. 叔父 and 伯父, both read おじ, mean “uncle” (JMdict entry 1607070).

## Distinctive meaning

伯叔 groups older and younger brothers together; Digital Daijisen's second sense names them as 伯父 and 叔父, a parent's older and younger brothers. Neither sense covers aunts (伯母 and 叔母).
