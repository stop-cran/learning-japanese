# いらいら (いらいら) - irritation; impatience

## Meaning

いらいら expresses irritation, fretfulness, or losing patience. The intransitive construction
いらいらする means "get irritated"; いらいらしている describes being in that state.
A cause can be marked with に or explained in a preceding clause.

- バスが来なくて、いらいらしています。 (バスがこなくて、いらいらしています。) - The bus isn't coming, and I'm getting impatient.
- 彼の話し方にいらいらした。 (かれのはなしかたにいらいらした。) - His way of speaking irritated me.

As an adverb, it also appears with と: いらいらと待つ (いらいらとまつ), "wait impatiently."

## How the kanji combine

This is a native mimetic expression, normally written in kana, including イライラ.
Its repeated shape represents a feeling rather than a literal sound that must be audible.
The dictionary also records 苛々, but a kanji decomposition is unnecessary for the kana form.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[緊張](緊張.md) (きんちょう) is tension or nervousness, such as before a speech; annoyance is not required.
[怒る](怒る.md) (おこる) means becoming angry.
落ち着く (おちつく) describes calming down, while 落ち着いている (おちついている) describes a calm state.

## Distinctive meaning

English "nervous" is too broad as a stand-alone gloss. いらいら focuses on being on edge or bothered,
not simply anticipating an important event.
The person feeling irritated is the subject; the second example does not say that the speaker deliberately irritated someone else.
