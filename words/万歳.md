# 万歳 (ばんざい) - banzai; hurrah

## Meaning

万歳 is the cheer "Banzai!" or "Hurrah!" It can also name the
celebratory action, often raising both arms. 万歳する means to
give that cheer or make that gesture.

- 合格の知らせを聞いて、みんなで万歳をしました。 (ごうかくのしらせをきいて、みんなでばんざいをしました。) - On hearing the news of the exam success, we all cheered "Banzai!"
- 「万歳！」と両手を上げました。 (「ばんざい！」とりょうてをあげました。) - I raised both hands and shouted "Hurrah!"

The action is celebratory in these examples, not a statement that someone
literally lives for thousands of years.

## How the kanji combine

[万](../kanji/万.md) contributes ten thousand, and
[歳](../kanji/歳.md) contributes years or age. The compound also
records the wish for long life or prosperity.
Learn the established reading ばんざい, including the voiced ざい.
This whole-word pronunciation does not add an independent ザイ reading
to the 歳 card's sourced metadata.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[おめでとう](おめでとう.md) says "Congratulations!"
[祝う](祝う.md) (いわう) means celebrate or congratulate more broadly.
[乾杯](乾杯.md) (かんぱい) is a toast; not every English "cheers"
calls for the same Japanese expression.

## Distinctive meaning

Other recorded meanings include something worth celebrating and, in a
very different setting, giving up or throwing up one's hands.
That last use overlaps [諦める](諦める.md) (あきらめる), not
celebrating a defeat automatically. Context decides whether raised hands
signal joy or surrender.

The separate performance word まんざい is not an alternative reading
to substitute in these ばんざい examples.
