# あらゆる (あらゆる) - every; all kinds of

## Meaning

あらゆる means "every" or "all kinds of," considering the full range of things in a category. It is a pre-noun modifier: put it directly before a noun, without な or の.

- あらゆる方法を試しました。 (あらゆるほうほうをためしました。) - I tried every possible method.
- この店では、あらゆる種類の本を扱っています。 (このみせでは、あらゆるしゅるいのほんをあつかっています。) - This shop carries books of every kind.

In the second example, あらゆる modifies 種類, "kind"; 種類の then connects to 本. The の belongs to that noun phrase, not directly to あらゆる.

## How the kanji combine

The normal teaching spelling is hiragana. Dictionaries also record spellings such as 凡ゆる and 有らゆる, but these are rare written forms, not components learners need to assemble. Treat あらゆる as one fixed modifier rather than conjugating its final る.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[全て](全て.md) (すべて) can form 全ての方法, "all methods." [全部](全部.md) (ぜんぶ) can stand as a noun or work adverbially. あらゆる needs a following noun. 一部の (いちぶの), "some of," contrasts with its comprehensive scope.

## Distinctive meaning

The phrase often emphasizes breadth: every possibility, direction, or kind. That can be a rhetorical claim rather than proof of literal exhaustive coverage. For "I ate everything," use a construction such as 全部食べた, not あらゆる食べた.
