# 回る (まわる) — turn; revolve

## Meaning

回る describes something turning, rotating, or revolving.
In this use it is intransitive: the turning thing is the subject, rather than an object someone turns.

- こまが回っています。 (こまがまわっています。) — The spinning top is turning.
- 車のタイヤがゆっくり回っています。 (くるまのタイヤがゆっくりまわっています。) — The car's tires are turning slowly.

The polite form is 回ります (まわります), and the て-form is 回って (まわって).
Despite ending in る, this verb does not conjugate as まわます.

回る also means going around something or making a round of several places.
In these uses, を can mark the route or area visited, rather than an object being rotated.

- 地球は太陽の周りを回っています。 (ちきゅうはたいようのまわりをまわっています。) — The Earth goes around the sun.
- 午後、町の店を回りました。 (ごご、まちのみせをまわりました。) — In the afternoon, I went around the shops in town.

## How the kanji combine

[回](../kanji/回.md) expresses turning or going around, and る is the changing verb ending.
This is a native Japanese word with the reading まわる.
The on reading カイ appears in 回転 (かいてん), "rotation," instead.

## Synonyms and antonyms

回す (まわす) is the matching transitive verb: someone turns something.
手でこまを回しました。 (てでこまをまわしました。) — I spun the top by hand.
Compare こまが回る with こまを回す: が introduces what turns, while を marks what someone spins.
[止まる](止まる.md) (とまる), "stop," contrasts with continuing to turn in this physical situation.

## Distinctive meaning

English can use "turn" for both examples, but Japanese chooses 回る or 回す according to the relationship.
The intransitive verb does not mean that no outside force exists: a motor or a hand may cause the motion.
It simply presents the turning thing as the subject.
