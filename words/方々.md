# 方々 (かたがた; ほうぼう) - people; here and there

## Meaning

Two distinct words share this spelling. **かたがた** is a respectful noun for people; it does not mean "everywhere." **ほうぼう** is a noun or adverb meaning "here and there; all over," not "persons."

- 参加してくださった方々に感謝します。 (さんかしてくださったかたがたにかんしゃします。) - I thank the people who took part.
- 鍵を方々探しました。 (かぎをほうぼうさがしました。) - I searched all over for the key.

For the people reading, a preceding description can identify the group: 近所の方々 (きんじょのかたがた), "people in the neighborhood." For places, 方々から (ほうぼうから) means "from various places."

## How the kanji combine

[方](../kanji/方.md) can refer to a person or a direction. 々 repeats the preceding kanji. The native reading かた becomes かたがた; the separate on-reading word is ほうぼう. The article's primary reading and type describe the people word.

## Synonyms and antonyms

人たち (ひとたち), "people," lacks the same respectful wording as かたがた. [あちこち](あちこち.md) overlaps with ほうぼう for scattered places. These comparisons cannot be exchanged between the two readings.

## Distinctive meaning

Context determines the reading, not merely the repeated character. Treat the two meanings as separate lexical identities. The people entry also records an archaic pronoun use; it is not needed for the modern noun examples here. The source list mixed both English glosses, so neither reading should inherit all of them.
