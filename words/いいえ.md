# いいえ — no

## Meaning

いいえ is a negative response: "no." It is an interjection, so it does not conjugate or take a following です by itself.

- 学生ですか。いいえ、会社員です。 (がくせいですか。いいえ、かいしゃいんです。) — Are you a student? No, I'm an office worker.

If someone thinks you are not going, いいえ can correct that assumption:

- 行かないんですか。— いいえ、行きます。 (いかないんですか。— いいえ、いきます。) — Aren't you going? — Actually, I am going.

Here いいえ rejects "you are not going." The full reply makes the intended meaning clear. English answers to negative questions depend on context and emphasis, so translate the whole exchange rather than mechanically replacing いいえ with "no" or "yes."

## How the kanji combine

This word is written in hiragana. There is no kanji compound to break down. いえ is also a recognized shorter response.

## Synonyms and antonyms

はい is the corresponding affirmative response. ううん is a casual "no," appropriate among close acquaintances rather than in every polite conversation.

## Distinctive meaning

いいえ can also modestly dismiss thanks or praise, with the force of "not at all." A bare, emphatic "no" is not always the gentlest refusal; context and the following explanation matter.
