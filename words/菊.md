# 菊 (きく) - chrysanthemum

## Meaning

菊 names a **chrysanthemum**, either the plant or its flowers in context. Many varieties are grown ornamentally, and they are often associated with autumn. That association does not mean every variety flowers only in autumn.

- 庭に菊が咲いています。 (にわにきくがさいています。) - Chrysanthemums are blooming in the garden.
- 白い菊を飾りました。 (しろいきくをかざりました。) - I displayed white chrysanthemums.

Some chrysanthemums are cultivated for food, but the plant name is not a guarantee that any ornamental flower is suitable to eat.

## How the kanji combine

[菊](../kanji/菊.md) alone writes the plant noun きく. There is no compound to divide. Dictionaries classify キク as the character's on-reading; a one-character noun need not have a native kun-reading.

The kana spelling キク also occurs in botanical writing. It represents the plant name, not a different species or a second reading. The frozen JMdict record's katakana alternative is marked kana-only; its 菊 spelling pairs with きく.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[花](花.md) (はな), flower, and [植物](植物.md) (しょくぶつ), plant or vegetation, are broader descriptions, not names of this particular plant. [草](草.md) (くさ) can describe herbaceous plants rather than only lawn grass. [植木](植木.md) (うえき) can name a garden or potted plant without identifying its species; [植える](植える.md) (うえる) is the planting action, not the plant noun.

There is no direct antonym. 春菊 (しゅんぎく), crown daisy, is a distinct plant name; do not interpret it as simply "spring-flowering 菊."

## Distinctive meaning

In specialist or historical descriptions, 菊 can also name a chrysanthemum motif or crest, a traditional clothing color scheme, or a chrysanthemum-shaped cord ornament. Those uses do not make it an unrestricted substitute for "pattern," "color" or "cord."

Seisenban also records a register-specific anatomical euphemism and a Kyoto/Osaka produce-market code for nine. These are not the neutral anatomy word or the ordinary numeral [九](九.md) (きゅう / く). This lesson focuses on the flower noun; the direct quiz guards conservatively protect broader plant labels and documented secondary-sense overlaps, not general synonymy.
