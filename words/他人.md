# 他人 (たにん) — other people

## Meaning

他人 means another person or other people. In this use it contrasts someone else with the person whose perspective is relevant.
Japanese does not require a singular/plural change, so context determines whether one person or several are meant.
Use の to connect it to a following noun.

- 他人の物を勝手に使ってはいけません。 (たにんのものをかってにつかってはいけません。) — You must not use other people's things without permission.
- 他人の意見も聞いてください。 (たにんのいけんもきいてください。) — Please listen to other people's opinions too.

At the reading たにん, the dictionary also records a person unrelated by blood and an outsider or stranger.

- 二人は似ていますが、血のつながりのない他人です。 (ふたりはにていますが、ちのつながりのないたにんです。) — The two look alike, but they are not related by blood.
- 家族のことを他人に話したくありません。 (かぞくのことをたにんにはなしたくありません。) — I do not want to discuss my family with outsiders.

In the last example, the relevant boundary is the family rather than just the individual speaker.

## How the kanji combine

[他](../kanji/他.md), "other," modifies [人](../kanji/人.md), "person."
The on readings combine as タ + ニン. This word uses ニン, not ジン, for 人.
Do not build its pronunciation from the separate native words ほか and ひと.

## Synonyms and antonyms

他の人 (ほかのひと) is another way to refer to other people.
自分 (じぶん), "oneself," provides the useful perspective contrast:
自分の物 (じぶんのもの), "one's own things," versus 他人の物, "other people's things."

## Distinctive meaning

For this other-person sense, the important contrast is "not oneself," not necessarily "someone never met."
A familiar classmate's belongings can still be 他人の物.
Do not translate every occurrence as "stranger"; the whole sentence determines the relevant boundary.
