# 王冠 (おうかん) — royal crown; bottle cap

## Meaning

王冠 is a royal crown, worn as a sign of royal status. It can also name a crown awarded as an honor.
A separate everyday-object sense is a crown-shaped metal bottle cap, such as the closure on a beer bottle.

- 王は王冠をかぶっています。 (おうはおうかんをかぶっています。) — The king is wearing a crown.
- 瓶の王冠を外しました。 (びんのおうかんをはずしました。) — I removed the bottle's crown cap.

The second sentence concerns a bottle closure, not royal headgear. Use the context to choose the English meaning.

## How the kanji combine

[王](../kanji/王.md) means "king; ruler," and [冠](../kanji/冠.md) means "crown."
This is a modifier + head compound: a ruler's crown. The on readings are オウ + カン.
The bottle-cap sense extends the name to an object shaped like a crown; it is not a miniature item of royal clothing.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[冠](冠.md) (かんむり) is the broader crown or ceremonial-headgear word.
[帽子](帽子.md) (ぼうし) means a hat or cap, not specifically a royal crown.
For containers, [蓋](蓋.md) (ふた) is a broader lid or cap word, while [栓](栓.md) (せん) can mean a stopper or plug.
These words overlap for some closures, but are not interchangeable for every object. There is no useful single opposite noun.

## Distinctive meaning

王冠 does not name every lid or screw cap: the bottle sense concerns the crown-type metal closure.
For the royal object, use かぶる for putting it on and かぶっている for wearing it.
Do not transfer the separate 冠たる (かんたる), "peerless," use of 冠 to 王冠.
