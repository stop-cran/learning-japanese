# 痛い (いたい) — painful; sore

## Meaning

痛い describes pain or soreness. It is an い-adjective, even where English uses the verb "hurt."
A useful pattern is body part + が + 痛い.

- 頭が痛いです。 (あたまがいたいです。) — My head hurts.
- この傷はまだ痛いです。 (このきずはまだいたいです。) — This wound still hurts.

The ordinary negative is 痛くない (いたくない), "not painful," and the past is 痛かった (いたかった), "was painful."
For a polite statement, use 痛いです, not 痛いだ.

## How the kanji combine

[痛](../kanji/痛.md) carries いた-, and the final い marks the adjective.
This is a native word using a kun reading.
The on reading ツウ appears in compounds such as 頭痛 (ずつう), "headache," rather than replacing いたい.

## Synonyms and antonyms

痛む (いたむ) is a related verb meaning to hurt or ache.
Compare 頭が痛む (あたまがいたむ), "my head aches," with the adjective sentence above.
[疲れる](疲れる.md) つかれる means get tired; fatigue does not necessarily involve pain.
For the straightforward opposite condition, use 痛くない rather than inventing a separate antonym.

## Distinctive meaning

痛い reports the painful experience, not its diagnosis or cause.
頭が痛い does not itself explain why your head hurts.
Although the word has other uses, this article focuses on physical pain; the body-part pattern is the starting point.
