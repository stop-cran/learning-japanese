# 身 (み) - body; oneself; main part

## Meaning

身 is a noun for one's body or person, oneself, or the main substance of something.
The surrounding phrase matters: **身を守る** (みをまもる) concerns protecting oneself,
while **魚の身** (さかなのみ) is the flesh of a fish rather than its bones or skin.

- 危険から身を守る方法を学びました。 (きけんからみをまもるほうほうをまなびました。) - I learned ways to protect myself from danger.
- この魚は身が柔らかいです。 (このさかなはみがやわらかいです。) - This fish has tender flesh.

自分の身に起きたこと (じぶんのみにおきたこと) means "what happened to oneself."
Here the focus is the person affected, not simply an anatomical body part.

## How the kanji combine

身 is a single-character native word read み.
Its bodily meaning connects with its use for the main substance or central part of an object.
Do not infer that every compound containing 身 uses the same reading or the same narrow meaning.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[体](体.md) (からだ) is a broad everyday word for the body.
[自分](自分.md) (じぶん) overlaps with the self-reference use.
[肉](肉.md) (にく) names flesh or meat, but 身 emphasizes the substantial part as opposed to surrounding parts.

## Distinctive meaning

The main-part sense depends on a contrast.
For a sword, 身 can name the blade as opposed to its handle; it is not a general word for the entire sword.
Similarly, the fish example contrasts edible flesh with other parts.
Translate the particular object and phrase rather than assigning "body" mechanically to every use.
