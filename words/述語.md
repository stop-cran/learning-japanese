# 述語 (じゅつご) - predicate

## Meaning

述語 is a predicate: the part of a sentence that says something about its subject, such as an action, a state, or an identity. In a basic Japanese school-grammar analysis, it is not limited to a verb.

- 花が咲く。 (はながさく。) - Flowers bloom. Here 咲く is the predicate.
- この花は赤い。 (このはなはあかい。) - This flower is red. Here 赤い is the predicate.
- 私は学生です。 (わたしはがくせいです。) - I am a student. Here 学生です supplies the predicate.

The last two examples use a property and an identity, not an action. The term also has a technical use in logic; that use is additional to this introductory grammar explanation.

## How the kanji combine

[述](../kanji/述.md) concerns stating or relating something, and [語](../kanji/語.md) language or a word. They provide an association with what is stated in the sentence, not a rule that every predicate is one written word.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[主語](主語.md) is the subject about which the predicate says something. [動詞](動詞.md), "verb," names a word class: many predicates contain verbs, but the terms are not synonyms.

## Distinctive meaning

A subject need not be an agent doing an action, and a predicate need not describe an action. は marks a topic and does not guarantee that the phrase before it is the grammatical subject. Different grammatical frameworks draw predicate boundaries differently; the examples above introduce the school-grammar use rather than a universal analysis.
