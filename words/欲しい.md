# 欲しい (ほしい) — want to have

## Meaning

欲しい describes wanting to have something. Although English uses the verb "want," Japanese uses an い-adjective here.
A basic pattern is thing + が + 欲しいです.

- 新しい靴が欲しいです。 (あたらしいくつがほしいです。) — I want new shoes.
- 今は何も欲しくありません。 (いまはなにもほしくありません。) — I do not want anything right now.

The plain negative is 欲しくない (ほしくない). Do not make it by adding ない directly to 欲しい.

## How the kanji combine

[欲](../kanji/欲.md) supplies the desire meaning and the reading ほ; the kana しい complete the adjective.
There is no second kanji. The source reading ほ.しい marks the boundary before those written kana.

## Synonyms and antonyms

いる, also written 要る, means "be needed; be necessary" and is usually written in kana.
Wanting a new pair of shoes and needing one are not the same claim.
To want to do an action yourself, use the verb's ます-stem + たい:
本を読みたい (ほんをよみたい), "I want to read a book," versus 本が欲しい (ほんがほしい), "I want a book."

## Distinctive meaning

欲しい is straightforward for your own wishes and questions about another person's wishes.
For another person's observable desire, 欲しがる (ほしがる) is useful:
妹は新しいかばんを欲しがっています。 (いもうとはあたらしいかばんをほしがっています。) — My younger sister has been showing that she wants a new bag.
This avoids presenting another person's inner feeling as if it were directly your own.
