# 果たして (はたして) - sure enough; really?

## Meaning

果たして has two useful adverbial uses: confirming what was expected, and questioning whether something really is so.
The spelling 果して represents the same word and reading.

- 雨になると思っていたら、果たして雨が降り出した。 (あめになるとおもっていたら、はたしてあめがふりだした。) - I thought it would rain, and sure enough it started raining.
- 果たして彼は来るだろうか。 (はたしてかれはくるだろうか。) - Will he really come?

The second use belongs especially in questions or hypothetical statements.
It is not an unrestricted replacement for English "really" as an intensifier.

## How the kanji combine

[果](../kanji/果.md) appears with the native stem はた- also seen in 果たす はたす.
Learn 果たして as an established adverb; it does not mean "do fruit."
Both 果たして and the shorter spelling 果して are dictionary-recognized.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[やはり](やはり.md) and [やっぱり](やっぱり.md) overlap with the confirmation use,
but also have other uses such as choosing an earlier preference.
[本当](本当.md) (ほんとう) concerns truth or reality.
[実際](実際.md) (じっさい) and [現に](現に.md) (げんに) can present actual circumstances;
they do not automatically convey the doubt in 果たして～だろうか.

## Distinctive meaning

Read the whole sentence: an observed result can mean "sure enough," whereas a question can mean
"really?" or "whether ... at all." [結局](結局.md) (けっきょく), "in the end," focuses on an outcome;
an outcome need not confirm an expectation.
