# 一杯 (いっぱい) — full; one cupful

## Meaning

一杯 means **full**, usually written いっぱい in this use. This describes a state rather than counting one serving.
Use で for what fills a place or container, and いっぱいです as the predicate.

- 電車は人でいっぱいです。 (でんしゃはひとでいっぱいです。) — The train is full of people.
- お腹がいっぱいです。 (おなかがいっぱいです。) — I am full.

Before a noun, の is useful: 人でいっぱいの電車 (ひとでいっぱいのでんしゃ), "a train full of people."

一杯 also counts one serving in a cup, glass, or bowl. The container depends on what is being served.
It is a counter expression, not a fixed volume measurement.

- コーヒーを一杯ください。 (コーヒーをいっぱいください。) — One cup of coffee, please.
- 一杯の水を飲みました。 (いっぱいのみずをのみました。) — I drank a glass of water.

The quantity can follow the object, as in コーヒーを一杯, or precede the noun with の, as in 一杯の水.

## How the kanji combine

In the serving-count use, [一](../kanji/一.md) supplies "one"; [杯](../kanji/杯.md) counts cupfuls, glassfuls, or bowlfuls.
The on-reading combination is pronounced いっぱい, not いちはい.
Learn the small っ and the ぱ sound as part of this complete counter expression.

## Synonyms and antonyms

English "one cup," "one glass," and "one bowl" can all translate the countable-serving use, depending on the drink or food.
コップ (a drinking glass) names a container; 一杯 counts a serving, so the two are not interchangeable.
A quantity of one does not need an invented lexical antonym.

## Distinctive meaning

The full-state and countable-serving meanings need different sentence patterns.
The 一杯 in a one-cup coffee order counts a serving; it does not by itself say the cup is filled to the brim.
