# 主語 (しゅご) - grammatical subject

## Meaning

主語 is a grammatical subject. In straightforward Japanese learner examples, が can mark the subject. A subject is not necessarily a person doing an action.

- この文の主語は何ですか。
  (このぶんのしゅごはなんですか。) - What is the subject of this sentence?
- 空が青い。
  (そらがあおい。) - The sky is blue.

In the second example, 空, "sky," is the subject noun. 青い, "is blue," is the predicate: the part stating its property. The sky is not an agent performing an action.

Japanese can leave an understood subject unspoken. That does not mean the missing subject must always be 私 (わたし), "I"; the surrounding conversation supplies the interpretation.

## How the kanji combine

[主](../kanji/主.md) contributes "main; principal," and [語](../kanji/語.md) a word or linguistic expression. Learn their combination as the grammatical term, not as a claim that the subject is always the most important information. The readings are シュ + ゴ, with a short しゅ.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[話題](話題.md) (わだい) can mean a topic of discussion, while [科目](科目.md) (かもく) is a school subject. Neither is automatically a grammatical subject.

A は-topic and a subject can coincide, but need not. Compare この本は、田中さんが読みました (このほんは、たなかさんがよみました), "Tanaka read this book." この本 is the topic and the thing read; 田中さん is the が-marked subject, the reader.

## Distinctive meaning

Do not turn these examples into universal rules that が always marks a subject or that は always identifies one. Analyses of some が-constructions differ across grammatical frameworks. This card supplies a basic orientation to the term rather than a complete theory of Japanese subjects and topics.
