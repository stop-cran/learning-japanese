# ローマ字 (ローマじ) - Roman alphabet; romanized Japanese

## Meaning

ローマ字 names the Roman or Latin alphabet.
It also means writing Japanese in that alphabet: romanization.

- 名前をローマ字で書いてください。 (なまえをローマじでかいてください。) - Please write your name in Roman letters.
- この地図には地名がローマ字で書かれています。 (このちずにはちめいがローマじでかかれています。) - The place names on this map are written in Roman letters.

で identifies the writing system used. The surrounding context
determines which language the letters represent.

## How the kanji combine

Borrowed ローマ, Rome, combines with [字](../kanji/字.md),
ジ, a written character: Roman letters.
This is a mixed formation, categorized here with its borrowed base
as gairaigo, not an all-on Sino-Japanese compound or a single English loan.
The source ローマじ writes the final syllable in kana;
the headword here uses the actual kanji 字.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[文字](文字.md) (もじ) and [字](字.md) (じ) mean written characters
more generally. [仮名](仮名.md) (かな) names kana, a different
writing system. [英語](英語.md) (えいご) is a language, not
another name for the alphabet.

## Distinctive meaning

Roman letters do not automatically mean English: Japanese names can
be romanized. Different romanization conventions can spell the same
Japanese sounds differently; this card does not prescribe one system.
The rare ateji spelling 羅馬字 is recognized by the dictionary,
but is not the primary written form or kanji metadata here.
