# コック - cook; chef; tap valve

## Meaning

コック is a cook or chef. This occupation word comes from Dutch
kok, not from the English spelling "cock."

- 兄はホテルでコックとして働いています。
  (あにはホテルでコックとしてはたらいています。) - My older brother works as a cook at a hotel.
- コックがスープの味を確かめました。
  (コックがスープのあじをたしかめました。) - The cook checked the taste of the soup.

A separate word with the same spelling means a tap, spigot, or
flow-control valve, for example on a gas or water pipe.

- ガスのコックを閉めてください。
  (ガスのコックをしめてください。) - Please close the gas valve.

This third example concerns equipment, not a person.
The two meanings have separate entries in the pinned dictionary.

## How the kanji combine

There are no kanji components. Keep the small ッ in コック;
it marks the consonant closure before ク.
The English-derived equipment word and the Dutch-derived occupation
word share Japanese spelling, not one common origin.

## Synonyms and antonyms

料理人 (りょうりにん) also means a cook or chef.
調理師 (ちょうりし) identifies a qualified cook.
For the equipment sense, 蛇口 (じゃぐち) is a water faucet;
コック can also concern gas, so the English word "tap" does not
make every use a kitchen water faucet.

## Distinctive meaning

The source row combines the two entries' meanings.
Do not assign the valve sense to the Dutch kok entry or interpret
a gas コック as a cooking professional.
The separate English-derived family has other senses as well;
this article selects the source's cook and valve meanings, not
every meaning of English "cock."
