# 地球 (ちきゅう) — Earth

## Meaning

地球 is Earth, the planet on which we live. It can also be translated as "the globe" when the physical planet is meant.
The final きゅう has a long vowel.

- 地球は太陽の周りを回っています。 (ちきゅうはたいようのまわりをまわっています。) — Earth revolves around the sun.
- 私たちは地球に住んでいます。 (わたしたちはちきゅうにすんでいます。) — We live on Earth.

## How the kanji combine

[地](../kanji/地.md) supplies the earth or land association, and [球](../kanji/球.md) supplies the sphere or globe.
Together they name Earth as a globe, rather than a small ball made of soil.
The on readings are チ + キュウ.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[世界](世界.md) せかい, "world," overlaps when discussing the whole world, but can also refer to human society or a particular sphere of activity.
地面 (じめん) is the ground or surface underfoot. It does not name the planet in the same way.
There is no useful ordinary antonym to memorize for 地球.

## Distinctive meaning

Choose 地球 when the planet itself matters: its motion, environment, or place in space.
地球の[環境](環境.md) (ちきゅうのかんきょう) means "Earth's environment."
English "earth" can also mean soil or ground, but that does not make 地球 appropriate for dirt on your shoes.
The planet-versus-surface distinction is more useful than treating 地球 and 世界 as interchangeable translations of "world."
