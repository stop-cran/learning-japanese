# わざわざ (わざわざ) - going out of one's way; taking the trouble

## Meaning

わざわざ is a common adverb meaning "going out of one's way; taking the trouble." It presents an action as something done specially for a purpose, rather than incidentally or while doing something else.

- わざわざ来てくれてありがとう。 (わざわざきてくれてありがとう。) - Thank you for going out of your way to come.
- わざわざ言わなくてもいい。 (わざわざいわなくてもいい。) - You do not need to go out of your way to say that.

The first example appreciates someone's special effort. The second can suggest that the remark is unnecessary or unwelcome. The effort need not involve a long journey or a difficult task.

## How the kanji combine

The usual spelling is わざわざ. 態々 and 態態 are recorded rare kanji spellings; 々 repeats the preceding character. Learn the kana spelling for ordinary use.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[わざと](わざと.md), "deliberately; on purpose," emphasizes intention rather than accident. わざわざ emphasizes doing something specially or taking extra trouble. Dictionaries also record an overlapping "deliberately" sense, so these are tendencies, not completely separate meanings.

ついでに, "while one is at it; taking the opportunity," contrasts with making a special effort just for that purpose. Neither expression is an opposite in every context.

## Distinctive meaning

わざわざ is useful in both thanks and criticism: context determines whether the extra effort is appreciated or considered unnecessary. It does not by itself mean "kindly" or "maliciously."

Its repeated form resembles many Japanese mimetic words, but repetition alone does not establish onomatopoeia. Learn it as a native Japanese adverb expressing special effort or intention, not as an imitation of a sound.
