# どなた - who politely

## Meaning

どなた is a respectful way to ask "who?" It refers to a person, not a thing or place.

あちらはどなたですか。 (あちらはどなたですか。) - Who is that person over there?

どなたにお渡ししますか。 (どなたにおわたししますか。) - To whom should I give it?

The particle follows the pronoun and shows its role, just as with a person's name.

## How the kanji combine

The normal spelling is hiragana. 何方 is a dictionary spelling, but it is not needed for ordinary polite questions. Learn どなた as one pronoun rather than assigning readings to those characters.

## Synonyms and antonyms

誰 (だれ) is the neutral "who?" どちら can ask "which way?" or "which one?" and has additional polite contextual uses; it is not always a substitute for どなた.

## Distinctive meaning

どなた raises the politeness of the reference to the unknown person. It does not, by itself, make every surrounding sentence respectful; the verb and situation matter too. どなたですか is more courteous than the bare 誰, but a tactful full question is preferable when requesting someone's identity.
