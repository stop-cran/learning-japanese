# 高層 (こうそう) - high-rise; upper atmosphere

## Meaning

高層 concerns high layers: high altitude or the upper atmosphere, and
also a tall, many-storied building. It is a noun and の-modifier used
in compounds such as 高層ビル (こうそうビル), "a high-rise building."
高層気流 (こうそうきりゅう) means "high-altitude air currents."

- 高層気流の動きを調べています。 (こうそうきりゅうのうごきをしらべています。) - We are studying the movement of high-altitude air currents.
- 駅の近くに高層ビルが建ちました。 (えきのちかくにこうそうビルがたちました。) - A high-rise building has gone up near the station.

The upper-atmosphere use accounts for "upper"; the building use is another
useful meaning, not a reason to translate every occurrence as a building.

## How the kanji combine

[高](../kanji/高.md) contributes high, and [層](../kanji/層.md) contributes
layers or stories. High plus layers suits both higher air layers and
buildings rising through many stories. The on readings are コウ + ソウ.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[高い](高い.md) (たかい) is the broader adjective "high; tall" and can
also mean expensive. 低層 (ていそう), "low-rise; lower layer," provides
a contextual contrast. [上](上.md) (うえ) is broader upper-position
wording, not a replacement for 高層 in every compound.

## Distinctive meaning

高層 is not a general word for high quality or senior rank.
It also does not supply a universal legal number of stories for a high-rise:
technical definitions depend on the relevant standard.
Keep atmospheric height and building height separate when interpreting
the noun that follows.
