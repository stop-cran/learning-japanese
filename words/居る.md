# 居る (おる) - be present; humble existence

## Meaning

This card teaches 居る as **おる**, a godan verb, usually written おる. The independent existence sense is marked **humble** in the dictionary: it can present oneself or one's own side as being somewhere. Forms include おります and おりました.

- 私は午後も事務所におります。 (わたしはごごもじむしょにおります。) - I will be at the office in the afternoon too.
- ご連絡をお待ちしております。 (ごれんらくをおまちしております。) - I am waiting to hear from you.

The first example uses location に. The second contains a continuing-action construction, not a location statement.

## How the kanji combine

[居](../kanji/居.md) conveys being or staying. This spelling also appears with the distinct reading [いる](いる.md); the pronunciation おる has its own godan conjugation. Compare おって with いて.

## Synonyms and antonyms

いる is the basic existence verb to learn alongside おる. Respectfully describing a customer's presence requires attention to whose status is being presented; changing いる to おる is not a general way to honor that person.

## Distinctive meaning

Keep three constructions separate. Independent おる has the humble label. **Verb te-form + おる** can express continuing action or state; for example, 知っておる (しっておる), "know," describes a state. The dictionary does not label every such auxiliary use humble. A separate **masu-stem + おる** suffix expresses contempt for another's action; it is not polite existence language. These labels do not establish that おる has one uniform register in every context.

In standard Japanese, plain ～ておる can sound old-fashioned or overbearing.
For your own ongoing activity in polite speech, use ～ております, as in
待っております (まっております), "I am waiting," rather than copying the plain form.
