# 緊張 (きんちょう) - tension; nervousness

## Meaning

緊張 is tension, strain, or nervousness. 緊張する is an intransitive verb: a person becomes tense,
and 緊張している describes the resulting state.
The noun also describes tension between countries or groups.

- 面接の前はいつも緊張します。 (めんせつのまえはいつもきんちょうします。) - I always get nervous before an interview.
- 両国の間で緊張が高まっています。 (りょうこくのあいだできんちょうがたかまっています。) - Tension is rising between the two countries.

In the first example, the interview is the situation causing nervousness, not a direct object marked with を.
緊張しないで means "Don't be nervous."

## How the kanji combine

緊 contributes tightness, and 張 contributes stretching or tension.
Together they suggest being held taut, a useful image for both emotional and political strain.
The on-readings are キン + チョウ.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[不安](不安.md) (ふあん) is anxiety or unease, often about an uncertain outcome.
[いらいら](いらいら.md) emphasizes irritation or impatience.
[安心](安心.md) (あんしん), "peace of mind; relief," is a useful contextual contrast, though not a mechanical opposite in every sentence.

## Distinctive meaning

緊張 need not involve anger, hostility, or dislike. Someone may be tense before an enjoyable performance.
Conversely, diplomatic 緊張 describes a strained relationship rather than one person's stage fright.
The subject and setting tell you which kind of tension is meant.
