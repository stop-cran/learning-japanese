# 天皇 (てんのう) - Emperor of Japan

## Meaning

天皇 is the title "Emperor of Japan."
It identifies this particular sovereign's title, rather than any emperor or king in the world.

- 天皇の即位についての記事を読みました。 (てんのうのそくいについてのきじをよみました。) - I read an article about the emperor's accession.
- 天皇陛下が式典に出席されました。 (てんのうへいかがしきてんにしゅっせきされました。) - His Majesty the Emperor attended the ceremony.

天皇陛下 (てんのうへいか) is a respectful form of reference.
The second example uses respectful 出席される for attending.

## How the kanji combine

[天](../kanji/天.md) contributes a heavenly or imperial association, and 皇 "emperor."
Learn the whole reading **てんのう**: the ん before おう produces のう through the sound change
called 連声 (れんじょう), from てんおう. It is not evidence for a general 皇 reading ノウ.
This reading explanation is separate from the historical origin of the title.

## Synonyms and antonyms

皇帝 (こうてい) is the broader title "emperor."
[国王](国王.md) (こくおう) and [王](王.md) (おう) concern a king or monarch;
do not replace an established national title mechanically with one of those words.

## Distinctive meaning

Japanese 天皇 specifically names the Japanese imperial title in the modern use taught here.
English "emperor" alone does not establish that 天皇 is appropriate for a ruler of another country.
The examples teach vocabulary and respectful reference, not a claim about the emperor's political powers.
