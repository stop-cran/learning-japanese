# 何か (なにか) — something, anything

## Meaning

何か is an indefinite pronoun and adverb: 何か飲みますか (Will you drink something?), 何か問題がありますか (Is there some problem?), 何か食べたい (I want to eat something).
It can also mean "somehow, for some reason" (何かおかしい, "something is off").

- 何か飲みますか。 (なにかのみますか。) - Would you like something to drink?
- 今日は何かおかしい。 (きょうはなにかおかしい。) - Something feels off today.

The first asks about an unspecified thing; the second conveys an impression
without identifying a particular cause.

## How the kanji combine

[何](../kanji/何.md) (なに, what) plus the particle か (indefinite or question marker): "what-or-other". This page teaches なにか. The dictionary also records the shorter なんか, so the reading cannot be explained by a rule that なん is impossible before か.

## Synonyms and antonyms

- Related: 何も〜ない (nothing, with a negative), [何でも](何でも.md) なんでも (anything), [誰か](誰か.md) だれか (someone), どこか (somewhere).
- Antonym: 何も (nothing at all, with negative verb).

## Distinctive meaning

何か (something) in a positive or question sentence is the counterpart of 何も (nothing) in a negative one: 何か食べた, 何も食べなかった.

Read those examples as なにかたべた, "I ate something," and
なにもたべなかった, "I ate nothing." Negation in the whole construction
matters; 何も on its own is not an all-purpose translation of "nothing."
