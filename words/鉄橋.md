# 鉄橋 (てっきょう) - iron bridge; railway bridge

## Meaning

鉄橋 is a bridge made of iron or steel. The word is often used for a railway bridge, but does not make every bridge carrying trains an iron bridge.

- 列車が鉄橋を渡っています。 (れっしゃがてっきょうをわたっています。) - A train is crossing the iron bridge.
- 川に架かる古い鉄橋を写真に撮りました。 (かわにかかるふるいてっきょうをしゃしんにとりました。) - I photographed the old iron bridge spanning the river.

## How the kanji combine

[鉄](../kanji/鉄.md) gives the material and [橋](../kanji/橋.md) the structure: iron + bridge. The on readings テツ + キョウ combine as てっきょう, with a small っ; not てつきょう.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[橋](橋.md) is the broader word for a bridge, regardless of material. 鉄道橋 (てつどうきょう) explicitly identifies a railway bridge. A wooden bridge is 木橋 (もっきょう); materials, functions, and structures are different ways to describe bridges, not a single pair of antonyms.

## Distinctive meaning

Learn both the material sense and the railway association. A mixed dictionary page may also point to 鉄灸 (てっきゅう), a grilling implement that can be written 鉄橋; that is not the bridge sense taught here.
