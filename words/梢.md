# 梢 (こずえ) - treetop; tip of a branch

## Meaning

梢 names a **treetop or the tip of a branch**. The important idea is the tree's extremity, not the entire branch, trunk or tree.

- 梢が風に揺れています。 (こずえがかぜにゆれています。) - The treetops are swaying in the wind.
- 小鳥が梢に止まっています。 (ことりがこずえにとまっています。) - A small bird is perched at a branch tip.

Japanese does not require a plural ending here; context decides whether English needs "treetop" or "treetops." In the second example に identifies the bird's position.

## How the kanji combine

The single character [梢](../kanji/梢.md) writes the whole noun こずえ, without okurigana. There is no compound to divide. The 木 side is a useful shape cue to the tree context, not a rule that every word containing 木 means a tree.

末梢 (まっしょう) uses ショウ and can name a twig tip, an end, or minor details. Its 末梢神経 (まっしょうしんけい), peripheral nerve, use belongs to anatomy, not to the independent noun こずえ.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[枝](枝.md) (えだ) is a branch or twig; 梢 focuses on the tip. [先端](先端.md) (せんたん) is a more general tip or far end and also has a figurative leading-edge sense.

[端](端.md) (はし) and [末](末.md) (すえ) can describe an end more broadly. [木](木.md) (き) names the whole tree; none of these words replaces 梢 across all its tree-tip contexts. There is no fixed direct antonym.

## Distinctive meaning

English "top" is much broader than 梢. A mountain summit, a leading team or a page's top is not automatically こずえ. The tree or branch context matters.

The dictionary also lists a rare alternative kanji spelling for this same こずえ word. That is a spelling qualification, not evidence that the pronunciation itself is rare. This article uses 梢 consistently.
