# 神社 (じんじゃ) — Shinto shrine

## Meaning

神社 is a Shinto shrine. It names a religious place, not the deity worshipped there.
It is a noun: 神社に行く (じんじゃにいく), "go to a shrine," uses に to mark the destination.

- 駅の近くに神社があります。 (えきのちかくにじんじゃがあります。) — There is a Shinto shrine near the station.
- 明日、友達と神社に行きます。 (あした、ともだちとじんじゃにいきます。) — Tomorrow I am going to a shrine with a friend.

## How the kanji combine

[神](../kanji/神.md) contributes "deity," and [社](../kanji/社.md) has its shrine sense here.
Read the established compound じんじゃ: 神 has ジン, and 社's シャ is voiced to じゃ.
The company meaning of 社, familiar from [会社](会社.md) かいしゃ, does not make 神社 a "god company."

## Synonyms and antonyms

寺 (てら) means a Buddhist temple. English "temple" and "shrine" can be used loosely in conversation,
but these Japanese words identify different kinds of religious sites.
寺 is therefore a useful contrast, not an antonym or a free substitute for 神社.

神 (かみ), "deity," names what is worshipped rather than the shrine itself.

## Distinctive meaning

Use 神社 when the place is specifically a Shinto shrine, rather than as a generic term for any religious building.
In the station example, the word tells the listener what kind of place to look for;
it says nothing by itself about the building's size or the visitor's religious beliefs.
