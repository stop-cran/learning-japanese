# 判事 (はんじ) - judicial judge

## Meaning

判事 is a judge in the judicial system. In modern Japanese legal
terminology it is also a specific title or rank among 裁判官
(さいばんかん), rather than the name of every person working in a court.

- 彼女は判事として働いています。 (かのじょははんじとしてはたらいています。) - She works as a judge.
- 判事が判決を言い渡しました。 (はんじがはんけつをいいわたしました。) - The judge delivered the ruling.

判事として identifies the professional role. 判決 (はんけつ) is
a judgment or ruling, not another name for the person delivering it.

## How the kanji combine

[判](../kanji/判.md) contributes judging or deciding, and
[事](../kanji/事.md) contributes a matter or affair.
Together they associate the role with deciding judicial matters.
The on readings are ハン + ジ, not はんこと.

## Synonyms and antonyms

裁判官 is the broader Japanese term for a judge; the exact official title
matters when discussing ranks.
[裁判](裁判.md) (さいばん) names a trial or judicial judgment.
[審判](審判.md) (しんぱん) can name a referee or umpire:
English "judge" does not make 判事 the normal name of a sports official.
There is no useful single antonym for the profession.

## Distinctive meaning

Do not translate the source's "judiciary" as permission to call the whole
court staff 判事. The word names a judicial role or title, not the building
or every legal profession.
Historical offices and the unrelated ceramic reading はんす are outside
this はんじ article.
