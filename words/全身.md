# 全身 (ぜんしん) - whole body; full-length

## Meaning

全身 means the whole body, or all over the body.
It also describes a full-length depiction, one showing the entire person rather than
only the face or upper body.

- 雨で全身がぬれました。 (あめでぜんしんがぬれました。) - My whole body got wet in the rain.
- 全身の写真を撮ってください。 (ぜんしんのしゃしんをとってください。) - Please take a full-length photograph.

全身の modifies the following noun. In 全身に汗をかく
(ぜんしんにあせをかく), "sweat all over one's body," に marks the bodily extent.
The dictionary also records the related medical modifier "systemic,"
concerning the body as a whole; 全身性 (ぜんしんせい) makes that modifier explicit.

## How the kanji combine

[全](../kanji/全.md) contributes all or whole, and 身 contributes body.
The first character specifies the extent of the second.
The on readings are ゼン + シン, not the standalone native reading み for 身.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[体](体.md) (からだ) and [身体](身体.md) (しんたい; also read からだ)
name the body without necessarily emphasizing its entirety.
上半身 (じょうはんしん), the upper body, gives a useful scope contrast.
[心身](心身.md) (しんしん) means mind and body together, not merely the whole physical body.

## Distinctive meaning

Full-length describes what the image shows, not whether the person is especially tall.
全身 is also not by itself a synonym of "all one's strength":
全身の力をこめる (ぜんしんのちからをこめる) supplies 力, strength, in the complete phrase.
