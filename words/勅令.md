# 勅令 (ちょくれい) — imperial edict

## Meaning

勅令 is an imperial edict or an order issued by a sovereign. Learn it for historical and governmental writing, not as the ordinary word for any instruction.

- 歴史の本で、この勅令について読みました。
  (れきしのほんで、このちょくれいについてよみました。)
  "I read about this imperial edict in a history book."
- この勅令は、いつ出されましたか。
  (このちょくれいは、いつだされましたか。)
  "When was this imperial edict issued?"

These are original usage examples, not quotations from a particular historical document.

## How the kanji combine

[勅](../kanji/勅.md) (チョク) contributes the imperial association; [令](../kanji/令.md) (レイ) contributes an order or decree. This is a modifier + head relation: a decree issued by a sovereign. It explains the compound's meaning, not the historical origin of either character.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[命令](命令.md) (めいれい), "order; command," is broader. It can describe an ordinary instruction from a superior; 勅令 specifically identifies a sovereign's edict. A supervisor's everyday order is not normally called 勅令.

There is no single fixed antonym for every use. The absence or withdrawal of an edict is a situation, not an opposite word supplied by this compound.

## Distinctive meaning

The general dictionary sense includes orders of emperors or kings. In Japanese history, 勅令 also names a particular legal form under the Meiji Constitution: an order made through the emperor's prerogative without the Imperial Diet's assent. That historical legal sense is not the word's only possible geographic or historical use.

Recognizing that legal category does not by itself establish whether a particular old edict remains in force. Nor does every law, regulation or command count as a 勅令.
