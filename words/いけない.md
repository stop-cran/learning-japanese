# いけない (いけない) - not acceptable; must not

## Meaning

いけない can judge something as wrong or unacceptable. It also makes a
prohibition with **verb て-form + はいけない**: "must not do."
The polite form is いけません.

- ここで写真を撮ってはいけません。 (ここでしゃしんをとってはいけません。) - You must not take photographs here.
- 人の物を黙って使うのはいけない。 (ひとのものをだまってつかうのはいけない。) - It is wrong to use someone else's things without asking.

In the second sentence, の turns the preceding action into the thing being judged.

## How the kanji combine

This expression is written in kana here, so there are no kanji to divide.
Keep it distinct from 行けない (いけない), "cannot go," the negative potential
form of [行く](行く.md). Identical pronunciation does not make every use a
statement about going.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[だめ](だめ.md) can also reject an action: 撮ってはだめ, "You must not take it."
[悪い](悪い.md) (わるい) describes something as bad but does not replace
いけない in every prohibition. Verb てもいい, "may do," gives permission
instead of forbidding.

## Distinctive meaning

Polarity changes the construction. 行ってはいけない (いってはいけない) means
"must not go"; 行かなければいけない (いかなければいけない) means "must go,"
literally that not going is unacceptable. A rule stated with てはいけません
is firm even though its ending is polite.
