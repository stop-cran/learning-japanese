# 伯仲 (はくちゅう) — being evenly matched

## Meaning

伯仲 describes two sides whose strength is so close that it is hard to say which is better. JMdict glosses it “being evenly matched; being equal with; being on a par with; being well contested”, and it can be used as a verb, 伯仲する.

- 二つのチームの力は伯仲しています。
  (ふたつのチームのちからははくちゅうしています。)
  "The two teams are evenly matched."
- 決勝戦は、伯仲した試合になりました。
  (けっしょうせんは、はくちゅうしたしあいになりました。)
  "The final turned into a closely matched game."

These are original usage examples, not quotations from a dictionary or other source.

## How the kanji combine

[伯](../kanji/伯.md) (ハク) contributes the eldest of brothers; [仲](../kanji/仲.md) (チュウ) contributes the second of brothers. This is a relation of two characters side by side: together they name the eldest and second brothers, which is the word's archaic sense. It explains the written form, not the historical origin of either character.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[匹敵](匹敵.md) (ひってき), "be a match for; equal," is close in meaning. 互角 ごかく, “equal (in ability); even; evenly matched; well-matched; on par (with)”, is also close. [釣り合う](釣り合う.md) (つりあう), "balance; go well together," is broader: things can balance or suit each other without any contest. [引き分け](引き分け.md) (ひきわけ), "draw; tie game," is the result of a game that nobody won; 伯仲 describes how close the two sides are, not the result.

There is no single fixed antonym. A one-sided contest is a situation, not an opposite word.

## Distinctive meaning

In its main sense, 伯仲 is about two sides whose strength is too close to rank.

The other sense, “eldest and second eldest brother; older and younger brother”, is marked archaic in JMdict. Digital Daijisen lists it as its first sense, 兄と弟。長兄と次兄。
