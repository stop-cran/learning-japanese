# 鶏 (にわとり) — chicken

## Meaning

鶏 names the **domestic chicken**, whether male or female. The reading にわとり
makes the animal meaning clear.

- 庭で鶏を飼っています。 (にわでにわとりをかっています。) — We keep chickens in the yard.
- 鶏が鳴いています。 (にわとりがないています。) — A chicken is calling.

The first example uses を for the animal being kept; the second uses が for
the animal making a sound. [鳴く](鳴く.md) does not by itself say whether the bird is a
rooster or a hen, or specify the exact call.

## How the kanji combine

[鶏](../kanji/鶏.md) writes the whole native noun にわとり, with no okurigana.
The shorter reading とり is also documented. In [鶏肉](鶏肉.md) とりにく,
鶏 supplies native とり and [肉](肉.md) supplies on にく; learn that compound as a unit.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[鳥](鳥.md) とり means bird more broadly; a chicken is one kind of bird.
雄鶏 おんどり is a rooster and 雌鶏 めんどり a hen. 鶏 alone does not select
one sex. There is no direct antonym of the animal noun.

## Distinctive meaning

In a food context, 鶏 can also refer to chicken meat, especially with the shorter
reading とり. [鶏肉](鶏肉.md) とりにく explicitly names the meat.
The bare English word "chicken" can likewise mean either the bird or its meat.

Do not import English "chicken" meaning "coward" into the ordinary animal noun
にわとり. The borrowing チキン has that separate figurative use as well as a food sense.
