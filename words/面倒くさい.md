# 面倒くさい (めんどうくさい) - bothersome; tiresome

## Meaning

面倒くさい describes something that feels troublesome, tiresome, or
too much bother to do. It is an i-adjective.

- この手続きは面倒くさいです。 (このてつづきはめんどうくさいです。) - This procedure is a nuisance.
- 今日は料理をするのが面倒くさい。 (きょうはりょうりをするのがめんどうくさい。) - I cannot be bothered to cook today.

Use 面倒くさい仕事 (めんどうくさいしごと), "a bothersome job,"
without な. The past form is 面倒くさかった (めんどうくさかった).

## How the kanji combine

The on-reading base [面倒](面倒.md) (めんどう) combines with native
くさい, making an on-first mixed expression. [面](../kanji/面.md)
and [倒](../kanji/倒.md) write the base, not a literal "fallen face."
面倒臭い is another recognized spelling; めんどくさい is a recognized
shorter reading. Kana めんどうくさい is also usual.

## Synonyms and antonyms

面倒 and [厄介](厄介.md) (やっかい) overlap with trouble or bother,
but use different adjective-like attachment patterns.
[退屈](退屈.md) (たいくつ) can mean tedious or tiresome, though boredom
and reluctance to go to the trouble are not identical.

## Distinctive meaning

くさい here does not mean that the task literally smells bad.
The speaker may judge a simple task bothersome; the adjective does not
prove that it is objectively difficult or that the speaker is physically tired.
Keep it distinct from [疲れる](疲れる.md) (つかれる), "get tired."
