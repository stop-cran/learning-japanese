# 猪 (いのしし) — wild boar

## Meaning

猪 names a **wild boar**. The noun does not itself distinguish male from
female or singular from plural. JMdict marks it as usually written in kana;
イノシシ is a useful spelling alongside 猪.

- 山で猪を見ました。 (やまでいのししをみました。) — I saw a wild boar in the mountains.
- この山には猪がいます。 (このやまにはいのししがいます。) — There are wild boars in these mountains.

In the first sentence, で marks where the act of seeing takes place.
In the second, に marks where the animals exist. Context supplies the number.

## How the kanji combine

[猪](../kanji/猪.md) writes the whole native animal noun いのしし.
There is no second written character to divide off and no okurigana.
The character's on-reading チョ does not replace いのしし in this noun.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[豚](豚.md) ぶた is the domestic pig noun, not the usual word for a wild boar.
[動物](動物.md) どうぶつ, animal, is broader: it does not identify which
kind of animal is meant. [熊](熊.md) くま, bear, and [犬](犬.md) いぬ, dog,
name different animals, not antonyms.

## Distinctive meaning

English **boar** can also name a male domestic pig. Do not import that
male-only meaning into 猪: this lesson teaches the wild animal.

The spelling alone does not settle every historical reading. JMdict separately
records 猪 い as an archaic hog noun and 猪 しし in an archaic beast family.
Those are recognition notes, not alternative reading answers for this
いのしし article. Compound meanings also need checking: 猪口 ちょこ has a
sake-cup sense, not an animal sense.
