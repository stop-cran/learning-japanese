# 針金 (はりがね) - metal wire

## Meaning

針金 is wire: metal drawn into a thin, long form. It is a noun.
The word does not specify one particular metal or require an electrical use.

- 針金を曲げて輪を作りました。 (はりがねをまげてわをつくりました。) - I bent the wire to make a loop.
- この箱は針金で留めてあります。 (このはこははりがねでとめてあります。) - This box is fastened with wire.

In the second example, で identifies the means of fastening.
The noun is the material object, not the act of bending or fastening.

## How the kanji combine

[針](../kanji/針.md) はり contributes a needle-like slenderness
association, and [金](../kanji/金.md) contributes metal.
The native かね is voiced がね in this compound: はり + がね.
Do not read it はりきん or assume the wire must be gold.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[線](線.md) (せん) can also name a wire, besides a drawn line.
電線 (でんせん) specifies an electric wire or cable.
[紐](紐.md) (ひも), "cord; string," can perform similar fastening
jobs but is not automatically metal wire.
There is no useful single-word antonym.

## Distinctive meaning

Thinness alone does not make an object 針金.
A line drawn on paper is 線, not metal wire, and a sewing
[針](針.md) (はり) is a needle rather than this material noun.
The complete compound matters more than translating either character
in isolation.
