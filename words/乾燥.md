# 乾燥 (かんそう) - dryness; drying

## Meaning

乾燥 is dryness or drying. 乾燥する can be intransitive or transitive:
something dries, or someone dries something. The particles and context identify
the construction.

- 冬は空気が乾燥しています。 (ふゆはくうきがかんそうしています。) - The air is dry in winter.
- この機械で木材を乾燥します。 (このきかいでもくざいをかんそうします。) - I dry timber with this machine.

Compare 空気が乾燥する, "the air is dry," with 木材を乾燥する, "dry timber."
乾燥させる, "cause to dry," is another explicit way to express causing dryness.
乾燥した空気 (かんそうしたくうき) is "dry air."

## How the kanji combine

[乾](../kanji/乾.md) and [燥](../kanji/燥.md) both contribute dryness.
They reinforce the central meaning rather than forming an opposite pair.
The on readings are カン + ソウ.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[乾く](乾く.md) (かわく) describes becoming dry; [干す](干す.md) (ほす)
specifies putting something out to dry or air. 乾燥 does not itself specify
hanging something outside. [湿度](湿度.md) (しつど) is humidity, a measure of
moisture in the air, not simply an antonym noun for every use of 乾燥.

## Distinctive meaning

Dryness can be described without evaluating it as pleasant or unpleasant.
[からから](からから.md) emphasizes thorough dryness,
[かさかさ](かさかさ.md) can add roughness, and [ぱさぱさ](ぱさぱさ.md) or
[ぼそぼそ](ぼそぼそ.md) can describe undesirably dry food. Their sensory and
sound uses do not follow automatically from 乾燥. The examples above teach
physical dryness.

The dictionary also records figurative insipidity: a lack of interest or appeal.
This can overlap with [つまらない](つまらない.md), uninteresting, and
[退屈](退屈.md) (たいくつ), tedious, without making them unrestricted substitutes.

[切れる](切れる.md) (きれる) has a narrower "dry off" sense for moisture
draining or being shaken off. This limited English "dry" overlap does not make
it interchangeable with every use of 乾燥する.
