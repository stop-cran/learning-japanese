# 蛇 (へび) — snake; serpent

## Meaning

蛇 names a **snake**, without specifying its species, size or number.
The kana spellings へび and ヘビ also name the animal.

- 草の中に蛇がいます。 (くさのなかにへびがいます。) — There is a snake in the grass.
- 道で蛇を見ました。 (みちでへびをみました。) — I saw a snake on the road.

に marks where the living animal exists; で marks where the sighting took
place. Use いる for the living snake in the first example.

## How the kanji combine

The single character [蛇](../kanji/蛇.md) writes the whole native noun
へび, with no okurigana. Compounds have their own readings:
[蛇口](蛇口.md) じゃぐち is a faucet, while [蛇行](蛇行.md) だこう
describes meandering. Do not substitute へび into those words.

## Synonyms and antonyms

The standalone reading **じゃ** is also dictionary-supported and is
associated especially with serpents or large snakes; this is not an
absolute size rule. 大蛇 だいじゃ explicitly names a large snake.

[動物](動物.md) どうぶつ, animal, and [生き物](生き物.md) いきもの,
living creature, are broader categories, not words that identify a snake.
There is no useful direct antonym for this animal noun.

## Distinctive meaning

A snake is not necessarily venomous; the noun alone does not tell you
whether a particular animal is dangerous.

Digital Daijisen separately records **じゃ** as a heavy-drinker noun,
shortened from 蛇之助 じゃのすけ. That is not the animal **へび** sense
used in these examples. [酔っ払い](酔っ払い.md) よっぱらい instead
can name someone who is drunk on the present occasion; it does not require
a habitual drinking pattern. Neither word is a medical diagnosis.

The word-reading question for this article uses **へび**. Documented
alternative readings are not permission to swap readings in every compound.
