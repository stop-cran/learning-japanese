# さすが - as expected; even someone such as

## Meaning

さすが often acknowledges that someone or something lives up to an expectation or reputation.
It can be an adverb or a na-adjective in this sense.

- さすがプロですね。説明が分かりやすいです。 (さすがプロですね。せつめいがわかりやすいです。) - Just what you would expect from a professional! The explanation is easy to follow.
- さすがの先生も、この問題には困っていました。 (さすがのせんせいも、このもんだいにはこまっていました。) - Even the teacher was stumped by this problem.

The second pattern is **さすがの + noun + も**: even someone whose ability or standing is acknowledged meets a limit.
It is not permission to put さすがの before any noun without that relationship.

## How the kanji combine

流石 is the source spelling, with the complete irregular reading さすが. Kana is usual.
[流](../kanji/流.md), flow, and [石](../kanji/石.md), stone, do not supply predictable syllables for this expression.
Do not read it as りゅうせき or invent separate さ/すが readings for the two characters.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[やはり](やはり.md) and conversational [やっぱり](やっぱり.md) overlap in "as expected; after all."
さすが often acknowledges a reputation or an impressive result, rather than simply reporting any correct prediction.
[感心](感心.md) (かんしん) concerns admiration; さすが is not an unrestricted adjective for "clever" or "adept."

## Distinctive meaning

さすがに also marks a limit or a concession: "all the same; after all."

- 甘い物は好きですが、これだけ食べるとさすがに飽きます。 (あまいものはすきですが、これだけたべるとさすがにあきます。) - I like sweets, but after eating this much, even I get tired of them.

This accepts the first point while showing what happens under the stated conditions.
[それでも](それでも.md), "even so," overlaps in concession but does not replace every construction.
English "still" here is not the continuing-time sense of [まだ](まだ.md) or the physical stillness of [じっと](じっと.md).
Not every さすがに is negative: it can also confirm an expectation.
