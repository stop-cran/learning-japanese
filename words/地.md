# 地 (ち) - ground; land; place

## Meaning

地, read ち, is a noun for earth, ground, or land. It also names a place, especially in expressions such as **この地**, "this place; this land." It can occur as a suffix in place-related compounds.

- 鳥が地に降りました。 (とりがちにおりました。) - A bird descended to the ground.
- 祖父はこの地で生まれました。 (そふはこのちでうまれました。) - My grandfather was born in this place.

The first example uses に for a destination; the second uses で for where an event occurred. The short standalone 地 can have a literary or elevated feel compared with an ordinary description using 地面 or 場所.

## How the kanji combine

[地](../kanji/地.md) supplies the whole word. チ is the reading here, including in 目的地 (もくてきち), "destination." Do not assume every occurrence of this character is read ち; Japanese also has compounds using ジ.

## Synonyms and antonyms

地面 (じめん) explicitly names the ground surface. [土地](土地.md) (とち) is land or a locality. [場所](場所.md) (ばしょ) is a general place.

## Distinctive meaning

English "earth" can mean the ground or the planet. For the planet Earth, use [地球](地球.md) (ちきゅう), not a mechanical substitution of standalone 地. Likewise, この地 emphasizes a location as a land or place, not its soil composition.
