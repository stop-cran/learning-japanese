# 歯医者 (はいしゃ) - dentist

## Meaning

歯医者 is the everyday word for a dentist. In expressions about going there, it can also stand for the dentist's practice.

- 明日、歯医者に行きます。 (あした、はいしゃにいきます。) - I am going to the dentist tomorrow.
- 姉は歯医者です。 (あねははいしゃです。) - My older sister is a dentist.

The first sentence uses に for the destination; the second names someone's occupation.

## How the kanji combine

[歯](../kanji/歯.md), は, means "tooth." [医](../kanji/医.md) and [者](../kanji/者.md) form 医者 (いしゃ), "doctor." This is a mixed native-plus-Sino-Japanese reading: は + いしゃ, not an all-on compound.

## Synonyms and antonyms

歯科医 (しかい) and 歯科医師 (しかいし) are more formal occupational terms. 医者 alone means "doctor" without specifying dental work. These professions have no natural antonym.

## Distinctive meaning

歯医者さん is a common polite conversational expression. Although the place use is natural in 歯医者に行く, the underlying word names the practitioner; a formal name for a dental clinic is 歯科医院 (しかいいん).
