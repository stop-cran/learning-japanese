# 並木 (なみき) - roadside trees; row of trees

## Meaning

並木 names trees planted in a row, especially along a road.
It can refer to the line of trees collectively, rather than requiring
one English singular "roadside tree."

- 川沿いに桜の並木があります。 (かわぞいにさくらのなみきがあります。) - There is a row of cherry trees along the river.
- 並木の下を歩きました。 (なみきのしたをあるきました。) - I walked beneath the roadside trees.

桜並木 (さくらなみき) specifies cherry trees.
並木道 (なみきみち) is a tree-lined road, adding the road itself
to the expression.

## How the kanji combine

[並](../kanji/並.md) なみ contributes a row or alignment, and
[木](../kanji/木.md) き contributes trees: trees in a row.
This is native なみ + き, not なら + き.
並樹 is a rare dictionary spelling; search-only 並み木 is not needed
as a separate article identity.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[木](木.md) (き) is a tree or wood without specifying an arrangement.
[林](林.md) (はやし) is a grove or wood, not necessarily a roadside row.
[列](列.md) (れつ) is the broader idea of a line or row, without
specifying trees.

## Distinctive meaning

Do not use 並木 for every collection of trees simply because there is
more than one. Their line-like arrangement is important.
The word does not itself identify the species or season, so cherry
blossoms are not implied unless 桜 or the context supplies them.
Likewise, a tree-lined road and the trees lining it are related but
not identical referents.
