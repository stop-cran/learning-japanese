# 都 (と) - Tokyo metropolitan prefecture

## Meaning

都, read と, is the administrative designation used for Tokyo. It appears after 東京 in 東京都 (とうきょうと) and can refer to that metropolitan prefecture when the context is clear.

- 東京都に住んでいます。 (とうきょうとにすんでいます。) - I live in Tokyo.
- 都の広報を読みました。 (とのこうほうをよみました。) - I read the Tokyo metropolitan government's bulletin.

The same spelling also has the native noun reading **みやこ**, "capital; seat of government,"
especially in reference to Kyoto as Japan's former capital. It can also describe a city celebrated
as a center of an activity, without claiming it is a political capital.

- 京都はかつて都でした。 (きょうとはかつてみやこでした。) - Kyoto was once the capital.
- ウィーンは音楽の都と呼ばれます。 (ウィーンはおんがくのみやことよばれます。) - Vienna is called a capital of music.

## How the kanji combine

[都](../kanji/都.md) uses its on reading と here. In 東京都, 東京 identifies the place and 都 gives its administrative designation.
The additional reading みやこ is a whole native word, not a different pronunciation to substitute for と in 東京都.

## Synonyms and antonyms

県 (けん) is the designation used for most other Japanese prefectures. 市 (し), "city," belongs to a different administrative category. 都 is not a generic replacement for either.
For the political-capital meaning of みやこ, compare [首都](首都.md) (しゅと), "capital city."

## Distinctive meaning

The list gloss "metropolitan" should not be learned as a freely usable adjective: English "a metropolitan area" cannot always be translated by placing 都 before a noun. This card preserves と as its primary reading while also teaching the separate capital reading みやこ.
