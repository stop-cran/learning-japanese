# いつのまにか - before one knows; unnoticed

## Meaning

いつのまにか describes a change or event that happens without someone
noticing when it occurred. English often uses "before I knew it" or
"without my noticing." The relevant observer comes from context.

- いつのまにか雨がやんでいました。 (いつのまにかあめがやんでいました。) - The rain had stopped without my noticing.
- 話しているうちに、いつのまにか夜になっていました。 (はなしているうちに、いつのまにかよるになっていました。) - While we were talking, night had fallen before we knew it.

These examples report an unnoticed development from a later viewpoint.
They do not say that the change itself was instantaneous.

## How the kanji combine

いつの間にか and 何時の間にか are dictionary spellings. Here
[間](../kanji/間.md) is ま, an interval; 何時 is read いつ as a whole,
using [何](../kanji/何.md) and [時](../kanji/時.md).
Do not read this expression's 何時 as なんじ, "what time."

## Synonyms and antonyms

[突然](突然.md) (とつぜん) and [いきなり](いきなり.md) emphasize
suddenness or lack of warning. An unnoticed change can instead be gradual.
[気付く](気付く.md) (きづく), "notice," describes becoming aware.

## Distinctive meaning

This is not a question asking when something happened. The final か
belongs to the established adverbial expression; no answer giving a clock
time is required. Not noticing a departure does not imply that the
departing person deliberately acted secretly.
