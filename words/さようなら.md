# さようなら (さようなら) — goodbye; farewell

## Meaning

さようなら is a farewell greeting. The shorter さよなら is also used; the full form has a long よう, while the shorter form does not.

先生、さようなら。また明日。（せんせい、さようなら。またあした。） — Goodbye, teacher. See you tomorrow.

The greeting can be treated as something said: さようならを言う（さようならをいう）, say goodbye.

## How the kanji combine

Usually written in kana, this conventional Japanese expression is associated with さようならば, "if that is so, then..." Its farewell use is not a literal instruction about the direction [左](../kanji/左.md), left.

Rare 左様なら uses 左 and [様](../kanji/様.md); rare 然様なら uses [然](../kanji/然.md) and 様. Learn the expression's whole reading instead of decoding it as a regular kanji compound. The wago label here does not assert that every element of the older phrase has an exclusively native origin. JMdict's 左様奈良 is search-only, not a recommended spelling.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[別れ](別れ.md) わかれ is parting/separation, rather than simply an interchangeable spoken greeting. [お先に](お先に.md) おさきに is useful when leaving before others; it expresses that leaving-first relationship, not every goodbye.

## Distinctive meaning

The first example shows that さようなら need not mean a permanent final separation. Choose a farewell for the relationship and situation, rather than assuming one expression must fit every departure. As an interjection, さようなら stands on its own; it is not an adjective meaning sad or lonely.
