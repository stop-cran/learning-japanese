# 御無沙汰 (ごぶさた) - not having been in touch

## Meaning

御無沙汰 means having gone a long time without visiting or contacting someone. It is especially useful for acknowledging one's own lapse in contact politely. The spelling ご無沙汰 is also recorded.

- ご無沙汰しています。お元気ですか。 (ごぶさたしています。おげんきですか。) - It has been a long time since I was in touch. How are you?
- 長い間ご無沙汰して、申し訳ありません。 (ながいあいだごぶさたして、もうしわけありません。) - I am sorry I have not been in touch for so long.

The noun combines with する; ご無沙汰しています describes the continuing lack of contact.

## How the kanji combine

[御](../kanji/御.md) (ご) adds respect toward the person addressed. 無沙汰 (ぶさた) is the base: 無 supplies absence, and 沙汰 has a news or communication sense here. Learn 沙汰 as a unit rather than translating 沙 and 汰 separately as a literal account of the greeting.

The full spelling 御無沙汰 contains all four characters even when some do not yet have individual cards.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[久しぶり](久しぶり.md) (ひさしぶり) means that a long time has passed since the last occasion. It can concern anything done again after a gap. 御無沙汰 specifically concerns the lapse in contact; it is not a replacement for every 久しぶり.

## Distinctive meaning

In this greeting, you acknowledge **your own** silence rather than politely accusing the other person of neglect. The respectful prefix does not turn the expression into a general word for being busy or absent from any activity.
