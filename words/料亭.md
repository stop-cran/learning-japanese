# 料亭 (りょうてい) — traditional Japanese restaurant

## Meaning

料亭 is a traditional Japanese restaurant, especially an upscale one. It chiefly suggests Japanese cuisine, but the word is not a guarantee of an establishment's exact menu, price or facilities.

- 昨夜、料亭で食事をしました。（さくや、りょうていで しょくじを しました。）— Last night, I had a meal at a traditional Japanese restaurant.
- この料亭の料理はおいしいです。（この りょうていの りょうりは おいしいです。）— The food at this traditional Japanese restaurant is delicious.

Use で for eating there: 料亭で食事をする (りょうていで しょくじをする), "have a meal at a ryotei."

## How the kanji combine

[料](../kanji/料.md) リョウ connects with food or cooking here, as in [料理](料理.md) りょうり. [亭](../kanji/亭.md) テイ supplies the dining-establishment idea. Read it as a modifier-plus-place compound: a restaurant associated with prepared cuisine, not a "pavilion fee." Both are on readings.

## Synonyms and antonyms

- [レストラン](レストラン.md) is a broader restaurant word, especially associated with Western-style dining; the actual cuisine and naming vary.
- [食堂](食堂.md) しょくどう can mean a dining hall or cafeteria, or an ordinary eatery. That is a different emphasis from 料亭, not a rule that their possible referents can never overlap.
- [旅館](旅館.md) りょかん names a traditional Japanese inn: overnight lodging rather than the restaurant category. An inn may serve meals without making the two words synonyms.
- There is no direct antonym.

## Distinctive meaning

Learn 料亭 as a restaurant noun, not as the general word for Japanese food, a hotel, or every place with a traditional-looking building. Its upscale association does not establish a universal requirement for private rooms, reservations or entertainment.

The different spelling 涼亭 (also りょうてい) is an archaic garden-pavilion word, not an alternative spelling for this restaurant noun.
