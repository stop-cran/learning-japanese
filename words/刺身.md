# 刺身 (さしみ) - sashimi; sliced raw seafood

## Meaning

刺身 is sashimi: a dish of raw sliced fish, shellfish, or crustaceans.
It is a noun, not an adjective meaning "raw."

- この店では新鮮な刺身が食べられます。 (このみせではしんせんなさしみがたべられます。) - You can eat fresh sashimi at this restaurant.
- 夕食に刺身を注文しました。 (ゆうしょくにさしみをちゅうもんしました。) - I ordered sashimi for dinner.

The dish takes を with 注文する, "order."

## How the kanji combine

[刺](../kanji/刺.md) is read さし here, and 身 is native み, with a flesh or body association.
The whole food word is さしみ, not an on-reading compound.
刺し身 is another accepted spelling; adding the written し does not add a syllable to the pronunciation.
This reading explanation is not a reconstruction of how the dish was historically named.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[魚](魚.md) (さかな) names fish as an animal or food, more broadly.
焼き魚 (やきざかな), grilled fish, provides a cooked-food contrast.
[生](生.md) (なま), "raw," describes a state and does not itself name this dish.

## Distinctive meaning

The source's "sliced raw fish" is a useful starting gloss but does not exclude shellfish.
刺身 is not automatically sushi: rice is not part of this word's definition.
The name also does not establish whether a particular food is safe to eat raw.
