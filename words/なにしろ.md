# なにしろ - at any rate; after all

## Meaning

なにしろ brings a fact or reason to the listener's attention:
"at any rate," "for you see" or "after all." It can emphasize why the
speaker's conclusion or situation makes sense.

- なにしろ初めてなので、よく分かりません。 (なにしろはじめてなので、よくわかりません。) - You see, it is my first time, so I do not really know.
- なにしろ時間がありません。 (なにしろじかんがありません。) - In any case, we have no time.

It is an adverb, not a verb ending to conjugate. A following clause
supplies the actual fact or explanation.

## How the kanji combine

何しろ is the recorded written form; this article retains the source's
kana identity. [何](../kanji/何.md) is the character in that spelling.
Learn なにしろ as a whole discourse expression rather than translating
何 as an unanswered "what?" in each sentence.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[とにかく](とにかく.md) and [ともかく](ともかく.md) overlap with
"anyway; at any rate," often setting other considerations aside.
[何分](何分.md) (なにぶん) can also introduce a reason, particularly
when asking the listener to take circumstances into account.

## Distinctive meaning

English "after all" can mark several different relationships.
[やはり](やはり.md) may confirm an expectation, while なにしろ can
foreground a reason. Do not assume an English gloss makes them freely
interchangeable. Likewise, the expression does not itself assert that
the listener already knew the fact, even when "as you know" is a suitable
contextual translation. It can introduce new explanatory information.
