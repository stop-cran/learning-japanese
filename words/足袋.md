# 足袋 (たび) - tabi; traditional split-toe socks

## Meaning

足袋 are traditional Japanese socks with the big toe separated from
the other toes. They are associated with Japanese-style dress and
can be worn with thonged footwear.

- 着物に合わせて白い足袋を履きました。
  (きものにあわせてしろいたびをはきました。) - I put on white tabi to go with my kimono.
- 新しい足袋を一足買いました。
  (あたらしいたびをいっそくかいました。) - I bought one pair of new tabi.

Use 足袋を履く (たびをはく) for putting them on or wearing them.
The noun itself does not mark singular or plural.

## How the kanji combine

[足](../kanji/足.md), foot, and [袋](../kanji/袋.md), bag or
pouch, provide a useful image of a covering for the foot.
Learn **足袋 = たび as a whole-word reading**. Do not assign
た to 足 and び to 袋 as productive individual readings.
単皮 is a rare dictionary spelling, not the primary form taught here.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[靴下](靴下.md) (くつした) means socks more generally.
[草履](草履.md) (ぞうり) and [下駄](下駄.md) (げた) are
footwear rather than other names for the socks.

## Distinctive meaning

The split-toe form is the useful identifying feature; not every pair
of socks worn in Japan is 足袋. Distinguish the homophones
[旅](旅.md), "journey," and [度](度.md), "occasion":
neither supplies a different sense of this clothing word.
