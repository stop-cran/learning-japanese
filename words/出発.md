# 出発 (しゅっぱつ) — departure, setting off

## Meaning

出発 means departure or setting off. Use 出発する for "depart" or "set out."

- バスは九時に出発します。 (バスは くじに しゅっぱつします。) — The bus departs at nine.
- 明日、東京に向けて出発します。 (あした、とうきょうに むけて しゅっぱつします。) — I will set off for Tokyo tomorrow.

Use から for the starting point, as in 駅から出発する (えきからしゅっぱつする), "depart from the station."
The second example uses に向けて (にむけて) for the destination being headed toward.

## How the kanji combine

[出](../kanji/出.md), "go out," and [発](../kanji/発.md), "set out," reinforce the idea of beginning a journey.
This is an on + on compound, but the sounds change: シュツ + ハツ → しゅっぱつ.
The first part ends in small っ and the second begins with ぱ, not は.

## Synonyms and antonyms

- 出かける (でかける), "go out, set out," overlaps in everyday situations. It can emphasize leaving home for an outing rather than the departure event itself.
- 到着 (とうちゃく), "arrival," is the natural contrast. 出発する starts the trip; 到着する reaches the destination.

## Distinctive meaning

出発 focuses on the start of travel, so it works naturally for people as well as buses, trains, and other transport.
It does not mean that the traveler has already arrived, and it does not describe the whole journey.
