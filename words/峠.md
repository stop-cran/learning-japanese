# 峠 (とうげ) - mountain pass; critical peak

## Meaning

峠 is a mountain pass, especially the highest point of a route crossing a ridge.
It also names the peak or critical stage of a situation.

- 車で峠を越えました。 (くるまでとうげをこえました。) - We crossed the mountain pass by car.
- 熱は峠を越えました。 (ねつはとうげをこえました。) - The fever has passed its peak.

峠を越える (とうげをこえる) can describe crossing the actual pass
or getting beyond the critical or most intense stage.
The second example does not claim complete recovery.

## How the kanji combine

峠 writes the whole native noun とうげ; it is not read as a sequence of on readings.
The character was created in Japan, a 国字 (こくじ).
As a **modern-shape mnemonic**, 山 with 上 and 下 can suggest the point between going up and coming down.
That visual aid is not a historical derivation of the spoken word.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[頂上](頂上.md) (ちょうじょう) is a summit or top; a pass need not be the mountain's summit.
[山](山.md) (やま) can also figuratively mean a critical point.
[盛り](盛り.md) (さかり) is the height or prime of a season or activity,
not the name of a road crossing a ridge.

## Distinctive meaning

Learn the route or the situation with the noun.
A general ridgeline is not necessarily a 峠, and a generic difficult task is not automatically a critical peak.
The figurative construction often focuses on getting beyond the worst or most intense point,
rather than merely reaching a boundary.
