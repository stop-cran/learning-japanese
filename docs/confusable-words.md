# Confusable Japanese words: candidate inventory

**Stage: collection, not a finished contrast reference.** This is an editorial list of questions to refine into
word-card notes and comparison articles. It starts with the repository's vocabulary and existing comparisons, then adds
externally sourced learner traps. It is not an exhaustive list, an official JLPT list, or a claim that every item is
equally common or equally difficult.

English glosses are search cues, not definitions. Two Japanese words can overlap in one sense and differ in another;
one English word can also translate several Japanese words that are not synonyms. A useful article may explain genuine
overlap rather than manufacture a neat distinction.

## Reading the notation

- `CW001`, etc. identify **study questions**, not word IDs or dictionary senses. Keep these IDs when reordering the list.
- A row is a **candidate cluster**, not a list of interchangeable words. Relationships need not hold between every pair,
  and they are not transitive. A word can occur in several rows for different reasons.
- Readings identify the intended forms; alternative readings and unrelated homographs must be separated during refinement.
  Japanese words without an existing card remain plain text rather than acquiring a broken link.
- Tags are provisional and can overlap:

| Tag | Kind of confusion |
| --- | --- |
| `O` | Possible overlap between Japanese meanings or referents. |
| `G` | An English gloss hides a Japanese distinction or suggests the wrong choice. |
| `F` | Similar pronunciation, spelling, or word formation; not necessarily similar meaning. |
| `U` | Usage: grammar, collocation, viewpoint, register, or social relationship. |
| `X` | Cross-language false friend: resemblance to an English word tempts the learner toward a misleading meaning. |
| `P` | Polysemy or a context-dependent interpretation of the same expression. |

Here, **false friends** has the narrower cross-language meaning. Japanese near-synonyms and homophones belong in other
categories. A Japanese borrowing is not automatically a false friend, and a misleading English resemblance does not by
itself establish the word's historical origin.

Every inventory row remains at the **candidate** stage. Repository leads include explicit card comparisons as well as
title/gloss collisions that still need investigation. Card links provide existing starting points; they do not mean that
the card already compares every member, or that all senses received a new independent verification. External source
labels identify the particular entry or lesson supporting a lead, not blanket approval of the entire cluster.
The question column deliberately stops short of giving unrestricted substitution rules.

## Refinement, later

For a selected cluster, identify the intended senses and constructions, check dictionary and usage evidence, then write
translated examples showing both overlap and non-substitution. Record tendencies as tendencies, including register and
viewpoint; do not turn them into absolute rules. Split a cluster if its members require different questions, and link to
an existing comparison where that already answers the question.

This file lives under `docs/`, outside the app content manifest. Its IDs and tags are editorial notation only: no new
card metadata, study modes, quiz behavior, or automatic `quiz_exclusions` are introduced. An easily confused pair is
**not automatically** a pair that should be excluded from a meaning quiz.
