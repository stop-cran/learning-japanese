L16:# 幹 — tree trunk; main part
L17:
L18:**Strokes:** 13 · **Key (radical):** 干 (no. 51) · **JLPT:** N1 (community list)
L19:
L20:## Meaning and origin
L21:
L22:幹 means a tree's trunk and, by extension, a main or central part. In 幹線 かんせん, "main line; trunk line," it identifies a principal route rather than a branch.
L23:
L24:**Modern-shape mnemonic, not etymology:** Use the tall left-hand stack and the right-hand [干](干.md) shape as a reminder of an upright trunk supporting the main structure. This is a visual memory aid, not an account of the character's origin.

L39:## Notes
L40:
L41:- [木](木.md) names a tree or wood more generally; 幹 singles out its trunk. 幹部 refers to an organization's leadership, not its wooden parts.
L42:- Compare [朝](朝.md), morning, and [乾](乾.md), dry. Their similar left-hand shapes do not give them 幹's trunk meaning.
L43:- The dictionary also records 幹 から, "trunk; stem; stalk," in a separate word family. That word does not change this card's preserved KANJIDIC2 reading list or make every written 幹 interchangeable between readings.

L16:# 氏 — clan; family name
L17:
L18:**Strokes:** 4 · **Key (radical):** 氏 (no. 83) · **JLPT:** N1 (community list)
L19:
L20:## Meaning and origin
L21:
L22:氏 concerns a family name or lineage. It is also used after a person's name as a respectful suffix, normally read し in modern reporting. The suffix does not specify marital status.
L23:
L24:**Modern-shape mnemonic, not etymology:** Picture a family label resting along the lower sweeping stroke. This is a memory aid for the whole shape, not a reconstruction of its origin.
L25:
L26:## Readings
L27:
L28:| Reading | Type | Typical words |
L29:| --- | --- | --- |
L30:| シ | on | [氏](../words/氏.md) し - respectful name suffix; [氏名](../words/氏名.md) しめい - full name |
L31:| うじ | kun | [氏](../words/氏.md) うじ - family name; lineage; clan (the article distinguishes this from its primary し) |
L32:| -うじ | kun suffix | 山田氏 やまだうじ - Mr. Yamada (older name-suffix use; see below) |
L33:
L34:The suffix example illustrates an older use documented by Japanese dictionaries. For a contemporary reference to Mr./Ms. Yamada, learn 山田氏 やまだし instead; do not replace modern name-suffix し with うじ mechanically.
L35:
L36:## Common words
L37:
L38:- [氏名](../words/氏名.md) しめい - full name
L39:- [氏](../words/氏.md) し - respectful suffix after a name; also clan in the relevant context
L40:- 氏 うじ - family name; lineage
L41:
L42:## Notes
L43:
L44:- The linked 氏 word article has primary reading **し**, not うじ. Both readings can have a clan-related meaning; the difference is not a strict split between "respectful title" and "clan."
L45:- [姓](姓.md), surname, overlaps with 氏's family-name meaning. It is a useful comparison, not a safe wrong answer for this card.
L46:- Distinguish [民](民.md), populace, and [低](低.md), low. 氏 is also visible within [紙](紙.md), paper, but 紙 is a different character.
L47:- KANJIDIC2 lists both うじ and suffix -うじ. The dash marks attachment, not an extra sound.

L20:## Meaning and origin
L21:

L45:

L48:- Compare [吸](吸.md), inhale or suck in: its left side is 口, not 扌. [坂](坂.md), slope, has 土 on the left; [投](投.md), throw, has a different right side.

L16:# 乃 — thereupon; possessive no
L17:
L18:**Strokes:** 2 · **Key (radical):** 丿 (no. 4) · **JLPT:** N1 (community list)
L19:
L20:## Meaning and origin
L21:
L22:乃 appears in connecting expressions such as [乃至](../words/乃至.md) ないし, "from ... to; or." Character dictionaries also give **すなわち**, "thereupon; namely," **なんじ**, "you," and use for the sound **の**. These are not instructions to replace ordinary hiragana の with 乃 throughout a modern sentence.
L23:
L24:**Modern-shape mnemonic, not etymology:** Follow the curved, hooked outline and the separate slanting stroke. Think of one phrase curving into the next. The shape association does not explain the character's history.
L25:
L26:## Readings
L27:
L28:| Reading | Type | Typical words |
L29:| --- | --- | --- |
L30:| ナイ | on | [乃至](../words/乃至.md) ないし - from ... to; or, usually written ないし |
L31:| ダイ | on | 乃公 だいこう - dated, rough or arrogant male "I; me" |
L32:| ノ | on, source-only here | KANJIDIC2 lists this as an on reading; no separate on-reading word example is established here |
L33:| アイ | on, source-only here | KANJIDIC2 lists this reading; no word example is established here |
L34:| の | kun | 乃 の - a historical spelling associated with the possessive particle の; JMdict treats 乃 in this family as search-only |
L35:| すなわ.ち | kun | 乃ち すなわち - namely; thereupon, a rare spelling in the すなわち family |
L36:| なんじ | kun | 乃 なんじ - "you" in character-dictionary/kanbun usage; not a modern conversational form of address |
L37:
L38:**Kanbun** is the Japanese reading tradition for texts written in Classical Chinese. Recognizing a character's kanbun gloss does not make it an everyday standalone word. The on ノ and kun の labels above preserve the source's separate classifications; identical kana do not establish two interchangeable modern uses.
L39:
