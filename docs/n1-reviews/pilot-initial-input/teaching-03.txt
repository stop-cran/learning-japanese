L14: # 莉 — jasmine in compounds
L15: 
L16: **Strokes:** 10 · **Key (radical):** 艸 (as 艹, no. 140) · **JLPT:** N1 (community list)
L17: 
L18: ## Meaning and origin
L19: 
L20: 莉 is a character used in **jasmine names**, especially 茉莉 まつり and [茉莉花](../words/茉莉花.md) まつりか, "Arabian jasmine." Learn it inside these words rather than treating 莉 as an ordinary standalone Japanese noun.
L21: 
L22: **Modern-shape mnemonic, not etymology:** Spot the plant-shaped 艹 above [利](利.md). The plant top can remind you that this character belongs in a flower name; 利's "benefit" meaning is not a literal definition of jasmine.

L10: 
L11: # 乃至 (ないし) - from ... to; or
L12: 
L13: ## Meaning
L14: 
L15: 乃至, usually written **ないし**, connects a range or presents alternatives. Between quantities it can mean "from ... to," including intermediate amounts; between categories it can mean "or."
L16: 
L17: - 作業には二日ないし三日かかります。 (さぎょうにはふつかないしみっかかかります。) - The work takes two to three days.
L18: - 連絡は電話ないしメールでお願いします。 (れんらくはでんわないしメールでおねがいします。) - Please contact us by phone or email.
L19: 
L20: The first example gives a range. The second offers communication methods, not a numerical interval. Do not assume that every ないし has the same English translation.
L21: 
L22: ## How the kanji combine
L23: 
L24: [乃](../kanji/乃.md) supplies a connecting sense; 至 has the sense of reaching or extending to. The compound is read ナイ + シ. Learn the established conjunction as a whole, not as a rule for translating either character in every word.
L25: 
L26: ## Synonyms and antonyms
L27: 
L28: For ranges, AからBまで explicitly gives the two endpoints. [または](または.md) and [あるいは](あるいは.md) overlap with the alternative "or" use. [それとも](それとも.md) often introduces another possibility in a question; it is not a replacement for numerical-range ないし. There is no single useful antonym for both uses.
L29: 
L30: ## Distinctive meaning
L31: 
L32: ないし suits written instructions and explanations; in conversation, から…まで or a suitable ordinary "or" expression may be clearer. The kanji spelling 乃至 is a recognition target, not a recommendation to avoid kana. Use the units and surrounding context to tell a range from separate alternatives.
L33: 
L34: Dictionary: [JMdict](https://www.edrdg.org/wiki/index.php/JMdict-EDICT_Dictionary_Project), entry 1469810, senses 1-2. Range/alternative distinction: [Kanjipedia, 乃至](https://www.kanjipedia.jp/kotoba/0004474100).

L9: 
L10: # 彗星 (すいせい) - comet
L11: 
L12: ## Meaning
L13: 
L14: 彗星 is a **comet**.
L15: 
L16: - 望遠鏡で彗星を見ました。 (ぼうえんきょうですいせいをみました。) - I saw a comet through a telescope.
L17: - この写真には彗星が写っています。 (このしゃしんにはすいせいがうつっています。) - A comet appears in this photograph.
L18: 
L19: The telescope is the means of observation in the first sentence; the photograph is where the comet's image appears in the second.
L20: 
L21: ## How the kanji combine
L22: 
L23: [彗](../kanji/彗.md) has broom and comet senses; [星](../kanji/星.md) supplies the celestial-object element. Read the compound **スイ + セイ**. The broom association is a memory aid for a comet's tail, not a claim that a comet is a star in the modern astronomical sense.
L24: 
L25: ## Synonyms and antonyms
L26: 
L27: 流星 (りゅうせい), also called 流れ星 (ながれぼし), is a meteor or "shooting star," not a comet. [星](星.md) (ほし) is a broader everyday sky-word, but it does not replace the specific astronomical term 彗星. There is no direct antonym.
L28: 
L29: ## Distinctive meaning
L30: 
L31: Do not call every brief streak of light a 彗星. This word identifies a comet, not simply anything bright that appears to move across the sky. The reading すいせい also has unrelated homophones; the written form and context identify this word.
L32: 
L33: Dictionary: [JMdict](https://www.edrdg.org/wiki/index.php/JMdict-EDICT_Dictionary_Project), entry 1566680, sense 1.

L10: 
L11: # 栞 (しおり) - bookmark; guidebook
L12: 
L13: ## Meaning
L14: 
L15: 栞, usually written **しおり**, can be a **bookmark** placed between pages or a **guidebook** giving information or instructions.
L16: 
L17: - 本にしおりを挟みました。 (ほんにしおりをはさみました。) - I put a bookmark in the book.
L18: - 旅行のしおりを読みました。 (りょこうのしおりをよみました。) - I read the trip guide.
L19: 
L20: In the first example, the object marks a place in a book. In the second, the しおり itself contains information to read. A bookmark need not be a booklet, and a trip guide need not mark a page.
L21: 
L22: ## How the kanji combine
L23: 
L24: The one character [栞](../kanji/栞.md) represents the native word **しおり**. 枝折り and 枝折 are additional dictionary spellings of the same word; they do not make the kana pronunciation an on-reading compound.
L25: 
L26: ## Synonyms and antonyms
L27: 
L28: [本](本.md) (ほん) is a book more generally. [案内](案内.md) (あんない) concerns guidance or information; a しおり in the guidebook sense can provide that information. Neither word by itself means the page-marking object. There is no useful direct antonym.
L29: 
L30: ## Distinctive meaning
L31: 
L32: Context distinguishes the two uses: placing something between pages points to a bookmark, while reading a trip or activity's しおり points to its guide. Do not translate every occurrence as "bookmark" merely because that is the first sense learned.
L33: 
L34: Dictionary: [JMdict](https://www.edrdg.org/wiki/index.php/JMdict-EDICT_Dictionary_Project), entry 1568040, senses 1-2.

L10: 
L11: # 茉莉花 (まつりか) - Arabian jasmine
L12: 
L13: ## Meaning
L14: 
L15: 茉莉花 names **Arabian jasmine (Jasminum sambac)**. The dictionary also records 茉莉 (まつり) for the same plant.
L16: 
L17: - 茉莉花の花が咲きました。 (まつりかのはながさきました。) - The Arabian jasmine flowers bloomed.
L18: - 庭で茉莉花を育てています。 (にわでまつりかをそだてています。) - I am growing Arabian jasmine in the garden.
L19: 
L20: These examples name the plant. They do not claim that every plant called "jasmine" in English is this species.
L21: 
L22: ## How the kanji combine
L23: 
L24: Treat **茉莉** as the plant-name unit まつり, not as two independently meaningful everyday nouns. Within it, [莉](../kanji/莉.md) contributes リ; [花](../kanji/花.md) contributes カ, "flower," in the longer name **まつりか**. The whole word names the plant, not only a cut blossom.
L25: 
L26: ## Synonyms and antonyms
L27: 
L28: 茉莉 (まつり) names the same plant. ジャスミン is a broader jasmine name; identifying a plant specifically as 茉莉花 requires the intended species, not merely a pleasant floral smell. [花](花.md) (はな), "flower," is not a synonym for this particular plant. There is no useful antonym.
L29: 
L30: ## Distinctive meaning
L31: 
L32: Read this entry **まつりか**. Do not confuse the shorter plant name まつり with 祭り (まつり), "festival." The shared sound does not give the plant a festival meaning or establish an etymology.
L33: 
L34: Dictionary: [JMdict](https://www.edrdg.org/wiki/index.php/JMdict-EDICT_Dictionary_Project), entries 1641120 and 1909630.

L10: 
L11: # 鷹 (たか) - hawk; falcon
L12: 
L13: ## Meaning
L14: 
L15: 鷹 is a hawk or falcon, a bird of prey. The word is also written **たか** or **タカ**; the dictionary marks kana as usual.
L16: 
L17: - 空を鷹が飛んでいます。 (そらをたかがとんでいます。) - A hawk is flying through the sky.
L18: - 鷹の写真を撮りました。 (たかのしゃしんをとりました。) - I took a photograph of a hawk.
L19: 
L20: These sentences identify the bird, not a precise species from its appearance.
L21: 
L22: ## How the kanji combine
L23: 
L24: This is one native noun, not a multi-kanji compound. The whole character [鷹](../kanji/鷹.md) is read **たか** here. Do not substitute the character's on readings ヨウ or オウ for this word.
L25: 
L26: ## Synonyms and antonyms
L27: 
L28: [鳥](鳥.md) (とり), "bird," is the broader category. 鷲 (わし), "eagle," is a related bird-of-prey name, not an unrestricted synonym for 鷹. There is no useful antonym.
L29: 
L30: ## Distinctive meaning
L31: 
L32: English bird names and Japanese common names do not form a simple one-to-one species key. Learn たか for the documented hawk/falcon word without assuming it names every bird of prey. Writing an animal name in katakana does not make it a foreign loanword.
L33: 
L34: Dictionary: [JMdict](https://www.edrdg.org/wiki/index.php/JMdict-EDICT_Dictionary_Project), entry 1415510, sense 1.

L10: 
L11: # うがい - gargling; rinsing the mouth
L12: 
L13: ## Meaning
L14: 
L15: うがい is gargling or rinsing one's mouth. It is a noun used with する: うがいする or うがいをする. The liquid used can take で.
L16: 
L17: - 水でうがいをしました。 (みずでうがいをしました。) - I gargled with water.
L18: - うがいをしてから、コップを洗いました。 (うがいをしてから、コップをあらいました。) - After rinsing my mouth, I washed the cup.
L19: 
L20: The ordinary verb forms come from する: うがいします, うがいした, うがいしない. The action need not be described with a separate object naming the throat or mouth.
L21: 
L22: ## How the kanji combine
L23: 
L24: The teaching spelling is うがい. The community vocabulary source uses 嗽, but JMdict marks that kanji spelling as rare and the word as usually written in kana. [漱](../kanji/漱.md) is another rare kanji spelling of the same noun, read うがい here. Recognizing these spellings is useful when reading; there is no need to invent a kanji-by-kanji explanation for this kana headword.
L25: 
L26: ## Synonyms and antonyms
L27: 
L28: [洗う](洗う.md) (あらう) means "wash" more generally and takes the thing washed with を. 口をすすぐ (くちをすすぐ), "rinse the mouth," makes the mouth-rinsing action explicit.
L29: 
L30: ## Distinctive meaning
L31: 
L32: English "gargle" can name either an action or a preparation used for it. うがい here names the action, not a particular medicine. These examples teach the language pattern and do not imply that gargling prevents or treats any illness.
L33: 
L34: Dictionary: [JMdict](https://www.edrdg.org/wiki/index.php/JMdict-EDICT_Dictionary_Project), entry 1577660, sense 1.
