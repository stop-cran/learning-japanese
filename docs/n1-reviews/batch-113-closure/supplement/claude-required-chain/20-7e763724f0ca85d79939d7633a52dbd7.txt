# 恩恵 (おんけい) - benefit; blessing

## Meaning

恩恵 is a benefit, favor, or blessing received from a person, institution, natural condition, or other source. Useful patterns are 恩恵を受ける (おんけいをうける), "receive a benefit," and ～の恩恵, "the benefits of ..."

- 私たちは技術の進歩の恩恵を受けています。 (わたしたちはぎじゅつのしんぽのおんけいをうけています。) - We benefit from advances in technology.
- この地域は温暖な気候の恩恵を受けています。 (このちいきはおんだんなきこうのおんけいをうけています。) - This region benefits from its mild climate.

The thing or condition providing the benefit can precede の. Receiving an 恩恵 does not require being paid money.

## How the kanji combine

[恩](../kanji/恩.md) contributes favor or kindness received; 恵 contributes blessing or benefit. Their related meanings reinforce the idea of a favorable benefit. The on readings are オン + ケイ.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[恩](恩.md) (おん) emphasizes favor received and the resulting gratitude or obligation. [利益](利益.md) (りえき) also means benefit or advantage and can refer to financial profit. [害](害.md) (がい), "harm," offers a contextual contrast, not a fixed opposite for every blessing.

## Distinctive meaning

The benefit need not come from a deliberate gift: climate and technology can provide 恩恵. Conversely, calling something a benefit does not by itself state how much it is worth or who benefits most. Identify the recipient and source rather than reading the noun as automatic proof of universal good.

# 感謝 (かんしゃ) - gratitude; appreciation

## Meaning

感謝 is gratitude or appreciation. 感謝する means to feel or express thanks.
Use person + に + 感謝する, or name the help or kindness with に.

- 手伝ってくれた友人に感謝しています。 (てつだってくれたゆうじんにかんしゃしています。) - I am grateful to the friend who helped me.
- ご協力に感謝します。 (ごきょうりょくにかんしゃします。) - Thank you for your cooperation.

感謝している describes an ongoing feeling of gratitude. The noun also appears
in 感謝の気持ち (かんしゃのきもち), "a feeling of gratitude."

## How the kanji combine

[感](../kanji/感.md) contributes feeling, and [謝](../kanji/謝.md) contributes thanking here.
Together they name grateful feeling or appreciation. The on readings are
カン + シャ. Although 謝 also appears in words about apologies, 感謝 does not
mean apologizing.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[ありがとう](ありがとう.md) directly thanks someone. 感謝 is a noun and
する verb, so it can discuss gratitude as well as express it. お礼 (おれい)
can refer to thanks or an act of thanking; its construction differs.

## Distinctive meaning

感謝します is useful in a deliberate or courteous statement of appreciation,
but 感謝 is not limited to ceremonies. 感謝を伝える (かんしゃをつたえる),
"convey one's gratitude," takes を because gratitude is what is conveyed.
Do not copy that particle into every 感謝する sentence: the person thanked
is readily expressed with に.

# 我慢 (がまん) - endurance; self-restraint

## Meaning

我慢 names enduring something unpleasant or restraining a desire. It is a noun and a する-verb. What someone puts up with can take を; a whole action can be expressed with verb + のを我慢する.

- 痛みを我慢しないで、医師に相談してください。 (いたみをがまんしないで、いしにそうだんしてください。) - Don't put up with the pain; consult a doctor.
- 今日は甘い物を食べるのを我慢しました。 (きょうはあまいものをたべるのをがまんしました。) - Today I resisted eating sweets.

The first example concerns bearing discomfort; the second concerns holding oneself back. 我慢できない means "cannot bear it" or "cannot resist," depending on the situation.

## How the kanji combine

我 concerns the self, and [慢](../kanji/慢.md) can suggest pride or arrogance. The modern meaning is not transparently obtained by adding those glosses. Learn 我慢 as the established compound がまん; the character meanings alone do not explain its semantic history.

## Synonyms and antonyms

耐える (たえる), "endure," overlaps with bearing difficulty. [苦しむ](苦しむ.md) (くるしむ) describes suffering, without itself saying that someone exercises restraint. 辛抱 (しんぼう) also concerns patient endurance.

## Distinctive meaning

我慢 is not automatically praise. 我慢して can ask someone to tolerate a difficulty, while 我慢しないで can encourage them to speak up or seek help. The situation determines whether restraint is useful; the vocabulary does not prescribe enduring every hardship.

Dictionary: [JMdict](https://www.edrdg.org/wiki/index.php/JMdict-EDICT_Dictionary_Project), entry 1196970, senses 1-2.

# 握る (にぎる) - grasp; grip

## Meaning

握る means to grasp or grip something in the hand. It is a transitive godan verb: 握ります, 握った, 握らない. The object held takes を. It also describes forming food by pressing it with the hands.

- 子供が母の手を握っています。 (こどもがははのてをにぎっています。) - The child is holding their mother's hand.
- 母と一緒におにぎりを握りました。 (ははといっしょにおにぎりをにぎりました。) - I made rice balls with my mother.

In the rice-ball example, 握る describes shaping the rice, not merely picking up finished food.

## How the kanji combine

[握](../kanji/握.md) supplies the grasping idea and is read にぎ before る. It is one native verb, not an ichidan verb despite ending in る. Its past 握った helps distinguish the conjugation.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[持つ](持つ.md) (もつ) is a broader word for holding or possessing. 握る makes a grip or clasp salient. [放す](放す.md) (はなす), "let go of," is a useful contrast when releasing what is held.

## Distinctive meaning

The grip can be figurative: 権力を握る (けんりょくをにぎる) means "hold or seize power." Context tells you whether the object is physically grasped, shaped by hand, or controlled. The sushi-related sense does not make 握る a general verb for preparing every kind of food.

Dictionary: [JMdict](https://www.edrdg.org/wiki/index.php/JMdict-EDICT_Dictionary_Project), entry 1152720, senses 1, 3, and 4.

# 握手 (あくしゅ) - handshake

## Meaning

握手 is a noun meaning "a handshake." With する it means "to shake hands."
The other person takes と: 人と握手する. It does not mean simply holding
someone's hand while walking.

- 試合の後で、相手と握手しました。 (しあいのあとで、あいてとあくしゅしました。) - After the match, I shook hands with my opponent.
- 握手を求められました。 (あくしゅをもとめられました。) - Someone asked to shake hands with me.

握手する describes the shared action; 握手をする expresses the same action
with the noun marked by を. Learn the partner with と rather than translating
"shake someone's hand" word for word.

## How the kanji combine

[握](../kanji/握.md) means "grip" or "grasp," and [手](../kanji/手.md) means "hand."
The compound has a verb-object relationship: gripping a hand. Its on readings
combine as あく + しゅ.

## Synonyms and antonyms

挨拶 (あいさつ), "greeting," names a broader social act; a handshake can be
part of a greeting. 手を握る (てをにぎる) describes physically clasping a
hand without necessarily performing a handshake. Neither is an exact opposite.

## Distinctive meaning

The word identifies a gesture, not a complete statement of the participants'
relationship. A handshake may accompany greeting, thanks, congratulations, or
reconciliation; the surrounding scene explains its purpose.

Dictionary: [JMdict](https://www.edrdg.org/wiki/index.php/JMdict-EDICT_Dictionary_Project), entry 1152730, sense 1.
