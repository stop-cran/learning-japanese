16: # 刃 — blade; cutting edge
17: 
18: **Strokes:** 3 · **Key (radical):** 刀 (no. 18) · **JLPT:** N1 (community list)
19: 
20: ## Meaning and origin
21: 
22: 刃 concerns a blade or cutting edge. The standalone word [刃](../words/刃.md) は names the cutting part of a knife or sword, not automatically the whole tool. The character also appears in words concerning swords.
23: 
24: **Modern-shape mnemonic, not etymology:** Compare [刀](刀.md), "sword; blade," and notice the extra short inner mark, shaped like 丶. Let that mark remind you of the cutting edge. This is a memory aid for the modern shape, not a historical explanation.
25: 
26: ## Readings
27: 
28: | Reading | Type | Typical words |
29: | --- | --- | --- |
30: | ジン | on | 白刃 はくじん — drawn sword; the same word is also read しらは |
31: | ニン | on | 刃傷 にんじょう — injury or bloodshed involving a blade; recognize the whole word |
32: | は | kun | [刃](../words/刃.md) は — blade; cutting edge |
33: | やいば | kun | 刃 やいば — blade; sword; a separate documented word reading |
34: | き.る | kun, source-only here | KANJIDIC2 lists this reading; no independently verified word example is established here |
35: 
36: This small lesson practices **は**. ジン, ニン and やいば remain recognition material here; leaving them out of practice does not make them invalid or uniformly rare. The source-only き.る is not a recommendation to replace the spelling of [切る](../words/切る.md) きる, "cut," with an invented spelling.
37: 
38: ## Common words
39: 
40: - [刃](../words/刃.md) は — blade; cutting edge
41: - 刃物 はもの — edged tool; cutting instrument
42: 
43: ## Notes
44: 
45: - 刀 かたな names a sword; 刃 は can name its cutting part. Learn the whole word and context rather than treating the two characters as freely interchangeable.
46: - [乃](乃.md), with connecting or particle-related uses, [凹](凹.md), "concave; sunken," and [凸](凸.md), "convex; protruding," do not share the blade/cutting-edge meaning.
47: - The same spelling 刃 also has the reading やいば. Its dictionary senses are not all automatically senses of the noun は.

11: # 刃 (は) — blade; cutting edge
12: 
13: ## Meaning
14: 
15: 刃 is a noun naming the blade or cutting edge of a knife, sword or similar tool. This article teaches the reading **は** and the physical cutting-part sense.
16: 
17: - この包丁の刃は鋭いです。 (このほうちょうのははするどいです。) — This kitchen knife's blade is sharp.
18: - 写真では、刃がよく見えます。 (しゃしんでは、はがよくみえます。) — The blade is clearly visible in the photograph.
19: 
20: In 包丁の刃 (ほうちょうのは), "the blade of a kitchen knife," の connects the tool with its cutting part. In 刃が見える (はがみえる), "the blade is visible," が marks what is visible.
21: 
22: ## How the kanji combine
23: 
24: [刃](../kanji/刃.md) represents the whole native noun は here. There is no compound to split. The same written character also has the separate word reading やいば, "blade; sword"; that does not make every sense under やいば a sense of は.
25: 
26: ## Synonyms and antonyms
27: 
28: [ナイフ](ナイフ.md), "knife," and [包丁](包丁.md) ほうちょう, "kitchen knife," name whole tools. 刃 は focuses on the cutting part. Likewise, [刀](刀.md) かたな and [日本刀](日本刀.md) にほんとう name swords rather than just their cutting edges.
29: 
30: [端](端.md) はし, "edge; end; tip," and [縁](縁.md) ふち, "rim; edge," describe boundaries more broadly. Not every edge is a cutting edge. There is no single lexical antonym for 刃.
31: 
32: ## Distinctive meaning
33: 
34: English "edge" can mean the edge of a desk or the boundary of an area. 刃 は does not generally replace 端 or 縁 for those spatial meanings. Learn it with a cutting tool and keep the distinction between the tool and its blade.
35: 
36: For figurative English "cutting edge" meaning the forefront of a field, use words such as [先端](先端.md) せんたん, not 刃 は.
37: 
38: Dictionary: [JMdict](https://www.edrdg.org/wiki/index.php/JMdict-EDICT_Dictionary_Project), entry 1369820, sense 1; separate やいば family 1369830. Physical は sense: [Digital Daijisen and Seisenban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, 刃](https://kotobank.jp/word/刃-536578).

11: 
12: # 先端 (せんたん) - tip; leading edge
13: 
14: ## Meaning
15: 
16: 先端 is the tip or far end of something, especially a long or pointed object.
17: It also means the forefront or leading edge of a development, field or trend.
18: 
19: - 棒の先端に印を付けました。 (ぼうのせんたんにしるしをつけました。) - I put a mark on the tip of the stick.
20: - この研究所は技術の先端を行っています。 (このけんきゅうじょはぎじゅつのせんたんをいっています。) - This research institute is at the forefront of technology.
21: 
22: Nの先端 identifies what has a tip or forefront.
23: In the second example, 先端を行く (せんたんをいく) is figurative;
24: the institute is not walking to the end of a stick.
25: 
26: ## How the kanji combine
27: 
28: [先](../kanji/先.md) contributes the foremost point, and 端 an end or extremity.
29: The related meanings reinforce the tip or leading part.
30: The on readings are セン + タン, not the standalone native readings さき + はし.
31: 尖端 is a rare dictionary spelling for the same word.
32: 
33: ## Synonyms and antonyms
34: 
35: [端](端.md) (はし) more broadly names an edge or end;
36: [先](先.md) (さき) also has tip/end meanings besides time and sequence uses.
37: 最先端 (さいせんたん) emphasizes the very forefront or cutting edge.
38: [頂上](頂上.md) (ちょうじょう) names a top or summit, not every projecting tip.
39: 
40: ## Distinctive meaning
41: 
42: A 先端 need not be needle-sharp: a stick or rod can have a blunt end.
43: The figurative use concerns being out in front, not simply the main point of an argument.
44: Nor does calling technology 先端 establish that it is safe, effective or better
45: for every purpose; the word identifies a leading position.
46: 
47: Dictionary: [JMdict](https://www.edrdg.org/wiki/index.php/JMdict-EDICT_Dictionary_Project), entry 1388110, senses 1-2 and spelling flag.
48: Usage: [Digital Daijisen: 先端](https://www.weblio.jp/content/先端).
