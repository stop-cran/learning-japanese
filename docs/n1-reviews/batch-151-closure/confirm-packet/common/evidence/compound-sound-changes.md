# Sound changes in Japanese compounds

Why is 花火（はなび）not はなひ, or 学校（がっこう）not がくこう?
When elements combine in a word, their sounds can differ from familiar readings of the
parts. The patterns below help explain **attested readings**, not predict every new
compound. Learn each word with its reading.

## Rendaku: voicing a later element

In **rendaku**（連濁・れんだく, sequential voicing）, an initial consonant of a
later element becomes voiced in some compounds. For example:

| Parts | Compound | Change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 花 はな (flower) + 火 ひ (fire) | [花火](../words/花火.md) はなび — fireworks | ひ → び |
| 井 い (well) + 戸 と (door) | [井戸](../words/井戸.md) いど — well | と → ど |

Dakuten (゛) marks the voiced kana here. In 花火, the h-row sound becomes /b/,
not /p/. Rendaku is common in native Japanese compounds, but it does not happen
automatically: for example, 草花（くさばな, flowering plants）has ば, while
草木（くさき, plants and trees）keeps き. Do not decide a reading from the kanji
alone.

花火を見ました。 (はなびをみました。) — I watched the fireworks.

## Small っ: gemination in a compound

**Gemination** is a lengthening of a consonant. In these examples, small っ
marks the extra time before the following consonant; it is not pronounced as a
full つ. This sound is called **sokuon**（促音・そくおん）. It counts as one
mora, a unit of Japanese rhythm; its actual sound
depends on the following consonant:

| Familiar readings of the parts | Compound |
| --- | --- |
| 学 ガク + 校 コウ | [学校](../words/学校.md) がっこう — school |
| 傑 ケツ + 作 サク | [傑作](../words/傑作.md) けっさく — masterpiece |

As a reading aid, compare がく + こう with がっこう: the first part ends in
small っ, and the /k/ is held longer. Similarly, けつ + さく corresponds to
けっさく, with a longer /s/. This kind of small-っ change is called
**sokuonization**（促音化・そくおんか）. The breakdown compares familiar
readings with the word's modern reading; it is not necessarily a historical
account of how the word was formed.

学校に行きます。 (がっこうにいきます。) — I go to school.

Not every compound containing 学 changes ガク to がっ: [学生](../words/学生.md)（がくせい,
student）keeps がく. Check the actual word rather than applying a universal rule.

## Small っ plus /p/

In [失敗](../words/失敗.md)（しっぱい, failure; mistake）, compare the familiar
on-readings 失 シツ and 敗 ハイ with しっぱい: the first part corresponds to
しっ and the second begins with ぱ. The small っ gives a longer /p/ sound.
The h-row to p-row change is also called **handakuon-ka**
（半濁音化・はんだくおんか）.

Although the circle in ぱ is called **handakuten**（半濁点・はんだくてん）,
/p/ is voiceless, not "half-voiced." This /p/ change is **not rendaku**:
compare 花火's ひ → び (/b/) with 失敗's ハイ → ぱい (/p/).
Both the small っ and the /p/ are part of the established reading, not
instructions to make a new word.

料理に失敗しました。 (りょうりにしっぱいしました。) — My attempt at cooking went wrong.

## Similar sounds, different tasks

- **Verb forms:** [待つ](../words/待つ.md)（まつ, wait）→ 待って（まって）is a verb's て-form,
  not a compound reading. This small-っ pattern is traditionally called
  促音便（そくおんびん）; terminology can overlap with the broader term
  促音化. See [verb forms](japanese-verb-foundations.md#the-て-form-and-the-plain-past-share-sound-patterns).
- **Counters:** 一本（いっぽん, one long object）also has っ and /p/, but
  number-and-counter combinations have their own readings and sometimes
  alternatives. For example, 八本 can be はっぽん or はちほん. Learn the
  complete expression in [numbers and counters](numbers-and-counters.md).
- **Sound-symbolic words:** a small っ or voiced kana in onomatopoeia is not
  automatically a compound change. See [onomatopoeia sound patterns](onomatopoeia-sound-patterns.md).
