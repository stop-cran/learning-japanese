**Reassessment of the direct word guards on 桃 and 白桃:**
- **桃 ("peach tree; peach") excludes ピンク ("pink"): justified.** Digital Daijisen もも sense 2 says 「桃色」の略 (an abbreviation of 桃色, "pink"), and words/桃.md teaches this. A learner could reasonably accept "pink" for 桃. ピンク's own senses (the colour, and the erotic sense) add no reverse conflict.
- **桃 and 白桃 exclude 実 ("fruit; substance"): justified.** 実 sense 1 means fruit or nut, and both 桃 and 白桃 name fruit. A learner could accept "fruit" for either. 実's own guards (果物, 内容) are separate pairs, and exclusions are not transitive.
- **白桃 does not exclude ピンク: correct.** Neither JMdict 1475340 nor Seisenban gives 白桃 a pink or colour-abbreviation sense, so "pink" is not a defensible answer for 白桃.
- **The remaining hypernym and verb guards (木, 植物, 植える, 果物, 果実) and 白桃–桃 are unchanged and remain defensible.** 白桃 is a kind of 桃, and these broader titles could be accepted as answers. Extra guards only shrink the pool of choices; they never make a quiz answer wrong.

- **What the text says:** "Learn the whole reading はくとう for the fruit word here. Dictionaries also record 白桃 read しろもも for an ornamental white-flowered peach, distinguished from the fruit cultivar, and for a white-fruited yamamomo cultivar."
- **Evidence** (Seisenban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, from my original live fetch, not a new one):
  - Under the はくとう heading, sense ① is 花色の白い桃。しろもも (a white-flowered peach) and sense ② is the cultivar.
  - Under the しろもも heading, sense ① is an ornamental white-flowered variety, explicitly separate from the fruit-tree cultivar はくとう, and sense ② is a yamamomo (bayberry) variety with white fruit.
- **Assessment:**
  - The wording never says only しろもも can mean the white-flowered peach. It deliberately limits はくとう to "the fruit word here" and says the other uses are "also" recorded.
  - Everything the passage states about しろもも matches the dictionary.
  - What's missing is that はくとう sense ① also names a white-flowered peach, mainly as a spring season word (季語) in haiku. A reader could infer the flower sense belongs only to しろもも, but this has little practical effect for learners.
  - This is a scope choice, not a false distinction.
- **Resolution: still optional.** It is not actionable, and I'm downgrading it from "implies exclusivity" to "a minor omission within a deliberately scoped passage."
