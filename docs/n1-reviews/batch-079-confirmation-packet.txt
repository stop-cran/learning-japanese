11: # 王冠 (おうかん) — royal crown; bottle cap
12: 
13: ## Meaning
14: 
15: 王冠 is a royal crown, worn as a sign of royal status. It can also name a crown awarded as an honor.
16: A separate everyday-object sense is a crown-shaped metal bottle cap, such as the closure on a beer bottle.
17: 
18: - 王は王冠をかぶっています。 (おうはおうかんをかぶっています。) — The king is wearing a crown.
19: - 瓶の王冠を外しました。 (びんのおうかんをはずしました。) — I removed the bottle's crown cap.
20: 
21: The second sentence concerns a bottle closure, not royal headgear. Use the context to choose the English meaning.
22: 
23: ## How the kanji combine
24: 
25: [王](../kanji/王.md) means "king; ruler," and [冠](../kanji/冠.md) means "crown."
26: This is a modifier + head compound: a ruler's crown. The on readings are オウ + カン.
27: The bottle-cap sense extends the name to an object shaped like a crown; it is not a miniature item of royal clothing.
28: 
29: ## Synonyms and antonyms
30: 
31: [冠](冠.md) (かんむり) is the broader crown or ceremonial-headgear word.
32: [帽子](帽子.md) (ぼうし) means a hat or cap, not specifically a royal crown.
33: For containers, [蓋](蓋.md) (ふた) is a broader lid or cap word, while [栓](栓.md) (せん) can mean a stopper or plug.
34: These words overlap for some closures, but are not interchangeable for every object. There is no useful single opposite noun.
35: 
36: ## Distinctive meaning
37: 
38: 王冠 does not name every lid or screw cap: the bottle sense concerns the crown-type metal closure.
39: For the royal object, use かぶる for putting it on and かぶっている for wearing it.
40: Do not transfer the separate 冠たる (かんたる), "peerless," use of 冠 to 王冠.
41: 
42: Dictionary: [JMdict](https://www.edrdg.org/wiki/index.php/JMdict-EDICT_Dictionary_Project), entry 1181450, senses 1-2.
43: Usage and bottle-cap sense: [Digital Daijisen: 王冠](https://www.weblio.jp/content/王冠), おうかん general-word entry.

12: # 冠 (かんむり) - crown; ceremonial cap
13: 
14: ## Meaning
15: 
16: 冠, read かんむり here, names a crown or diadem, and also traditional ceremonial headgear worn by courtiers or Shinto clergy. It does not mean every everyday hat.
17: 
18: - 王様は金の冠をかぶっていました。
19:   (おうさまはきんのかんむりをかぶっていました。) - The king was wearing a gold crown.
20: 
21: In kanji terminology, かんむり is a component at the top of a character. For example, 宀 is called うかんむり; this use is not a physical item of clothing.
22: 
23: ## How the kanji combine
24: 
25: This is one [kanji](../kanji/冠.md) with the native reading かんむり. Learn the whole pronunciation rather than dividing it into separate sound values. The same written character also has other readings with different uses.
26: 
27: ## Synonyms and antonyms
28: 
29: [帽子](帽子.md) (ぼうし) is the broader everyday hat or cap word. 冠 can also refer to a name or title attached to a program or event, including named sponsorship; [名前](名前.md) (なまえ) is not restricted to that use.
30: 
31: ## Distinctive meaning
32: 
33: The source list's English "first; best" belongs to the separate reading **かん**, not かんむり. Recognize 冠たる (かんたる), "foremost; peerless," as in 世界に冠たる技術 (せかいにかんたるぎじゅつ), "world-leading technology." This is supplementary reading-specific teaching, not a new meaning pronounced かんむり.
34: 
35: Dictionary: [JMdict](https://www.edrdg.org/wiki/index.php/JMdict-EDICT_Dictionary_Project), entry 1577620, senses 1-3 and 6; supplementary sense 5 is restricted to かん.
36: Usage: [Digital Daijisen, 冠](https://www.weblio.jp/content/冠), separate かん and かんむり entries.
