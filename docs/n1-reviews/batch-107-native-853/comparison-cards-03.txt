16: # 猿 — monkey
17:
18: **Strokes:** 13 · **Key (radical):** 犬 (no. 94; visible as 犭 on the left) · **JLPT:** N1 (community list)
19:
20: ## Meaning and origin
21:
22: 猿 writes the native animal noun **さる**, "monkey." Depending on context, the Japanese word can also cover apes and other non-human primates; do not assume it always matches a strict English monkey-versus-ape division.
23:
24: **Modern-shape mnemonic:** the animal radical 犭 sits beside 袁 (えん), the detailed shape also inside [園](園.md), "park; garden." Picture a monkey beside a garden. This is a memory aid, not a historical derivation or a rule that every character with 犭 names an animal.

32:

46:

48:

50:
51: [園](園.md) encloses the shared right-hand shape rather than putting 犭 beside it. [鳥](鳥.md), "bird," is a different animal category; [風](風.md), "wind; style," is not an animal.

17:

19:
20: ## Meaning and origin
21:
22: [畝](../words/畝.md) うね is a **raised row of earth for planting crops**. The word also describes ribs or ridges in fabric and other surfaces. Do not confuse the raised planting strip with the lower furrow between strips.
23:
24: The same character also writes land-area units. Japanese 畝 せ and the Chinese measure called 畝 ほ in Japanese are different units, not alternative pronunciations with one fixed conversion.
25:
26: **Modern-shape mnemonic, not etymology:** Notice the capped 田-shaped part on the left beside a [久](久.md)-shaped part on the right. Imagine a long raised row beside a field. This is a memory aid, not a claim about the character's history.

19:
20: ## Meaning and origin
21:
22: 繭 means **cocoon**: a protective covering made by an insect larva, especially a silkworm. The covering and the developing insect inside it are different things.
23:
24: **Modern-shape mnemonic, not etymology:** Under the grass-shaped top, find [糸](糸.md), thread, and [虫](虫.md), insect, inside a divided enclosure. 27:
28: | Reading | Type | Typical words |
29: | --- | --- | --- |
30: | ケン | on | 繭糸 けんし - thread from cocoons; a silk-production term, included for recognition rather than quiz practice |

32: | きぬ | kun | source-only character reading retained from KANJIDIC2; not selected for questions; see the qualification below |

16: # 蚊 — mosquito
17:
18: **Strokes:** 10 · **Key (radical):** 虫 (no. 142), at the left · **JLPT:** N1 (community list)
19:
20: ## Meaning and origin
21:
22: 蚊 means **mosquito**, not an insect in general. The ordinary animal noun is a short **か**.
23:
24: **Modern-shape mnemonic, not etymology:** Find [虫](虫.md), insect, beside [文](文.md). Imagine a mosquito landing on a written page. This is a visual memory aid, not a claim about the character's historical formation or the origin of its pronunciation.
25:
26: ## Readings
27:
28: | Reading | Type | Typical words |
29: | --- | --- | --- |
30: | ブン | on | 飛蚊症 ひぶんしょう - seeing floaters in one's vision; a medical term, retained for recognition rather than quiz practice |
31: | か | kun | [蚊](../words/蚊.md) か - mosquito |
32:
33: Use **か** for the standalone noun. **ブン** occurs in compounds; Digital Daijisen explicitly lists 飛蚊症 under that character reading. The compound names a visual symptom, not an actual mosquito flying inside the eye. This card practices the animal noun without requiring a separate medical support article.
34:
35: ## Common words
36:
37: - [蚊](../words/蚊.md) か - mosquito
38: - 蚊帳 かや - mosquito net
39: - 蚊取り線香 かとりせんこう - mosquito coil
40: - 蚊に刺される かにさされる - be bitten by a mosquito
41:
42: ## Notes
43:
44: - The kana spelling **カ** also names the insect. It does not make the pronunciation long: ordinary modern か is not かあ.
45: - In 蚊に刺される, [刺す](../words/刺す.md) さす describes the biting or stinging action; 蚊 names its actor. The noun does not mean a bite, a net or a repellent.
46: - [虫](虫.md), insect/bug, is a broader category and can describe a mosquito. It is therefore not a good opposing meaning-quiz answer here.
47: - Compare [文](文.md), sentence/writing, with no left-hand 虫. [枚](枚.md), the counter for thin flat objects, has 木 at the left; [改](改.md), revise/reform, has 己. Those two characters share a four-stroke right-hand component with a slanting top stroke, unlike the dot-and-horizontal top of 文 in 蚊.
