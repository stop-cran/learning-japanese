11: 
12: # 活力 (かつりょく) - vitality; energy
13: 
14: ## Meaning
15: 
16: 活力 is vitality, energy, or dynamism: the power to act vigorously. It is a noun and can describe a person, an organization, or a community.
17: 
18: - 十分な睡眠で活力を取り戻しました。 (じゅうぶんなすいみんでかつりょくをとりもどしました。) - I regained my energy through sufficient sleep.
19: - 新しい店が町に活力を与えています。 (あたらしいみせがまちにかつりょくをあたえています。) - New shops are bringing vitality to the town.
20: 
21: Useful patterns are 活力がある, have vitality, 活力を取り戻す, regain vitality, and 活力にあふれる, be full of energy.
22: 
23: ## How the kanji combine
24: 
25: [活](../kanji/活.md) contributes activity or life, and [力](../kanji/力.md) strength or power. The compound names the power behind lively activity. The on readings are カツ + リョク.
26: 
27: ## Synonyms and antonyms
28: 
29: [活気](活気.md) (かっき) also describes liveliness or vigor, often apparent in a place or group. [元気](元気.md) (げんき) overlaps in a person's energy or well-being. [体力](体力.md) (たいりょく) emphasizes physical strength or stamina in its ordinary bodily use. These are tendencies, not mutually exclusive kinds of energy.
30: 
31: ## Distinctive meaning
32: 
33: [エネルギー](エネルギー.md) can also mean energy in a technical physical sense. 活力 instead focuses on vitality or dynamism; it is not the general name for a quantity measured in joules. Compare [活躍](活躍.md) (かつやく), energetic activity or an active contribution.
34: 
35: Dictionary: [JMdict](https://www.edrdg.org/wiki/index.php/JMdict-EDICT_Dictionary_Project), entry 1208480, sense 1.

12: 
13: # ばりばり — ripping or crunching; working vigorously
14: 
15: ## Meaning
16: 
17: Forceful ripping or crunching sounds, or energetic, full-on activity.
18: 
19: - 厚（あつ）い紙をばりばり破（やぶ）いた。 — I tore the thick paper with a loud ripping sound.
20: - 兄（あに）は新しい職場（しょくば）でばりばり働（はたら）いている。 — My older brother is working energetically at his new workplace.
21: 
22: ## Synonyms and antonyms
23: 
24: [びりびり](びりびり.md) overlaps in ripping; [ぱりぱり](ぱりぱり.md) in crisp crunching. [もりもり](もりもり.md) can also suggest vigorous activity.
25: 
26: ## Distinctive meaning
27: 
28: ばりばり働く does not require noise. The word also describes stiffness or starchiness, and continuous loud sound. Do not turn the common heavier impression relative to ぱりぱり into an exact loudness rule.
29: 
30: See [Japanese onomatopoeia](../articles/japanese-onomatopoeia.md) and [motion and sound](../articles/onomatopoeia-motion-and-sound.md).

14: # 山 — mountain
15: 
16: **Strokes:** 3 · **Key (radical):** 山 (no. 46) · **JLPT:** N5 (community list)
17: 
18: ## Meaning and origin
19: 
20: 山 is a pictograph of a mountain with peaks, the middle one taller. As a key it appears in kanji about mountains and terrain:
21: 岩, 岸, 峠, 島, 崖.
22: 
23: Don't confuse it with 出 ([出](出.md)): 出 looks like two 山 stacked, but its origin is a different picture (a foot leaving a pit) and
24: the character is not formed from 山.
25: 
26: ## Readings
27: 
28: | Reading | Type | Typical words |
29: | --- | --- | --- |
30: | サン | on | 富士山 ふじさん, [火山](../words/火山.md) かざん, [山水](../words/山水.md) さんすい |
31: | ザン | on (voiced) | 登山 とざん |
32: | セン | on | rare; not needed at N5 |
33: | やま | kun | 山 やま, 山道 やまみち, 山登り やまのぼり |
34: 
35: ## Common words
36: 
37: - [火山](../words/火山.md) かざん — volcano
38: - [山水](../words/山水.md) さんすい — landscape
39: - 山 やま — mountain (also a pile, or the crux of a matter, in idioms like 山場 やまば)
40: 
41: ## Notes
42: 
43: 山 and 川 ([川](川.md)) often go together as "mountains and rivers" for the natural landscape: 山川 やまかわ／さんせん (literary).

15: 
16: # 刀 — sword; blade
17: 
18: **Strokes:** 2 · **Key (radical):** 刀 (no. 18) · **JLPT:** N1 (community list)
19: 
20: ## Meaning and origin
21: 
22: 刀 concerns swords and blades. The standalone word [刀](../words/刀.md) かたな means a sword, especially a Japanese single-edged sword. The character's wider cutting-tool senses do not make かたな a general word for every knife.
23: 
24: **Modern-shape mnemonic, not etymology:** Picture a blade inside the bent, hooked outline, with the separate sweeping stroke starting inside it. This is a memory aid for the modern shape, not a reconstruction of its history.
25: 
26: ## Readings
27: 
28: | Reading | Type | Typical words |
29: | --- | --- | --- |
30: | トウ | on | [日本刀](../words/日本刀.md) にほんとう — Japanese sword; also read にっぽんとう |
31: | かたな | kun | [刀](../words/刀.md) かたな — sword; katana |
32: | そり | kun, source-only here | KANJIDIC2 lists this reading; no independently allocated word example is established here |
33: 
34: [剃刀](../words/剃刀.md) かみそり means "razor," usually written in kana. It has a special whole-word reading: the kana ending そり is not by itself evidence that 刀 independently supplies that reading. Keep the source reading そり for lookup, not as a new standalone word for "razor."
35: 
36: ## Common words
37: 
38: - [刀](../words/刀.md) かたな — sword; katana
39: - [日本刀](../words/日本刀.md) にほんとう — Japanese sword
40: - [剃刀](../words/剃刀.md) かみそり — razor; recognize the kanji spelling, but the word is usually written in kana
41: 
42: ## Notes
43: 
44: - Compare [力](力.md), "power." In 力 the slanting stroke crosses the upper stroke; in 刀 it starts inside the hooked outline. Both have two strokes.
45: - [乃](乃.md), with connecting or particle-related uses, [凹](凹.md), "concave; sunken," and [凸](凸.md), "convex; protruding," do not share 刀's sword/blade meaning.
46: - Learn each whole word with its reading. The とう-only scalpel and engraving-tool senses in the standalone 刀 dictionary family do not automatically belong to かたな.
