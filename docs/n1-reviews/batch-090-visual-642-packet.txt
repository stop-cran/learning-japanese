# 刀 — sword; blade

**Strokes:** 2 · **Key (radical):** 刀 (no. 18) · **JLPT:** N1 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

刀 concerns swords and blades. The standalone word [刀](../words/刀.md) かたな means a sword, especially a Japanese single-edged sword. The character's wider cutting-tool senses do not make かたな a general word for every knife.

**Modern-shape mnemonic, not etymology:** Picture a blade inside the bent, hooked outline, with the separate sweeping stroke starting inside it. This is a memory aid for the modern shape, not a reconstruction of its history.

## Readings

| Reading | Type | Typical words |
| --- | --- | --- |
| トウ | on | [日本刀](../words/日本刀.md) にほんとう — Japanese sword; also read にっぽんとう |
| かたな | kun | [刀](../words/刀.md) かたな — sword; katana |
| そり | kun, source-only here | KANJIDIC2 lists this reading; no independently allocated word example is established here |

[剃刀](../words/剃刀.md) かみそり means "razor," usually written in kana. It has a special whole-word reading: the kana ending そり is not by itself evidence that 刀 independently supplies that reading. Keep the source reading そり for lookup, not as a new standalone word for "razor."

## Common words

- [刀](../words/刀.md) かたな — sword; katana
- [日本刀](../words/日本刀.md) にほんとう — Japanese sword
- [剃刀](../words/剃刀.md) かみそり — razor; recognize the kanji spelling, but the word is usually written in kana

## Notes

- Compare [力](力.md), "power." In 力 the slanting stroke crosses the upper stroke; in 刀 it starts inside the hooked outline. Both have two strokes.
- [乃](乃.md), with connecting or particle-related uses, [凹](凹.md), "concave; sunken," and [凸](凸.md), "convex; protruding," do not share 刀's sword/blade meaning.
- Learn each whole word with its reading. The とう-only scalpel and engraving-tool senses in the standalone 刀 dictionary family do not automatically belong to かたな.

# 日本刀 (にほんとう) — Japanese sword

## Meaning

日本刀 is a Japanese sword made in the traditional Japanese manner. It is a noun naming the object, not an action. The dictionary also records the reading にっぽんとう.

- 博物館で古い日本刀を見ました。 (はくぶつかんでふるいにほんとうをみました。) — I saw an old Japanese sword at the museum.
- これは日本刀の写真です。 (これはにほんとうのしゃしんです。) — This is a photograph of a Japanese sword.

In 日本刀を見る (にほんとうをみる), "look at a Japanese sword," を marks the object. 日本刀の写真 uses の to connect the sword with its photograph.

## How the kanji combine

[日](../kanji/日.md) and [本](../kanji/本.md) together spell the country name 日本 (にほん／にっぽん), "Japan." Here 日本 modifies [刀](../kanji/刀.md), read トウ and meaning "sword": a Japanese sword. Treat 日本 as the country name, not as a new literal "sun + book" definition.

The whole compound is にほんとう or にっぽんとう. The two readings name the same category; learn them as documented forms, not as a general rule for changing sounds in other compounds.

## Synonyms and antonyms

[刀](刀.md) かたな, "sword; katana," overlaps with 日本刀. A katana is a familiar example of a Japanese sword, but 日本刀 is not limited to one narrowly defined historical subtype.

[ナイフ](ナイフ.md), "knife," [包丁](包丁.md) ほうちょう, "kitchen knife," and [剃刀](剃刀.md) かみそり, "razor," name different kinds of cutting tools. They are not general substitutes for 日本刀. There is no natural lexical antonym.

## Distinctive meaning

日本刀 identifies a Japanese sword tradition, not simply any knife made in Japan. The category is often associated with a curved, single-edged sword, but do not turn that familiar image into a rule that every 日本刀 must have exactly the same shape.
