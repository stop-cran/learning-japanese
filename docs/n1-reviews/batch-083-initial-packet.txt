16: # 凌 — endure; surpass
17: 
18: **Strokes:** 10 · **Key (radical):** 冫 (the two left-hand strokes, no. 15) · **JLPT:** N1 (community list)
19: 
20: ## Meaning and origin
21: 
22: 凌 has two useful branches: **enduring or getting through a difficulty**, and
23: **surpassing something else**. In [凌ぐ](../words/凌ぐ.md) (しのぐ), the
24: context selects between these branches. [凌駕](../words/凌駕.md) (りょうが)
25: concerns surpassing, not sheltering from bad weather.
26: 
27: **Modern-shape mnemonic, not etymology:** imagine the two 冫 marks as drops
28: beside the right-hand 夌 shape, pictured as a barrier. Think of weathering
29: the drops or getting beyond the barrier. This is an invented memory scene,
30: not a historical derivation or a verified explanation of the sound.

10: 
11: # 凌ぐ (しのぐ) — endure; surpass
12: 
13: ## Meaning
14: 
15: 凌ぐ can mean **endure, keep out, or get through** something difficult.
16: It can also mean **surpass or outdo** someone or something in ability,
17: degree or quality. Learn the whole phrase rather than assigning one
18: English equivalent to every occurrence.
19: 
20: - 風雨を凌ぐ (ふううをしのぐ) — shelter from wind and rain
21: - 前作を凌ぐ傑作 (ぜんさくをしのぐけっさく) — a masterpiece surpassing the previous work
22: 
23: These short phrases illustrate the two branches. The difficulty faced or
24: the thing surpassed takes を. 凌ぐ is a transitive godan verb ending in
25: ぐ: しのぎます, しのいで, しのいだ, しのがない.
26: 
27: ## How the kanji combine
28: 
29: [凌](../kanji/凌.md) supplies the enduring or surpassing idea. The native
30: verb is しのぐ: ぐ is written as okurigana, while 凌 represents しの-.
31: The same word, not a different reading, has both meaning branches.
32: 
33: ## Synonyms and antonyms
34: 
35: [こらえる](こらえる.md) and [我慢](我慢.md) (がまん), with する, overlap in
36: endurance. Their restraint uses do not all transfer to 凌ぐ.
37: [凌駕](凌駕.md) (りょうが), with する, overlaps with the surpassing branch,
38: not with sheltering from the weather.
39: 
40: 忍ぶ (しのぶ) is a separate verb that can also mean endure.
41: Its ending is ぶ, not 凌ぐ's ぐ: do not confuse しのいで with しのんで.
42: 
43: ## Distinctive meaning
44: 
45: Getting through a difficulty does not by itself promise a permanent
46: solution. Conversely, 凌ぐ is not restricted to brief emergencies.
47: In a comparison, it means going above the other thing, not merely
48: matching it. The weather phrase above expresses protection or endurance;
49: the masterpiece phrase expresses comparative quality.
50:

34: 通る (とおる) can describe going along or through a route.
35: 過ぎる draws attention to being beyond a reference point.
36: 過ごす (すごす), "spend time," instead takes the time spent with を: [休日](休日.md)を過ごす (きゅうじつをすごす), "spend a day off."
37: 
38: ## Distinctive meaning
39: 
40: A related excessive-use suffix is usually written すぎる in kana.
41: Attach it to a verb's ます-stem: 食べます → 食べすぎる (たべすぎる), "eat too much."
42: For an ordinary い-adjective, remove い: 高い → 高すぎる (たかすぎる), "too high/expensive."
43: This suffix use is distinct from passing a station.
44:

+# 凌ぐ (しのぐ) — endure; surpass
+
+## Meaning
+
+凌ぐ can mean **endure, keep out, or get through** something difficult.
+It can also mean **surpass or outdo** someone or something in ability,
+degree or quality. Learn the whole phrase rather than assigning one
+English equivalent to every occurrence.
+
+- 風雨を凌ぐ (ふううをしのぐ) — shelter from wind and rain
+- 前作を凌ぐ傑作 (ぜんさくをしのぐけっさく) — a masterpiece surpassing the previous work
+
+These short phrases illustrate the two branches. The difficulty faced or
+the thing surpassed takes を. 凌ぐ is a transitive godan verb ending in
+ぐ: しのぎます, しのいで, しのいだ, しのがない.
+
+## How the kanji combine
+
+[凌](../kanji/凌.md) supplies the enduring or surpassing idea. The native
+verb is しのぐ: ぐ is written as okurigana, while 凌 represents しの-.
+The same word, not a different reading, has both meaning branches.
+
+## Synonyms and antonyms
+
+[こらえる](こらえる.md) and [我慢](我慢.md) (がまん), with する, overlap in
+endurance. Their restraint uses do not all transfer to 凌ぐ.
+[凌駕](凌駕.md) (りょうが), with する, overlaps with the surpassing branch,
+not with sheltering from the weather.
+
+忍ぶ (しのぶ) is a separate verb that can also mean endure.
+Its ending is ぶ, not 凌ぐ's ぐ: do not confuse しのいで with しのんで.
+
+## Distinctive meaning
+
+Getting through a difficulty does not by itself promise a permanent
+solution. Conversely, 凌ぐ is not restricted to brief emergencies.
+In a comparison, it means going above the other thing, not merely
+matching it. The weather phrase above expresses protection or endurance;
+the masterpiece phrase expresses comparative quality.
+

+Usage and short example phrases: [Digital Daijisen: 凌ぐ](https://kotobank.jp/word/凌ぐ-522598), senses 2 and 4.

+
+# 凌駕 (りょうが) — surpass; outdo
+
+## Meaning
+
+凌駕 names surpassing or outdoing another thing. It is a noun and a
+する-verb: 凌駕する, 凌駕します, 凌駕した, 凌駕しない.
+The thing surpassed takes を.
+
+- 前作を凌駕する売れ行き (ぜんさくをりょうがするうれゆき) — sales surpassing those of the previous work
+
+Here the comparison concerns sales, not a claim that the new work is
+artistically better. The context determines the dimension being compared.
+
+## How the kanji combine
+
+[凌](../kanji/凌.md) contributes surpassing, read リョウ here.
+駕 (ガ) also has a rising-above-others sense, documented with this
+compound. The two reinforce the surpassing idea; do not force 駕's
+separate vehicle meaning into a literal story about this word.
+
+## Synonyms and antonyms
+
+[凌ぐ](凌ぐ.md) (しのぐ) overlaps in surpassing, but also has endurance and
+weather-protection uses that 凌駕 does not share.
+[優れる](優れる.md) (すぐれる) describes excellence or superiority; it is
+an intransitive verb, so its grammar is not 凌駕する's object + を pattern.
+
+上回る (うわまわる), "exceed; surpass," also suits numerical comparisons.
+These related words are not unrestricted substitutes in every phrase.
+
+## Distinctive meaning
+
+凌駕する means going above the comparison target, not simply reaching
+the same level. Identify what is surpassed and in what respect.
+It is not a general expression for surviving a hardship.
+
+陵駕 (りょうが) is also recognized, but JMdict marks that spelling as
+rarely used. This does not make the pronunciation りょうが rare or make
+凌 and 陵 freely interchangeable elsewhere.
+

# Writing and reviewing Japanese-learning articles

Follow the [README's audience, scope, and approach](../README.md#approach): standalone English explanations, Japanese examples with English translations, and practical rather than exhaustive coverage.
Reviews are read-only unless the user requests edits. A review request alone does not authorize commits, pushes, or issue creation.

## Review report

For substantive article reviews written in chat or the CLI, use this compact contract:

```text
Snapshot: <commit; identify any uncommitted changes included>
Scope: <articles/sections reviewed; reviewers and actual models if used>
Verdicts: <for each article: correctness, scoped coverage, comprehensibility>
Findings: <severity; facet; file:lines; claim and impact; remedy; evidence; confidence>
Optional: <nonblocking expansion or polish, or none>
Limits: <unverified claims and review limitations, or none>
```

Assess the three facets separately. Use **CLEAN**, **NEEDS FIX**, or **UNVERIFIED** for each: CLEAN means no actionable defect established within the reviewed scope, not exhaustive correctness. Do not label a consequential unresolved claim CLEAN. No finding quota is required.

Enforcement is self-attended: the reviewing agent checks its report against this contract before responding; the requester adjudicates recommendations. This is not an automated linguistic-correctness gate.

Native PR reviews keep their platform's supported format. Do not assume support for custom report formatting or following external links; see [GitHub's review limitations](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/tutorials/customize-code-review#unsupported-instruction-types). Apply the substantive checks below and disclose unavailable evidence.

## Article checks

- **Correctness:** Check whole constructions, including attachment forms, tense, polarity, register, and translations. An isolated correct example may still leave a necessary rule unexplained.
- **Scoped coverage:** Check prerequisites and distinctions needed to answer the article's stated question. Do not demand every related word, historical development, or grammatical framework.
- **Comprehensibility:** Prefer contrastive examples that actually demonstrate the claimed distinction. Supply selective kana readings for unfamiliar words, ambiguous spellings, and classical forms; explain technical terms when needed.
- **Qualified claims:** Separate strict rules from tendencies, learning shortcuts, and context-dependent translations. Acknowledge meaningful differences between grammatical frameworks instead of treating a classification as universal.
- **Linguistic layers:** Distinguish grammatical function, lexical identity, spelling, pronunciation, contraction, register, historical usage, and present-day naturalness. Another reading of the same kanji does not invalidate a documented reading. Kanji associations are not by themselves etymology or evidence of fixed semantic compartments.
- **Natural examples:** Dictionary recognition of a rare spelling does not establish the idiomaticity of an invented collocation. Use attested rare expressions or clearly qualify the uncertainty; do not invent examples to manufacture a distinction.

## Evidence and severity

For grammar authoring, consult the [source guide](../docs/grammar-authoring-sources.md) and existing batch records before
searching afresh. Use the guide for navigation; keep exact evidence and qualifications in the per-batch source records.

- Prefer reputable dictionaries and established teaching sources for consequential claims. Inspect the actual entry, heading, reading, sense, and register; a search summary, plausible URL, or successful but irrelevant page fetch is not verification.
- Match evidence to the claim: a dictionary spelling list supports recognition, not frequency; a grammatical example supports that use, not an unrestricted substitution rule. Link relevant sources so readers can check the reasoning.
- Treat reviewer findings as hypotheses. Resolve disagreements using the precise claim and evidence, not majority vote, model reputation, or whichever criticism sounds strongest. Preserve unresolved disagreements explicitly.
- Separate defects from optional expansion and editorial preference. Severity reflects demonstrated impact on learning; confidence reflects the strength of evidence. A localized omission can be minor even when the reviewer is highly confident.

## Grammar exercises and companion articles

Use the [exercise/group/vocabulary contract](../docs/content-format.md#grammar-exercises), the
[source guide](../docs/grammar-authoring-sources.md), and the
[reusable review lessons](../REVIEW.md#reusable-grammar-review-lessons) together. Review the complete learner-visible
task, not just the Japanese sentence or intended key:

- Check typed alternatives and fixed choices against the visible operation and register. Conversational contractions
  can retain polite endings; a fitting alternative is not wrong merely because it is not the teaching model.
- Read every completed option and feedback branch against that scenario's actual roles, polarity and event stage.
  Shared templates must not import another example's facts.
- Check lexical boundaries and senses by occurrence. Correct offsets and hashes do not prove that a matched substring
  is a word, or that its gloss fits this prompt.
- Count genuinely different contexts, not item IDs or noun substitutions. Paired forms and interpretations share keys;
  useful repetition is not independent transfer evidence or demonstrated mastery.
- For group/dependency/chapter changes, follow the [shared curriculum contract](../docs/content-format.md#prerequisites-and-roadmap-chapters).
  Review the actual prerequisite operation, incoming/outgoing edges, both leaf and collapsed chapter cycles, partial availability,
  unmapped versus unowned content, stable identities and old-reader compatibility. Keep reasons and exact ownership in the
  [curriculum ledger](../docs/grammar-curriculum-sources.json); chapter placement must not create a whole-chapter lock.

These regroup existing checks, not a new review layer. A clean bounded review needs no manufactured finding or rule.

## Listening exercises

Follow the [listening authoring instructions](instructions/listening-authoring.instructions.md)
and the [practical authoring sequence](../docs/listening-tasks.md#practical-authoring-sequence).
Review the complete heard/seen task, including every spoken alternative and feedback branch,
not only the transcript. Keep owner acceptance, exact media bindings, spending authorization
and actual native/phone evidence distinct. The selected illustration style and role markers
are part of the exercise, not interchangeable decoration.

## Independent review and reconciliation

- When multi-model review is requested, use different model families on the same identified snapshot. Keep initial reviewers independent of one another's findings and the author's prior review rationale. Record the models actually used rather than pinning versions in this guide.
- Cover correctness, scoped coverage, and comprehensibility. A small article normally fits one reviewer per model; a large card batch
  may use bounded facet-specific reviewers to avoid context overload. State each reviewer's files and facet, so no unassigned area is
  accidentally reported as reviewed. Do not require multi-model review for every small edit.
- Keep initial findings blind to other reviewers' reports and the author's rationale. If comparing model contributions, give the models
  a common snapshot, scope, rubric, and source access for at least one shared sample; do not rank models from disjoint facet assignments.
  Deduplicate by underlying defect, not wording. Record verified useful findings unique to each model, shared findings, unsupported
  suggestions, and coverage limitations. This is evidence about the reviewed sample, not a general model benchmark.
  When identifying unique contributions, compare equivalent observations anywhere in a report, including findings, optional suggestions,
  limits, tentative hypotheses, and per-file notes; a different label or severity does not make the same observation a new discovery.
- After authorized fixes, reuse the original reviewers where possible. Give them their original findings, the revised snapshot, and the actual diff.
- Reconcile each finding as **resolved**, **partially resolved**, **unresolved**, **withdrawn**, or **optional**. Reassess severity when the evidence changes, and check changed passages for regressions.
- Stop when no actionable defect or consequential unresolved claim remains in scope. Do not expand the assignment just to prolong the review or force agreement.

## Large card expansions

These are self-attended author/reviewer checks, not an automated linguistic gate. The
[N2 project retrospective](../docs/n2-vocabulary-notes.md#n2-project-retrospective) records the cases behind them;
the [content format](../docs/content-format.md) remains canonical.

- Pin source versions/hashes and the baseline before authoring. Map each source row to the actual article,
  spelling, reading and selected senses; preserve raw fields separately from justified teaching corrections.
  An existing filename or shared dictionary ID is a candidate, not verified coverage.
- Inspect complete applicable dictionary families and actual reused/comparison articles, including restrictions
  and sense-local flags. Audit every new kanji's metadata readings against its reading table or qualified
  source-only explanation, even where the validator does not enforce that level.
- Preserve stable identities, readings, easier sourced levels and original exclusions; document narrow exceptions.
  Judge quiz ambiguity from actual displayed labels and ordinary secondary senses. Exclusions are direct pairs,
  not transitive cliques; different titles or shared topics alone do not decide them.

# Repository structure and composing cards

Format reference: [docs/content-format.md](../../docs/content-format.md). Writing and review rules: [copilot-instructions.md](../copilot-instructions.md).

## Authoring checks

The Android importer, the Python validator, and editorial review enforce different parts of this contract. A successful validation
does not establish linguistic correctness. Read a real card alongside the format reference, but do not copy its factual claims without checking them.

- Front matter uses only single-line scalars and inline lists such as `onyomi: [アン]`. No block lists, nested YAML, multiline values,
  inline comments, escaped quotes, or commas inside list items: the app is not a full YAML parser.
- Keep a source record for each batch: the exact kanji set, source URL/revision or retrieval date, metadata records, and the dictionary
  entries used for word/reading pairs. Inspect the actual entry; a search summary is not verification.
- Follow the [JLPT source policy and exceptions](../../docs/jlpt-levels.md). A missing API level is missing evidence, not a level to guess.
- Include every written kanji in a word's metadata, even when that character has no card yet. Missing cards affect links, not the spelling.
- Link an existing card at the substantive introduction or comparison, not necessarily every repeated mention. A backlink from every
  mentioned kanji is not required; each kanji card should have at least one useful linked word article.
- Follow the [quiz-authoring rule](../../docs/content-format.md#quiz-authoring): inspect answer meanings and account for the app's
  fallback choices, rather than treating different title strings as proof that only one answer is defensible.

## Structure

- `kanji/<char>.md` – one card per kanji; file name = the character (stable ID, never rename).
- `words/<word>.md` – one article per word; file name = the word as written.
- `articles/<slug>.md` – free-form comparison or grammar articles (kebab-case names, no front matter needed).
- `strokes/<char>.json` – generated by `python tools/generate_strokes.py`; never edit by hand.
- `images/<word>.webp` – optional text-free scene picture for a word (`image`/`image_scene` front matter), generated by `python tools/generate_images.py`; at most 100 KiB, one word each.
- `manifest.json` – generated by `python tools/build_manifest.py`; never edit by hand.
- After any change run `python tools/validate.py` (needs `pyyaml`), then `python tools/build_manifest.py`, and commit the manifest with the change.
- When changing validation code, also run `python -m unittest discover -s tools -p "test_*.py"`.

## Kanji card

1. Front matter fields: `kanji`, `title` (short English meaning, unique across cards because it is the quiz answer), `jlpt` (1–5), `tags`
   (`jlpt-nX`, school grade, structure type such as `pictograph`, `indicative`, `compound`, `phonetic-compound`), `strokes`, `radical`,
   `radicalNumber`, optional `phonetic`, `onyomi` (katakana), `kunyomi` (hiragana, `.` before okurigana), `distractors` (2+ existing, easily
   confused kanji, distinct and not the card itself).
2. Take readings, stroke count and radical from KANJIDIC2; do not rely on memory. Nanori are out of scope.
   Every `kunyomi` entry must be explained in `## Readings`: a row giving at least one real word (written form, kana, short gloss) or, for a prefix, suffix or
   bound stem, an example compound and what the part means. Check each word in a dictionary (e.g. 明るむ あかるむ "grow light" is real) and mark rare or
   literary readings as such. Do not drop a reading from the metadata because it is rare. `tools/validate.py` enforces this for N5 and N4 cards.
   Optional `quizOnyomi` / `quizKunyomi` curate exact raw-string subsets separately; omission is legacy full-list behavior and `[]`
   disables that kind. Each selected entry needs its own reading row and linked word example, plus editorial dictionary/allocation/usefulness
   verification. Links alone do not prove readings. See the format contract; older APKs ignore curation and must be upgraded first.
3. Body sections in order: heading `# 字 — title` and a facts line (strokes, key, JLPT, and phonetic only when a verified component is known); `## Meaning and origin`; `## Readings`
   (table: reading, on/kun, typical words with kana); `## Common words`; `## Notes` (look-alikes, differences, special readings).
4. Origin: name the components and the idea of their composition. Hedge with "commonly explained" or "debated" where scholars disagree;
   mnemonic associations are not etymology and must be labelled as mnemonics. Cite a source for substantive historical or phonetic claims.
   When the history is unverified, give an explicitly labelled modern-shape mnemonic instead of inventing an origin. Use
   `structure-unclassified` if the structural type has not been established, and omit the Phonetic item from the facts line (no placeholder);
   absence of a `phonetic` field does not prove that no phonetic component exists.
   If the canonical indexing radical looks different in the character, explain its visible form in the facts line or body
   (for example, 艸 appearing as 艹); do not replace source metadata with the positional glyph.
5. Link kanji and words that have cards with relative `.md` paths; leave others as plain text with kana.
   In app-imported content, relative links must stay within `kanji/`, `words/`, or `articles/`; use an HTTPS link for external source notes.
   Files under `docs/` are contributor documentation, not imported app pages.

## Word article

1. Front matter: `word`, `reading`, `title` (short gloss), `type` (`kango`, `wago`, `jukujikun`, `gairaigo`), `kanji` (every kanji in the word),
   `tags`.
2. Sections: `## Meaning` (with examples, each with English translation), `## How the kanji combine` (relation: opposites, synonyms,
   modifier+head, verb+object, etc.; optional for kana-only words with `kanji: []`), `## Synonyms and antonyms`, `## Distinctive meaning` (register, nuance, when not to use it).
3. For kango, explain what each kanji contributes and the relation between them (e.g. opposite pair 長 + 短).
4. Verify word/reading pairs in JMdict. Use natural or attested examples only; do not invent collocations to prove a point.
   Respect restrictions that tie a reading or sense to particular spellings. A dictionary's inclusion of an item does not establish that it is common.

## Safety

No raw HTML. Links are relative `.md` or `https://`. Do not reuse a title across kanji cards.

### Quiz authoring

The meaning quiz displays a kanji and offers other cards' English titles as answers. For new cards, curate **three** semantically
distinct wrong options for the default four-choice quiz. Two remain structurally valid for older cards, but the app fills missing
choices from other cards, preferring shared tags. It only deduplicates exact title strings: different English synonyms can still
produce two defensible answers. Keep useful semantic comparisons in the body even when they are unsuitable as wrong answers.

Editorial review must check the displayed titles against the target's ordinary meanings, not merely look for duplicate strings.
This behavior was checked in [QuizBuilder](https://github.com/stop-cran/kanji-cards-android/blob/8c130a25bdb4e444a80a2f78ef0d11b04bddc12f/app/src/main/java/io/github/stopcran/kanji/core/quiz/QuizBuilder.kt);
larger quizzes or a changed app can still require fallback choices. The validator checks structure, not semantic ambiguity.

### Curated reading questions

Keep complete sourced `onyomi` / `kunyomi` metadata and its order, including rare readings. Use the optional quiz lists to select
useful question material independently: declare each once with an unquoted, unindented key and an inline list of distinct,
nonempty kana strings exactly as written in the matching full list, including script, dots and dashes. Simple quotes around
values are allowed; quoted keys, duplicates, indentation, block lists, nulls and malformed lists are not.
For example, `quizOnyomi: []` disables on'yomi questions without erasing any dictionary metadata.

Each selected reading must have its own `## Readings` table row (reading in the first column, on/kun in the second),
with a relative link to an existing `words/*.md` example in a later column. The raw reading label may have simple emphasis
or backticks. This new check applies only to explicit quiz selections, not every legacy full reading.
The linked word is structural navigation evidence, **not** proof that this character supplies that reading, that a dictionary
supports the spelling/reading pair, or that the use is common. Check those claims independently against actual dictionary entries
and restrictions; do not infer per-character readings from a substring of the whole word's kana.
Record why rare, name-only or otherwise unsuitable source readings are not selected in the batch's editorial evidence.

The app retains the existing three-distinct-stems-per-kind cap and curated order; readings sharing a stem are still one card.
Excluded formerly started reading keys retain their progress but stop entering normal and extra queues.
All full readings remain protected against wrong-answer use for that kanji, while distractor candidates use selected quiz lists.
This requires a compatible updated APK: old clients ignore the optional fields and still ask full readings.
This transport addition does not authorize or require bulk curation of existing levels.

For kana-only words (`kanji: []`), the `## How the kanji combine` section is optional: omit it unless there is a spelling or
word-formation point worth making. The other three sections remain required, in order.

Vocabulary-aware app versions use an explicit word `jlpt` first. For legacy articles without this field, they retain the previous
fallback based on the hardest known written kanji; that fallback is not a vocabulary-level source. Earlier app versions ignore
word `jlpt` and always use the kanji-based calculation. N4 word study includes N5; N3 word study includes N5 and N4.
Unlike kanji cards, word articles do not require a matching JLPT tag; `tags` remain free study metadata.

The word quiz also uses `title` as the English answer label. The earlier
[candidate builder](https://github.com/stop-cran/kanji-cards-android/blob/8c9713f77083a1ff8b10b5ccb4438b51f0231fec/app/src/main/java/io/github/stopcran/kanji/core/words/Words.kt)
deduplicates exact titles and written forms, not semantic equivalents. Related tags, word type and shared kanji influence candidate
selection. Check glosses against actual meaning and register; different English wording alone does not make two synonyms valid
opposing choices. Do not distort a word's meaning merely to make its title look unique.

Exclusion-aware app versions additionally honor `quiz_exclusions`, for example `quiz_exclusions: [気分]` on 気.
That pair is excluded in both directions even if only one article declares it. Exclusions also apply between other choices
within a question, in both Japanese-to-English and English-to-Japanese quizzes. They are pairwise, not transitive:
excluding A/B and B/C does not automatically exclude A/C. The app never restores an excluded choice just to fill four slots;
a small pool can yield fewer options, as with exact-title deduplication.

Curate these pairs from actual overlapping meanings and register, not merely shared tags or similar spelling. This mechanism
does not discover every synonym or guarantee semantic uniqueness for uncurated pairs. Earlier apps ignore this field.
The validator checks declaration shape and reference integrity; editorial review establishes which pairs belong in the list.

When to add one: a pair needs an exclusion when a learner could reasonably accept either `title` as the answer for either word
(for example あげる/くれる, 開く/開ける, 良い/いい). Titles that are exactly equal need none, because the app already keeps them apart.
Homophones such as 暑い/熱い need none either: the reading quiz asks for the reading of a written word and compares readings, and
the meaning quizzes show different titles. Work through the pairs in [confusable-words.md](confusable-words.md) when you add or
reword a `title`; declare the exclusion on one of the two cards.

# JLPT study levels and sources

The `jlpt` field is a community study label, not an official assignment of an individual kanji, word or grammar item to an examination.
The [official JLPT FAQ](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/faq/index.html#anchor28) explains why vocabulary, kanji, and grammar lists
have not been published for the test revised in 2010. Official competence descriptions and sample questions are not exhaustive lists.
