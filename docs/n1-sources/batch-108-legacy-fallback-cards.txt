# 人 — person

**Strokes:** 2 · **Key (radical):** 人 (no. 9; as a left-hand component 亻, にんべん) · **JLPT:** N5 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

人 is a pictograph of a person seen from the side: a body with an arm or leg leaning forward. Compare [大](大.md), a
frontal figure with arms spread. As 亻 on the left it marks people and human activity: 休 ([休](休.md)), 住, 作, 体, 何, 他.

Because 人 and 入 ([入](入.md)) are nearly mirror images, they are a classic confusion. In 人 the long left-falling stroke is written first from the top and
the right-falling stroke starts from its middle; in 入 the short left-falling stroke comes first and the long right-falling stroke starts at the top, to its upper left.

## Readings

| Reading | Type | Typical words |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ジン | on | [人口](../words/人口.md) じんこう, 日本人 にほんじん, 外国人 がいこくじん |
| ニン | on | 人間 にんげん, 三人 さんにん, 人気 にんき, 職人 しょくにん |
| ひと | kun | 人 ひと, 女の人 おんなのひと, 人々 ひとびと |
| -り | kun | counting one and two persons: 一人 ひとり, 二人 ふたり (special forms) |

Special reading: [大人](../words/大人.md) おとな (adult).

## Common words

- [人口](../words/人口.md) じんこう — population
- [大人](../words/大人.md) おとな — adult
- 人 ひと — person, people

## Notes

人々 ひとびと (people) repeats the kanji with the repetition mark 々 and voices the second ひと.

# 休 — rest

**Strokes:** 6 · **Key (radical):** 人 (as 亻, no. 9) · **Phonetic:** none (an ideographic compound) · **JLPT:** N5 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

休 combines 亻 (person, [人](人.md)) and 木 (tree, [木](木.md)): a person resting against a tree. This is the standard explanation
and a good mnemonic, even though some details of its early history are debated.
It means "rest", "take a break" and "be off work".

## Common words

- [休日](../words/休日.md) きゅうじつ — day off, holiday
- 休憩 きゅうけい — break

## Notes

# 何 — what

**Strokes:** 7 · **Key (radical):** 人 (as 亻, no. 9) · **Phonetic:** 可 (カ) · **JLPT:** N5 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

何 is a phonetic compound: 亻 ([人](人.md), person) and 可 (カ, "can, possible"). In classical Chinese it was an interrogative "what?", "which?",
and the original sense "carry" (later written 荷) is commonly cited for the character. How the "what" use arose from it is not firmly settled, so treat the history as debated.
In Japanese it is the basic question word "what", used also for "how many" before counters.

## Readings

| Reading | Type | Typical words |
| --- | --- | --- |
| カ | on | 幾何 きか (geometry), rare in everyday words |
| なに | kun | 何 なに, [何か](../words/何か.md) なにか, 何も なにも, 何色 なにいろ, 何人 なにじん (what nationality) |
| なん | kun | [何時](../words/何時.md) なんじ, [何人](../words/何人.md) なんにん, 何分 なんぷん, 何歳 なんさい, 何年 なんねん |

## Common words

- [何時](../words/何時.md) なんじ — what time
- [何人](../words/何人.md) なんにん — how many people
- [何か](../words/何か.md) なにか — something

## Notes

- **なに vs なん:** 何 is read なん before the sounds t, d, n (なんでも, なんだ, なんの) and before most counters (何時, 何人, 何分); otherwise なに (何を, 何が, 何か). This is a tendency with exceptions, so learn common words as units.
- 何人 is なんにん "how many people" but なにじん "what nationality".
- Look-alike: 河 (river) and 荷 (load) share the 可 component; 何 has 亻 on the left, and is not [今](今.md).

# 入 — enter, put in

**Strokes:** 2 · **Key (radical):** 入 (no. 11) · **JLPT:** N5 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

入 is often described as a pictograph of something pointed going inward (an entrance or tip), but the origin is debated.
Use it as a mnemonic: two strokes converging, opposite to the outward 出 ([出](出.md)).
It means "enter", "go in" and "put in".

It looks like 人 ([人](人.md)) but the strokes differ: the short left-falling stroke is written first, then the long right-falling stroke from the top (人 is the other way round).

## Readings

| Reading | Type | Typical words |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ニュウ | on | 入学 にゅうがく, 入院 にゅういん, 入力 にゅうりょく, 出入国 しゅつにゅうこく |
| ジュ | on | rare, e.g. 入内 じゅだい |
| い.る | kun | 入る いる (literary or in set phrases): 気に入る きにいる |
| い.れる | kun | 入れる いれる (put in), 入り口 いりぐち |
| はい.る | kun | 入る はいる (enter), 入口 → 入り口 |

## Common words

- [入口](../words/入口.md) いりぐち — entrance
- [出入口](../words/出入口.md) でいりぐち — entrance/exit
- 入る はいる — enter

## Notes

Opposite: [出](出.md). Compare 入る はいる (enter) and 入れる いれる (put in).

# 口 — mouth

**Strokes:** 3 · **Key (radical):** 口 (no. 30) · **JLPT:** N4 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

口 is a pictograph of an open mouth. It extends naturally to openings: an entrance or exit is a "mouth", and the
word for "mouth" counts persons ("mouths to feed" is the usual explanation for the 人口 sense).
As a key it appears in kanji about speaking, eating, and sound: 味, 吸, 叫, 吐, 呼, 咲.

The outer box in 国 and 回 is the enclosure 囗 (くにがまえ), a different key, not a mouth.

## Readings

| --- | --- | --- |
| コウ | on | [人口](../words/人口.md) じんこう, 口座 こうざ, 口頭 こうとう |
| ク | on | 口調 くちょう, 異口同音 いくどうおん |
| くち | kun | 口 くち, [出口](../words/出口.md) でぐち, [入口](../words/入口.md) いりぐち, 口癖 くちぐせ |
| -ぐち | kun (voiced) | 非常口 ひじょうぐち, 改札口 かいさつぐち |

## Common words

- [出口](../words/出口.md) でぐち — exit
- [入口](../words/入口.md) いりぐち — entrance

# 女 — woman, female

**Strokes:** 3 · **Key (radical):** 女 (no. 38) · **JLPT:** N5 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

女 is a pictograph of a kneeling figure with crossed hands. As a key it appears in many kanji about women and family: 母, 姉, 妹, 好, 姫, 嫁.
In 好 (like), 女 and 子 (child) are commonly explained as mother and child. Component meanings are a historical layer, not a description of the word today.

## Readings

| Reading | Type | Typical words |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ジョ | on | [男女](../words/男女.md) だんじょ, 女性 じょせい, 彼女 かのじょ, 少女 しょうじょ |
| ニョ | on | 老若男女 ろうにゃくなんにょ |
| ニョウ | on | 女房 にょうぼう |
| おんな | kun | 女 おんな, 女の子 おんなのこ, 女の人 おんなのひと |
| め | kun | 女神 めがみ, 女滝 めだき |

## Common words

- [男女](../words/男女.md) だんじょ — men and women
- 女性 じょせい — female (formal/polite)
- 女の子 おんなのこ — girl

## Notes

Opposite: [男](男.md). 女 can read おんな or め; 女の子 is the everyday word for "girl".

**Stroke-order mnemonic: くノ一.** The three strokes of 女, in order, look like the characters く, ノ and 一. Japanese fiction uses this as the word くノ一 (くのいち, "kunoichi"), now popularly "female ninja". The word is a playful spelling of 女 itself; it was a slang term for "woman" in the Edo period, and its "female ninja" meaning was popularised by novelist Yamada Fūtarō in the 1960s. Historians have found no records of female ninja working exactly like the men, so treat the image as a memory aid and pop-culture word, not as history. The first stroke (く) has the bend; then the long diagonal (ノ); the horizontal (一) comes last and crosses both.

# 日 — sun, day

**Strokes:** 4 · **Key (radical):** 日 (no. 72) · **Phonetic:** none (not a phonetic compound) · **JLPT:** N5 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

日 is a pictograph of the sun. The oldest forms are round, often with a dot or short line inside; brush and carving
styles squared the circle. What the inner mark originally represented is uncertain, so treat any story about it
(a sunspot, a core) as a mnemonic rather than established fact.

The same word covers both "the sun" and "a day" (one sun-cycle), which is why one character serves for both
and for the days of the week and the month (日曜日, 十日). As a component, 日 usually hints at the sun, daylight or time,
as in 明 (日 + 月, bright), 時, 晴, 昨.

It is easy to confuse with 目 (eye, taller with two inner lines), 白 (white, one extra stroke on top) and [月](月.md) (moon, slimmer, with two inner strokes).

## Readings

| Reading | Type | Typical words |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ニチ | on | 日曜日 にちようび, 毎日 まいにち, 日本 にほん／にっぽん, 日記 にっき |
| ジツ | on | [休日](../words/休日.md) きゅうじつ, 平日 へいじつ, 祝日 しゅくじつ, 先日 せんじつ |
| ひ | kun | 日 ひ (sun, day), 日の出 ひので, 日当たり ひあたり |
| -び | kun | 誕生日 たんじょうび, 記念日 きねんび (voiced form of ひ in compounds) |
| -か | kun | day counting: 二日 ふつか, 三日 みっか, 十日 とおか; 一日 ついたち is special |

Special readings: 今日 きょう, 昨日 きのう, 明日 あした／あす.

## Common words

- [日中](../words/日中.md) にっちゅう — daytime; also Japan–China (日中関係)
- [月日](../words/月日.md) つきひ／がっぴ — time passing; calendar date
- [休日](../words/休日.md) きゅうじつ — day off, holiday
- 毎日 まいにち — every day
- [日本刀](../words/日本刀.md) にほんとう — Japanese sword; 日本 names Japan in this compound

## Notes

The five-element kanji [火](火.md) [水](水.md) [木](木.md) (plus 金 土) combine with 日 and [月](月.md) in the weekday names:
日曜日, 月曜日, 火曜日, 水曜日, 木曜日, 金曜日, 土曜日.

# 月 — moon, month

**Strokes:** 4 · **Key (radical):** 月 (no. 74) · **JLPT:** N5 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

月 is a pictograph of a crescent moon. Old forms show the crescent with a mark inside it, usually explained as
shading on the moon or a way to tell it apart from 夕 (evening), which historically descends from the same picture.
Like 日 ([日](日.md)), it serves for both a celestial body and the time unit it measures: a lunar cycle gives "month", and
つき is both "moon" and "month" in Japanese.

Two things to keep apart:

- In 明 (bright) it is really the moon, paired with the sun: 日 + 月.
- In many body-part kanji (e.g. 腕, 脳) the 月 on the left is not the moon but the flesh radical 肉 (にくづき), which
  has a similar shape in modern print.

## Readings

| Reading | Type | Typical words |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ゲツ | on | 月曜日 げつようび, 今月 こんげつ, 来月 らいげつ |
| ガツ | on | month names: 一月 いちがつ, 十二月 じゅうにがつ; 正月 しょうがつ |
| つき | kun | 月 つき (moon, month), 月日 つきひ, 三日月 みかづき (voiced and shortened) |

ガツ is used for the numbered month names (一月–十二月); ゲツ for "month" as a counted span (一ヶ月 いっかげつ) and the weekday.

## Common words

- [月日](../words/月日.md) つきひ／がっぴ — time passing; calendar date
- 月曜日 げつようび — Monday
- 毎月 まいつき／まいげつ — every month (both readings are used)

## Notes

For the weekday series see [日](日.md); 月 pairs with [日](日.md) as the "sun and moon" of 日月.

# 男 — man, male

**Strokes:** 7 · **Key (radical):** 田 (no. 102) · **Phonetic:** none (an ideographic compound) · **JLPT:** N5 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

男 combines 田 (rice field) and 力 (strength, often explained as a plough or an arm). The traditional reading is "someone who
works the field with strength", reflecting a historical picture of male labour.
It is a compound ideogram: both parts contribute meaning, neither contributes sound.

# 買 — buy

**Strokes:** 12 · **Key (radical):** 貝 (shell, no. 154) · **JLPT:** N4 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

買 is commonly explained as 罒 (a net, a form of 网) over 貝 (a shell): shells were an early form of money, so the picture
is of "gathering in" goods with money. As the details of the early forms are debated, take the net image as a mnemonic.
The key 貝 appears in many money and trade words: 財, 貯 and 貨. (売 looks similar but has no 貝 in its modern form; its radical is 士.)

## Readings

| Reading | Type | Typical words |
| --- | --- | --- |
| バイ | on | 売買 ばいばい (buying and selling), 購買 こうばい, 買収 ばいしゅう |
| か.う | kun | [買う](../words/買う.md) かう, [買い物](../words/買い物.md) かいもの, 買い方 かいかた |

## Common words

- [買う](../words/買う.md) かう — to buy
- [買い物](../words/買い物.md) かいもの — shopping
- 買い手 かいて — buyer

## Notes

- Opposite: 売る うる (sell), written 売; the two kanji look alike at a glance, but 買 has 罒 over 貝 while 売 has 士 over a different base. かう is also 飼う (keep an animal), a homophone with another spelling.
- Look-alikes: [見](見.md) (see) is 目 over 儿 (legs); 買 has 罒 over 貝, and 貝 itself contains 目 over 八. 貝 is also a part of 貸 (lend) and 貯.
- 買う is a regular godan verb: 買います, 買わない (note the わ, not あ), 買って, 買った.
