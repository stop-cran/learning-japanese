# Warmth and texture: choose the sensation, not just the English adjective

[Onomatopoeia](japanese-onomatopoeia.md) can describe sensations with no audible noise.
"Warm," "soft," and "sticky" are useful first glosses, but each covers several different
Japanese choices.

## ぽかぽか, ほかほか, and ひんやり

| Word | Typical focus | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [ぽかぽか](../words/ぽかぽか.md) | Pleasant, comfortable warmth, often sunlight or the body | 日なたはぽかぽかしている。（ひなたはぽかぽかしている。） — It is pleasantly warm in the sun. |
| [ほかほか](../words/ほかほか.md) | Fresh warmth, especially food, also a warmly glowing body | ほかほかのご飯を食べた。（ほかほかのごはんをたべた。） — I ate warm, freshly cooked rice. |
| [ひんやり](../words/ひんやり.md) | A cool or chilly sensation on contact or entering a place | 石の床がひんやりする。（いしのゆかがひんやりする。） — The stone floor feels cool. |

These are tendencies, not exclusive domains: ほかほか is not restricted to food, and ぽかぽか
does not require sunshine. [あつあつ](../words/あつあつ.md) focuses on being hot, especially
freshly prepared food; pleasant warmth and very hot food are not identical.
[ほくほく](../words/ほくほく.md) often describes the warm, floury texture of cooked potatoes
or similar foods, rather than simply measuring temperature.

## Different kinds of soft

- [ふわふわ](../words/ふわふわ.md): light, airy softness, or floating without a firm position.
  ふわふわのパン — light, fluffy bread.
- [ふかふか](../words/ふかふか.md): thickness and soft cushioning.
  ふかふかの布団（ふとん） — a soft, well-padded futon.
- [もちもち](../words/もちもち.md): springy, pleasantly chewy texture.
  もちもちした麺（めん） — springy, chewy noodles.

A cushion may invite both ふわふわ and ふかふか depending on what the speaker notices.
These are not mutually exclusive scientific categories. A scene or quiz needs enough context
to distinguish the intended sensation; a generic picture of a pillow cannot guarantee one right word.

## Smooth is not one sensation

[つるつる](../words/つるつる.md) often highlights a smooth, slippery surface;
[すべすべ](../words/すべすべ.md) highlights smoothness pleasant to the touch.
肌がすべすべしている（はだがすべすべしている） means "the skin feels smooth."
The words can overlap, but つるつるした床（ゆか）, "a smooth/slippery floor," can suggest
a danger of slipping that すべすべ alone does not foreground.

Contrast [ざらざら](../words/ざらざら.md), a rough or gritty surface, with
[ごつごつ](../words/ごつごつ.md), hard, irregular projections, and
[でこぼこ](../words/でこぼこ.md), unevenness with bumps and hollows.
A bumpy road and gritty paper are not the same type of "rough."

## ぺたぺた versus べたべた

- 紙にシールをぺたぺた貼った。（かみにシールをぺたぺたはった。） — I stuck stickers
  all over the paper.
- 手がべたべたする。（てがべたべたする。） — My hands feel sticky.

[ぺたぺた](../words/ぺたぺた.md) here describes repeated sticking actions;
[べたべた](../words/べたべた.md) describes the resulting tactile sensation.
Their senses are not confined to these examples: ぺたぺた can also convey light contact sounds,
while べたべた can describe excessive physical affection or clinginess.

[ねばねば](../words/ねばねば.md) emphasizes sticky, viscous or stringy material;
[ぬるぬる](../words/ぬるぬる.md) emphasizes slippery sliminess.
Food texture and a person's behavior are different senses, even when the same word describes both.

## Moist, damp, or drenched?

[しっとり](../words/しっとり.md) often describes pleasant or appropriate moisture, such as a moist
cake. [じめじめ](../words/じめじめ.md) often suggests unpleasant dampness.
[じとじと](../words/じとじと.md) likewise describes persistent damp or clammy conditions.
[びしょびしょ](../words/びしょびしょ.md) and [ぐっしょり](../words/ぐっしょり.md) describe
being thoroughly wet or soaked. They overlap substantially; do not make them competing quiz
answers merely because the English titles happen to differ.

Compare [ぱさぱさ](../words/ぱさぱさ.md) for an undesirably dry texture,
[かさかさ](../words/かさかさ.md) for dry roughness or a dry rustling sound, and
[からから](../words/からから.md) for complete dryness, among other uses.
