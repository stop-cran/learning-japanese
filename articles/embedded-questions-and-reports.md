# Embedded questions, reports and noticing

**Who bought the book?** is a question. **I know who bought the book** is a statement containing a question.
Japanese likewise separates the **inner content** from what the **outer predicate** does with it.
This distinction also helps with reporting information, realizing something and reporting an instruction.

This N4 companion builds on [questions and pointing](pointing-and-asking-questions.md),
[quotation](explanations-quotations-and-endings.md#と-and-って-mark-quoted-content),
and [noun-like activities](noun-descriptions-and-activities.md#5-give-an-activity-a-noun-like-role).
The five practice groups move from embedded questions to reports, nominalized content and reported instructions.
Before the last group, revisit the linked goal and request support; no new passive or honorific prerequisite chain is imposed.

## 1. Put an information question inside a sentence

> 友達が何を買ったか知っています。  
> ともだちが なにを かったか しっています。  
> I know what my friend bought.

Read it in layers:

| Layer | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| 友達が何を買った | The friend bought what? 友達が is the buyer; 何を is the unknown object. |
| 友達が何を買ったか | Packages that information question. |
| …知っています | Says the speaker knows its answer. |

The inner か does **not** make the entire sentence a request for information.
Change the outer predicate and the purpose changes:

> 友達が何を買ったか教えてください。  
> ともだちが なにを かったか おしえてください。  
> Please tell me what my friend bought.

The unknown can have another role:

> だれがパンを食べたか忘れました。  
> だれが パンを たべたか わすれました。  
> I have forgotten who ate the bread.

Here だれが asks for the **eater**, not the food. The outer 忘れました reports lost knowledge.
In だれがパンを食べたか教えてもらえませんか
(だれが パンを たべたか おしえてもらえませんか, could you tell me who ate the bread?),
the outer negative favor question is a polite **request**, not a claim that the listener refuses.
See [favor requests](benefactive-actions.md).

### Basic attachment

Use the embedded predicate's ordinary plain form in these basic models:

| Predicate | Models before the outer predicate |
| --- | --- |
| Verb | 買うか / 買ったか (かうか / かったか, whether one buys / bought) |
| い-adjective | 重いか / 重かったか (おもいか / おもかったか, whether it is / was heavy) |
| な-adjective | 静かか / 静かだったか (しずかか / しずかだったか, whether it is / was quiet) |
| Noun predicate | 休みか / 休みだったか (やすみか / やすみだったか, whether it is / was a day off) |

Question words and the rest of the clause supply the missing detail where there is one.
Preserve negative and past content rather than making every embedded predicate nonpast.
For instance, 買いました becomes 買ったか, not 買いますか or 買うか, in the tense-preserving embedding drill.

The table gives useful basic nominal forms without nonpast だ; it is **not a universal ban on embedded だか**.
For example, 次の会議がいつだか覚えていません
(つぎの かいぎが いつだか おぼえていません, I do not remember when the next meeting is)
has a grammatical embedded だか. Do not generalize a direct-question shortcut to every embedded question.
Some embedded questions also take a following case particle, such as を; the short formation drills explicitly omit that extra step.

Nor is every question word followed by か a whole embedded question:
だれか (someone) can be an indefinite word. Compare the position and role of か in **だれが食べたか**
(who ate) with **だれかが食べた** (someone ate).
The [N5 indefinite-word foundation](pointing-and-asking-questions.md) remains in its original set.

## 2. Ask whether something holds

An information question seeks a missing detail. A **polar question** asks whether a proposition holds:

| Sentence | Information left open |
| --- | --- |
| バスがいつ来るか調べます。 | I will check **when** the bus comes. |
| バスが来るか調べます。 | I will check **whether** the bus comes. |
| バスが来るかどうか調べます。 | I will check **whether or not** the bus comes. |

Readings: バスが いつ くるか しらべます; バスが くるか しらべます;
バスが くるかどうか しらべます.

**Bare か can also embed a polar question.** どうか is not universally compulsory.
It is useful for making the whether-or-not alternative explicit, but its absence does not turn every question into a question-word question.

An adjective shows another contrast:
この箱がどのくらい重いか調べます
(この はこが どのくらい おもいか しらべます, I will check how heavy this box is)
asks about degree; この箱が重いかどうか調べます
(この はこが おもいかどうか しらべます, I will check whether this box is heavy)
asks whether it meets the context's standard for heavy.

A negative proposition can be left open too:
バスが来ないかどうかまだ分かりません
(バスが こないかどうか まだ わかりません, I do not yet know whether the bus is not coming).
That is **not** a statement that the bus definitely will not come.
The positive and negative versions can divide the same possible outcomes, but they frame the proposition differently.
A drill asking you to preserve 来ません therefore expects the negative predicate, not a reversal of it.

## 3. Report information without turning it into proof

> 私は友達から、この町は静かだと聞いた。  
> わたしは ともだちから、この まちは しずかだと きいた。  
> I heard from a friend that this town is quiet.

**と聞いた** presents heard information. The friend is its source; the sentence does not independently verify the town's quietness.
The present state of received information can also be expressed with **と聞いている**:
…富士山が見えると聞いている
(ふじさんが みえると きいている, I have heard / understand from what I have heard that Mount Fuji is visible).
This is not a claim to have seen it personally.

> この町は静かだと言われている。  
> この まちは しずかだと いわれている。  
> This town is said to be quiet.

In this ordinary **impersonal report**, the claim is presented as something people say.
That does not mean everyone agrees, that it is objectively true, or that the speaker has no opinion.
Other uses of 言われる can involve particular speakers and addressees; not every passive 言う sentence is a public-consensus formula.

### Keep the content's time separate from the reporting time

| Content before と | Basic full plain attachment |
| --- | --- |
| Verb | 見える / 見えた (みえる / みえた, is / was visible) |
| い-adjective | 重い / 重かった (おもい / おもかった, is / was heavy) |
| な-adjective | 静かだ / 静かだった (しずかだ / しずかだった, is / was quiet) |
| Noun predicate | 休みだ / 休みだった (やすみだ / やすみだった, is / was a day off) |

静かだったと聞いた (しずかだったと きいた) puts past quietness **inside** a hearing report.
以前は、ここから富士山が見えると言われていた
(いぜんは、ここから ふじさんが みえると いわれていた,
it used to be said that Mount Fuji was visible from here) puts the **report's circulation** in the past.
Neither statement by itself settles the present situation.

The formation tasks request full short nominal predicates. Conversational omission of だ can be possible outside that named operation;
it is not globally ungrammatical. Direct quoted speech can retain its original polite ending:
see [quoted predicates](explanations-quotations-and-endings.md).
For **thinking** rather than hearing or impersonal reporting, see [thought, possibility and expectation](thinking-possibility-and-expectation.md).

### Optional recognition: related forms are not interchangeable evidence labels

| Example | Bounded reading |
| --- | --- |
| この町は安全だと考えられている。 | This town is considered safe. |
| この町は安全だとされている。 | This town is regarded as safe. |
| この町では人口の増加が見られる。 | Population growth is observed in this town. |
| この仕事は終わったと言ってもいい。 | One could say that this work is finished. |

Readings: この まちは あんぜんだと かんがえられている; この まちは あんぜんだと されている;
この まちでは じんこうの ぞうかが みられる; この しごとは おわったと いってもいい.

**と考えられている／とされている** can present an attributed or generally accepted view, not proof or a literal vote.
**が見られる** can describe an observable pattern, including evidence in records; it is not automatically hearsay or a test of physical effort.
**と言ってもいい** qualifies an assertion: "one could say." It does **not** say the speaker heard someone make that assertion.
These are recognition bridges, not additional scored targets.
The separately indexed bare と考えられる is not silently assigned the same N4 scope as と考えられている.

## 4. Package content, then say what happens to it

Nominalization lets a clause fill a noun-like role; it does not automatically turn it into hearsay.

> 店が休みだということを知っています。  
> みせが やすみだということを しっています。  
> I know that the shop is closed for a day off.

**店が休みだ** is the proposition. **ということ** packages that content; **を** marks it as the object of 知っています.
The knowledge predicate presents it as known. The packaging by itself does not identify a source or assert that any event happened.

Before ということ, ordinary full plain predicates follow the same basic attachment as before quotation と:
休みだ／休みだった, 静かだ／静かだった, 重い／重かった, 来る／来た (くる／きた).
Do not insert an extra copula after a verb or い-adjective, or drop a required nominal copula in a full-form drill.

Bare **こと** also nominalizes clauses: 友達が来たことを知っています
(ともだちが きたことを しっています, I know that my friend came).
The familiar activity/perception patterns remain in [noun-based grammar](koto-wake-and-formal-nouns.md#こと-the-action-event-fact-or-content);
this is not a rule that こと and の substitute freely in every construction.

### Notice a situation with に気が付く

> かばんに財布がないのに気が付いた。  
> かばんに さいふが ないのに きがついた。  
> I noticed that my wallet was not in the bag.

The wallet's absence becomes a noun-like complement: **財布がない + の + に + 気が付いた**.
Here のに is **nominalizing の plus に**, not the concessive "although."
**財布がないことに気が付いた** also fits this noticing meaning.
Both の and こと are accepted in the linked noticing tasks, in typed answers and fixed choices.

Compare positive 友達が来ているのに気が付いた
(ともだちが きているのに きがついた, I noticed that my friend had arrived / was here)
with negative 友達が来ていないことに気が付いた
(ともだちが きていないことに きがついた, I noticed that my friend had not arrived).
Preserve the state and polarity. Nonarrival is not automatically deliberate refusal.
The full-form drills retain い in ている; ordinary conversational shortening is not declared invalid.

Naming something with **という** is a separate use, with its own N3 curriculum entry.
Likewise, sentence-final hearsay **ということだ**, hearsay **そうだ**, naming **って**, and **のは〜だ** focus
are not silently taught by an N4 nominalizing-ということ drill.
The existing [noun-based comparisons](koto-wake-and-formal-nouns.md) and
[impression/hearsay boundary](appearance-resemblance-and-impressions.md) provide optional reading, not new compulsory distractors.

## 5. Report an instruction and keep the people straight

First revisit [direct requests](japanese-requests-permission-and-rules.md), [favor requests](benefactive-actions.md),
and [よう goal patterns](koto-wake-and-formal-nouns.md).
The existing positive/prevented-goal and request groups are reused without moving or duplicating their exercises.

For these two-person examples, take the named people as the **issuer and addressee of the instruction**,
with the addressee asked about their **own** action. This supplies the clause scope without telling you which person fills which role.

> 先生は学生に本を読むように言った。  
> せんせいは がくせいに ほんを よむように いった。  
> The teacher told the student to read the book.

The teacher **issues** the instruction. The student is the **addressee and requested reader**.
The person now reporting this sentence need not be either of them.
**Plain nonpast verb + ように + 言う** reports the requested action; the reporting predicate may itself be past.

| Requested action | Indirect report |
| --- | --- |
| 読みます (よみます, read) | 読むように言った (よむように いった, told someone to read) |
| 読みません (よみません, do not read) | 読まないように言った (よまないように いった, told someone not to read) |
| 説明します (せつめいします, explain) | 説明するように頼んだ (せつめいするように たのんだ, asked someone to explain) |
| 説明しません (せつめいしません, do not explain) | 説明しないように頼んだ (せつめいしないように たのんだ, asked someone not to explain) |

The negative is **inside the requested action**, not a negation of the telling or requesting.
In this use, 頼む (たのむ) makes requesting explicit, while 言う alone does not tell you exactly how forceful the instruction was.
Actual command/request force still depends on the exchange. Neither frame proves compliance.

Word order is not a role label:

> 先生に学生が答えを説明しないように頼んだ。  
> せんせいに がくせいが こたえを せつめいしないように たのんだ。  
> The student asked the teacher not to explain the answer.

Within that stated scope, initial **先生に** is the addressee; **学生が** is the requester.
The teacher is asked to refrain from explaining. Do not identify the first person mentioned as the issuer.

Without this framing, the sentence also allows **先生に［学生が答えを説明しないように］頼んだ**:
someone asked the teacher for the student's non-explanation. Then 学生が belongs **inside the requested clause**,
and the requester is unstated. A visible が does not by itself establish which clause its subject belongs to.
The role questions therefore state the two-person, addressee-action scope **before** answering.
They test case-role mapping within that scope, not guessing an unavailable conversation or excluding every other grammatical reading.

## Practice and limits

The new set contains **40 N4 exercises in five groups**: 24 formation tasks and 16 bounded interpretations.
The response labels are defined with readings before answering, in answer-independent order:
陳述／依頼 for outer purpose, 内容疑問／真偽疑問 for the information gap, 自分／世間 for the local reporting-perspective task,
and 先生／学生 for the explicitly requested participant. Kana responses are accepted.
These are task labels, not a new universal classification of every sentence or information source.

Eight additional correct options retain fitting bare-か and ことに completions.
Typed recognition also accepts documented spellings without treating rare forms as equally frequent:
for example, 静か／閑か, ある／有る／在る, ない／無い, and nominalizing こと／事／縡.
The displayed teaching forms remain ordinary.
Eleven shared context-key strings link paired forms, roles and messages; they are not forty independent conversations or proof of mastery.
Ten soft recommendations reuse the earlier foundations. There are no new hard prerequisites.
