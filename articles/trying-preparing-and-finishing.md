# Trying, preparing and getting things done

**読んでみる, 読んでおく and 読んでしまう all contain reading, but add different perspectives.**
One presents a trial, another preparation, and another completion or an outcome with a reaction.
The situation can support more than one perspective; the endings are not mutually exclusive facts about the world.

This is a focused companion to [verb extensions](verb-extensions-direction-and-aspect.md),
not another survey of direction, starting, continuing and compound verbs.
For an observed state versus an already prepared result, use
[actions, events and results](actions-events-and-results.md).
The earlier [actions and states introduction](japanese-actions-states-and-time.md) supplies basic ている practice.

## Build the て/で form, then add the auxiliary

| Base verb | Trial | Preparation | Completion / ending up doing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 食べる（たべる）, eat | 食べてみる | 食べておく | 食べてしまう |
| 読む（よむ）, read | 読んでみる | 読んでおく | 読んでしまう |

The second row needs **で**, not て. Return to the
[verb-form chart](japanese-verb-foundations.md)
if you need the original verb's attachment form.
Then inflect the **last** verb, not the first one again:

| Auxiliary | Plain nonpast | Plain past | Plain negative | Polite nonpast |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| みる: ichidan | みる | みた | みない | みます |
| おく: godan | おく | おいた | おかない | おきます |
| しまう: godan | しまう | しまった | しまわない | しまいます |

Thus **読んでみました**, "tried reading"; **読んでおきました**, "read beforehand";
and **読んでしまいました**, "finished reading / ended up reading."
The forms alone do not supply the entire reason or emotional context.
These grammatical auxiliaries are ordinarily written in kana; the formation drills use that spelling.

## てみる: give the action a try

> このチーズを少し食べてみます。  
> このちーずをすこしたべてみます。  
> I'll try a little of this cheese.
>
> 別の道を歩いてみました。  
> べつのみちをあるいてみました。  
> I tried walking along a different route.

The action is tried out to see what it is like or what happens. This is not literal visual seeing:
you can try a taste or a way of doing something. A trial can be repeated; てみる does not require
the very first time in your life.

Trying an action is not a guarantee of the hoped-for outcome:
**電話してみましたが、誰も出ませんでした**（でんわしてみましたが、だれもでませんでした）,
"I tried calling, but nobody answered." The calling was tried; successful contact did not follow.
A future statement, wish or question about a trial does not assert that the trial already occurred.

**てみる is not a general replacement for ようにする**, making a point of doing something:
**毎日歩くようにしています**（まいにちあるくようにしています） describes a maintained practice,
not just trying a walk to see what it is like. Existing
[change/effort practice](koto-wake-and-formal-nouns.md) retains that distinction.
Volitional + とする has its own attempt/about-to uses at N3 in the primary curriculum;
it is not a required choice in these N4 drills.

## ておく: preparation, or leaving something in a state

> 明日の会議の前に、資料を読んでおきます。  
> あしたのかいぎのまえに、しりょうをよんでおきます。  
> I'll read the materials in preparation for tomorrow's meeting.
>
> 窓はもう開いています。今日は開けておきます。  
> まどはもうあいています。きょうはあけておきます。  
> The window is already open. I'll leave it open today.

The first foregrounds advance preparation. In the second, the context establishes an existing state
that will be retained; no fresh opening is required.
The uses can overlap: leaving a window open can itself be a useful arrangement.
There need not be a precisely named future appointment, and leaving something as it is is not always
a carefully planned benefit.

Past **読んでおいた** reports earlier preparation, not future tense.
For the comparison with **読んである**, a prepared result being in place, return to
[actions/events/results](actions-events-and-results.md#5-ておく-do-something-as-preparation-or-leave-it-that-way).
Those contextual state/result choices remain in that original group; this companion adds basic
auxiliary formation, interpretation and contraction recognition.

## てしまう: completion is not automatically regret

> 今日中に、残りの資料を読んでしまいます。  
> きょうじゅうに、のこりのしりょうをよんでしまいます。  
> I'll get the remaining materials read by the end of today.

This can be a deliberate, welcome plan to finish. The ending does not mean that the reading is accidental.
Compare an unwanted development:

> 困った。大事な手紙を捨ててしまいました。  
> こまった。だいじなてがみをすててしまいました。  
> Oh no. I ended up throwing away an important letter.

Here the context supplies the unwelcome consequence. Likewise, **寝てしまった**, "ended up falling asleep,"
need not mean "finished sleeping." Not every しまう example is a completed task, and not every completed
task is regretted. Context can combine completion and disappointment; the practice uses stated circumstances,
not a universal emotion attached to the ending.

The [broader verb-extension comparison](verb-extensions-direction-and-aspect.md)
also introduces stem + 終わる and 切る. Those neighboring patterns are not new required alternatives here.

## Recognize とく／どく and ちゃう／じゃう

| Full sequence | Casual contraction | Past example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ておく | とく | 食べておいた → 食べといた |
| でおく | どく | 読んでおいた → 読んどいた |
| てしまう | ちゃう | 食べてしまった → 食べちゃった |
| でしまう | じゃう | 読んでしまった → 読んじゃった |

Replace the **whole sequence**: do not retain て and then attach とく or ちゃう again.
The contracted endings still inflect: とく → といた／とかない, ちゃう → ちゃった／ちゃわない.
These are common conversational forms, not separate verbs with a mandatory new emotion.
For example, 読んじゃった can report completion or an unwelcome reading, depending on context.

A full form is also possible in casual conversation. These recognition questions explicitly ask you
to **expand** a supplied contraction while retaining its tense; they do not pretend that only a contraction
could fit an otherwise unrestricted conversation. There is no universal ban based just on a workplace location.

## Practice boundaries and sources

The trial, preparation, completion and contraction groups distinguish building the auxiliary,
interpreting the contextual contribution, and expanding a contraction.
Interpretation labels and their readings are supplied in a fixed order before answering.
They describe the contribution foregrounded here, not a claim that the wider uses never overlap.
Paired forms share context keys; the number of exercises is not the number of independent conversations.

- Bunpro: [てみる](https://bunpro.jp/grammar_points/てみる), [ておく](https://bunpro.jp/grammar_points/ておく)
  and [てしまう／ちゃう](https://bunpro.jp/grammar_points/てしまう-ちゃう), actual lessons and examples.
- [Maggie Sensei: てみる](https://maggiesensei.com/2014/05/12/how-to-use-%E3%80%9C%E3%81%A6%E3%81%BF%E3%82%8B%E3%80%80-te-miru%E3%80%80/):
  trial formation and contextual use. Its first-ever restriction on てみたい is not adopted.
- [Tofugu: ておく](https://www.tofugu.com/japanese-grammar/teoku/):
  preparation, leaving a state, overlapping uses and contractions.
- [Tae Kim: trying](https://guidetojapanese.org/learn/grammar/try) and
  [unintended actions](https://guidetojapanese.org/learn/complete/unintended):
  attachment, inflection, completion/outcome senses and contractions.
