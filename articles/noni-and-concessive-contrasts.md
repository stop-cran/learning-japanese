# のに: unexpected outcomes, comparisons, and the other のに

**A のに B** often presents B as surprising or contrary to an expectation associated with A:
"although A, B" or "B despite A." It does not simply mean that both things happened, and it does not always
express an unwelcome result.

This is a follow-up to [noun-based grammar](koto-wake-and-formal-nouns.md) and
[conditionals](japanese-conditionals.md). The learning target is the relationship between complete clauses,
not a rule that every English "although" has exactly one Japanese equivalent.

## An expectation, not a logical contradiction

> 説明を二度読んだのに、まだ使い方が分からない。  
> （せつめいをにどよんだのに、まだつかいかたがわからない。）  
> Although I read the explanation twice, I still do not understand how to use it.

Reading normally supports understanding in this context. The speaker contrasts that expectation with the actual result.
The sentence does not claim that reading and failing to understand are logically incompatible.

An appreciated result works too:

> 初心者なのに、難しい曲を最後まで弾けた。  
> （しょしんしゃなのに、むずかしいきょくをさいごまでひけた。）  
> Although I am a beginner, I managed to play the difficult piece to the end.

Surprise, disappointment, gratitude and criticism come from the construction together with the surrounding context.
Do not use "good result" versus "bad result" as an answer key.

## Attachment: what comes immediately before のに?

Use the appropriate plain predicate. The following are common forms, not an exhaustive register paradigm:

| Predicate | Example | Meaning of the first part |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Verb, nonpast | 練習するのに | although someone practices / will practice |
| Verb, negative | 練習しないのに | although someone does not practice |
| Verb, past | 練習したのに | although someone practiced |
| Verb, past negative | 練習しなかったのに | although someone did not practice |
| い-adjective | 高いのに／高かったのに | although it is / was expensive |
| Negative い-adjective | 高くないのに | although it is not expensive |
| な-adjective, affirmative nonpast | 静かなのに | although it is quiet |
| Noun predicate, affirmative nonpast | 初心者なのに | although someone is a beginner |
| Past noun or な-adjective predicate | 初心者だったのに／静かだったのに | although someone was a beginner / it was quiet |
| Negative noun or な-adjective predicate | 初心者ではないのに／静かじゃないのに | although someone is not a beginner / it is not quiet |

**練習（れんしゅう）** means practice; **高い（たかい）** means expensive here; **静か（しずか）** means quiet.
Add **な** in the affirmative nonpast noun/な-adjective pattern, but not after **だった** or **ない**.
**初心者のに** does not supply the required connector in this standard noun-predicate frame.

More written **であるのに** also exists. The opening exercises explicitly practice the shorter standard
**のに／ので／なのに／なので** endings; that named-form task is not a declaration that every fuller formulation is wrong.
Likewise, a plain subordinate predicate can precede a polite main clause: **静かなのに、眠れません** is not a politeness mismatch.

## のに versus ので: against an expectation or because of a reason

| Sentence | Relationship |
| --- | --- |
| 説明を読んだのに、まだ操作が分からない。 | I read the explanation, yet still do not understand the controls. |
| 説明を読んでいないので、操作が分からない。 | I do not understand the controls because I have not read the explanation. |

**操作（そうさ）** means operating something or its controls.
The first presents a contrast with an expectation; the second gives a reason.
The endings are similar, but exchanging them changes the relationship being asserted.

A speaker can frame the same facts differently in a richer context. The exercise instructions state whether the task
asks for an unexpected contrast or an explanation, rather than pretending that a pair of clauses mechanically forces
one possible discourse interpretation. They request uncontracted endings; conversational **んで** is outside that drill.
Other connectors such as **けど／けれども／が** are useful, but are not all-purpose synonyms: they can also introduce
background information without an unexpected outcome.

## のに, わりに and くせに: overlapping territory, different framing

| Construction | What it contributes |
| --- | --- |
| のに | A contrast with what the preceding situation would lead one to expect |
| わりに | An assessment against a standard suggested by that situation: "considering / for ..." |
| くせに | A conventionally reproachful or mocking contrast |

Compare two ways to describe the same capacity:

> この部屋は狭いのに、十人座れる。  
> （このへやはせまいのに、じゅうにんすわれる。）  
> Although this room is small, ten people can sit in it.
>
> この部屋は狭いわりに、たくさん人が座れる。  
> （このへやはせまいわりに、たくさんひとがすわれる。）  
> For its small size, this room can seat a lot of people.

The first highlights counterexpectation; the second evaluates capacity relative to size.
Both can suit a context where ten seats are unexpectedly many for the room. This is not a universal substitution rule.
The speaker's expectation must be supported by the situation, not by an assertion that every small room has the same capacity.

In a critical context, **のに** can convey dissatisfaction too:

> 手伝うと言ったのに、何もしてくれなかった。  
> （てつだうといったのに、なにもしてくれなかった。）  
> They said they would help, yet did nothing for me.

**言ったくせに** makes the reproach more pointed; **言ったわりに** assesses the action against what the promise suggested.
Several formulations can fit the complaint without contributing exactly the same nuance.
Conversely, くせに can occur in teasing praise. Neither anger nor a favorable outcome proves that only one connector is possible.
わりに also does not guarantee tact: the standard someone assumes about another person can itself be unwelcome.

Keep the attachment differences:

| Preceding predicate | のに | わりに | くせに |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Noun, affirmative nonpast | 初心者なのに | 初心者のわりに | 初心者のくせに |
| な-adjective, affirmative nonpast | 暇なのに | 暇なわりに | 暇なくせに |
| Verb or い-adjective | 言ったのに／安いのに | 言ったわりに／安いわりに | 言ったくせに／安いくせに |

**暇（ひま）** means free or unoccupied; **安い（やすい）** means inexpensive.
Kana is common, but **割に／割りに** and **癖に** are possible spellings in the practiced constructions.
The contrast exercises accept the suitable alternatives together. A different accepted wording is never a wrong button.

## Another のに: doing something, the time it takes, or a tool's use

The same spelling can instead contain **nominalizing の + the case particle に**:

> この説明書を読むのに、四十分かかった。  
> （このせつめいしょをよむのに、よんじゅっぷんかかった。）  
> It took forty minutes to read this instruction booklet.
>
> この道具は、固いふたを開けるのに便利だ。  
> （このどうぐは、かたいふたをあけるのにべんりだ。）  
> This tool is useful for opening stiff lids.

**説明書（せつめいしょ）** means instruction booklet; **道具（どうぐ）** means tool.
Here, 読む and 開ける name activities. The following predicates describe the time needed or the tool's usefulness
for those activities. No contrary expectation is being asserted.

This connects to the nominalizer の discussed in the [formal-nouns article](koto-wake-and-formal-nouns.md).
It is not the same grammatical use as the concessive connector. Dictionaries and grammatical frameworks may describe
their historical relationship differently; a shared spelling or origin does not make the present-day uses interchangeable.

Do not classify the use by the preceding tense alone. Both uses can follow a dictionary-form verb:

> この道具は毎日使うのに、なかなか慣れない。  
> （このどうぐはまいにちつかうのに、なかなかなれない。）  
> Although I use this tool every day, I cannot seem to get used to it.

Here **慣れる（なれる）** means get used to something. It is the unexpected difficulty, not the shape 使う,
that distinguishes the relationship from a tool's usefulness or a required resource.
Likewise, dictionary-form 読む in the time-needed construction does not imply that the reading remains in the future:
**かかった** can report a completed episode.

Purpose **ために** can also discuss a goal, but do not replace every のに mechanically with ために.
Study the complete predicate, such as **時間がかかる**, **便利だ** or **必要だ**.
The initial meaning questions identify required time, tool use and an unexpected outcome; they do not ask the learner
to prove a universal boundary between every purpose construction.

## Combining のに with はず and つもり

These are combinations, not necessarily competing answers:

| Sentence | What is contrasted with the outcome? |
| --- | --- |
| 荷物はもう届いているはずなのに、見当たらない。 | The evidence-based expectation that the parcel has arrived; it cannot be found. |
| 昨日は早く帰るつもりだったのに、仕事が終わらなかった。 | An earlier intention to go home early; the work did not finish. |
| 全部確認したつもりなのに、まだ間違いがある。 | A belief that everything has been checked; mistakes remain. |

Read **荷物（にもつ）**, **届く（とどく）**, **見当たらない（みあたらない）**, **確認（かくにん）**,
and **間違い（まちがい）** as parcel, arrive, cannot find, checking, and mistake respectively.

**はず** concerns an expectation supported by reasons. **Vるつもり** concerns an intention;
**Vたつもり** can concern a belief about completion. のに then links that expectation, intention or belief to the
contrasting situation. **つもりだった** alone does not prove failure, and **つもり** does not independently verify an event.
For the contextual qualifications of past **はずだった**, see the linked article's detailed discussion.

The practice also includes **ので** when an expectation or intention explains preparation. A fulfilled plan is not
automatically a concession, and a later cancellation does not erase an earlier intention that motivated preparation.
These questions practice the connective around supplied はず／つもり, not independent selection between those nouns.

## A short bridge to ても and sentence-final のに

**ても** can express "even if" or "even when," including hypothetical conditions:

> 明日雨が降っても、予定どおり出発します。  
> （あしたあめがふっても、よていどおりしゅっぱつします。）  
> Even if it rains tomorrow, we will leave as planned.

But **ても is not hypothetical-only**:

> 何度呼んでも、返事がなかった。  
> （なんどよんでも、へんじがなかった。）  
> Even though I called repeatedly, there was no reply.

のに often foregrounds an expectation being disappointed or overturned; ても can present a result holding despite a condition.
Actual uses overlap. Do not turn this teaching contrast into a categorical factual/hypothetical split.

The focused [ても and paired-alternative supplement](reasons-and-contrasts.md#ても-and-paired-alternatives)
adds formation and interpretation practice without moving these existing のに exercises or making the
two connectors universally interchangeable.

Sentence-final のに can leave the contrasting situation understood and express disappointment or an unfulfilled wish:

> 時間が足りなかった。もう少し時間があれば、最後まで読めたのに。  
> （じかんがたりなかった。もうすこしじかんがあれば、さいごまでよめたのに。）  
> There was not enough time. With a little more time, I could have read to the end.

The reading-to-the-end is not being asserted as an actual event. This is the counterfactual bridge already encountered
in the [conditionals article](japanese-conditionals.md), not a reason to interpret every clause-final のに as a regret.
Sentence-final uses, ても and further neighbors such as **ものの／ながらも／にもかかわらず** are not independent
targets in this initial exercise set.

## What the initial practice covers

The 26 exercises have four introduction groups:

- Eight controlled **のに／ので** relationship-and-attachment questions.
- Six **meaning-label questions**: two unexpected outcomes, two required-time descriptions and two tool-use descriptions.
  The visible labels are **意外な結果（いがいなけっか）**, **必要な時間（ひつようなじかん）** and
  **道具の用途（どうぐのようと）**. These identify a meaning, not a missing connector.
- Six contextual contrasts allowing overlapping **のに／わりに／くせに** answers where appropriate.
- Six **はず／つもり** compositions: four unexpected outcomes and two reasons for preparation.

There is one correct displayed option per question; typing accepts the authored alternatives.
The shared answers do not independently test every difference between those alternatives.
The fixed-option format does not yet ask the learner to select several correct buttons.
All new prompts have optional vocabulary support, with the tested grammar protected from hints.

## Sources and scope

- [Digital Daijisen and Seisenban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten: のに](https://kotobank.jp/word/のに-597141) distinguish
  connective and sentence-final uses; Daijisen separately lists nominalizing の plus case に.
- [Tae Kim: compound sentences](https://guidetojapanese.org/learn/grammar/compound) explains concessive のに,
  noun/な-adjective attachment and contrasts with other connectors.
- [Bunpro: purpose/resource のに](https://bunpro.jp/grammar_points/のに) supplies activity, required-time and tool-use
  patterns. Its suggestion that the concessive and purpose uses simply have the same meaning is not adopted here.
- [Bunpro: わりに](https://bunpro.jp/grammar_points/わりに) and
  [Daijisen: くせに](https://kotobank.jp/word/癖に-483705) support comparative framing, attachment and marked reproach.
- [Tofugu: つもり](https://www.tofugu.com/japanese-grammar/tsumori/) discusses intention and beliefs about completion.
  A past ending is not treated here as a universal failure marker; the linked formal-nouns article gives fuller qualifications.
- [Seisenban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten: ても](https://kotobank.jp/word/ても-576900) includes both hypothetical and
  established concessive conditions.

This is practical introductory coverage, not an exhaustive account of dialects, register, clause-final interaction,
speech-act restrictions or every possible paraphrase.
