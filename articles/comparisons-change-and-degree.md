# Comparisons, changes and degree

**Lighter than what? The cheapest among which choices? Has something changed, or is it simply expensive now?**
These questions use related descriptions, but they make different claims.

| Goal | Starting pattern | What it establishes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Compare two alternatives | AよりBのほうが軽い | B is lighter than A |
| Identify a maximum within a set | この三つの中でBが一番軽い | B is the lightest within these three |
| Report a change | 軽くなった | It became lighter |
| Report excess | 軽すぎる | It is too light for the relevant purpose or standard |
| Describe a high degree | とても軽い | It is very light, without itself saying "too light" |

Read **軽い（かるい）**, light in weight; **一番（いちばん）**, most; and **三つ（みっつ）**, three things.
The examples and exercises supply their comparison sets and goals. These patterns do not establish a universal
ranking of prices, weights or preferences.

## より and ほう: follow the property, not the word order alone

> 赤い箱より青い箱のほうが軽いです。  
> （あかいはこよりあおいはこのほうがかるいです。）  
> The blue box is lighter than the red box.

**より** marks the comparison reference, the red box here. **のほうが** identifies the blue box as
higher on the stated property, **lightness**. That means a *lower* weight, not a larger number.

The order can change without reversing the relationship:

> 青い箱のほうが赤い箱より軽いです。  
> （あおいはこのほうがあかいはこよりかるいです。）  
> The blue box is lighter than the red box.

Do not memorize "the second item wins." Find the particles and read the predicate.
Likewise, **朝の切符より昼の切符のほうが安い**, "the midday ticket is cheaper than the morning ticket,"
makes the *morning* ticket more expensive. Read **切符（きっぷ）**, ticket, and **安い（やすい）**, inexpensive.
These can be particular discounted tickets; the sentence is not a general rule about train fares.

### Nouns take の; a plain verb clause can attach directly

| Compared item | Basic attachment | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Noun | Nのほうが | バスより電車のほうが早いです。 - The train is quicker than the bus. |
| Verb clause | Vるほうが | バスに乗るより歩くほうが時間がかかります。 - Walking takes longer than taking the bus. |

Read **電車（でんしゃ）**, train; **早い（はやい）**, quick/early here; **乗る（のる）**, ride;
**歩く（あるく）**, walk; and **時間がかかる（じかんがかかる）**, take time.
The exercises give particular journey times rather than assume that one transport mode is always quicker.

**方** is read **ほう** here. It is not the **かた** in **書き方（かきかた）**, "how to write."
For the actual verb-stem conversion and object-to-の restructuring, see
[かた method nouns](ease-methods-and-descriptive-forms.md#2-かた-turn-an-action-into-a-method-noun).
The formation drills request the basic noun or dictionary-verb attachment, without adding a nominalizing の
to the verb or expanding a noun into a clause. That is a bounded formation goal, not a claim that
nominalized activities can never be compared.

For **ほう versus ほか**, and comparison versus advice with **たほうがいい**, see
[the existing comparison section](koto-wake-and-formal-nouns.md#ほう-and-ほか-compare-alternatives-or-add-something-else).
Those harder contrasts are not extra choices in this installment.

## 一番: identify the set before choosing its maximum

> この三つのかばんの中で、青いかばんが一番安いです。  
> （このみっつのかばんのなかで、あおいかばんがいちばんやすいです。）  
> Of these three bags, the blue bag is the cheapest.

The set is **these three bags**, not all bags in the shop or the world.
**一番** maximizes the named property: 一番安い is cheapest, 一番高い is most expensive,
and 一番薄い is thinnest. Read **薄い（うすい）**, thin.
The predicate still determines the direction.

| Starting expression | Set phrase |
| --- | --- |
| 三つの店 - three shops | 三つの店**の中で** |
| この - this, before a noun | この**中で** |
| その - that/previously mentioned, before a noun | その**中で** |

Read **店（みせ）**, shop, and **中（なか）**, inside/among here.
この and その already modify 中; do not insert another の to make *このの中で*.
Both **中** and **なか** are recognized spellings in the practice.
The exercises keep the full この／その forms rather than contracted こん／そん.

The same three-bag collection can support a narrower statement:

> 赤いかばんより青いかばんのほうが安いです。  
> （あかいかばんよりあおいかばんのほうがやすいです。）  
> The blue bag is cheaper than the red bag.

This compares **two named bags**. It does not establish the green bag's price or prove that blue is
cheapest among all three. Conversely, a within-set maximum does not automatically rank every remaining
pair. The practice asks what the sentence itself establishes, not what might also be true.
Do not infer a mandatory minimum of three members as a universal grammatical rule.

## くなる and になる: describe a transition

Compare **高いです**, "it is expensive," with **高くなりました**, "it became more expensive."
The second foregrounds change relative to an earlier state. A price rising from 200 to 300 yen can
become higher without becoming expensive by everybody's standards.

| Predicate type | Change form | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Regular い-adjective | Replace final い with く, then add なる | 明るい → 明るくなる - become brighter |
| な-adjective | Base + に + なる | 静か → 静かになる - become quiet |
| Noun | N + に + なる | 店長 → 店長になる - become the shop manager |

Read **明るい（あかるい）**, bright/well-lit; **静か（しずか）**, quiet; and **店長（てんちょう）**,
shop manager. **きれい**, clean or beautiful, is a な-adjective despite its final い sound:
**きれいになる**, not *きれくなる*. The familiar exception **いい／よい** uses **よくなる**, "become better."

Inflect **なる** for the sentence:

- **静かになります** - will become quiet, or becomes quiet in the relevant recurring situation.
- **静かになりました** - became quiet.

The formation questions supply before/after evidence and request the ordinary polite past ending
**なりました** in kana. The missing part still includes the adjective's く form or the noun/な-adjective
に connection. Different tense, explanatory and expanded predicates are outside that named operation.

### A change does not have to happen without human action

> 掃除をして、床がきれいになりました。  
> （そうじをして、ゆかがきれいになりました。）  
> After cleaning, the floor became clean.

**掃除（そうじ）** means cleaning; **床（ゆか）** means floor.
なる foregrounds the changed condition. It does not forbid an intentional cause.
Likewise, deliberately accepting a new job can result in **店長になりました**.

If you only know that a person is well now, **元気です** reports that current state.
**元気になりました** additionally reports a transition to being well.
Read **元気（げんき）**, well/energetic. Neither sentence is a universal substitute for the other.
Both descriptions can be true after a recovery; the question's stated goal determines which claim to make.

The [retained-state comparison](koto-wake-and-formal-nouns.md#まま-with-nouns-and-adjectives-staying-the-same-versus-becoming)
contrasts なる with まま. That is optional later reading, not an N5 distractor here.
An earlier change and a later retained state need not contradict each other.

For making a quality/role change with くする／にする versus describing its becoming different,
see [actions, events and results](actions-events-and-results.md#2-make-something-different-or-describe-its-becoming-different).
The N5 なる exercises remain here; the new comparison does not make human agency incompatible with なる.

## すぎる: too much for a relevant standard

**すぎる** adds an excess judgment. The standard can be a budget, comfortable quantity, intended use
or the speaker's expectations; it need not be an official numerical limit.

| Starting expression | Formation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 読む（よむ）, read | 読みすぎる | read too much |
| 食べる（たべる）, eat | 食べすぎる | eat too much |
| 高い（たかい）, expensive | 高すぎる | be too expensive |
| 辛い（からい）, spicy | 辛すぎる | be too spicy |
| 静か（しずか）, quiet | 静かすぎる | be too quiet |
| 複雑（ふくざつ）, complicated | 複雑すぎる | be too complicated |

For a verb, use its **ます-stem**: a godan final む changes to み in **読む → 読み**;
an ichidan verb drops る in **食べる → 食べ**. Do not attach すぎる to the ordinary dictionary or past
form indiscriminately. For a regular い-adjective, remove final い; for a な-adjective, use its base
without adding な or だ. With **いい／よい**, learn **よすぎる** as a standard model, not *いすぎる*
made by mechanically clipping いい. The new exercises do not test that adjective's variation.

Because すぎる itself inflects as an ichidan verb, ordinary polite past forms include
**読みすぎました**, **高すぎました**, and **静かすぎました**.
Compare the corresponding ordinary predicates:

| Without the excess suffix | With the excess suffix |
| --- | --- |
| 読みました - read | 読みすぎました - read too much |
| 高かったです - was expensive | 高すぎました - was too expensive |
| 静かでした - was quiet | 静かすぎました - was too quiet |

The matched formation tasks request one of these two operations explicitly.
An ordinary predicate can describe a situation that was excessive, but does not itself express that excess.
Conversely, すぎる adds a claim beyond the requested ordinary predicate.
Keep the named added endings in kana; recognized spellings of the supplied word share one answer.

### "Too" is not always a complaint

In expressive speech, すぎる can amplify admiration:

> わあ、この景色はきれいすぎる！  
> （わあ、このけしきはきれいすぎる！）  
> Wow, this scenery is incredibly beautiful!

**景色（けしき）** means scenery. Here the speaker is admiring it, not requesting less beauty.
Do not turn the practical excess exercises into a universal ban on positive uses.
Casual **すぎ** without final る and more intricate negative combinations are beyond this assessed batch.

## High degree, large quantity and excess are not the same claim

| Expression | Core use here | What it does not establish by itself |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [とても](../words/とても.md)重い | very heavy | heavier than an allowed or desired limit |
| [結構（けっこう）](../words/結構.md)重い | quite/rather heavy | a fixed numeric strength, or necessarily too heavy |
| [たくさん](../words/たくさん.md)読む | read a lot | more reading than appropriate |
| 重すぎる | too heavy | one permanent limit applying to every person or purpose |

Read **重い（おもい）**, heavy. A load can be very heavy yet within a stated limit.
It can also exceed one person's comfortable limit but not another's.
The contextual questions specify the intended claim and relevant standard; they do not pretend that
numbers alone determine every possible speaker judgment.

The high-degree responses accept both とても and 結構 where the task permits them, not a made-up
uniquely correct strength ranking. とっても is an emphatic spoken variant; 迚も is a recognized rare
spelling of とても, not the recommended default.
The separate **結構です** acceptance/refusal uses and **とても...できない**, "simply cannot,"
need their own contexts; the linked word cards explain them. **たくさんの本** names many books,
while **本をたくさん読む** describes reading a lot. たくさん is not a replacement for とても before
every adjective.

## Keep the existing quantity practice

An **estimate** such as **一時間くらい**, "about an hour," differs from an **exclusive limit**
such as **三個だけ**, "only three items." Read **一時間（いちじかん）**, one hour,
and **三個（さんこ）**, three items.

The [existing quantity section](koto-wake-and-formal-nouns.md#practicing-quantities-and-action-patterns)
already explains shared approximate answers and their limits.
Five already-N5 exercises are grouped in their existing set: three estimates and two limits.
They keep their original IDs, levels and wording. Other, harder exercises in that set are not relabeled
to make the group look larger. Reuse does not imply a new vocabulary review of those older prompts.

For inclusive **以上／以下** versus strict comparisons, and quantities per period, see
[quantity, ranges and frequency](quantity-ranges-and-frequency.md). That supplement preserves these
existing comparison and estimate/limit owners rather than raising their levels or copying their cards.

For optional extensions, see [restriction and its scope](only-and-unexpected-inclusion.md#だけ-identify-the-limit-and-its-scope),
[advanced degree and extent](koto-wake-and-formal-nouns.md#degree-comparison-and-desired-extent), and
[representative or alternating states](japanese-listing-and-examples.md#たり-representative-activities-and-variable-states).
None is a hard prerequisite for the new groups.

## What the new practice covers

There are **42 new exercises**: 26 formation tasks, eight direction/scope interpretations and eight
contextual selections. Eight small groups separate comparison attachment, direction, set attachment,
pair-versus-set scope, change formation, change-versus-current-state meaning, excess formation and
degree-versus-excess meaning. All interpretation labels have readings and definitions before answering.
Paired word orders, predicates and scenarios share context keys; this is not 42 independent conversations
or a claim of unrestricted mastery.

The lexical degree words support those goals rather than receive separate synonym-guessing sets.
The tasks do not require ほか, まま, negative すぎる composition, or every construction that can compare
two things. The [verb foundations](japanese-verb-foundations.md) and
[adjective foundations](japanese-adjective-foundations.md) supply further inflection practice.
