# Correction and contrast connectives: not A but B, rather, instead, mind you

**Is the first idea wrong, or is it being reworded more accurately? Did something turn out contrary to expectation,
or is one option simply better? Are two facts both true, or does one replace the other? Is the speaker adding a point, or limiting
the one just made?** This article pairs eight connectives by the question each pair answers. English often uses
"rather", "instead" or "but" for several of them, so the practice always asks which job the connective does.

| Question | Patterns |
| --- | --- |
| Is A wrong, or is it reworded? | A ではなくて B, A というより B |
| The more fitting option, or contrary to expectation? | むしろ, かえって |
| Both sides true, or one replaces the other? | 〜一方で, 〜代わりに |
| A reservation, or another point? | もっとも, それに |

The JLPT labels in this series are editorial estimates. Bunpro's N3 placement is the primary reference, and there
is no official JLPT grammar list. それに is placed at N4 by Bunpro; it appears here as the partner of もっとも.

## 1. Correcting a description: ではなくて and というより

### A ではなくて B: not A but B

ではなくて says that A is **wrong** and B is right. It is the て-form of ではない, so it links two clauses. In
conversation it is usually じゃなくて; in writing, ではなく is common.

| Before the pattern | Form |
| --- | --- |
| Noun, な-adjective | 学生ではなくて (がくせいではなくて, "not a student") |
| Verb, い-adjective | 行くのではなくて (いくのではなくて, "not going") |
| Casual | 学生じゃなくて (がくせいじゃなくて), 行くんじゃなくて (いくんじゃなくて) |

> A：これ、田中さんのかさ？　B：いいえ、田中さんのではなくて、山本さんのです。  
> A：これ、たなかさんの かさ？　B：いいえ、たなかさんの ではなくて、やまもとさんの です。  
> A: Is this Tanaka's umbrella? B: No, it's not Tanaka's, it's Yamamoto's.
>
> A：これ、砂糖？　B：ううん、砂糖じゃなくて塩だよ。  
> A：これ、さとう？　B：ううん、さとうじゃなくて しおだよ。  
> A: Is this sugar? B: No, it's not sugar, it's salt.
>
> 集合場所は東口ではなく、西口です。  
> しゅうごうばしょは ひがしぐちではなく、にしぐちです。  
> The meeting place is not the east exit but the west exit.

### A というより B: B is a better way to put it

というより **rewords** A: the speaker reformulates what was said or might be said, in favour of B, which describes
the situation more appropriately. It says nothing definite about whether A is partly true; the point is that B is the
better choice of words. It often follows a noun or a な-adjective directly; だ before it is optional. In casual speech
it becomes っていうより (also written って言うより); と言うより, というよりも and というよりは are also seen. むしろ
often strengthens B (see section 2).

> 今朝の風は冷たいというより痛かった。  
> けさの かぜは つめたいというより いたかった。  
> This morning's wind was not so much cold as painful.
>
> 山田さんは先生だが、先生というより友だちのような人だ。  
> やまださんは せんせいだが、せんせいというより ともだちの ような ひとだ。  
> Yamada is a teacher, but she is more like a friend than a teacher.
>
> 彼は怒っているというより、疲れているだけだと思う。  
> かれは おこっているというより、つかれている だけだと おもう。  
> I don't think he's angry so much as just tired.

**Telling them apart:** with ではなくて, A and B are **different things** (two owners, two seasonings, two exits),
and A is wrong while B is right. With というより, A and B are two wordings for **the same thing**, and B is the
better wording: a stronger word, a more accurate category, a truer explanation.

## 2. Rather, or on the contrary: むしろ and かえって

### むしろ: rather, if anything

むしろ compares two options or descriptions and leans to the one that fits better or is preferable, often against
what the listener might expect. It commonly appears with Aより, 〜くらいなら, Aではなく and Aというより.

> 暑いのが苦手なので、夏よりむしろ冬のほうが好きだ。  
> あついのが にがてなので、なつより むしろ ふゆの ほうが すきだ。  
> I don't cope well with heat, so I actually prefer winter to summer.
>
> 途中でやめるくらいなら、むしろ最初からやらないほうがいい。  
> とちゅうで やめる くらいなら、むしろ さいしょから やらない ほうが いい。  
> If you're going to quit halfway, you'd be better off not starting at all.

### かえって: on the contrary, contrary to expectation

かえって marks an **outcome or state that is contrary to what was expected**. One common case is an action that
backfires: it was meant to help, and it made things worse. Typical set-ups for that case are 〜(よ)うと思って,
〜ようとして and 〜つもりで. かえって also describes states: a big room is expected to be comfortable, but:

> 大きすぎる部屋は、かえって落ち着かない。  
> おおきすぎる へやは、かえって おちつかない。  
> A room that is too big actually makes you feel ill at ease.
>
> 早く治そうと思って薬をたくさん飲んだら、かえって具合が悪くなった。  
> はやく なおそうと おもって くすりを たくさん のんだら、かえって ぐあいが わるく なった。  
> I took a lot of medicine to get better quickly, but it made me feel worse instead.
>
> 近道をしようとして細い道に入ったら、迷って、かえって時間がかかった。  
> ちかみちを しようとして ほそい みちに はいったら、まよって、かえって じかんが かかった。  
> I took a narrow road as a shortcut, got lost, and it actually took longer.

The kanji spelling is 却って; 反って is also seen, and the kana form かえって is common in learner materials.

**Telling them apart:** ask what the sentence measures against. With かえって it is an expectation (more space
should feel better; medicine should help), and the outcome or state goes the other way. With むしろ it is a second
option or description, and the speaker picks the one that fits better (Aより むしろ B, Aというより むしろ B). The
two can sometimes come close, so the practice uses sentences where the frame is clear and asks you to name the
function of the printed word.

## 3. Both sides, or instead: 一方で and 代わりに

### 〜一方で: while, on the other hand

一方で sets two facts side by side, and **both are true**. They may be two people, two settings, or two activities
carried on at the same time. で can be dropped (一方). At the start of a sentence, use (その)一方で.

| Before 一方で | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Verb, い-adjective | 働く一方で (はたらく いっぽうで, "while working"), 多い一方で (おおい いっぽうで, "while there are many") |
| な-adjective + な | 好きな一方で (すきな いっぽうで, "while (someone) likes") |
| Noun + の／である | 学生である一方で (がくせいである いっぽうで, "while being a student") |

> 姉は外で体を動かすのが好きな一方で、妹は家で絵をかくのが好きだ。  
> あねは そとで からだを うごかすのが すきな いっぽうで、いもうとは いえで えを かくのが すきだ。  
> My older sister likes being active outdoors, while my younger sister likes drawing at home.
>
> このアプリは使う人が増えている一方で、使い方が難しいという声も多い。  
> この アプリは つかう ひとが ふえている いっぽうで、つかいかたが むずかしいという こえも おおい。  
> While more people are using this app, many also say it is hard to use.

Do not confuse this with 〜一方だ ("keeps getting more and more"), which describes a one-way trend.

### 〜代わりに: instead of, in exchange for

代わりに says that one thing **replaces** another, or that one thing is given **in return** for another.

| Before 代わりに | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Verb | 行く代わりに (いく かわりに, "instead of going") |
| Noun + の | 父の代わりに (ちちの かわりに, "in place of my father") |
| い-adjective / な-adjective + な | 高い代わりに (たかい かわりに, "it's expensive, but in exchange"), 不便な代わりに (ふべんな かわりに, "it's inconvenient, but in exchange") |

> 今日は雨なので、公園に行く代わりに、家で映画を見ることにした。  
> きょうは あめなので、こうえんに いく かわりに、いえで えいがを みる ことに した。  
> It's raining today, so instead of going to the park I decided to watch a film at home.
>
> 引っ越しを手伝ってもらった代わりに、友だちに晩ご飯をごちそうした。  
> ひっこしを てつだって もらった かわりに、ともだちに ばんごはんを ごちそう した。  
> In return for helping me move, I treated my friend to dinner.

**The test:** after the sentence, is the first part still true? If the speaker went to the park **and** watched a
film, nothing was replaced. If the first part did not happen, or is paid for by the second, use 代わりに. With
drawbacks and advantages (高い代わりに質がいい, たかい かわりに しつが いい, "it's expensive, but the quality is good
in exchange"), both can be
heard, so the practice avoids that use when it contrasts the two.

に代わって ("in place of, on behalf of") and the formal noun かわり are covered in other articles; see the links
below.

## 4. A reservation, or another point: もっとも and それに

### もっとも: mind you, having said that

Sentence-initial もっとも accepts what was just said but adds a **reservation** or an exception, so the reader does
not take the first statement too far. The added sentence often ends with が or けれど, but it does not have to.

> この部屋は駅から近くて便利だ。もっとも、少し狭いけれど。  
> この へやは えきから ちかくて べんりだ。もっとも、すこし せまい けれど。  
> This flat is close to the station and convenient. Mind you, it's a bit small.
>
> 兄は毎朝走っている。もっとも、雨の日は休んでいる。  
> あには まいあさ はしっている。もっとも、あめの ひは やすんでいる。  
> My older brother runs every morning. Except, he takes rainy days off.

Do not confuse it with 最も (もっとも, "most"), as in 最も高い山 (もっとも たかい やま, "the highest mountain"). The
kanji 尤も is rare. ただし ("however, provided that") is a close neighbour for exceptions and rules.

### それに: besides

それに adds a **further point in the same direction**: another advantage after an advantage, more activity after
activity.

> この部屋は駅から近い。それに、家賃も安い。  
> この へやは えきから ちかい。それに、やちんも やすい。  
> This flat is close to the station. Besides, the rent is cheap too.
>
> 兄は毎朝走っている。それに、夜はジムで泳いでいる。  
> あには まいあさ はしっている。それに、よるは ジムで およいでいる。  
> My brother runs every morning. On top of that, he swims at the gym in the evenings.

**The test:** does the second sentence pull the first back (a drawback, an exception)? Use もっとも. Does it push
the same way (another plus, more of the same)? Use それに.

## Common confusions

| Pair | What decides it |
| --- | --- |
| ではなくて vs というより | Are A and B different things, with A wrong and B right (ではなくて), or the same thing, with B the better wording (というより)? |
| むしろ vs かえって | The more fitting of two options (むしろ), or an outcome or state contrary to expectation (かえって)? |
| 一方で vs 代わりに | Are both sides true (一方で), or is one replaced or paid for (代わりに)? |
| もっとも vs それに | Does the next sentence limit the first (もっとも) or add to it (それに)? |
| もっとも vs 最も | Sentence-initial "mind you" versus "most" before an adjective. |
| 一方で vs 一方だ | Two facts side by side versus a trend that keeps going one way. |

しかし, けれども and ところが also join contrasting sentences. On their own they mark that the next sentence goes against the
first, without spelling out whether it is a correction, a reversed effect or a reservation. The practice does not
contrast these general connectives with the patterns above.

## Practice scope and sources

The practice set has 31 exercises in four groups, all estimated at N3. In the first two groups, a sentence prints
ではなくて／じゃなくて／ではなく, というより／っていうより, むしろ or かえって, and you type a label for its function
(否定 or 言い換え; 逆 or 比較), defined in the instruction. These items check that you recognise each word's job.
In the other two groups you fill the blank; each offers the same two options on every item, and the instructions list
common spelling variants (一方, 替わりに, 尤も) as accepted answers. 代わりに already has noun-based practice elsewhere; here it appears with verb clauses as
the partner of 一方で. The exercises check recognition in context; they do not cover every use of these patterns,
and they have not been checked by a native speaker.
