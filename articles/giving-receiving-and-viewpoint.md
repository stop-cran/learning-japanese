# Giving, receiving and choosing a viewpoint

**Someone gives something; someone else receives it.** Japanese can describe the same transfer
in different ways. Ask three separate questions:

1. Who actually gives the thing?
2. Who actually receives it?
3. From whose side is the speaker presenting the event?

The basic verbs are **あげる**, **くれる** and **もらう**. This lesson uses ordinary transfers
of things, not the later patterns for doing an action for someone.

## One gift, two descriptions

You are speaking about your mother giving you some cookies:

> 母が私にクッキーをくれました。  
> ははがわたしにクッキーをくれました。  
> My mother gave me some cookies.
>
> 私は母からクッキーをもらいました。  
> わたしはははからクッキーをもらいました。  
> I received some cookies from my mother.

These describe **one gift**, not two. In the first sentence, 母 is the giver and the grammatical
subject. In the second, [私](../words/私.md) is the receiver, presented as the topic.
The cookies still travel from the mother to the speaker.

**くれる still means give, not receive.** Its inward viewpoint does not turn its giver into a
receiver. Conversely, もらう makes receiving the predicate: the person receiving takes the
subject/topic position in these basic participant-centered frames.

| Basic frame | Giving/receiving roles |
| --- | --- |
| Giver は／が + receiver に + thing を + あげる | The giver gives to the receiver, presented from the giving side. |
| Giver は／が + receiver に + thing を + くれる | The giver gives toward the speaker or a person whose side the speaker adopts. |
| Receiver は／が + giver に／から + thing を + もらう | The receiver receives the thing from the giver. |

The は／が column describes these frames, not a rule that every は marks a subject.
An object can be made a topic too, and understood participants can be omitted.
The [topic and inclusion lesson](wa-ga-mo-topic-and-inclusion.md#you-do-not-have-to-repeat-an-understood-subject-or-topic)
explains that omission does not automatically mean "I."

## あげる: giving from the giving side

Now you give a book to your younger sister:

> 私は妹に本をあげました。  
> わたしはいもうとにほんをあげました。  
> I gave my younger sister a book.

The speaker is the giver; [妹](../words/妹.md) is the receiver.
**あげる is not restricted to giving outside your family.** Your own act of giving to your sister
is an ordinary あげる example.

It can also describe giving between other people. Imagine you are reporting Tanaka's act of
giving Yamada a spare travel ticket, from Tanaka's giving side:

> 田中さんが山田さんに切符をあげました。  
> たなかさんがやまださんにきっぷをあげました。  
> Tanaka gave Yamada a ticket.

[切符](../words/切符.md) here is a travel ticket. Nothing in あげる itself makes the giver
the speaker: read the actual named participants.

These are ordinary gift descriptions. Do not replace every English "give" with あげる:
lending something, giving permission and giving someone a task are different lexical/construction
questions. The [basic-particle lesson](japanese-nouns-and-basic-particles.md#a-recipient-or-another-predicate-specific-participant)
uses 貸す, "lend," and treats the borrower as a predicate-specific participant rather than evidence
about giving viewpoint.

## くれる: giving toward the speaker's side

For an ordinary neutral report of someone giving **to you**, use くれる:

> 兄が私にノートをくれました。  
> あにがわたしにノートをくれました。  
> My older brother gave me a notebook.

[兄](../words/兄.md) is the giver. [ノート](../words/ノート.md) is the notebook, not the person
receiving it. Your brother need not be an outsider to your family.

The receiver need not literally be "me." Suppose a friend gives flowers to your mother, and you
present the gift from your mother's side:

> 友達が母に花をくれました。  
> ともだちがははにはなをくれました。  
> A friend gave my mother some flowers.

The speaker identifies with the mother's receiving side. This is a useful third-person use of
くれる: the mother is the receiver, while the friend remains the giver.

**Viewpoint is not a permanent membership list.** Family relationships often help establish
the speaker's side, but they do not mechanically assign a verb to every possible event.
The speaker can give to a family member with あげる, and a family member can give to the speaker
with くれる.

For third-person transfers, changing the adopted viewpoint can make another description natural.
An あげる account can foreground the giver's act; a くれる account can present the same giving
toward the side the speaker adopts. The exercises state the intended viewpoint when it matters.
They do not call every different framing ungrammatical.

The lesson's contrast concerns ordinary neutral giving. It is not a ban on every marked or
rhetorical use of くれる in the language, and it does not guarantee that a recipient actually
liked a present.

## もらう: put the receiver in the receiving frame

You can describe the teacher as the source of a map you received:

> 私は先生に地図をもらいました。  
> わたしはせんせいにちずをもらいました。  
> I received a map from my teacher.
>
> 私は先生から地図をもらいました。  
> わたしはせんせいからちずをもらいました。  
> I received a map from my teacher.

In these basic **human-source** examples, both に and から identify the giver.
に marks the participant selected by もらう; から makes the source explicit.
[地図](../words/地図.md) is the thing received, so it takes を.

Receiving is not restricted to the speaker. Return to Tanaka's ticket gift:

> 山田さんは田中さんに切符をもらいました。  
> やまださんはたなかさんにきっぷをもらいました。  
> Yamada received a ticket from Tanaka.

This describes the same event as the earlier あげました sentence, with Yamada now in the
receiving frame. Simply replacing the old あげました with もらいました **without changing the
participants' roles** would instead make Tanaka the receiver.

Nor is "someone received it from me" universally forbidden with もらう; an account can foreground
the other person's receipt or emphasize its source. These beginner exercises do not need every
marked viewpoint arrangement to establish the three ordinary frames.

## A particle does not have one role for every verb

Compare the same written frame:

| Sentence | Actual direction |
| --- | --- |
| 私は友達に切符をあげました。 | I gave the ticket to the friend. |
| 私は友達に切符をもらいました。 | I received the ticket from the friend. |

Read 私は as わたしは, 友達 as ともだち and 切符 as きっぷ.
**友達に names a receiver with あげる, but a source with もらう.**
It is not safe to translate every に as "to" and stop reading.

The に／から alternatives above apply to the human source with basic もらう.
They do not make the particles interchangeable at every position:
the receiver に in 私は友達に本をあげました cannot become source から while keeping
the claim that the friend is the receiver.
Likewise, the transferred thing takes を in these ordinary frames, not the source or receiver marker.

The participant-marker drills explicitly request the table's basic case particles:
が, に, から or を, without topic/inclusion additions or longer phrases.
Their subject tasks ask for the grammatical subject, rather than an alternative source or topic frame.
The two source drills accept **both に and から**, with an additional fitting quiz option.

## Form the requested verb, then keep the roles

All three verbs have ordinary plain and polite forms. Polite endings do not change the actual
giver and receiver:

| Dictionary form | Polite nonpast | Plain affirmative past | Polite affirmative past |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| あげる | あげます | あげた | あげました |
| くれる | くれます | くれた | くれました |
| もらう | もらいます | もらった | もらいました |

For the displayed あげる／くれる forms, remove final る before ます, た or ました.
もらう is godan: final う becomes い before ます／ました, but the plain past has **った**.
Do not build the plain past by adding た to the polite stem: もらいました and もらった
use different shapes.

The [verb foundations](japanese-verb-foundations.md) give the wider conjugation tables.
Here, formation questions name the verb and request either its ordinary plain affirmative past
or its ます-series affirmative past. The alternative form may be grammatical but does not perform
that requested conversion.

These verbs are often written in kana. The corresponding dictionary spellings **上げる, 呉れる
and 貰う** are also recognized in the same requested forms; they are not separate semantic choices.
A shared reading alone is not enough: 揚げる, "deep-fry," is not a spelling to substitute into a
gift sentence.

This is not a complete honorific lesson. Ordinary あげた versus あげました and the choice of an
honorific verb are different questions; a person's status alone is not a license to invent a blanket
ban on every sentence mentioning them.

## What the practice separates

- **Requested formation:** conjugate the named verb without silently switching the giver or receiver.
- **Participant marking:** choose a case marker with the actual predicate, accepting the shared source forms.
- **Role interpretation:** identify who gave or received across matched descriptions.
- **Contextual expression:** use the stated event and viewpoint, not a one-word English translation.

Interpretation answers use **前者（ぜんしゃ）**, the first-mentioned participant, and
**後者（こうしゃ）**, the second-mentioned participant. The question defines both in that order
before answering. They refer to the two people in the Japanese sentence, not to shuffled button order.
Neither "first-mentioned" nor "speaker's side" universally identifies the giver.

Several questions transform the same event. That makes the role changes visible; it is not a claim
that every question supplies an independent conversation or measures free-speaking mastery.

## Short bridges, not prerequisites

An actual receiver is not the same relation as an evaluative standpoint or an intended beneficiary.
[Standpoint versus intended benefit](about-targets-and-perspectives.md#standpoint-is-not-the-same-as-intended-benefit)
contrasts にとって and ために; the
[formal-noun lesson](koto-wake-and-formal-nouns.md#ため-おかげ-and-せい-a-goal-a-cause-or-an-evaluation)
also distinguishes purpose from cause. These are optional later comparisons, not required quiz choices.

**てあげる／てくれる／てもらう** describe doing an action for someone and deserve their own
construction and context checks. They are not assessed here, nor are a full honorific system or
the interpretation of every English phrase containing "give."
