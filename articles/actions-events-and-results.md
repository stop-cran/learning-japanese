# Actions, events and the state left behind

**I opened the window, the window opened, and the window is open describe different parts of a situation.**
Japanese can make those differences visible in the verb, its noun phrases and the ending.
Do not reduce them to "a person did it" versus "it happened without any cause."

The [basic particle guide](japanese-nouns-and-basic-particles.md) supplies the surrounding noun roles.
The [actions and states introduction](japanese-actions-states-and-time.md) already shows that ている
can present an action in progress, a resulting state or a routine.

## 1. An action on an object, or a change in the thing?

| Act on something: transitive | The thing changes: intransitive |
| --- | --- |
| 窓を開ける（まどをあける） - open a window | 窓が開く（まどがあく） - a window opens |
| ドアを閉める（どあをしめる） - close a door | ドアが閉まる（どあがしまる） - a door closes |
| 電気を消す（でんきをけす） - turn off the light | 電気が消える（でんきがきえる） - the light goes out |

**他動詞（たどうし）**, a transitive verb, takes a direct object: the thing the action acts on.
**自動詞（じどうし）**, an intransitive verb, does not take that direct object.
In the paired clauses above, the object with を becomes the changed thing with が.
The two verbs are lexical partners, not a universal conjugation rule: learn each pair and its reading.
Here 開く is **あく**, not another reading of the same spelling.

> 妹が窓を開けました。  
> いもうとがまどをあけました。  
> My younger sister opened the window.
>
> 窓が開きました。  
> まどがあきました。  
> The window opened.

The second sentence does not say that nobody opened it. A person, the wind or an automatic mechanism
could cause the change. Conversely, a transitive clause can describe an accident:
**うっかりコップを落としました**, "I accidentally dropped the glass."
Read 落とす（おとす）, drop something. Transitivity is not an intention detector.

Nor is **every を an object marker**: in 駅を出る（えきをでる）, "leave the station,"
it marks the place left, and 出る is intransitive. The paired object/change questions use the supplied
verbs and specified participants, not a rule that any を proves transitivity.
Topics with は and omitted understood participants are also possible outside the displayed basic frames.

## 2. Make something different, or describe its becoming different?

The adjective connection is shared, but the last verb changes the presentation:

| Starting predicate | Make it that way | Become that way |
| --- | --- | --- |
| い-adjective: 短い（みじかい）, short | 短くする | 短くなる |
| な-adjective: 静か（しずか）, quiet | 静かにする | 静かになる |
| Noun: 会議室（かいぎしつ）, meeting room | 会議室にする | 会議室になる |

For a regular い-adjective, replace final い with **く**. For a な-adjective or noun, use **に**.
きれい is a な-adjective despite its final い sound: きれいにする.
The familiar いい／よい exception gives **よくする／よくなる**, "make better / become better."
Inflect the final verb: 短くしました, "made shorter"; 短くなりました, "became shorter."

> 説明を短くしました。  
> せつめいをみじかくしました。  
> I made the explanation shorter.
>
> 説明が短くなりました。  
> せつめいがみじかくなりました。  
> The explanation became shorter.

The first frames someone as making the change; the second frames the explanation as changing.
Both can describe the result of the same deliberate editing. **なる does not mean that humans had no control.**
Likewise, 部屋を会議室にしました means "I made the room into a meeting room," while
部屋が会議室になりました means "The room became a meeting room."
For these completed noun-role changes, **会議室となりました** and **公園となりました**
also fit: と can present the reached result in a more formal/written style.
This is not a rule to replace every adjective's に with と.

This is change-of-quality or change-of-role する. Choosing a drink with コーヒーにする is a neighboring
use already introduced in [wants, plans and choices](wants-plans-and-invitations.md), not the same task.
The [comparison/change guide](comparisons-change-and-degree.md) retains the earlier なる exercises.

## 3. ている: a state need not be an action in progress

Compare the pair itself:

- **窓が開いています。**（まどがあいています） The window is open.
- **妹が窓を開けています。**（いもうとがまどをあけています） My younger sister is opening the window,
  or, in a suitable context, keeping it open.

Changing 開く to 開ける changes the predicate and its roles, not just a spelling.
Do not teach transitive + ている as invariably an action still in progress either.
For a plain report of the window's open state, the first is the basic frame.
It does not establish who opened it or why, and it does not deny an earlier deliberate opening.
Conversational **開いてます／消えてます** retain the polite ending while dropping い.
Polite grammar does not by itself require a fully uncontracted, formal register.

## 4. てある: the result of a purposeful action is in place

In the basic result pattern here, use a **transitive verb's て/で form + ある**:

> 換気のために、窓が開けてあります。  
> かんきのために、まどがあけてあります。  
> The window has been opened and left open for ventilation.

The window is presented as the thing in the arranged state. It is not the actor opening another object.
You need not name, or even know, the person who performed the action.
The purpose need not always be written out with ために; the construction can carry that perspective.

### Both が and を occur

> 明日の切符が買ってあります。  
> あしたのきっぷがかってあります。  
> Tomorrow's ticket has been bought and is ready.
>
> 明日の切符を買ってあります。  
> あしたのきっぷをかってあります。  
> I have bought tomorrow's ticket in preparation.

The が version foregrounds the prepared result; the を version retains the object of the preparatory action.
The latter need not explicitly name the person either. These are overlapping ways to present the same preparation,
not a blanket good-particle/bad-particle pair. は or も can also shape the topic or contrast.

A prepared result can be a reservation or an arrangement, not just a visible object left at a location.
Do not use a supposed minimum preparation time to decide whether を is grammatical.
The practice accepts both frames where its requested meaning permits both.

The last verb carries the ending: **開けてある／開けてあります**, **開けてあった／開けてありました**.
The new result-frame formation practice keeps to affirmative polite forms; broader negative and
contracted-state comparisons are not additional assessed distinctions here.

## 5. ておく: do something as preparation, or leave it that way

| Wording | What it foregrounds |
| --- | --- |
| 窓が開いています。 | The open state |
| 窓が開けてあります。 | The result of a purposeful opening is in place |
| 窓を開けておきます。 | Opening/leaving it open as an arrangement |
| 窓を開けておきました。 | The preparatory action was performed |

ておく is not simply future tense: its past form can report preparation already done.
Similarly, past **窓が開けてありました** locates an arranged state at a past reference point.
Neither past sentence alone promises that the window is still open now.

An already prepared ticket can be described with **買ってあります** or **買っておきました**.
The first foregrounds the preparation being in place; the second the earlier preparatory action.
Do not reject a compatible description merely because another wording is a textbook model.
For an explicitly *not-yet-performed* arrangement, nonpast ておく is the relevant choice among these forms.
Conversational **開けときます／消しときます** contract ておきます without changing that purpose.
Where the contextual question leaves the particle open, topical **は** is also accepted:
窓は開いています／開けてあります／開けておきます preserve the respective reporting perspectives.

The [verb-extensions article](verb-extensions-direction-and-aspect.md#4-doing-it-beforehand-and-leaving-it-that-way)
also explains preparation, leaving a state in place and conversational とく／どく.
This installment owns the contextual result/preparation comparison; the neighboring auxiliary installment
owns additional basic ておく formation and uses.

## What the practice separates

- Select between supplied lexical partners while retaining the stated actor/object or changed thing.
- Build the quality/role change while preserving the noun roles and requested ending.
- Form ている and てある with the supplied verb and frame, including both が and を preparation frames.
- Choose wording for a stated reporting purpose or stage; fitting alternative frames share credit.

Paired events and viewpoints share context keys. These bounded operations do not demonstrate unrestricted mastery.
The optional **N3** [まま comparison](koto-wake-and-formal-nouns.md#まま-a-state-remains-unchanged)
adds unchangedness; it is not a required N4 distractor here.
