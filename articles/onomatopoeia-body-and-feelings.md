# Onomatopoeia for body sensations and feelings

A vivid Japanese expression can describe **what an experience feels like** without identifying its cause.
These are language-learning comparisons, not medical categories or diagnostic advice.
The [onomatopoeia index](japanese-onomatopoeia.md) links all 200 word cards.

## Several ways pain can feel

| Word | Useful starting impression | Example phrase |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [ずきずき](../words/ずきずき.md) | Repeated throbs | 歯（は）がずきずき痛む（いたむ） — a tooth throbs |
| [がんがん](../words/がんがん.md) | A pounding head; also loud sound | 頭ががんがんする — have a pounding headache |
| [きりきり](../words/きりきり.md) | Sharp, gripping pain | 胃（い）がきりきり痛む — the stomach hurts sharply |
| [ちくちく](../words/ちくちく.md) | Little needle-like pricks | セーターがちくちくする — a sweater feels prickly |
| [ひりひり](../words/ひりひり.md) | Raw or burning smarting | 日焼（ひや）けがひりひりする — sunburn stings |
| [ぴりぴり](../words/ぴりぴり.md) | A sharp sting or tingle | 舌（した）がぴりぴりする — the tongue tingles |
| [じんじん](../words/じんじん.md) | Lingering tingling or throbbing numbness | 足がじんじんする — the legs tingle |
| [びりびり](../words/びりびり.md) | An electric-shock-like sensation; also ripping/rattling | 手がびりびりする — the hand feels an electric tingle |

People can describe the same sensation differently, or use more than one of these words.
The phrases do not set a severity scale. Adding voicing does not automatically make a condition “worse.”
Where useful, say the body part and the situation as well:

- 昨日ぶつけた足がずきずきする。 — The foot I bumped yesterday is throbbing.
- このセーターは首がちくちくする。 — This sweater feels prickly around my neck.

Those examples contrast a throbbing sensation with a prickly contact sensation, not two diagnoses.
[むずむず](../words/むずむず.md) adds an itchy or tickly quality:
鼻（はな）がむずむずする, “my nose tickles.”

## A bodily image can describe an emotion

- 締め切り（しめきり）が近く、みんなぴりぴりしている。 — With the deadline near, everyone is on edge.
- 早く外に出たくてむずむずする。 — I am itching to go outside.

No actual skin sting or itch is required in those sentences. Do not assume the figurative meaning applies
to every body word: learn each expression's own uses. Similarly,
[がちがち](../words/がちがち.md) can describe nervous rigidity, whereas
[ぶるぶる](../words/ぶるぶる.md) highlights trembling.

[ぞくぞく](../words/ぞくぞく.md) may express a cold shiver, fear, or a pleasurable thrill.
It is separate from 続々（ぞくぞく）, “one after another.” An event's sequence does not imply excitement.

## Surprise now, fear continuing

- 犬が急（きゅう）にほえたので、[ぎょっと](../words/ぎょっと.md)した。 — The dog's sudden bark startled me.
- そのあとも、音がするたびに[びくびく](../words/びくびく.md)していた。 — Afterward, I remained jumpy whenever I heard a sound.

ぎょっと often presents one startled, alarmed reaction; びくびく often presents a fearful ongoing state.
[びっくり](../words/びっくり.md) is a broader surprise expression and can concern a pleasant surprise.
These are useful tendencies, not an absolute pleasant/unpleasant division.

[ほっと](../words/ほっと.md) often marks tension easing:
無事（ぶじ）だと分かって、ほっとした — “I felt relieved to learn that they were safe.”
It need not involve a visibly happy celebration.

## Cheerfulness, disappointment, and a subdued mood

[うきうき](../words/うきうき.md) and [わくわく](../words/わくわく.md) overlap strongly in happy anticipation.
They should not be forced into competing quiz answers solely through slightly different English glosses.

At the other end, [がっかり](../words/がっかり.md) often foregrounds disappointment, while
[しょんぼり](../words/しょんぼり.md) often foregrounds a downcast state or manner:

- 遠足（えんそく）が中止（ちゅうし）になって、がっかりした。 — I was disappointed that the excursion was canceled.
- 子どもはしょんぼり帰った。 — The child went home dejectedly.

Both words can describe the same reaction; they are not mandatory successive stages.
[しんみり](../words/しんみり.md) may instead concern a quiet, reflective shared atmosphere:
友達と昔のことをしんみり話した — “I talked reflectively with a friend about old times.”
It need not mean a failed expectation or personal defeat.

[うんざり](../words/うんざり.md) concerns being fed up, which is not identical to being disappointed once.
Likewise, [ぼろぼろ](../words/ぼろぼろ.md), [ぐったり](../words/ぐったり.md), and
[へとへと](../words/へとへと.md) can overlap in being worn down without sharing every physical-object sense.

## Inattention versus an inadvertent act

- 寝起（ねお）きで[ぼうっと](../words/ぼうっと.md)していた。 — I was in a daze after waking up.
- [うっかり](../words/うっかり.md)かぎを忘れた。 — I absent-mindedly forgot my keys.

ぼうっと, like [ぼんやり](../words/ぼんやり.md), can describe the unfocused state.
うっかり frames an act or oversight as inadvertent. A person can be in a daze without forgetting anything.
ぼうっと also has visual haziness and fire-related uses; the mental-state gloss is not universal.

## Sources and limits

The linked cards identify their inspected [JMdict](https://www.edrdg.org/wiki/index.php/JMdict-EDICT_Dictionary_Project)
entries. The teacher's notes for [うきうき](https://mainichi-nonbiri.com/onomatopoeia/ukiuki/) and
[しんみり](https://mainichi-nonbiri.com/onomatopoeia/shinmiri/) additionally support their mimetic classification.
The three broad category tags are teaching aids rather than a complete taxonomy.
Examples here are original; neither a dictionary entry nor this review establishes a medical inference or
native-speaker certification of every teaching sentence.
