# Adverbs with negative endings

**Does your friend rarely drink, or never drink? Is the bus late, or will it never come?
Is an expensive restaurant always good, or just not necessarily good?**
Many Japanese adverbs announce early in the sentence how a negative ending will be read.
The adverb comes first, but you only know the full message when you reach the predicate.

| Adverb | Reading | Usual ending | Message |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| めったに（滅多に） | めったに | 〜ない | rarely, hardly ever |
| 全く | まったく | 〜ない | not at all (complete) |
| 決して | けっして | 〜ない, 〜ないでください, 〜ません | never, definitely not (a firm promise or warning) |
| なかなか | なかなか | 〜ない | not easily; it takes a long time |
| どうしても | どうしても | 〜ない／〜たい／〜なければならない | (cannot) no matter what; (want or must) at any cost |
| とても | とても | potential verb + ない | there is no way (someone can) |
| 必ずしも | かならずしも | 〜とは限らない／〜わけではない／〜とは言えない／〜ない | not necessarily, not always |
| 別に | べつに | 〜ない | not particularly (a mild denial) |

The JLPT levels used here are editorial estimates. There is no official JLPT grammar list;
Bunpro places all eight adverbs at N3.

## 1. How often, and how completely: めったに, 全く and 決して

### めったに〜ない: rarely

**めったに** reports a low frequency. Something does happen, but seldom.

> 兄はめったに電話をくれない。  
> あには めったに でんわを くれない。  
> My older brother hardly ever calls me.

> この辺りでは、雪はめったに降らない。  
> このあたりでは、ゆきは めったに ふらない。  
> It rarely snows around here.

**辺り（あたり）** means area or neighbourhood. めったに is commonly written in kana; 滅多に is also seen.

### 全く〜ない: not at all

**全く** makes a negative complete: zero, with no exceptions.

> 昨日の試験は全くできなかった。  
> きのうの しけんは まったく できなかった。  
> I couldn't do yesterday's exam at all.

> 彼の説明は全く分からない。  
> かれの せつめいは まったく わからない。  
> I don't understand his explanation at all.

全く〜ない is close to 全然〜ない, which you may know from N4. 全然 is more casual and conversational;
全く is common in both speech and writing. **説明（せつめい）** means explanation.
With an affirmative ending, 全く means "completely" or "really" (全くその通りだ, "that's exactly right"),
so read the predicate before deciding.

### 決して〜ない: never, firmly

**決して** is a strong, deliberate denial. It suits promises, warnings, rules and firm statements
about what someone will or would do.

> このことは決して忘れません。  
> このことは けっして わすれません。  
> I will never forget this.

> 危ないので、決して触らないでください。  
> あぶないので、けっして さわらないでください。  
> It's dangerous, so please never touch it.

**触る（さわる）** means touch. In a promise or a warning, 決して expresses a speaker's commitment.
めったに does not fit there: "please rarely touch it" is not a warning. 全く describes amount or degree,
so in a firm promise or warning like these, 決して is the expected choice.

| Question | Choose |
| --- | --- |
| It happens, but seldom | めったに〜ない |
| The amount or degree is zero | 全く〜ない |
| A firm promise, warning or resolve | 決して〜ない |

## 2. Slow, insistent or impossible: なかなか, どうしても and とても

### なかなか〜ない: not easily, not for a long time

With a negative ending, **なかなか** says something takes much longer, or more effort, than hoped.

> 待っているのに、バスがなかなか来ない。  
> まっているのに、バスが なかなか こない。  
> I've been waiting, but the bus just won't come.

> 新しい言葉はなかなか覚えられない。  
> あたらしい ことばは なかなか おぼえられない。  
> New words don't stick easily.

With an affirmative ending, なかなか means "quite" or "rather" (なかなかおいしい, "pretty tasty").
That positive use is practised separately; in this set every なかなか answer has a negative ending.

### どうしても: no matter what

**どうしても** says that every possible approach has been tried or would be tried.
With a negative, it means "(cannot) however hard I try". With 〜たい or 〜なければならない,
it means "really want to" or "must, at any cost".

> 何度やっても、この問題はどうしても解けない。  
> なんど やっても、この もんだいは どうしても とけない。  
> However many times I try, I just can't solve this problem.

> 来週の試合には、どうしても勝ちたい。  
> らいしゅうの しあいには、どうしても かちたい。  
> I really want to win next week's match.

**解く（とく）** means solve; **試合（しあい）** means match or game.

### とても〜ない: there is no way

Before a negative ending, usually a potential verb, **とても** means that something is far beyond
what is possible.

> 一人では、とても運べない。  
> ひとりでは、とても はこべない。  
> There's no way I can carry it by myself.

> こんなに高い時計は、とても買えません。  
> こんなに たかい とけいは、とても かえません。  
> I can't possibly afford such an expensive watch.

**運ぶ（はこぶ）** means carry. This is the same word as the N5 "very" (とても大きい), so read the ending:
before an adjective it intensifies, and before a negative potential it marks impossibility.

### Telling them apart

| Situation | Choose |
| --- | --- |
| It will probably happen, but slowly or with difficulty | なかなか〜ない |
| You tried everything, or you want or need it at any cost | どうしても |
| The task is out of reach from the start | とても〜ない |

なかなか and どうしても can both appear after repeated effort, for example
なかなか覚えられない and どうしても覚えられない. The first stresses slowness; the second stresses
that every attempt fails. Of these three, only どうしても pairs with 〜たい or 〜なければならない to mean
'at any cost'. とても行きたい is ordinary 'very' plus a wish, not とても〜ない. なかなか does not combine with
either ending.

## 3. Partial or mild denials: 必ずしも and 別に

### 必ずしも: not necessarily

**必ずしも** denies that something is *always* true. It does not deny it completely.
It usually pairs with **とは限らない**, **わけではない** or **とは言えない**.

> 有名な店がおいしいとは限らない。必ずしも行列に並ぶ必要はない。  
> ゆうめいな みせが おいしいとは かぎらない。かならずしも ぎょうれつに ならぶ ひつようは ない。  
> A famous shop isn't always good. You don't necessarily have to queue.

> 高い薬が必ずしもよく効くわけではない。  
> たかい くすりが かならずしも よく きく わけではない。  
> Expensive medicine doesn't necessarily work better.

**行列（ぎょうれつ）** means queue; **効く（きく）** means work or take effect.
The partial-denial endings themselves (とは限らない, わけではない) are practised in other sets;
here they appear as printed frames, and the choice is the adverb.

必ずしも also works with an ordinary negative predicate. The meaning is still partial: "not always".

> 家族でも、意見は必ずしも一致しない。  
> かぞくでも、いけんは かならずしも いっちしない。  
> Even within a family, opinions don't always agree.

**意見（いけん）** means opinion; **一致する（いっちする）** means agree or match.
The sentence does not say that the family never agrees, only that agreement is not guaranteed.

Compare **全く〜ない**: 全く効かない says the medicine has no effect at all,
while 必ずしもよく効くわけではない only denies a general rule.

### 別に〜ない: not particularly

**別に** plays down a reply about one particular thing. It often sounds casual and neutral.

> 「何か食べたい物ある？」「別にない。」  
> 「なにか たべたい もの ある？」「べつに ない。」  
> "Is there anything you want to eat?" "Not really."

> その映画は別に面白くなかった。  
> その えいがは べつに おもしろくなかった。  
> That film wasn't particularly interesting.

Compare: 全く面白くなかった is a strong "not interesting at all". 別に is mild and,
depending on tone, can sound indifferent. Answering 「別に。」 alone can sound curt.

| Message | Choose |
| --- | --- |
| Not always; there are exceptions to a rule | 必ずしも |
| Not especially; a mild, personal reaction | 別に〜ない |
| Not at all; a complete denial | 全く〜ない |

## 4. Read what the sentence claims

Because these adverbs set the strength of the negative, two sentences with the same verb can make
different claims:

| Sentence | Reading | What it says |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 父はめったに怒らない。 | ちちは めったに おこらない。 | Father does get angry, but rarely. |
| 父は決して怒らない。 | ちちは けっして おこらない。 | Father never gets angry. |
| 天気予報は必ずしも当たらない。 | てんきよほうは かならずしも あたらない。 | The forecast is sometimes wrong (not always right). |
| この本はなかなか読み終わらない。 | このほんは なかなか よみおわらない。 | Finishing the book is taking a long time. |
| 別に好きじゃない。 | べつに すきじゃない。 | I'm not especially fond of it. It does not say "I hate it". |
| 今の給料では、とても買えない。 | いまの きゅうりょうでは、とても かえない。 | Buying it is out of reach on the current salary. |

**怒る（おこる）** means get angry; **予報（よほう）** means forecast;
**当たる（あたる）** here means be accurate; **給料（きゅうりょう）** means salary.

## Common confusions

- **Positive とても and なかなか.** とても大きい is "very big" and なかなか上手 is "quite good".
  The impossibility and slowness meanings need a negative ending.
- **全く and 全然.** Both make a complete negative; 全然 is the more casual one. A colloquial affirmative
  use of 全然 (全然大丈夫, "totally fine") is covered in
  [quantity, ranges, frequency and scope](quantity-ranges-and-frequency.md#全然-can-also-emphasize-an-affirmative-colloquial-reply).
- **あまり and 別に.** あまり〜ない ("not very") describes a low degree; 別に〜ない plays down interest or
  importance. Both are milder than 全く.
- **必ずしも is a partial denial.** 必ずしも usually pairs with an ending such as とは限らない or
  わけではない, but an ordinary negative predicate also works: 必ずしも正しくない, "not always correct".
  Either way, it means "not always", not "never".
- **めったに versus 決して.** めったに allows that something sometimes happens. 決して does not.

## Practice scope

The practice selects one of these adverbs for a sentence with a printed ending, and asks what
a sentence with one of them does or does not claim. All exercises are labelled N3.
あまり〜ない, 全然〜ない and the partial-denial endings are practised in existing sets and are not
repeated as targets here. Positive なかなか is covered by a separate set.
