# Quantity, ranges, frequency and scope

**Does the boundary count? How often does something happen? What, exactly, does a negative deny?**
A number alone cannot answer these questions. Read its attached expression and the whole predicate.

The core practice covers numerical ranges, minimum benchmarks, unspecified quantities, frequency,
negative scope, and time/price frames. Existing restriction, listing and comparison exercises keep
their original owners. The short recognition bridges remain unscored.
The separate [ずつ distribution and increments](#7-distribution-and-increments) section adds
focused **N3** interpretation practice; it is not an extra N4 coverage claim.

## 1. Inclusive bounds versus strict comparisons

| Expression | Reading | Numerical range |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 六歳以上 | ろくさい いじょう | Age six or older: six is included |
| 五個以下 | ごこ いか | Five items or fewer: five is included |
| 十人より多い | じゅうにん より おおい | More than ten people: ten is excluded |
| 十人より少ない | じゅうにん より すくない | Fewer than ten people: ten is excluded |

For a numerical threshold, **以上 includes the lower boundary** and **以下 includes the upper boundary**.
An English gloss such as "over" or "under" can hide that important equality case.

> 千円以下の品物を選んでください。  
> せんえん いかの しなものを えらんでください。  
> Please choose an item costing 1,000 yen or less.

An item at exactly 1,000 yen meets this price limit. An item at 1,200 yen does not.
**品物（しなもの）** means an item or goods; **選ぶ（えらぶ）** means choose.

With **より**, the adjective determines the comparison:

> この傘は千円より高いです。  
> このかさは せんえんより たかいです。  
> This umbrella costs more than 1,000 yen.
>
> この傘は千円より安いです。  
> このかさは せんえんより やすいです。  
> This umbrella costs less than 1,000 yen.

These are alternative statements, not two simultaneous facts about one price.
**傘（かさ）** means umbrella. Here **高い（たかい）** and **安い（やすい）** describe price.
Do not translate より by itself as "a larger number": **より少ない** goes in the other direction.
Neither strict comparison includes equality.

The range questions ask whether a test value is compatible with the stated numerical range.
They do **not** identify the actual unknown quantity. A report of more than ten participants allows
eleven as a possible count without asserting that there are exactly eleven.

These rules concern numerical amounts. 以上 can also mean "the above" or close a list;
以下 can introduce following material. Do not apply a numerical inequality to every occurrence.
For ordinary paired comparisons and identifying the comparison set, reuse
[comparisons and degree](comparisons-change-and-degree.md).

## 2. A benchmark, an unspecified amount, and "only"

### Quantity + は: an at-least benchmark in context

> ここから駅まで三十分はかかります。  
> ここから えきまで さんじゅっぷんは かかります。  
> It takes at least thirty minutes to get from here to the station.
>
> 五千円は持って来てください。  
> ごせんえんは もってきてください。  
> Please bring at least 5,000 yen.

The quantity is presented as a benchmark, with contrast to a smaller amount.
The first statement concerns required time; the second requests an amount to bring.
This does **not** make every topic は a lower-bound operator.

**三十分くらいは** can soften the benchmark to approximately thirty minutes.
Context, estimation and the surrounding predicate still matter: a conversational estimate is not
the same kind of exact numerical rule as a formally stated 以上 condition.

### 何 + counter + か: some unspecified number

> 本を何冊か借りました。  
> ほんを なんさつか かりました。  
> I borrowed some books.
>
> 何台かの車が並んでいます。  
> なんだいかの くるまが ならんでいます。  
> Some cars are lined up.

**冊（さつ）** counts books; **台（だい）** counts vehicles and machines.
The first quantity accompanies the borrowing; the second modifies 車 through の.
Neither message supplies a particular count. This is not the direct question **何冊ですか**,
"How many books?"

Other productive phrases use 幾, as in **いくつか**, some things.
Counter choice and reading still need to fit the noun; do not freely exchange every 何/幾 combination.

### Reuse the existing limits rather than relearn duplicate cards

**五百円しかありません**, "there is only 500 yen," retains a positive amount despite its negative ending.
**一人も来ませんでした**, "not even one person came," gives zero visitors.
An estimate such as **一時間くらい**, "about an hour," is different again.

The existing [restriction and inclusion](only-and-unexpected-inclusion.md),
[listing and examples](japanese-listing-and-examples.md), and
[quantity/limit practice](koto-wake-and-formal-nouns.md#practicing-quantities-and-action-patterns)
already own these distinctions. Their original IDs and levels remain unchanged.

## 3. Repetitions per period versus each occurrence

### A period with a count

> 図書館に一週間に二回行きます。  
> としょかんに いっしゅうかんに にかい いきます。  
> I go to the library twice a week.
>
> 一時間に四本の電車がこの駅に来ます。  
> いちじかんに よんほんの でんしゃが このえきに きます。  
> Four trains arrive at this station per hour.

The frequency frame is **time period + に + occurrence count/quantity**.
In the first example, the earlier 図書館に marks the destination; 一週間に marks the period.
**一週間（いっしゅうかん）** is one week, **二回（にかい）** two occurrences,
**一時間（いちじかん）** one hour, and **四本（よんほん）** four counted train services here.

In the second example, **四本の** modifies **電車**: the count is part of the noun phrase.
The sentence also gives the per-hour rate. **この駅には一時間に四本電車が来ます** is another natural
arrangement, with an adverbial count instead. A drill asking for the count to modify 電車 requires
that noun-linking operation; it does not declare the adverbial arrangement ungrammatical.

A rate does not by itself promise equal spacing: four trains in an hour need not arrive exactly
fifteen minutes apart. The same broad per-period pattern can distribute a quantity, such as time
spent sleeping each day, rather than count only discrete actions.

### ごとに: each occurrence or unit

> 祖母に会うごとに写真を撮ります。  
> そぼに あうごとに しゃしんを とります。  
> I take a photo each time I meet my grandmother.
>
> 注文ごとにカードを入れます。  
> ちゅうもんごとに かーどを いれます。  
> We put a card in with each order.

Use a **dictionary-form event verb + ごとに**, or a **noun + ごとに**.
**会う（あう）** means meet; **注文（ちゅうもん）** means an order.
The spelling **毎に** is also used for ごとに.

The meeting sentence does not say how many meetings occur each week.
The order sentence distributes over orders, without specifying an hourly sales rate.
ごとに can also follow a time unit, so it is not universally incompatible with per-period descriptions.
The practice requests named assembly frames; it does not reject every other natural recurrence phrase.

These drills do not test **に** against **で**. Requesting the named に frequency frame is a teaching
boundary, not a declaration that every other time expression is ungrammatical.

## 4. Three different negative-scope messages

### Not even a little: 少しも + negative

> 今朝は水を少しも飲みませんでした。  
> けさは みずを すこしも のみませんでした。  
> I did not drink any water this morning.
>
> この部屋は少しも寒くありません。  
> このへやは すこしも さむくありません。  
> This room is not cold at all.

The negative denies even a small amount of the action, or a small degree of the property.
The second example is not a thermometer reading of zero degrees.

### Not few/little: 少なくない

> この町には自転車を使う人が少なくありません。  
> このまちには じてんしゃを つかうひとが すくなくありません。  
> There are quite a few people who use bicycles in this town.
>
> 旅行の費用は少なくない。  
> りょこうの ひようは すくなくない。  
> The travel expense is not small.

**少ない（すくない）** means few/little. Its negative denies smallness of the amount,
not the existence of the people or expense. The final ない in the base word 少ない is part of that
word, not an independent negative that can simply be removed.
No exact number follows from "not few."

### No counterexample: negative description + negative existence

> この箱には、使わない道具はありません。  
> このはこには、つかわない どうぐは ありません。  
> There is no tool in this box that I do not use.
>
> このクラスには、泳げない人はいません。  
> このくらすには、およげない ひとは いません。  
> There is nobody in this class who cannot swim.

The inner negative describes a possible counterexample; the outer negative says no such member exists.
**道具（どうぐ）** means a tool; **泳げない（およげない）** means cannot swim.
Keep the domain: this box or this class, not every tool or person in the world.
The statements do not give the size of those groups.

The practice label **例外なし（れいがいなし）** also accepts **例外無し**.
This is a spelling variant of the same no-exception label, not a different scope.

Do not confuse this structure with the advanced qualification **なくはない**.
For outside-versus-inside a named set, reuse the existing
[ほか／なか／うち practice](koto-wake-and-formal-nouns.md#common-formal-nouns-and-close-grammatical-neighbors).

## 5. Required resources, item prices and time spent acting

| Frame | Example | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| General resource noun + が + かかる | 時間がかかる（じかんが かかる） | It takes time |
| Specific amount + かかる | 三十分かかる（さんじゅっぷん かかる） | It takes thirty minutes |
| Required expense | 修理には三千円かかった（しゅうりには さんぜんえん かかった） | The repair cost 3,000 yen |
| Item + price + する | このカップは八百円します（このかっぷは はっぴゃくえん します） | This cup costs 800 yen |

**かかる** presents required time or money. Specific counter amounts commonly appear without が;
that observation is not a blanket ban on other contextually motivated marking.
**する** can attribute a price to an item. It does not automatically mean "approximately,"
"expensive," or "someone has already bought it."

Money alone does not create an exclusive する-versus-かかる test: an item's price and an expense
needed to obtain or repair something can describe related financial facts.
The reading tasks identify the predicate frame actually supplied, not every compatible paraphrase.

A price predicate can be inside a noun description rather than at the end of the sentence:

> 二千円した時計の修理に三十分かかりました。  
> にせんえんした とけいの しゅうりに さんじゅっぷんかかりました。  
> Repairing the watch that cost 2,000 yen took thirty minutes.

**二千円した** modifies **時計（とけい）**, the watch; it gives the item's price.
The outer **かかりました** gives the time needed for **修理（しゅうり）**, repair.
Identify which predicate the question selects instead of assigning the whole sentence one cost category.

Ordinary **する** also remains available for an activity with a duration:

> 今日は掃除を三十分します。  
> きょうは そうじを さんじゅっぷん します。  
> Today I will clean for thirty minutes.

This states time devoted to cleaning. It does not by itself state the time required to finish all
cleaning, or assert that the job will remain unfinished.
Do not learn a false rule that する can never occur after a duration.

The activity and its duration can also be presented through a noun phrase:

> 今朝、運動をした時間は五分でした。  
> けさ、うんどうをした じかんは ごふんでした。  
> The time I spent exercising this morning was five minutes.

Here **運動をした** describes **時間（じかん）**, time. **五分でした** identifies the length of that
activity, not a price or a required completion time. This is different from simply placing a duration
beside します. See [noun descriptions](noun-descriptions-and-activities.md) for how a clause modifies a noun.

The two ordinary-action reading controls retain **N5** labels; the defined response categories do not
import an absent N4 cost predicate. Four required-resource/item-price readings are **N4**.
More elaborate activity + のに resource descriptions keep their existing **N3** owner in the
[のに article](noni-and-concessive-contrasts.md); they are optional here.

## 6. Short recognition bridges

- **毎日のように（まいにちのように）** often means almost/nearly every day.
  It is not a numerical promise about how many days were missed.
- **各ページ（かくぺーじ）**, each page, uses the prefix 各.
  Learn actual combinations rather than attach it indiscriminately to every word or time unit.
- **この町は行きたい町の一つです。**
  **このまちは いきたいまちの ひとつです。**
  This town is one of the towns I want to visit: category membership, not uniqueness or first place.
- **以外（いがい）** points outside a named member/set.
  **水以外は飲みません（みずいがいは のみません）** excludes other drinks;
  **水以外にお茶もあります（みずいがいに おちゃも あります）** adds tea besides water.
  Read the predicate and particles. Do not confuse 以外 with **意外（いがい）**, unexpected.
- **の次に（のつぎに）** can describe rank as well as sequence.
  If Tanaka is explicitly the tallest in the class, **田中さんの次に背が高い**
  (**たなかさんのつぎに せがたかい**) describes the next position by height.
  Without establishing the reference's rank, "next after X" is not automatically second overall.
  Reuse the existing [comparison-set lesson](comparisons-change-and-degree.md#一番-identify-the-set-before-choosing-its-maximum).

**おきに**, concentration **ばかり**, and **たびに** are primary **N3** neighbors.
They are not required answers here. Interval expressions can need contextual clarification;
use explicit dates or a schedule when timing matters rather than an asserted universal conversion formula.
Concentration ばかり is also distinct from N4 recent-completion **たばかり** and the older optional
approximate-quantity wording. See the existing
[action/quantity patterns](koto-wake-and-formal-nouns.md#practicing-quantities-and-action-patterns).

## 7. Distribution and increments

**ずつ** follows an amount to describe allocation or repeated amounts:
**number + counter + ずつ**, **少しずつ（すこしずつ）**, or **ちょっとずつ**.
Do not attach it freely to any noun or verb. Learn the complete quantity phrase.
This is a useful bridge from [Numbers & counters](numbers-and-counters.md), but our
Bunpro-aligned Grammar classification places the tested construction at **N3**.

### An amount for each recipient or unit

> 三人の子供に、シールを二枚ずつ配りました。  
> さんにんの こどもに、しーるを にまいずつ くばりました。  
> I gave each of the three children two stickers.

The **per-child amount is two**; the **total is six**. 配る（くばる） means distribute.
Equal amounts do not tell us whether the children received their stickers simultaneously
or one after another. **ずつ alone does not establish simultaneous delivery.**

> 二つの器に、スープを百ミリリットルずつ入れました。  
> ふたつの うつわに、すーぷを ひゃくみりりっとるずつ いれました。  
> I put 100 milliliters of soup into each of two containers.

**器（うつわ）** is a container or bowl. Each receives 100 milliliters, so the combined amount
is 200 milliliters. The unit named in the question matters: one container or both together.

### Repeated amounts and groups

> 四日間、毎日新しいページを三ページずつ読みました。  
> よっかかん、まいにち あたらしいぺーじを さんぺーじずつ よみました。  
> For four days, I read three new pages each day.

The daily amount is three pages; the four-day total is twelve. **新しい（あたらしい）**
makes these new pages, not four readings of the same three pages.
The period is supplied by 毎日; ずつ does not independently specify "per day."

> 十人の学生が、二人ずつ部屋に入りました。  
> じゅうにんの がくせいが、ふたりずつ へやに はいりました。  
> Ten students entered the room in groups of two.

If each student enters exactly once, there are two students per group and five groups.
Count the requested unit, not whichever numeral appears first. This does not specify an
exact interval between groups.

### Small increments need not mean a small final total

> 春になって、この町も少しずつ暖かくなりました。  
> はるに なって、このまちも すこしずつ あたたかく なりました。  
> With spring, this town too became warmer little by little.

**少しずつ** describes small increments. Many small increments may accumulate into a large
change; the sentence does not quantify the overall increase in warmth.
It does not require an intentional human action.

> ちょっとずつ写真を整理して、昨日全部終えました。  
> ちょっとずつ しゃしんを せいりして、きのう ぜんぶ おえました。  
> I organized the photographs a little at a time and finished everything yesterday.

**整理する（せいりする）** means organize; **終える（おえる）** means finish.
**ちょっとずつ** also allows small amounts at a time.
Repeated or incremental activity **can already be completed**: ずつ does not impose an
unfinished present state. Read the final predicate and the supplied time.

### A cumulative total is not an all-at-once event

> タイマーの設定時間を三回、二分ずつ延ばしました。  
> たいまーの せっていじかんを さんかい、にふんずつ のばしました。  
> I extended the timer's set duration three times, by two minutes each time.

**設定時間（せっていじかん）** is the set duration; **延ばす（のばす）** means extend.
The cumulative increase is six minutes, produced here by three two-minute adjustments.
Calling that a six-minute total does not turn it into one all-at-once adjustment.
The starting duration is unspecified, so six minutes is not necessarily the final timer setting.
An exact repeated numerical amount and the unquantified small amounts in 少しずつ are not
the same kind of measurement.

## Practice scope

The original quantity/scope set has **28 tasks: 26 N4 and two N5 controls**. Four named formation tasks assemble complete
frequency expressions; 24 interpretations read supplied messages, ranges and predicate roles.
Five groups separate boundaries, quantity framing, frequency, negative scope and time/cost frames.
All external answer labels have readings and definitions. Related strict-comparison examples share
their scene keys; task counts are not independent conversations or mastery measurements.

The existing N4 restriction/listing and ほう／ほか／なか tasks, N5 comparisons, and five N5 estimate/limit
tasks are reused, not duplicated or relabeled. Supporting prefixes, rank/membership phrases and
higher-grade links do not receive a new exercise pack merely because they appear in an index.
Minimal-means **だけで** sufficiency and detailed interval/collocation practice remain separate future
goals; they are not silently counted as covered by ordinary restriction or frequency drills.

The separate **ずつ set has 16 N3 tasks in two groups**. Eight ask for a per-unit or whole-scenario
quantity using Arabic digits, without a unit; eight test a stated claim using **言える（いえる）**,
"follows," or **言えない（いえない）**, "not established." The blank is an external response slot,
not a missing part of the Japanese sentence. "Not established" does not always mean "false":
the handover timing, for example, is simply unspecified.

These are supplied-message interpretations, with elementary arithmetic where needed, not
free Japanese sentence production. Six qualifying scenario keys cover the sixteen operations.
Paired questions share their keys; sticker and container allocations also share one key because
changing the recipient and unit does not introduce a different scope operation. The sticker timing
questions retain that same allocation key.
No new prerequisite is imposed on the whole quantity chapter. Existing frequency, approximation,
restriction and range tasks retain their original owners and progress.
