# Respectful, humble and formal polite Japanese

Two sentences can both end politely while treating different people respectfully:

> 先生が手紙をお読みになります。  
> せんせいが てがみを およみになります。  
> The teacher will read the letter.

Here **お読みになる** respects the **reader**, the teacher.

> 私が先生に手紙をお読みします。  
> わたしが せんせいに てがみを およみします。  
> I will read the letter aloud to the teacher.

Here **お読みする** presents **my action toward the teacher** humbly. I still do the reading;
the teacher has not become the reader. Both sentences also use **ます** to address the listener politely.
See the ordinary meanings of [読む](../words/読む.md) and [先生](../words/先生.md).

This is a bounded introduction to **敬語（けいご）**, respectful language, not an exhaustive business-language course.

## Keep the people and the axes separate

- **Speaker:** the person saying the sentence.
- **Listener:** the person being addressed.
- **Actor:** the person doing the action.
- **Respected action-related party:** the person to whom an action relates in a humble-I expression.
  That person need not be the listener, or the physical destination of an object.

The teacher in the first opening example can be an absent third person: you might be talking to another student.
Conversely, when you ask a guest **この部屋をご利用になりますか**
（この へやを ごりようになりますか, “Will you use this room?”), the guest is both actor and listener.

**Plain/polite endings and the direction of respect are different choices.**
読む／読みます differ in ordinary ending politeness; お読みになる／お読みになります both respect the reader,
although only the latter has the polite ending. The
[giving/receiving lesson](giving-receiving-and-viewpoint.md#form-the-requested-verb-then-keep-the-roles)
keeps that ordinary conjugation axis separate too.

The official Japanese guidance distinguishes five categories. You need not memorize them all at once:

| Category | What it contributes in this introduction | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 尊敬語（そんけいご） | Respectful treatment of another person's action or referent; here, the actor | お読みになる |
| 謙譲語I（けんじょうごいち） | A humble action in relation to a respected party | 先生にお届けする |
| 謙譲語II／丁重語（けんじょうごに／ていちょうご） | Formal presentation of one's own or own-side action to the listener | いたす |
| 丁寧語（ていねいご） | Listener-directed polite wording | です、ます、ございます |
| 美化語（びかご） | Polished wording, without necessarily identifying an honored person | お茶（おちゃ） |

The interpretation exercises use the first, second and fourth labels for their selected predicates.
They do not redefine every kind of humility as 謙譲語I. More than one mechanism can occur in a sentence.
Nor does a respectful form prove sincere admiration or establish a person's intrinsic worth.

## Productive respectful forms: learn the base and its prefix

For compatible verbs, a useful pattern is **お + ます-stem + になる**:

| Ordinary base | Stem | Respectful polite example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 読む（よむ）, read | 読み（よみ） | お読みになります |
| 待つ（まつ）, wait | 待ち（まち） | お待ちになります |

With compatible **する** nouns, use **ご + verbal noun + になる**:

| Ordinary base | Respectful polite example |
| --- | --- |
| 利用する（りようする）, use | ご利用になります |
| 説明する（せつめいする）, explain | ご説明になります |

Do not leave the original する in **ご説明するになります**. The noun **説明** fills the middle.
Conjugate the endpoint for the intended time: **ご説明になりました** is polite past.
The [verb foundations](japanese-verb-foundations.md) explain the ordinary stem and ending operations.

These are **lexically restricted patterns**, not instructions to convert every verb mechanically.
The お/native versus ご/Sino-Japanese tendency has exceptions; learn the conventional prefix with the expression.
In these formation drills, keep the auxiliary endpoints **なる／する in kana**; recognized **御** prefixes are also accepted.
That writing boundary is not a claim that every other historical or marked spelling is impossible.

### Common special verbs

Some frequent counterparts are learned as words rather than generated from the ordinary stem:

| Ordinary meaning | Respectful verb | Usual polite form |
| --- | --- | --- |
| する, do | なさる | なさいます |
| 来る（くる）, come | いらっしゃる | いらっしゃいます |
| 行く（いく）, go | いらっしゃる | いらっしゃいます |
| いる, be present | いらっしゃる | いらっしゃいます |

> 先生は明日、学校にいらっしゃいますか。  
> せんせいは あした、がっこうに いらっしゃいますか。  
> Will the teacher come to school tomorrow?

The intended base and scene establish **coming** here. The same verb can instead report presence:
**先生はこの部屋にいらっしゃいました**
（せんせいは この へやに いらっしゃいました）, “The teacher was in this room.”
It does not automatically report a journey.

For recognition, **読んでいらっしゃいます（よんでいらっしゃいます）** can respectfully describe
someone who is reading. Here いらっしゃる participates in the ongoing **ている** frame; that use is not a new scored target.

Notice **なさいます／いらっしゃいます**, with their special polite stems.
Their polite past forms are **なさいました／いらっしゃいました**;
**いらっしゃいません** is polite negative nonpast. Outside those polite forms, learn the appropriate paradigm:
for example, plain past **なさった**, not a rule that every ending begins with なさい.

For recognition, also meet **ご覧になる（ごらんになる）**, respectfully see/look;
**おっしゃる**, respectfully say; and **召し上がる（めしあがる）**, respectfully eat/drink.
Their full paradigms are not newly scored here. This is another reason not to generate every counterpart from
[見る](../words/見る.md) or [言う](../words/言う.md) by a single mechanical recipe.

## Humble I: an action in relation to someone

Compatible patterns include **お + verb stem + する** and **ご + verbal noun + する**:

| Ordinary base | Humble polite example | Relevant situation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 届ける（とどける）, deliver | お届けします | Deliver something to the respected party |
| 持つ（もつ）, carry | お持ちします | Carry the guest's bags for them |
| 案内する（あんないする）, guide | ご案内します | Guide the visitor |
| 説明する（せつめいする）, explain | ご説明します | Explain something to the respected party |

See [案内](../words/案内.md) and [説明](../words/説明.md) for the underlying words.
The pattern needs the relevant relationship and a compatible lexical expression. It is not a device
for making every private action “more humble.”

> 姉が先生に本をお届けしました。  
> あねが せんせいに ほんを おとどけしました。  
> My older sister delivered the book to the teacher.

You can present an **own-side third person's** action humbly: the sister acts, and the teacher is the respected party.
This is not a first-person-versus-second-person rule. Some other-person uses are possible under the appropriate
relations too; the introductory own-side pattern is not a universal ban on them.

The relationship is also not simply the physical direction of the object:

> 私は先生から資料をお借りしました。  
> わたしは せんせいから しりょうを おかりしました。  
> I borrowed the documents from the teacher.

In **お借りする**, the teacher is the relevant respected party even though the documents come **toward me**.
Compare the ordinary [借りる](../words/借りる.md) and the
[actor/receiving-viewpoint distinction](benefactive-actions.md).

### Itasu: formal doing is not identical to target respect

**いたす** is a formal counterpart of **する**. In the official classification, bare いたす is **謙譲語II／丁重語**:
it presents own-side action formally to the listener.

- **説明いたします**（せつめい いたします）: I will explain, in formal own-side presentation.
- **ご説明いたします**（ごせつめい いたします）: the **ご...** pattern also marks a respected action-related party.
- **お待ちいたします**（おまち いたします）: a conventional combined form for waiting for that party.

Bare いたす can describe an activity without a particular respected destination.
For example, an employee's formal report **昨日は会議室の掃除をいたしました**
（きのうは かいぎしつの そうじを いたしました）, “Yesterday I cleaned the meeting room,”
does not make the room a respected recipient. **掃除（そうじ）** means cleaning.

Do not label every **お／ご...いたす** combination “double keigo” and reject it.
The official guidance distinguishes combining these mechanisms, linking separate honorific predicates,
and doubling honorific marking on one word. Some conventional doubled expressions also exist.
Detailed judgments about every combination are outside this introduction.

## Formal existence and the copula are different

**ございます** is formal polite existence in the following modern use:

> 会議室は二階にございます。  
> かいぎしつは にかいに ございます。  
> The meeting room is on the second floor.

**でございます** is the formal polite nominal copula:

> こちらが会議室でございます。  
> こちらが かいぎしつで ございます。  
> This is the meeting room.

The first locates a room; the second identifies one. Neither makes the room an honored actor.
Do not conflate **ございます** with respectful human **いらっしゃいます**, or use
**先生がございます** as the ordinary modern respectful way to say that a teacher is present.

| Meaning | Formal polite example |
| --- | --- |
| No blue bag is available | 青いかばんはございません。 |
| This is not the meeting room | こちらは会議室ではございません。 |
| Yesterday's meeting was at two | 昨日の会議は二時でございました。 |

Read **青い（あおい）**, **昨日（きのう）**, **会議（かいぎ）** and **二時（にじ）**.
Negative existence and a negative copula say different things; do not insert or remove **で** indiscriminately.

## Courteous requests: who will do the action?

The compact respectful request uses **お + verb stem + ください**, or a compatible **ご + verbal noun + ください**:

- **ロビーでお待ちください。**  
  ろびーで おまちください。 — Please wait in the lobby.
- **ここに名前をお書きください。**  
  ここに なまえを おかきください。 — Please write your name here.
- **この部屋をご利用ください。**  
  この へやを ごりようください。 — Please use this room.
- **ここに名前をご記入ください。**  
  ここに なまえを ごきにゅうください。 — Please fill in your name here.

**ご...ください is a legitimate respectful pattern.** Do not mechanically insert **して**:
**ご...する** can be a humble-I pattern, so the form and relevant respected party need to be considered.
This is not a rejection of ordinary **...してください** requests in their appropriate uses.
The [basic request lesson](japanese-requests-permission-and-rules.md) retains those foundations.

For a courteous **receiving-viewpoint question**, build **ていただけませんか**:

> 切符を見せていただけませんか。  
> きっぷを みせて いただけませんか。  
> Could you show me your ticket?

The listener shows the ticket; the speaker asks to receive that action. **切符（きっぷ）** is a ticket,
and **見せる（みせる）** means show.

The construction includes a potential step:

**いただく → いただける → いただけませんか**

Then attach it to the lexical **て** form: **見せて** or **書いて**.
**いただきませんか** is not that negative-potential form; dropping the potential step does not produce
the requested construction. This does not claim that the nonpotential sequence has no other uses.
The receiving auxiliary is written in **kana or 頂** in these drills; other marked spellings are not newly established here.

A negatively phrased question can ask for the **affirmative action**. It does not assert that the listener
will cooperate. Review [favor questions and action polarity](benefactive-actions.md#indirect-questions-locate-the-negative)
for the ordinary receiving forms.
No request acquires a universal appropriateness score just by becoming longer: relationship, tone and imposition still matter.

## Apply a viewpoint in a service scene

In ordinary customer service, a staff member can say:

| Whose action is being described? | Example | What the predicate does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The guest waits | お客様がロビーでお待ちになります。 | Respects the guest as actor |
| I promise to wait for the guest | 私が入り口でお待ちします。 | Presents my guest-related action humbly |
| My shop manager explained something to the outside customer | 店長がお客様にご説明しました。 | Presents an own-side third person's action humbly toward the customer |

Read **お客様（おきゃくさま）**, **入り口（いりぐち）** and **店長（てんちょう）**.
The manager is your superior, but belongs to your shop's side in this exchange.
That prevents the shortcut “always honor a boss's action, and only humble my own.”
It is a scoped service convention, **not** a universal ban on organizational titles or a permanent partition of all people.
The official guidance itself qualifies how such choices work in different institutional settings.

## What the practice covers

The 37 new tasks contain **27 formation/assembly tasks, six complete-message interpretations and four guided service transfers**.
Eight small groups separate the mechanisms, productive respectful forms, special verbs, humble actions,
formal doing, formal existence/copula, requests and service viewpoints.
Shared scenes keep shared context keys; different versions are not independent real conversations.

Ordinary request forms, ordinary plain/polite conjugation, actor/beneficiary roles and favor-question polarity
stay in their existing exercise owners. This introduction does not assess every special verb, lexical exception,
organizational convention or spontaneous choice of social stance. Recognition notes are not a claim of paradigm mastery.
The new scored mechanisms follow the inspected N4 curriculum anchors, an editorial study estimate rather than an official syllabus.
