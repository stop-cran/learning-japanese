# Water, weather, attention and social manner

These words add a particular impression to a sentence. Match the word's selected sense to the event, not just to an English gloss or a dictionary part-of-speech label. This guide continues the [introductory sentence guide](onomatopoeia-in-sentences.md).

## Drops, splashes and results

蛇口（じゃぐち）から水がぽたぽた落ちている。  
*Water is dripping from the faucet.*

[ぽたぽた](../words/ぽたぽた.md) depicts repeated drops. It does not diagnose a leaking mechanism. The grouped dictionary entry also contains ぼたぼた, but its wet-heavy snow/clay and old slow-motion senses are restricted to that reading, not automatically to ぽたぽた.

[じゃぶじゃぶ](../words/じゃぶじゃぶ.md), [ばしゃばしゃ](../words/ばしゃばしゃ.md) and [ざぶざぶ](../words/ざぶざぶ.md) overlap in repeated splashing. Their typical impressions are not precise water-depth, volume or container rules.

バケツの水で靴下をじゃぶじゃぶ洗った。  
*I washed the socks in bucket water with plenty of splashing.*

長靴（ながぐつ）で小川をざぶざぶ歩いた。  
*I splashed through the stream in rubber boots.*

Direct adverbs and と connections occur in these frames. Splashing during washing does not guarantee cleanliness; splashing through a stream does not establish that water entered the boots. A described activity and its possible result are different claims.

じゃぶじゃぶ can also depict lavish use of money. Conversely, the camera-shutter sense recorded for **ぱしゃぱしゃ** must not be copied indiscriminately to **ばしゃばしゃ**.

[ごぼごぼ](../words/ごぼごぼ.md) describes gurgling liquid and air. Water glugging from a bottle need not be hot, and a gurgling drain is not by itself proof of a blockage.

[ぽちゃん](../words/ぽちゃん.md) depicts a plop:

小石が池にぽちゃんと落ちた。  
*A little stone fell into the pond with a plop.*

A formation bridge converts ぽちゃんという音, “a plop sound,” to the と adverbial frame. It practises that particular conversion without declaring all other sound representations impossible. Neither the plop nor 落ちた establishes who, if anyone, deliberately threw the stone.

## Wind, flames and billows

[そよそよ](../words/そよそよ.md) describes a light breeze; it is not an all-purpose replacement for “gently” when handling an object. [びゅうびゅう](../words/びゅうびゅう.md) can depict wind howling or whistling. Their sentences give impressions, not measured wind speeds.

すき間（すきま）から、びゅうびゅうという音が聞こえる。  
*A whistling sound is audible through the gap.*

The という clause names the sound content before 音. Conversational っていう is another form. A task expressly building this noun phrase is different from adding びゅうびゅうと as an adverb to a later verb.

[めらめら](../words/めらめら.md) foregrounds visibly flaring flames, not an obligatory crackle. An emotional “flame,” such as 嫉妬（しっと）の炎, “a flame of jealousy,” supplies a figurative reading through the subject.

[もくもく](../words/もくもく.md) depicts billowing smoke or clouds:

煙突（えんとつ）から煙がもくもく上がる。  
*Smoke billows from the chimney.*

This visual mimetic is not **黙々（もくもく）と働く**, “work silently.” The shared pronunciation does not make the two one lexical item.

## Looking is not a motive detector

[きょろきょろ](../words/きょろきょろ.md) describes looking this way and that. [ちらちら](../words/ちらちら.md) can describe repeated brief glances, a flickering light or lightly falling snow. [じろじろ](../words/じろじろ.md) suggests scrutinizing or staring, often with an intrusive impression.

知らない人にじろじろ見られて、落ち着かなかった。  
*Being stared at by a stranger made me uneasy.*

Here the unease is explicitly stated. In a sentence merely describing a scrutinizing look, a particular hostile intention is not proved. Likewise, looking around does not prove dishonesty, and glancing at a clock does not prove boredom.

The する frames in selected tasks also differ: きょろきょろした reports an event; ちらちらしている describes a light's continuing flicker. してる is accepted where conversational contraction is allowed.

## Secrecy, clarity and directness

[こっそり](../words/こっそり.md) emphasizes concealment or avoiding notice. Secretly preparing a present is not wrongdoing merely because it is secret.

[ずけずけ](../words/ずけずけ.md) describes blunt, unreserved speech, often critically. [はきはき](../words/はきはき.md) concerns clear, brisk delivery. [きっぱり](../words/きっぱり.md) concerns a clear, resolute position.

- はきはき答えた。 — *Answered clearly and briskly.*
- ずけずけ私生活を聞かれた。 — *Was bluntly asked about my private life.*
- きっぱり断った。 — *Refused decisively.*

These impressions can overlap. Clear delivery does not guarantee truthful or considerate content. Bluntness does not verify a criticism, and firmness does not require shouting.

はきはきした話し方 means “a clear, brisk way of speaking.” This characteristic した clause describes a quality; it does not say the quality has ended.

## One spelling, different states and actions

[べらべら](../words/べらべら.md) can describe unrestrained chatter or thin, flimsy material. It is not automatically praise for language proficiency.

この紙は薄くてべらべらだ。  
*This paper is thin and flimsy.*

The material sense also has an attested direct な modifier, **べらべらな着物**, “a flimsy kimono,” and a change frame, **べらべらになる**, “become flimsy.” These do not license a universal な／する／になる checklist for every word. One task practises the direct adjectival connection; another explicitly practises change with なる. Fit alternatives to that visible operation, not to an unstated preference.

[ぐずぐず](../words/ぐずぐず.md) can describe dawdling or grumbling. ぐずぐずして、バスに遅れた links delay to being late for a bus; ぐずぐず文句を言う identifies complaints.

[だらだら](../words/だらだら.md) can describe trickling sweat, a meeting dragging on, or idling at home. Sweat flowing is not a criticism of someone's work ethic; a lazy day is not proof of a permanent character trait.
