# Movement, feelings and attention

These twenty words describe selected everyday movements, reactions and impressions. They do not form one interchangeable adjective class. Start with the sentence's verb, participants and intended sense. See the [introductory sentence guide](onomatopoeia-in-sentences.md) for the broader approach.

## A movement word does not replace the action

猫が木をするすると登った。  
*The cat climbed the tree smoothly.*

[するする](../words/するする.md) is one mimetic word, not two copies of the verb する. [すいすい](../words/すいすい.md) also describes smooth movement, and can extend to progress in solving problems. [ずんずん](../words/ずんずん.md) emphasizes vigorous onward progress, but can also depict booming drum sound. [ぐいぐい](../words/ぐいぐい.md) describes strong pulling or vigorous drinking in the appropriate verb frame.

With these movement uses, the word can modify the verb directly or with と: するする登る／するすると登る. This is not a recipe for adding と or する to every mimetic.

[ぴょんぴょん](../words/ぴょんぴょん.md) describes repeated light hopping; [ひらひら](../words/ひらひら.md) describes fluttering. Neither establishes the mover's happiness, and fluttering need not mean falling downward.

父は箱をひょいと持ち上げた。  
*My father lifted the box with apparent ease.*

[ひょいと](../words/ひょいと.md) already ends in と: do not make **ひょいとと** in this frame. It can also describe someone's unexpectedly turning up, not just a hop or lift.

[てくてく](../words/てくてく.md) foregrounds continued walking, not necessarily sadness or aimlessness. [ちょこちょこ](../words/ちょこちょこ.md) can describe small quick steps, but ちょこちょこ遊びに来る concerns recurring visits. It supplies no exact schedule.

## Match the sense to the predicate

列（れつ）がじりじり前に進んだ。  
*The queue inched forward.*

返事を待ちながら、じりじりしていた。  
*I was feeling impatient while waiting for a reply.*

[じりじり](../words/じりじり.md) also describes scorching sunshine. A sun-and-heat sentence does not automatically assert a person's impatience. Its sound and oozing senses are outside the exercises here.

## Experiencing a reaction versus causing it

私は知らせにびっくりした。  
*I was surprised by the news.*

その知らせは家族をびっくりさせた。  
*The news surprised the family.*

The subject of [びっくりする](../words/びっくり.md) experiences the surprise; びっくりさせる makes someone else experience it. The same participant distinction matters with [がっかり](../words/がっかり.md), disappointment, and [ぎょっと](../words/ぎょっと.md), a sudden startled reaction. Do not infer dangerous intent from a causative or bad news from surprise alone.

[うきうき](../words/うきうき.md) describes buoyant cheerfulness. [うんざり](../words/うんざり.md) describes being fed up, not simply bodily exhaustion. [しょんぼり](../words/しょんぼり.md) describes a downcast state or manner: しょんぼりしている and しょんぼり帰る have different sentence jobs.

The exercises distinguish simple past した, continuing している and clause-linking して when that operation is explicitly requested. Conversational してる is accepted in the continuing-state tasks. Some expressions also allow と before the する form; the accepted forms are word- and frame-specific, not a universal suffix table.

## Characteristic descriptions

弟はがっかりした顔をしていた。  
*My younger brother had a disappointed look.*

しんみりした雰囲気（ふんいき）になった。  
*The atmosphere became subdued and sentimental.*

The characteristic した clause attaches to the noun. It need not say that the characteristic has already ended. [しんみり](../words/しんみり.md) can also describe a quiet, heartfelt conversation, not just sadness or total silence. Named した-construction drills practise this particular description; they do not claim that every alternative noun phrase is forbidden.

## Relief, sighs and attention

無事（ぶじ）に着いたと聞いて、ほっとした。  
*I felt relieved to hear that they had arrived safely.*

[ほっと](../words/ほっと.md) already contains final と, as does ぎょっと. In ほっとため息をついた, the word can depict a deep sigh; the sigh's emotion still depends on context. Do not confuse it with the loanword ホット, “hot.”

[うっかり](../words/うっかり.md) presents an action as an attention lapse: うっかり名前を間違えた means “I inadvertently got the name wrong.” That is the speaker's account, not proof that an excuse is justified.

There are two sentence jobs in the wallet task: the adverb うっかり(と) before 忘れた, or the linked phrase うっかり(と)して before it. In 財布をうっかりとして忘れた, 財布を still belongs to 忘れた; the person who was inattentive is also the person who forgot the wallet.

うっかりとして、降りる駅を通り過ぎた。  
*I was not paying attention and went past my stop.*

The train task expressly allows うっかりする or うっかりとする as the starting expression and asks for its て linking form. The no-と version, うっかりして, also fits. Actual modern uses include [うっかりとして with forgetting](https://www.british-made.jp/stories/lifestyle/2014110400236) and [passing an exit](https://ameblo.jp/eigonaha/entry-12332562601.html); [NINJAL's missed-stop example](https://www2.ninjal.ac.jp/Onomatope/50_on/ukkari.html) uses うっかりして. These are word-and-sense-specific observations, not proof of equal frequency or permission to combine every adverb with とする.

[ぼうっと](../words/ぼうっと.md) describes an unfocused person or indistinct appearance:

霧（きり）の中に灯りがぼうっと見えた。  
*A light was dimly visible in the fog.*

ぼーっと is another established spelling. A form-building task may expressly preserve the supplied ぼうっと; this is a spelling scope, not a claim that the alternative is wrong. The separate flaring-fire sense is not practised here.
