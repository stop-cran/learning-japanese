# Voices, sounds and activity

The same expression can describe a sound, name something in child-directed speech, or characterize an action without any literal noise. Keep the surrounding verb and the intended sense in view. This guide continues the [onomatopoeia sentence introduction](onomatopoeia-in-sentences.md).

## Sound expressions and animal names

猫がにゃあにゃあ鳴いている。  
*The cat is meowing.*

犬がわんわん鳴いた。／子どもがわんわん泣いた。  
*The dog barked. / The child bawled.*

[にゃあにゃあ](../words/にゃあにゃあ.md), [わんわん](../words/わんわん.md), [かあかあ](../words/かあかあ.md) and [こけこっこー](../words/こけこっこー.md) can occur before a calling verb. と is also possible: わんわんと鳴く. The written verbs 鳴く and 泣く distinguish an animal's call from human crying in these scenes; a person imitating a call would be a different scenario.

These four words also have recorded child-directed animal-name uses. In a child's「あ、にゃあにゃあだ！」, にゃあにゃあ fills a noun slot: “Oh, a kitty!” In「わんわんを見つけたの？」, it is the animal spotted. Such noun uses are not a license to replace the ordinary animal name in every setting.

[ぴよぴよ](../words/ぴよぴよ.md) and [ちゅんちゅん](../words/ちゅんちゅん.md) depict small bird cheeps or chirps. They are associated with chicks and sparrows respectively, but not exact biological identification tests. The pinned dictionary's “moving gently” sense belongs specifically to **ひよひよ**, not automatically to ぴよぴよ.

[メーメー](../words/メーメー.md) is the existing katakana card identity for bleating. メーメーと鳴いた means “bleated.” メエメエ is another recorded spelling; spelling-preserving tasks retain the supplied メーメー. Katakana does not make a native sound expression an English loanword. Japanese references also use めえめえ for goats; this is not an exclusive sheep-identification test.

## Let the verb select the event

犬がにおいをくんくんかいだ。  
*The dog sniffed the smell.*

子犬がくんくん鳴いた。  
*The puppy whimpered.*

[くんくん](../words/くんくん.md) can accompany smelling or a small whimpering voice. The object においを belongs to かぐ, not to the intransitive calling verb. A closed-mouth picture alone would not establish either event.

[ぶうぶう](../words/ぶうぶう.md) can describe oinking, a phone or horn sound, or grumbling complaints. The separate child-directed noun can name a car. The exercises use the car noun with a stated toy-car context, not an unstated switch to a pig.

[はあはあ](../words/はあはあ.md) describes panting: はあはあしている. In conversational Japanese, はあはあしている can contract to はあはあしてる. The panting tasks here ask for interpretation with はい／不明, not production of those forms. Panting alone does not establish its cause or diagnose a condition.

## Sound, condition and secondary meanings

[がたがた](../words/がたがた.md) has several useful frames:

- 窓ががたがた鳴る。 — *The window rattles.*
- 机ががたがただ。 — *The desk is rickety.*
- がたがた文句を言う。 — *Grumble about things.*

Changing the consonant does not automatically transfer these meanings to [かたかた](../words/かたかた.md), which describes lighter clattering or rattling. Their sound impressions can overlap; there is no fixed decibel boundary.

[がちゃがちゃ](../words/がちゃがちゃ.md) can describe clanking keys or a cluttered drawer. Its capsule-toy-machine noun is a separate use. In がちゃがちゃで遊ぶ with a stated machine context, で marks the thing used; it is not simply “play noisily.”

[ばりばり](../words/ばりばり.md) describes forceful ripping and energetic work. ばりばり働く does not require literal ripping noise. The stiff/starchy sense has its own uses, including に; do not transfer that connector mechanically to a ripping-sound task.

[ばきばき](../words/ばきばき.md) depicts sharp cracks. Compare 折れた, “broke,” with 鳴った, “made a sound”: a cracking sound alone is not proof of injury or breakage.

[ぱちぱち](../words/ぱちぱち.md) covers crackling, clapping and blinking:

まぶしくて、目をぱちぱちさせた。  
*The brightness made me blink repeatedly.*

The 目を…させる frame describes bodily movement, not necessarily an audible sound. It does not imply that someone intentionally caused another person's reaction.

## Atmosphere and purposeful activity

[しーん](../words/しーん.md) expresses striking silence. Learn **しーんとする**, **しーんとした**, **しーんとしている**. The と is important in these ordinary constructions; do not simply append する to every mimetic.

[ざわざわ](../words/ざわざわ.md) can describe a crowd's murmur or rustling leaves. A crowd need not be arguing, and a silent classroom need not be empty. Neither expression establishes everyone's intention.

[せっせと](../words/せっせと.md) already contains final と:

旅行のために、せっせとお金を貯めた。  
*I diligently saved money for the trip.*

Write せっせと, not せっせとと. Diligent effort does not prove that the target amount was reached.

[きびきび](../words/きびきび.md) describes crisp, brisk movements: きびきび動く／きびきびした動作（どうさ）. This often gives a favorable impression, but does not measure work output or rank people.
