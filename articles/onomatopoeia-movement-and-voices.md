# Movement, voices and reactions

A word can help you picture an event without supplying the event's verb.
Compare **ころころ転がる**（ころがる）, “roll over and over,” with
**むかむかする**, “feel queasy / feel anger rising.”
The first keeps a rolling verb; the second uses する as its predicate.
Learn the word, its intended sense, and the complete frame together.

This companion to [Onomatopoeia in sentences](onomatopoeia-in-sentences.md)
practises selected everyday uses of twenty existing words. It is not a
universal suffix table or coverage of every dictionary sense.

## Rolling, turning and winding

[ころころ](../words/ころころ.md) often pictures a small round object rolling.
[くるくる](../words/くるくる.md) pictures spinning or winding, often with a
light, nimble impression. [ぐるぐる](../words/ぐるぐる.md) also describes
repeated turning or winding. Their overlap is real: there is no precise
object-weight rule dividing them.

- ボールがころころと転がった。  
  The ball rolled along.
- リボンをくるくると巻いた。（まいた）  
  I wound the ribbon around.
- 犬が木の周りをぐるぐる回った。（まわり・まわった）  
  The dog went round and round the tree.

With these action verbs, the direct adverb and the version with と both work.
The verb still matters: rotating in place is not necessarily travelling
around a route, and winding string is not the same event as rolling a ball.

The same shapes can describe change. 言うことがころころ変わる means “what
someone says keeps changing”; 表情がくるくる変わる（ひょうじょう） means
“someone's expressions keep changing.” Neither requires physical rotation.

A separate descriptive use is ころころした子犬（こいぬ）, “a round, chubby
puppy.” Here した attaches a characteristic to a noun. It does not necessarily
mean “was round in the past but is not round now.” The exercise explicitly
practises the direct する → した transformation. The dictionary also records
ころころとした; that is not a mistake, but inserting と is a different
operation from the specified direct transformation.

## Different ways of moving

| Word | What to notice |
| --- | --- |
| [ぶらぶら](../words/ぶらぶら.md) | A leisurely, aimless stroll; also dangling or idling in other frames |
| [うろうろ](../words/うろうろ.md) | Uncertain or restless wandering |
| [すたすた](../words/すたすた.md) | Brisk, unhesitating walking |
| [とぼとぼ](../words/とぼとぼ.md) | Slow, heavy steps, often with a tired or dejected impression |
| [のろのろ](../words/のろのろ.md) | Sluggish movement or progress, not limited to walking |
| [よちよち](../words/よちよち.md) | Small, uncertain steps, especially while learning to walk |
| [ふらふら](../words/ふらふら.md) | Physical unsteadiness, or a separately selected wavering/aimless sense |

These are descriptions, not diagnoses or reliable character judgments.
A person can wander うろうろ because they cannot find an exit. That does not
make them suspicious. すたすた describes a brisk gait, not every action done
quickly. A slow sightseeing walk need not be とぼとぼ.

The manner can reach its verb across other words:

彼はとぼとぼ家に帰った。（かえった）  
He trudged home.

家に names the destination; とぼとぼ still describes returning. Do not assume
that a blank before a noun must modify that noun.

Some words also have useful する frames:

- うろうろしている — be wandering about
- のろのろしないで — don't dawdle
- 家でぶらぶらした — loafed around at home
- 足をぶらぶらさせる（あし） — make one's dangling legs swing

The last is causative: the legs are what someone makes move. The adjective-like
dangling uses of ぶらぶら do not establish that な works with every strolling
or idling use.

よちよち歩き（あるき） is the noun “toddling” or “a toddling gait.” This batch
practises the walking-verb frames instead. Its two toddler examples are useful
repetition, not two independent areas of transfer.

## The subject and verb select the sense

[はらはら](../words/はらはら.md) illustrates why the word alone is not enough:

- 子供を見て、はらはらした。  
  Watching the child, I felt anxious about what might happen.
- 花びらがはらはらと落ちた。（はなびら・おちた）  
  Petals fluttered down.

The observer experiences suspense; the petals do not. Tears can also fall
はらはらと. The suspense exercise uses an explicitly supplied direct
はらはらする paradigm, rather than deriving every possible connector from
the falling sense.

[ふらふら](../words/ふらふら.md) likewise needs its frame. ふらふら歩く can
describe unsteady steps; 気持ちがふらふらしている（きもち） describes wavering
resolve; ふらふらと店に入る（はいる） can mean drifting into a shop on a whim.
The last two do not necessarily imply dizziness.

## Feelings, behavior and the speaker's stance

[びくびく](../words/びくびく.md) highlights apprehension or fearful trembling.
[おどおど](../words/おどおど.md) highlights timid, hesitant behavior that others
can notice. These can overlap, but feeling nervous does not always mean visibly
acting timidly. Neither is simply a synonym for polite behavior.

[むかむか](../words/むかむか.md) can describe nausea or rising anger:

- 胃がむかむかする。（い）  
  My stomach feels queasy.
- その言葉を思い出すと、むかむかする。（ことば・おもいだす）  
  Remembering those words makes my anger rise.

むかむかとする also occurs. The nausea example does not assert actual vomiting;
the anger example does not assert stomach illness.

[もやもや](../words/もやもや.md) can describe visual haze or unresolved unease.
Its feeling use can also be a noun:

胸のもやもやを言葉にした。（むね・ことば）  
I put my unresolved unease into words.

Here を marks what was expressed. In もやもやが残る（のこる）, “an unresolved
feeling remains,” が marks the feeling as the subject. Neither construction is
the same as an adverb with と. Giving the feeling a name does not necessarily
resolve it.

[くよくよ](../words/くよくよ.md) emphasizes repeatedly brooding or fretting;
[めそめそ](../words/めそめそ.md) describes tearful whimpering or snivelling.
Someone can brood without crying. めそめそ often sounds belittling or impatient.
Similarly, くよくよしないで, “don't dwell on it,” can sound dismissive even when
intended as encouragement. Context matters beyond the bare dictionary gloss.

## A grin is not necessarily a sound

[にやにや](../words/にやにや.md) describes a grin or smirk, often with private
amusement or satisfaction. It can suggest an unfavorable observer's judgment,
but does not by itself prove malice.

弟はにやにやと笑った。（おとうと・わらった）  
My younger brother grinned to himself.

笑う can mean smiling or laughing. This example does not require laughter
aloud. にやにやする and にやにやした顔（かお） are also useful frames.
The direct した formation exercise is deliberately narrower than an open
description: にやにやしている顔 is not universally wrong merely because it
does not perform that exact transformation.

For audible laughter, compare [げらげら](../words/げらげら.md), loud and
unrestrained, with [くすくす](../words/くすくす.md), quiet and restrained.
Both ordinarily modify 笑う, directly or with と. Quiet giggling can be
affectionate, embarrassed or mocking; loud laughter can be appropriate or
inappropriate. The adverb does not supply the motive.

## Answering these tasks

Most tasks supply a word and a sense, then ask you to fit that word into a
particular sentence. Read the English operation carefully:

- A **word-plus-particle** task keeps the printed action verb. Do not replace it
  or add an extra clause such as “while doing …”.
- A **supplied する-frame** task keeps the printed ending; do not repeat it.
  Where both direct and と attachments fit the selected frame, both are accepted.
- A **formation scaffold** requests the stated transformation, not every
  possible paraphrase. These tasks are explicitly labelled.
- A **choose-and-copy** task asks for exactly one of the printed words.
  Its wrong choice may be perfectly grammatical but describe the wrong stated gait
  or laughter. It is not a universal ban on that expression.

These tasks request **hiragana** to keep their spelling scope explicit.
The app does not automatically fold kana or grade unrestricted paraphrases.
Fill only the blank without extra punctuation. If two correct buttons are
shown, choose either one; accepted typed alternatives remain valid even when
not displayed.

Use **All** or **Unclassified** for new Grammar material and check the separate
browser filter if the article set is hidden. These tasks carry no new JLPT
labels or hard gates. The [pilot guide](onomatopoeia-in-sentences.md) explains
the shared controls and the distinction between practice and demonstrated
transfer. Learning benefit and owner-trial results remain unverified.
