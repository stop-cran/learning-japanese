# Ease, methods and descriptive forms

**Is a book easy to read? How do you read a name? Did someone speak for a long time, or measure a length?**
These questions use related building blocks, but the resulting expressions do different jobs.

| Expression | Reading | Job here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 読みやすい | よみやすい | Easy to read: a verb-based adjective |
| 読み方 | よみかた | A reading or way of reading: a noun |
| 長く話す | ながくはなす | Talk for a long time: describe the action's duration |
| 長さ | ながさ | Length: name a property as a noun |

The core practice covers these productive transformations. The final short bridges introduce related
descriptions for recognition; they are not additional scored groups.

## 1. やすい, にくい and づらい: start from the verb stem

Use the **ます-stem**, not the whole dictionary or past form:

| Verb type | Example base | Stem | With やすい |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Godan: change the final u-row sound to its i-row counterpart | 読む（よむ）, read | 読み | 読みやすい |
| Ichidan: remove final る | 食べる（たべる）, eat | 食べ | 食べやすい |
| Irregular する | 勉強する（べんきょうする）, study | 勉強し | 勉強しやすい |

Do not derive *読むやすい* or *食べたやすい*. Godan verbs ending in る still change to り:
**座る（すわる） → 座りづらい**, difficult/uncomfortable to sit.
For the underlying verb classes and stem formation, see
[Japanese verb foundations](japanese-verb-foundations.md).

### Ease and difficulty are descriptions, not automatic ability judgments

> この本は読みやすいです。  
> このほんは よみやすいです。  
> This book is easy to read.
>
> この画面は見にくいです。  
> このがめんは みにくいです。  
> This screen is difficult to look at.

**やすい** expresses ease here; **にくい** expresses difficulty. The standalone price adjective
**安い（やすい）**, inexpensive, is not the meaning of 読みやすい.
Something can be difficult yet possible, or easy without having been done already.
Keep this distinct from [ability and perception](ability-and-perception.md).

**づらい** often foregrounds difficulty or burden experienced in carrying out an action:

> このいすは座りづらいです。  
> このいすは すわりづらいです。  
> This chair is uncomfortable/difficult to sit on.

Do **not** memorize "にくい is objective; づらい is subjective" as an exclusive choice rule.
Physical and psychological difficulty can overlap, and にくい can describe personal strain too.
For someone struggling with tiny print, **読みにくい** and **読みづらい** can both fit.
The practice puts fitting にくい／づらい responses in the same difficulty answer class.
Separate named-suffix drills ask you to construct a specified form, not to declare its neighbor impossible.

The teaching spelling is **づらい**. The formation questions explicitly request the displayed hiragana
suffix while allowing supported spellings of the verb base; this is a bounded writing operation.

### やすい also describes proneness

> このおもちゃは壊れやすいです。  
> このおもちゃは こわれやすいです。  
> This toy breaks easily / is prone to breaking.
>
> 昨日、この道は滑りやすかったです。  
> きのう、このみちは すべりやすかったです。  
> Yesterday this road was slippery / easy to slip on.

The toy is not deliberately trying to break, and slipping need not be intentional.
These describe a tendency or susceptibility, not necessarily ease for a purposeful performer.
Neither sentence alone proves that a toy actually broke or that a person slipped.
The prone/likely use is part of this lesson's practice, not silently excluded from やすい.

### Inflect the resulting adjective

The suffixes behave as **い-adjectives**:

| Nonpast | Past | Negative nonpast |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 読みやすい | 読みやすかった | 読みやすくない |
| 歩きにくい | 歩きにくかった | 歩きにくくない |
| 座りづらい | 座りづらかった | 座りづらくない |

Read **歩く（あるく）**, walk. Add ordinary polite です after the relevant adjective form,
as in 読みやすかったです. They can also modify nouns: **読みやすい本**, an easy-to-read book.
See [the adjective endings](japanese-adjective-foundations.md#time-and-negation) for the underlying pattern.
The new drills practise affirmative nonpast/past formation; negative and noun-modifying examples here
are supporting explanations, not separately assessed facets.

"Not difficult" need not claim the same degree of ease as an affirmative "easy."
An exercise asking for one affirmative description does not accept a negated opposite merely because
both could be compatible with the wider situation.

## 2. かた: turn an action into a method noun

**Verb stem + 方（かた）** names a way of doing the action.
It is not the comparison **方（ほう）** in
[AよりBのほうが...](comparisons-change-and-degree.md#より-and-ほう-follow-the-property-not-the-word-order-alone).

| Starting action | Method noun phrase | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 名前を書く（なまえをかく） | 名前の書き方（なまえのかきかた） | How to write the name |
| 野菜を食べる（やさいをたべる） | 野菜の食べ方（やさいのたべかた） | A way to eat the vegetables |
| 日本語を勉強する（にほんごをべんきょうする） | 日本語の勉強のしかた | A way to study Japanese |
| 予約する（よやくする） | 予約のしかた | How to make a reservation |

The result is a **noun phrase**, so its internal organization changes:

- An ordinary verb's object becomes a **の** modifier here: 名前**を**書く → 名前**の**書き方.
- For the supplied する nouns, use **Nのしかた**. Do not leave the dictionary する before のしかた:
  勉強**のしかた**, not *勉強するのしかた*.
- **しかた／仕方（しかた）** is the method noun built on する. **かた／方** are spellings of the
  method suffix, not two different methods.

> 名前の書き方を教えてください。  
> なまえのかきかたを おしえてください。  
> Please tell me how to write the name.

The final を now marks the **whole method phrase** as the object of 教える（おしえる）, tell/teach.
It does not restore the original internal 名前を.
For the opening question about reading a name, **この名前の読み方が分かりません**
（このなまえのよみかたがわかりません）means "I do not know how to read this name."
Here the noun names its reading, not a comparison between alternative names.

Destination expressions can also occur with の, as in **新宿の行き方（しんじゅくのいきかた）**,
how to get to Shinjuku, illustrated in the linked method-noun reference.
This is not a universal instruction to replace every original case marker with の in every possible phrase.
The new scored argument conversions are the object and する-noun cases shown in the table.

## 3. く and に: describe an action

| Base | Adverbial form | Example meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 長い（ながい）, long | 長く | 長く話す: talk for a long time |
| 高い（たかい）, high | 高く | 高く投げる: throw high |
| 静か（しずか）, quiet | 静かに | 静かに話す: speak quietly |
| きれい, neat/beautiful | きれいに | きれいに書く: write neatly |
| いい／よい, good | よく | よく考える: think carefully/well in this context |

For an **い-adjective**, replace final い with **く**. For a **な-adjective**, keep its base and add **に**,
not なに or だに. **きれい** keeps its final い because it follows the な pattern.
**いい** uses the irregular よ- stem. See the
[basic adjective distinction](japanese-adjective-foundations.md#two-basic-patterns).

An English translation need not end in "-ly": 長く can express duration.
よく has other contextual uses, including frequency; not every よく means "carefully."
The practice supplies the intended sense and asks for an action modifier rather than a finite predicate.

### Do not require the modifier to touch the verb

> 丁寧にこの名前を書いてください。  
> ていねいに このなまえを かいてください。  
> Please write this name carefully.

**丁寧に** modifies the writing even though この名前を intervenes.
It does not become an adjective modifying 名前 merely because a noun follows next.
Word order is flexible within an appropriate clause and scope; that does not license inserting an
adverb arbitrarily inside another phrase.

**く／にする** can instead build a resultative change predicate: 字を大きくする（じをおおきくする）,
make the lettering larger. That scored contrast with **なる** stays in
[actions, events and results](actions-events-and-results.md#2-make-something-different-or-describe-its-becoming-different),
not in a duplicate group here.

## 4. さ: name the degree or quality

| Base | Derived noun | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 高い（たかい） | 高さ（たかさ） | Height |
| 長い（ながい） | 長さ（ながさ） | Length |
| 便利（べんり） | 便利さ（べんりさ） | Convenience |
| きれい | きれいさ | Beauty/cleanliness, according to context |
| いい／よい | よさ | Good qualities; merit |

Remove an い-adjective's final い, but keep the whole な-adjective base, then add **さ**.
With いい use **よさ**, not *いさ*. With きれい use **きれいさ**, not *きれさ*.
If the supplied base is past, first recover the adjective: 長かった → 長い → 長さ.
This productive pattern is a starting point, not a promise that every possible derived noun is
equally idiomatic in every context.

> このリボンの長さを測りました。  
> このりぼんのながさを はかりました。  
> I measured this ribbon's length.
>
> この道具の便利さが大切です。  
> このどうぐのべんりさが たいせつです。  
> This tool's convenience is important.

Length can be measured numerically; convenience need not be.
**さ does not mean "a number"** or guarantee an objective measurement.
The whole noun phrase takes the particle its sentence needs: 長さ**を**測る, 便利さ**が**大切だ.

Compare the opening **長く話す**: it describes how long the talking lasts.
**長さ** instead names a property as a noun. Neither automatically means "too long";
[すぎる and degree](comparisons-change-and-degree.md#すぎる-too-much-for-a-relevant-standard)
add a different judgment.

## 5. Small recognition bridges

These are useful neighboring expressions, not extra production targets or a claim of full coverage.

### Kinds: こんな, どんな and こういう

**どんな本が好きですか（どんなほんがすきですか）** asks what kind of books someone likes.
**こういう本が好きです**, "I like books like this," can refer to an illustrated or described type.
**こんな／そんな／あんな／どんな + noun** and **こういう／そういう／ああいう／どういう + noun**
can describe or ask about kinds. They also depend on discourse and the speaker's presentation;
do not turn them into a permanent distance-only code or assume that longer wording is always more polite.
Ordinary この／その and selecting **どの本**, "which book," remain in
[pointing and asking questions](pointing-and-asking-questions.md#asking-which-one-which-noun-or-where).
The そう in そういう is not by itself the appearance/hearsay auxiliary そう.

### Materials, features and accessories

> この机は木でできています。  
> このつくえは きでできています。  
> This desk is made of wood.
>
> このチーズは牛乳からできています。  
> このちーずは ぎゅうにゅうからできています。  
> This cheese is made from cow's milk.

Material **で** and source **から** overlap. Transformation and how a material is presented can affect
the choice, but visible versus invisible material is not a reliable exclusive grammar test.
These examples do not claim that only one of those particles could ever fit each product.

**赤いネクタイをしています（あかいねくたいをしています）** describes wearing a red tie.
**父は丸い顔をしています（ちちはまるいかおをしています）** describes a father as having a round face.
This descriptive **をしている** has lexical preferences; do not mechanically replace every "wear" or "have"
verb with する, or infer that all body-part/property statements using がある are forbidden.

### Contrastive くは and lexical 化する

**高くはない（たかくはない）**, "not expensive," puts contrastive は after the く form.
It can leave room for a contrast with some other aspect; it does not automatically mean "cheap"
or prescribe one hidden opposite property.

**機械化（きかいか）** means mechanization. **作業を機械化する（さぎょうをきかいかする）**
means mechanize the work; **作業の機械化**, mechanization of the work, uses the noun.
Established **化** compounds have their own meanings and grammatical behavior.
Do not add 化する freely to every word, or put の mechanically after an already complete する verb.
Compound recall is not scored as grammar in this installment.

## Practice scope and sources

The six groups distinguish named formation, interpreting complete messages and guided ease/difficulty selection.
Named frames still require stem recovery, inflection or argument restructuring; they are not unrestricted
synonym tests. Repeated reading, eating, name-entry and quality scenes share context keys, so thirty exercises
do not mean thirty independent conversations or demonstrated mastery.

- [Bunpro: やすい](https://bunpro.jp/grammar_points/やすい),
  [にくい](https://bunpro.jp/grammar_points/にくい) and [づらい](https://bunpro.jp/grammar_points/づらい):
  formation and examples, qualified against overlapping uses.
- [Tofugu: やすい](https://www.tofugu.com/japanese-grammar/yasui/) and
  [にくい](https://www.tofugu.com/japanese-grammar/nikui/): ease, difficulty, tendency and contextual readings.
- [Bunpro: かた](https://bunpro.jp/grammar_points/かた) and
  [Tae Kim: using 方 for a way of doing something](https://guidetojapanese.org/learn/grammar/comparison):
  method nouns and the change in argument structure.
- [Bunpro: adjective action modifiers](https://bunpro.jp/grammar_points/describing-verbs),
  [さ](https://bunpro.jp/grammar_points/さ), and
  [Tae Kim: adverbs](https://guidetojapanese.org/learn/grammar/adverbs): attachment and clause placement.
- [Bunpro: kinds](https://bunpro.jp/grammar_points/そんな-こんな-あんな-どんな),
  [そういう](https://bunpro.jp/grammar_points/そういう),
  [materials](https://bunpro.jp/grammar_points/でできる-からできる),
  [descriptive する](https://bunpro.jp/grammar_points/する-wear),
  [くは](https://bunpro.jp/grammar_points/くは) and
  [化する](https://bunpro.jp/grammar_points/化する): the bounded recognition bridges.

The patterns are curriculum-based learning targets, not an official JLPT syllabus.
Source shortcuts about exclusive difficulty types, mandatory adjacency or unrestricted compound formation
are not adopted here.
