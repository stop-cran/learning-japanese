# Japanese actions, states, and time

**読んでいます** can describe reading now. **開いています** can describe a window that is already open.
**毎朝走っています** can describe a routine. The shared ending ～ている is not a universal translation of
English "-ing."

Use the [verb foundations](japanese-verb-foundations.md) if you need the て／で forms first.

## Three readings of ～ている

| Context | Sentence | What is presented |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Someone is in the middle of reading. | 今、本を読んでいます。 | An action in progress. |
| A window has opened and is still open. | 窓が開いています。 | A resulting state. |
| Someone describes their regular exercise. | 毎朝、公園を走っています。 | Repeated activity, not necessarily happening at this instant. |

Read **今（いま）**, **本（ほん）**, **窓（まど）**, **開く（あく）**, **毎朝（まいあさ）**,
**公園（こうえん）**, and **走る（はしる）**.
The verb's meaning and the context select the reading. Do not use a shortcut such as "an event that can happen
only once": a window can open repeatedly, yet 開いている can describe its current open state.

Ordinary nonpast 走ります also describes habits in suitable contexts. We do not reject it merely because
毎朝 appears. A task about interpreting 走っています is different from claiming that it is the only possible
way to describe a routine.

## The ending changes; the first part stays in て-form

| Plain | Polite | Reading in an appropriate context |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 読んでいる | 読んでいます | is reading |
| 読んでいない | 読んでいません | is not reading / has not been reading |
| 読んでいた | 読んでいました | was reading |
| 読んでいなかった | 読んでいませんでした | was not reading |

Conversational speech often drops the い: 読んでる, 読んでます.
The full forms are the models here; ordinary contractions are accepted when the task permits them.
Past 読んでいた does not alone prove that the entire book was finished.
Compare the [action-stage lesson](koto-wake-and-formal-nouns.md#ところ-locating-an-actions-stage) later.

Some lexical patterns need individual care: **知っています（しっています）** means "know,"
but the ordinary "do not know" is **知りません／知らない**, not a mechanical copy of every ている negative.

## Already, still, and not yet

> もう昼ご飯を食べました。  
> もうひるごはんをたべました。  
> I have already eaten lunch.
>
> まだ昼ご飯を食べていません。  
> まだひるごはんをたべていません。  
> I have not eaten lunch yet.
>
> まだ食べています。  
> まだたべています。  
> I am still eating.

**まだ** does not always mean "not yet": the predicate's polarity matters.
**もう...ない** can mean "no longer/not anymore," as in もうここでは働いていません
（もうここでははたらいていません）, "I no longer work here."
Do not infer someone's earlier desire or plan from these time expressions alone.

## Before, after, and until an event

| Pattern | Example | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dictionary verb + 前に | 寝る前に、歯を磨きます。 | I brush my teeth before going to bed. |
| て-form + から | 手を洗ってから、料理をします。 | I wash my hands, then cook. |
| Nonpast verb + まで | バスが来るまで、ここで待ちます。 | I wait here until the bus comes. |

Read **寝る（ねる）**, **前（まえ）**, **歯（は）**, **磨く（みがく）**, **手（て）**,
**洗う（あらう）**, **料理（りょうり）**, **来る（くる）**, and **待つ（まつ）**.

In **前に**, the dictionary form locates the event relative to another event, not necessarily to now:
昨日は寝る前に本を読みました, "Yesterday I read before going to bed."
With event nouns, use の: 授業の前に（じゅぎょうのまえに）, "before class."
てから explicitly places the following action after the first; it does not specify an exact number of seconds.
The basic まで pattern gives a boundary for the waiting or continuing situation.

Ordinary [て connections](japanese-listing-and-examples.md#て-くて-and-で-connecting-is-not-always-listing-examples)
are broader than strict "first, then" sequences.
The existing [before/after comparison](koto-wake-and-formal-nouns.md#time-recurrence-and-occasions)
adds ～た後で／後に; [basic particles](japanese-nouns-and-basic-particles.md#まで-is-not-the-same-task-as-までに)
compares まで with a completion deadline, までに. Those richer contrasts remain separate practice.

## とき and relative event order

The form before **とき**, "when," need not match the main clause's past/nonpast ending:

| Viewpoint | Example | Reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The arrival is still ahead at the reference moment. | 明日は駅に着くとき、家族に電話します。 | Tomorrow I will call my family as I am arriving at the station. |
| The arrival is presented as accomplished at that moment. | 明日は駅に着いたとき、家族に電話します。 | Tomorrow I will call my family when I have arrived at the station. |
| The main event is already past. | 昨日は家を出るとき、電話が鳴りました。 | Yesterday the phone rang as I was leaving home. |

Read **着く（つく）**, arrive; **家族（かぞく）**, family; **電話（でんわ）**, phone/call;
**出る（でる）**, leave; and **鳴る（なる）**, ring.
In the second example, **着いた** does not put tomorrow's arrival before today.
The first and third examples do not assert that departure/arrival was already completed at the reference moment.

This is a viewpoint distinction, not a universal stopwatch rule for every verb.
With ongoing or stative descriptions, past and nonpast can overlap: **料理をしているとき** and
**料理をしていたとき** can both identify a past cooking episode.
Do not automatically reject a form merely because the main clause contains **昨日** or **明日**.
The formation tasks explicitly select the not-yet-completed or already-completed event viewpoint.

Attachment follows noun modification: **読むとき**, **寒いとき**, **静かなとき**, **学生のとき**.
Past/negative predicates attach without an extra な or の: **学生だったとき**, **忙しくないとき**.
Read **寒い（さむい）**, cold; **静か（しずか）**, quiet; **学生（がくせい）**, student;
**忙しい（いそがしい）**, busy.
**に** can follow とき; the new full-clause drills accept it where it fits.
The existing [とき／ころ comparison](koto-wake-and-formal-nouns.md#とき-versus-ころ) covers approximation,
not a short-period/long-period division.

## Simultaneous actions and intervals

**Verb ます-stem + ながら** presents one person's simultaneous activities, with the following main clause
foregrounded:

> 本を読みながら、パンを食べます。  
> （ほんをよみながら、ぱんをたべます。）  
> I eat bread while reading a book.

Build **読む → 読みながら**, **食べる → 食べながら**, **する → しながら** and
**来る（くる）→ 来ながら（きながら）**. Do not add ながら directly to every dictionary form.
In this simultaneous use the two actions have the same subject. This is not the separate concessive
**ながら（も）**, "although"; that onward use is not a new target here.

An interval can instead be supplied by someone else's action:

> 私が本を読んでいる間に、姉が帰ってきました。  
> （わたしがほんをよんでいるあいだに、あねがかえってきました。）  
> My older sister came home while I was reading a book.

**ている間に** places the main event within the ongoing interval. It does not require the same subject,
and the main event need not be accidental or beyond anyone's control.
The main event's past ending does not force **読んでいた**: **読んでいる** can describe the activity
ongoing at that past reference time. Ordinary **読んでる／食べてる** contractions are also possible.

**間は** can make the interval a topic, often contrasted with afterward; **間に** locates something within it.
The [existing interval practice](koto-wake-and-formal-nouns.md#うち-and-あいだ-a-window-a-developing-change-or-a-continuing-state) keeps that
framing contrast. It is not a universal rule that に forbids every lasting activity or は every single event.
The new formation drills add whole verb-plus-interval assembly rather than duplicate the old particle blanks.

## ないで and なくて

| Connection | Example | Reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| B without doing A | 名前を書かないで、紙を出しました。 | I handed in the paper without writing my name. |
| A negative circumstance as a reason | 電車が来なくて、困りました。 | The train did not come, and I was in difficulty because of that. |

Read **名前（なまえ）**, name; **書く（かく）**, write; **紙（かみ）**, paper;
**電車（でんしゃ）**, train; **困る（こまる）**, be in difficulty.
For the first pattern, make the ordinary verb negative and add **で**:
**書く → 書かないで**, **食べる → 食べないで**.
For **なくて**, connect the negative through **なく + て**:
**書かなくて**, **食べなくて**, **しなくて**, **来なくて（こなくて）**.

The distinction is useful, but **not an absolute division of all sentences**.
なくて commonly connects a negative reason to feelings, inability or another consequence.
ないで commonly presents the circumstances or manner of doing the main action.
Both fit some evaluative expressions, including the
[negative gladness forms](necessity-advice-and-instructions.md#てよかった-glad-about-what-happened-or-did-not-happen).
The named formation drills distinguish the requested connection, not every possible way to word that situation.

Adjectives and noun predicates also have negative links:
**高くなくて**, **よくなくて**, **静かではなくて／じゃなくて／でなくて** and
**学生ではなくて／じゃなくて／でなくて**.
Do not mechanically attach the verbal ないで pattern to an adjective or noun.
For example, **高くなくて助かりました**（たかくなくてたすかりました） means
"It was not expensive, which helped me."
These links are not inherently reasons in every context; they can also connect or contrast descriptions.

Interpretation tasks use **付帯（ふたい）** for an accompanying circumstance (here, doing B without A)
and **原因（げんいん）** for a reason. These are defined response labels, not a lookup assigning every
ないで or なくて sentence to one immutable category.
Negative [requests](japanese-requests-permission-and-rules.md) and
[no-obligation policies](necessity-advice-and-instructions.md) keep their separate owners.

## Apply a deadline to an actual completion

**十時までに本を返してください**（じゅうじまでにほんをかえしてください） sets a deadline:
"Please return the book by ten." Returning it at 9:50 meets that limit; 10:10 is late.
This does not ask the person to keep returning the book continuously until ten.

A nonpast event can supply the boundary too:
**先生が来るまでに、紙を出してください**（せんせいがくるまでに、かみをだしてください）,
"Please hand in the paper by the time the teacher arrives."
Compare **先生が来るまで待ちます**, "I will wait until the teacher comes":
here waiting continues to the boundary.
Use clear times/events rather than assume everyone interprets an unspecified "by summer" identically.

The [basic-particle practice](japanese-nouns-and-basic-particles.md#まで-is-not-the-same-task-as-までに)
already compares the markers. The new transfer tasks apply a quoted deadline to a stated completion;
**間に合う（まにあう）** means meeting that limit, **間に合わない** not meeting it.
A late completion is still a completion, not proof that the action never happened.

For **て links expressing reasons, means or parallel facts**, and **ても／でも paired alternatives**,
continue to [reasons and concessions](reasons-and-contrasts.md#て-links-for-reasons-means-and-parallel-facts).
The time/linking supplement connects these focused sections; it does not replace the existing conditionals
or のに sets. Named form drills, message interpretation and deadline transfer are different tasks, not
evidence of unrestricted conversational mastery.

## A past experience is not just any past sentence

> 京都に行ったことがあります。  
> きょうとにいったことがあります。  
> I have been to Kyoto.
>
> 去年、京都に行きました。  
> きょねん、きょうとにいきました。  
> I went to Kyoto last year.

**た-form + ことがある** presents having the experience. A plain past report locates or narrates an event.
The two can describe the same person's history; neither makes the other false.
The practice asks which wording explicitly presents experience, or visibly requests the named construction,
instead of treating any mention of a date as a ban on experience language.

For no such experience, use **～たことがない／ありません**.
Do not confuse it with **nonpast + ことがある**, occasional occurrence, or **ことができる**, ability:
these are linked in [noun-based grammar](koto-wake-and-formal-nouns.md#learn-the-whole-construction),
but that mixed contrast belongs to later practice.
