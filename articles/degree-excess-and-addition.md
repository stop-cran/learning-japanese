# Degree, excess and addition

**Is the convenience store about the only shop still open? Was someone so nervous that they forgot their speech?
Do prices just keep rising? Can your brother speak English "of course", and Chinese too?**
These N3 expressions measure a degree, push it to excess, or add something to an obvious case.

| Expression | Job here | Fragment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| N くらいです | about the only one | スーパーくらいです |
| 〜くらいです at the end | to the extent that | 泣きたいくらいです |
| N のあまり | so much (feeling) that… | 心配のあまり |
| あまりの + N | an extreme degree as a cause | あまりの寒さに |
| あまりに + adjective | excessively | あまりに高い |
| N ばかり | mostly, nothing but | 肉ばかり |
| dictionary form + ばかりだ | keeps changing one way | 増えるばかりだ |
| 〜は言うまでもない／言うまでもなく | goes without saying; needless to say | 大切なのは言うまでもない |
| A はもちろん B も | A of course, and B too | 英語はもちろん、中国語も |
| A をはじめ B | A as the leading example of B | 部長をはじめ、… |
| A ばかりでなく／だけでなく B も | not only A but also B | 安いだけでなく、… |
| A ばかりで、B | just A, and B is lacking | 長いばかりで、… |

## 1. くらいです: about the only one, or how far something goes

### N くらいです: about the only one

In a sentence such as 〜のは N くらいです, **くらい** (also **ぐらい**) narrows the topic down to roughly one member.
It often sounds modest or slightly regretful.

> この村にある店は、小さなスーパーくらいです。  
> このむらに ある みせは、ちいさな スーパーくらいです。  
> About the only shop in this village is a small supermarket.

When the predicate is negative, the same "about the only one" idea needs **くらいしか**:

> この村には、小さなスーパーくらいしかありません。  
> このむらには、ちいさな スーパーくらいしか ありません。  
> There's hardly anything in this village except a small supermarket.

| Predicate after the slot | Form | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| affirmative です／だ | N くらい | スーパーくらいです |
| negative | N くらいしか | スーパーくらいしかありません |

After a number or an amount, くらい usually means "about" (一時間くらい, about an hour).
That estimate use, and the "at least" minimum use (名前くらい書ける), are practised in existing material.

### Sentence-final くらいです: to the extent that

After a verb or adjective clause, sentence-final **くらいです** measures how strong a state is by comparing it
with an extreme result.

> 今朝は寒くて、手が動かないくらいでした。  
> けさは さむくて、てが うごかないくらいでした。  
> This morning it was so cold that my hands would hardly move.
>
> この本はおもしろくて、一晩で全部読んでしまったくらいです。  
> このほんは おもしろくて、ひとばんで ぜんぶ よんでしまったくらいです。  
> This book was so interesting that I read the whole thing in one night.

Read the clause before くらい literally. 読んでしまった reports something that happened.
泣きたいくらい (so sad I felt like crying) states a wish, so it does not say that anyone actually cried.
**ほどだ** is a more formal neighbour with the same job; it is not practised here.

## 2. Excess: のあまり, あまりの and あまりに

Choose the form by the slot: what comes before it and what follows it.

| Slot | Form | Fragment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| after a noun, often a feeling | N のあまり | 心配のあまり |
| before a noun, often a さ-noun | あまりの + N（に） | あまりの寒さに |
| before an adjective or adverb | あまりに（も） + adjective | あまりに寒くて |

**N のあまり** says a feeling or state was so strong that it caused the result that follows.
The result is often something the person did not choose. A dictionary-form verb can also come before あまり
(心配するあまり); this set practises the noun form.

> 父は怒りのあまり、顔が赤くなった。  
> ちちは いかりの あまり、かおが あかく なった。  
> My father was so angry that his face turned red.

**あまりの + noun** puts the extreme degree in front of a noun, usually followed by に and the result.

> あまりの人の多さに、駅に入れなかった。  
> あまりの ひとの おおさに、えきに はいれなかった。  
> There were so many people that we couldn't get into the station.

**あまりに（も）** modifies an adjective or adverb.

> この問題はあまりに簡単で、すぐに終わった。  
> このもんだいは あまりに かんたんで、すぐに おわった。  
> This question was so easy that I finished it right away.

**あんまり** is a casual variant. In an affirmative sentence, plain あまり before an adjective can carry the
same excess meaning. Do not confuse this with **あまり + negative**, "not very" (あまり高くない), a different
N4 sense practised elsewhere.

## 3. ばかり: mostly, keeps changing, or has only just happened

### N ばかり, だけ and しか

| Form | Claim | Exceptions? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| N ばかり + affirmative | N dominates; often critical | a few are tolerated |
| N だけ | exactly N and nothing else | none |
| N しか + negative | nothing but N | none; the negative excludes the alternatives |

> 弟は漫画ばかり読んでいる。  
> おとうとは まんがばかり よんでいる。  
> My younger brother reads nothing but manga.
>
> この店の客は、若い人ばかりだ。  
> このみせの きゃくは、わかい ひとばかりだ。  
> The customers at this shop are mostly young people.

Because ばかり describes what dominates, a sentence can admit a few exceptions first
(野菜も少しは食べますが、…) and still use ばかり. だけ would contradict those exceptions.
After a number, ばかり usually means "about" (三日ばかり, about three days), so it does not state an exact limit;
use だけ for that (一回だけ, only once). Verb て-form + ばかりいる (遊んでばかりいる) is practised in existing material.

### Dictionary form + ばかりだ versus た-form + ばかり

The verb form before ばかり changes the meaning completely.

| Form before ばかり | Meaning | Typical clues |
| --- | --- | --- |
| dictionary form: 増えるばかりだ | keeps changing in one direction, usually for the worse | 年々, 毎日, どんどん |
| た-form: 増えたばかりだ | happened only a short time ago | 先週, さっき, 昨日 |

> 仕事は増えるばかりで、休みが取れない。  
> しごとは ふえるばかりで、やすみが とれない。  
> My work just keeps increasing, and I can't take any time off.
>
> さっき起きたばかりなので、まだ眠い。  
> さっき おきたばかりなので、まだ ねむい。  
> I've only just got up, so I'm still sleepy.

**一方だ** is a close neighbour for continuing trends; it is practised in a separate set.

## 4. 言うまでもない and 言うまでもなく

The phrase is written with 言う but read **いう**. Choose the form by its position.

| Position | Form |
| --- | --- |
| predicate: sentence end, before が, before こと or でしょう | 〜は言うまでもない／言うまでもありません |
| opening or inserted aside; A は〜、B も… | 言うまでもなく（言うまでもないことですが） |

> 健康が大切なのは言うまでもない。  
> けんこうが たいせつなのは いうまでもない。  
> It goes without saying that health is important.
>
> 日本の夏が暑いことは言うまでもないが、今年は特に暑い。  
> にほんの なつが あついことは いうまでもないが、ことしは とくに あつい。  
> Japanese summers are hot, of course, but this year is especially hot.
>
> 言うまでもなく、図書館では静かにしてください。  
> いうまでもなく、としょかんでは しずかに してください。  
> Needless to say, please be quiet in the library.
>
> 彼女はピアノは言うまでもなく、バイオリンも弾ける。  
> かのじょは ピアノは いうまでもなく、バイオリンも ひける。  
> She can play the piano, needless to say, and the violin too.

Calling something obvious still asserts it. 言うまでもない cannot open a sentence with only a comma after it;
use 言うまでもなく or 言うまでもないことですが there.

## 5. はもちろん and をはじめ

### A はもちろん B も: the obvious case, and more

**はもちろん** (also はもちろんのこと, written は勿論) marks A as the obvious case and adds B with も.
It is more formal than だけでなく. A is asserted, not excluded.

> 夏休みは宿題はもちろん、部屋の掃除もしなければならない。  
> なつやすみは しゅくだいは もちろん、へやの そうじも しなければならない。  
> Over the summer holidays I have to do my homework, of course, and clean my room too.

With a negative predicate it works like "let alone": the harder case is obviously impossible, and even the
easier one is too.

> 私は泳ぐのが苦手で、海はもちろん、プールでも泳げない。  
> わたしは およぐのが にがてで、うみは もちろん、プールでも およげない。  
> I'm a poor swimmer: I can't swim in the sea, of course, and I can't even swim in a pool.

**さえ** marks B itself as an extreme example ("even B"); see the related articles below.

### A をはじめ B: a leading example of the group

**をはじめ** (written を始め) names A as the leading or representative member of a group B that the
sentence goes on to name. B includes A.

> 東京をはじめ、日本の大きな町には外国人の観光客が多い。  
> とうきょうを はじめ、にほんの おおきな まちには がいこくじんの かんこうきゃくが おおい。  
> Japan's big cities, Tokyo first among them, have many foreign tourists.
>
> すしをはじめとする日本料理は、外国でも人気がある。  
> すしを はじめとする にほんりょうりは、がいこくでも にんきが ある。  
> Japanese food, sushi above all, is popular abroad too.

| Form | What follows |
| --- | --- |
| N をはじめ、… | the rest of the group, then the predicate |
| N をはじめとして、… | the same, slightly more formal |
| N をはじめとする + noun | a noun naming the group |

The English gloss "starting with" can mislead: をはじめ ranks A as a representative, not as first in time.
It does not say who arrived first.

In many positive sentences both forms are possible (部長はもちろん、社員も… and 部長をはじめ、社員も…).
Only をはじめ continues with とする or として, and only はもちろん naturally builds the negative "let alone"
scale or adds something outside A's group. Most practice items use those slots; one is decided by meaning
(A heads a group B that it belongs to, rather than being a self-evident case that B goes beyond), and short
reading questions check the meanings.

## 6. ばかりでなく／だけでなく versus ばかりで

| Form | Claim | Typical continuation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A ばかりでなく B も | not only A but also B, in the same direction | B も… or B でも… |
| A だけでなく B も | the same; more conversational | B も… |
| A ばかりで、B | A is all there is (or all that happens), and B is lacking or goes wrong | B は…ない, 何も…ない |

> 彼は勉強ばかりでなく、スポーツもよくできる。  
> かれは べんきょう ばかりでなく、スポーツも よく できる。  
> He is good not only at studying but also at sports.
>
> このかばんは軽いだけでなく、とても丈夫だ。  
> このかばんは かるいだけでなく、とても じょうぶだ。  
> This bag is not only light but also very sturdy.
>
> 彼は文句を言うばかりで、何も手伝わない。  
> かれは もんくを いうばかりで、なにも てつだわない。  
> He just complains and doesn't help with anything.

Accepted variants include ばかりではなく, だけではなく, だけじゃなく(て), and ばかりか, which often adds a
stronger or more surprising B. In all of them A is still affirmed: "not only A" means "A, and B as well".

## Levels and scope

JLPT levels here are editorial estimates that follow Bunpro first; there is no official JLPT grammar list.
All exercises are labelled N3. That includes the だけ (N5), しか〜ない and た-form + ばかり (N4) contrast
sides, because each of those items asks you to rule out the N3 ばかり／ばかりだ choice. ほど, ば〜ほど,
ほど〜ない, the estimate and minimum uses of くらい, さえ and
て-form + ばかりいる are practised in existing sets and are not repeated here.
