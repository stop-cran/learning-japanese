# Describing nouns and talking about activities

How do you move from "a book" to "the book I bought yesterday", "the red one", or "I like reading books"?
Japanese puts descriptions **before** the noun. It can also use の when a noun is understood or when an activity
needs a noun-like role. Those uses look alike, but do different jobs.

This guide builds on [nouns and basic particles](japanese-nouns-and-basic-particles.md) and
[adjective forms](japanese-adjective-foundations.md). The linked practice is a bounded N5 introduction,
not a complete account of relative clauses or nominalizer choice.

## 1. Find the noun at the end

A **head noun** is the noun the whole phrase describes. Start at that noun and ask what the words before it add.

| Description + head noun | Reading | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 赤い本 | あかい ほん | a red book |
| 静かな部屋 | しずかな へや | a quiet room |
| 毎日読む本 | まいにち よむ ほん | a book someone reads every day |
| 昨日買った本 | きのう かった ほん | a book someone bought yesterday |
| 姉が読んでいる本 | あねが よんでいる ほん | a book my older sister is reading |
| 窓がある部屋 | まどが ある へや | a room with a window |

The whole phrase can then take a particle:

> 昨日買った本を読みます。  
> きのう かった ほんを よみます。  
> I will read the book I bought yesterday.

本, not the act of buying, is what を marks as the object of 読みます.
買った describes which book. It does not turn the whole sentence into "I read it yesterday".

In an ordinary Japanese noun-modifying clause, there is no English-style relative pronoun to insert:
昨日買った本 already does the work of "the book **that** I bought yesterday".
The person who bought it can remain understood, or be stated: 私が買った本 (わたしが かった ほん).

## 2. Attach the right form, not the polite sentence ending

For ordinary affirmative nonpast descriptions:

- **い-adjective + noun:** 赤い本, not 赤いな本.
- **な-adjective + な + noun:** 静かな部屋, not 静かだ部屋.
- **Plain verb + noun:** 読む本, 読んだ本, 読んでいる本.

The な rule is not "put な after every form of a な-adjective":

| What the description says | Before the noun |
| --- | --- |
| the room is quiet | 静かな部屋 (しずかな へや) |
| the room was quiet | 静かだった部屋 (しずかだった へや) |
| the room is not quiet | 静かではない部屋 (しずかではない へや) |
| the book was interesting | 面白かった本 (おもしろかった ほん) |

For a past description, replace the ordinary affirmative な link with the appropriate predicate form;
do not make 静かなだった部屋. A past description alone does not establish that the property has stopped.
See the [adjective guide](japanese-adjective-foundations.md) for formation and negatives.

> これは昨日読んだ本です。  
> これは きのう よんだ ほんです。  
> This is the book I read yesterday.

The modifier is plain 読んだ; the main sentence is polite because it ends in です.
In these beginner formation drills, convert 読みました to 読んだ before 本, rather than carrying
the polite ending into the modifier. This is the ordinary pattern being practised, not a ban on
every polite expression inside a noun phrase in every register.

ている has its usual range of meanings, not just "doing right now".
姉が読んでいる本 can describe reading in progress; 眼鏡をかけている人
(めがねを かけている ひと, a person wearing glasses) describes a continuing state.
See [actions, states and time](japanese-actions-states-and-time.md) for a wider comparison.

The existing elementary noun-link, な-link and plain-verb attachment drills remain in
[noun-based relations](about-targets-and-perspectives.md). Its later comparative relations are optional;
you do not need to learn the whole article before practising these basic descriptions.

## 3. A noun with a feature: がある + noun

> 庭がある家を探しています。  
> にわが ある いえを さがしています。  
> I am looking for a house with a garden.

庭がある describes 家: a house that has a garden. Inside that description, 庭 is marked with が.
Outside it, the whole phrase 庭がある家 takes を as the object of 探しています.
The head noun is therefore **not automatically the main sentence's subject or topic**.

This is the familiar existence verb ある inside a noun description, not a special suffix meaning "with".
Use the actual existence pattern; do not mechanically replace its が with は.
For living beings and the ordinary ある／いる distinction, return to
[basic particles and existence](japanese-nouns-and-basic-particles.md).

## 4. Leave an understood noun unspoken

### Possession: 私の

> これは私の傘です。あれは姉のです。  
> これは わたしの かさです。あれは あねのです。  
> This is my umbrella. That one is my older sister's.

In 姉の, the umbrella is understood. Compare:

- 姉の傘: の links 姉 and the stated noun 傘.
- 姉の: the owned item is understood from context.

Repeating 傘 is also possible when it helps clarity. Omitting a noun is not automatically better,
and の cannot replace every missing noun in every expression. This section practises clear, already identified items.
See [pointing and asking questions](pointing-and-asking-questions.md) for これ／それ／あれ and この + noun.

### Adjectives: 赤いの and 静かなの

> 赤いのをください。  
> あかいのを ください。  
> The red one, please.

> 静かなのがいいです。  
> しずかなのが いいです。  
> The quiet one would be good.

Use **い-adjective + の**, but **な-adjective + な + の** in the ordinary affirmative nonpast pattern.
The following particle depends on the whole sentence: を in a request for an item, が in the second example.
It is not a fixed のは or のが ending.

These are references to understood things, not activities. 私のです and 赤いのです can be ordinary
"it is mine" / "it is the red one" in an appropriate item-selection context. Do not assume that every の before
です is the explanatory construction; context and the preceding structure matter.

## 5. Give an activity a noun-like role

> 私は本を読むのが好きです。  
> わたしは ほんを よむのが すきです。  
> I like reading books.

Read it in layers:

1. 本を読む: read books. を marks the thing read.
2. 本を読むの: the activity of reading books.
3. 本を読むのが好きです: that activity is liked.

This use of の is **nominalization**: it lets a clause fill a noun-like slot.
It is not a substitute for the head noun 本. Compare:

| Sentence | What is liked? |
| --- | --- |
| 私はこの本が好きです。 | this book |
| 私は本を読むのが好きです。 | the activity of reading books |

English "like" does not make 好き a Japanese verb requiring を.
[好き](../words/好き.md) (すき), [嫌い](../words/嫌い.md) (きらい),
[上手](../words/上手.md) (じょうず) and [下手](../words/下手.md) (へた) are な-adjectives in these patterns:

| Pattern | Reading | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 本を読むのが好きです | ほんを よむのが すきです | like reading books |
| 皿を洗うのが嫌いです | さらを あらうのが きらいです | dislike washing dishes |
| 車を運転するのが上手です | くるまを うんてんするのが じょうずです | be good at driving a car |
| 字を書くのが下手です | じを かくのが へたです | be poor at writing characters |

The verb still determines the role **inside** the activity: [読む](../words/読む.md),
[洗う](../words/洗う.md), [運転する](../words/運転.md) and [書く](../words/書く.md)
all take を for the thing acted on in these examples.
The **whole activity** takes が in the presented preference/skill pattern.
For topic or contrast, は and も are also possible in suitable contexts; see
[topic and inclusion](wa-ga-mo-topic-and-inclusion.md).

嫌い ends in い but is not an い-adjective. Skill is not liking: someone can be 上手 at an activity they dislike.
Calling another person 下手 can sound blunt; calling yourself 上手 can sound self-praising.
These are contextual cautions, not absolute bans on who may use the words.

For a past preference, the ordinary activity pattern can keep its nonpast verb and change the final predicate:
本を読むのが好きでした (ほんを よむのが すきでした, I liked reading books).
Do not turn this useful pattern into a rule that every clause before nominalizing の must be nonpast.

The linked formation tasks deliberately request the **の pattern** and preserve が.
That is not a claim that こと can never nominalize an activity, or that が is mandatory in every discourse.
The broader の／こと choice, perception contrasts and explanatory の／ん are optional next reading in
[noun-based grammar](koto-wake-and-formal-nouns.md#a-particularly-important-neighbor-の).
Those harder selection tasks keep their existing levels and are not part of this N5 contrast.

## 6. Recognize a limited subject が／の alternation

Inside some noun-modifying clauses, subject が can be replaced by の:

> 母が焼いたパン / 母の焼いたパン  
> ははが やいた ぱん / ははの やいた ぱん  
> bread my mother baked

> 雨が降った日 / 雨の降った日  
> あめが ふった ひ / あめの ふった ひ  
> a day when it rained

Here の marks the subject **inside the description**, not possession of the following verb.
The construction is different from the noun-linking の in 母のパン (my mother's bread).

This is not unrestricted particle substitution:

- In an ordinary independent clause, preserve its subject marker: 母がパンを焼きました
  (ははが ぱんを やきました, Mother baked bread). の is not a drop-in replacement for that が.
- Do not mechanically carry the alternation into every longer clause. An overt object after the subject,
  intervening material and possible possessive interpretations can affect acceptability or clarity.
- Neither version is universally more natural, and avoiding repeated が is not a requirement.

The practice only asks you to recognize the two simple modifier patterns above and their independent-clause counterparts,
preserving the original subject and structure. It does not ask you to generate unrestricted が／の substitutions.
Other uses of の, including an understood possessed noun, are not being declared ungrammatical.

## Quick check

Before choosing a form, ask:

1. Is there a stated head noun? Put the appropriate adjective or plain clause before it.
2. Is an already identified item understood? Consider possessive の or adjective + の.
3. Is the whole activity what is liked or evaluated? Keep its inner verb roles, then nominalize with の.
4. Is this a subject marker inside a suitable noun modifier, or in the main clause? Do not generalize the が／の alternation.
