# Food, water and textures

Food texture, water movement and fit can overlap in English translations but need different
scenes in Japanese. For other bodily and surface descriptions, see
[body and texture](onomatopoeia-body-and-texture.md).

## Soft food, crisp food and bubbles

煮込んだ肉がほろほろと崩れた。  
*The braised meat was so tender that it fell apart.*

[ほろほろ](../words/ほろほろ.md) can describe tender food crumbling or petals and
tears falling quietly. [ぽろぽろ](../words/ぽろぽろ.md) can describe drops falling
one by one. [ぼろぼろ](../words/ぼろぼろ.md) can describe something worn or falling
apart: the three words have overlapping falling-apart uses, not a mechanical h/b/p scale.

[とろとろ](../words/とろとろ.md) can describe a soft, viscous or melted texture,
gentle simmering, or slow movement. The drills distinguish the adverb before 煮込む or
走る from a selected noun modifier before チーズ or オムレツ. They accept の, した,
or とした in those particular modifier tasks; that is not a blanket rule for all words.

[からっと](../words/からっと.md) can describe food fried crisp or laundry dried
nicely. [しゅわしゅわ](../words/しゅわしゅわ.md) can describe the fizz of a
carbonated drink. [ほやほや](../words/ほやほや.md) can describe something freshly
made, and also a newly begun status: 社会人ほやほや is not a hot-food sentence.
[ぷりぷり](../words/ぷりぷり.md) can describe a springy food texture or an angry
manner; the sentence chooses the sense.

## Wetness and stickiness

はちみつがついて、手がべとべとする。  
*My hand feels sticky from the honey.*

[べとべと](../words/べとべと.md) describes a sticky condition;
[べったり](../words/べったり.md) can describe something sticking thickly or a person
clinging closely. Compare [べたべた](../words/べたべた.md), which also has sticky
and clinging uses. A single English “sticky” does not decide the construction.
The drills use べとべとする for a condition and べとべとになる for a change.

[びちゃびちゃ](../words/びちゃびちゃ.md) can describe wet splashing;
[ぽちゃぽちゃ](../words/ぽちゃぽちゃ.md) can describe splashing in water.
Compare [びしょびしょ](../words/びしょびしょ.md), which describes being soaking wet.
A splash may make clothes wet, but an action and a resulting condition are not identical.
[ぴちぴち](../words/ぴちぴち.md) can depict a lively fish jumping; it has other
uses too, so the fish scene is not a universal definition.

## Loose or close fit

買ったズボンはだぶだぶだった。  
*The trousers I bought were baggy.*

[だぶだぶ](../words/だぶだぶ.md) can describe baggy clothes;
[ゆるゆる](../words/ゆるゆる.md) can describe a very loose belt or a leisurely manner.
Compare [ぶかぶか](../words/ぶかぶか.md), also used for oversized clothes.
The drills use だぶだぶのズボン and ベルトがゆるゆるだ; they do not prove that
all noun and predicate frames are available for every similar word.
