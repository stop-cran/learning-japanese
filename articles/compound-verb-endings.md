# Compound verb endings: 切る, 通す, 込む, 上げる and 合う

**Did you eat it all, or was there too much to finish? Did you keep a secret to the end, or did it slip out?
Did the bread finish baking, or did someone finish baking it? Did two friends help each other, or did one help
the other?**
This article covers N3 verb endings that attach to the ます-stem of another verb and add completion, persistence,
direction, depth or mutual action. Each is set against the neighbour it is most often confused with.

| Ending | Reading | Job here | Contrasted with |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ～切る | きる | Do something completely, to the very end | ～切れない |
| ～切れない | きれない | Cannot do something completely | ～切る, ～通す |
| ～通す | とおす | Keep doing something right through to the end | ～切れない |
| ～込む (into) | こむ | Move or put something into a place | ～出す (out of) |
| ～込む (deep) | こむ | Do something deeply or thoroughly; a state sets in | ～込む (into) |
| ～上がる | あがる | Something reaches completion | ～上げる |
| ～上げる | あげる | Someone completes something | ～上がる |
| ～合う | あう | Both sides do it to each other | てもらう, てあげる |

## Forming a compound verb

All of these endings attach to the **ます-stem**: the form before ます. The second verb then conjugates as usual.

| Verb | ます-stem | + ending | Compound |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 食べる（たべる, eat） | 食べ | 切る | 食べ切る, 食べ切れない |
| 隠す（かくす, hide） | 隠し | 通す | 隠し通す |
| 飛ぶ（とぶ, jump） | 飛び | 込む／出す | 飛び込む, 飛び出す |
| 書く（かく, write） | 書き | 上がる／上げる | 書き上がる, 書き上げる |
| 助ける（たすける, help） | 助け | 合う | 助け合う |
| 我慢する（がまんする, put up with） | 我慢し | 切れない | 我慢し切れない |

The second verb is often written in kana as well: 食べきる, 書きあげる. Not every verb combines with every ending,
so learn compounds as whole words and treat the patterns below as a guide to their meaning.

## 1. Doing it completely: 切る and 切れない

**～切る** says an action is carried out to the very end, so nothing is left: the money is all spent, the book is
read to the last page. With verbs of saying it means to state something firmly, without hedging.

> 一日で長い小説を読み切った。  
> いちにちで ながい しょうせつを よみきった。  
> I read the whole long novel in one day.
>
> 冷蔵庫の牛乳を使い切ったので、買いに行こう。  
> れいぞうこの ぎゅうにゅうを つかいきったので、かいに いこう。  
> We've used up the milk in the fridge, so let's go and buy some.
>
> 彼は「絶対に勝つ」と言い切った。  
> かれは「ぜったいに かつ」と いいきった。  
> He declared firmly, "We will definitely win."

With some verbs of state it means "utterly": **疲れ切る**（つかれきる, be completely exhausted）, **冷え切る**
（ひえきる, be chilled through）.

**～切れない** is the potential form in the negative: the action cannot be completed, usually because there is too
much of something or too little evidence.

> こんなにたくさんの宿題は、一日ではやり切れない。  
> こんなに たくさんの しゅくだいは、いちにちでは やりきれない。  
> I can't get through this much homework in one day.
>
> 数え切れないほどの人が、駅に集まっていた。  
> かぞえきれない ほどの ひとが、えきに あつまっていた。  
> Countless people had gathered at the station.
>
> 犯人が彼だとは言い切れない。  
> はんにんが かれだとは いいきれない。  
> I can't say for certain that he is the culprit.

The choice between them depends on the result the sentence describes. If food is left over, 食べ切れなかった fits
and 食べ切った does not; if not one yen remains, 使い切った fits and 使い切れなかった does not. 数え切れないほど is a
fixed way to say "countless"; 数え切るほど does not describe a large number.

As a word of its own, **やり切れない** also means "unbearable" (やり切れない気持ち, an unbearable feeling).

## 2. Keeping it up to the end: 通す

**～通す** says an action or state is kept up all the way through, often against difficulty: keep a secret to the
end, walk the whole distance, keep a promise for the whole period.

> 彼は最後まで自分の意見を通した。  
> かれは さいごまで じぶんの いけんを とおした。  
> He held to his opinion to the very end. (通す on its own)
>
> マラソンで、四十二キロを走り通した。  
> まらそんで、よんじゅうに きろを はしりとおした。  
> I ran the full forty-two kilometres of the marathon.
>
> 秘密を最後まで守り通すのは難しい。  
> ひみつを さいごまで まもりとおすのは むずかしい。  
> It is hard to keep a secret right to the end.

The exercises set 通す against **～切れない**. 隠し通す means the secret stayed hidden; 隠し切れない means it
could not be kept hidden. Context decides: if the person smiled without meaning to, the feeling showed, so
隠し切れなかった fits.

> 涙を隠し切れずに、彼女は部屋を出た。  
> なみだを かくしきれずに、かのじょは へやを でた。  
> Unable to hide her tears, she left the room.

切れず is a more written way of saying 切れなくて. 通す and 切る both concern reaching
the end; the difference is that 通す focuses on keeping something up throughout, 切る on leaving nothing undone.

## 3. Into a place: 込む against 出す

**～込む** often adds movement **into** something: a place, a container, a page.

> 猫が箱の中に入り込んだ。  
> ねこが はこの なかに はいりこんだ。  
> The cat got into the box.
>
> 申込書に名前と住所を書き込んでください。  
> もうしこみしょに なまえと じゅうしょを かきこんでください。  
> Please write your name and address on the application form.

Its opposite for direction is **～出す**: movement **out of** somewhere.

> 子どもが急に道路に飛び出してきた。  
> こどもが きゅうに どうろに とびだしてきた。  
> A child suddenly ran out into the road.
>
> 鳥がかごから逃げ出した。  
> とりが かごから にげだした。  
> The bird escaped from its cage.

| Into (込む) | Out (出す) |
| --- | --- |
| 飛び込む, jump into | 飛び出す, leap out, rush out |
| 持ち込む, bring in | 持ち出す, take out |
| 逃げ込む, flee into (a shelter) | 逃げ出す, escape from |
| 駆け込む, rush into | 駆け出す, break into a run, run out |

Note that 飛び出す can also take に when に marks where someone ends up after coming out (道路に, into
the road). Decide from the whole sentence which way the movement goes. 出す also has a separate sense, "start
suddenly" (降り出す, start to rain), which is covered in
[Japanese verb extensions](verb-extensions-direction-and-aspect.md).

## 4. Deeply: the second meaning of 込む

With other verbs **～込む** does not describe entering any place. It says the action goes deep, is done thoroughly,
or that a state settles in and lasts.

> 父はテレビの前で眠り込んでいた。  
> ちちは てれびの まえで ねむりこんでいた。  
> My father had fallen fast asleep in front of the TV.
>
> 試合の前に、毎日走り込んだ。  
> しあいの まえに、まいにち はしりこんだ。  
> Before the match I did intensive running practice every day.
>
> 彼女は窓の外を見て、何か考え込んでいる。  
> かのじょは まどの そとを みて、なにか かんがえこんでいる。  
> She is looking out of the window, lost in thought.

Common examples: 考え込む (be lost in thought), 黙り込む (fall silent), 話し込む (talk at length), 眠り込む
(fall fast asleep), 信じ込む (firmly believe).

To tell the two senses apart, look for what is entered. If the sentence names a place, container or page that
something goes into, 込む adds "into". If nothing is entered and the action itself becomes deep or long, it adds
"deeply". Some compounds have both: 走り込む is "run into (a building)" in one sentence and "do intensive running
practice" in another; 書き込む usually means writing into a space or page.

## 5. Finished: 上がる against 上げる

Like 上がる／上げる themselves, the endings form an intransitive and transitive pair.

- **～上がる** (intransitive): the thing itself reaches completion. Pattern: *thing* が ～上がる.
- **～上げる** (transitive): someone completes a piece of work. Pattern: *person* が／は *thing* を ～上げる.

> ケーキがきれいに焼き上がった。  
> けーきが きれいに やきあがった。  
> The cake came out beautifully.
>
> 職人が一か月かけて、この机を作り上げた。  
> しょくにんが いっかげつ かけて、この つくえを つくりあげた。  
> The craftsman spent a month making this desk.
>
> レポートがやっと書き上がった。  
> れぽーとが やっと かきあがった。  
> The report is finally finished.

The particles decide. If the finished thing is marked with が and no one is doing anything to it, use 上がる; if
there is an object marked with を, use 上げる. In a noun-describing clause, check who does what: in 母が焼き上げた
ケーキ (the cake my mother baked), 母 is the baker, so 上げる is needed.

上げる also forms compounds about upward direction (見上げる, look up at), which are a separate sense.

## 6. To each other: 合う

**～合う** says that two or more people do the same thing to each other. The partner is marked with と, often
together with お互いに (each other) or みんなで.

> 二人は手紙で近況を知らせ合っている。  
> ふたりは てがみで きんきょうを しらせあっている。  
> The two keep each other updated on their news by letter.
>
> 困ったときは、友達と相談し合うといい。  
> こまったときは、ともだちと そうだんしあうと いい。  
> When you are in trouble, it is good to talk it over with your friends.
>
> 選手たちは声をかけ合いながら走った。  
> せんしゅたちは こえを かけあいながら はしった。  
> The players ran, calling out to one another.

Compare a one-way action. When only one side acts and the other benefits, use **てもらう** (receive the action,
giver marked with に), **てあげる** (do it for someone) or **てくれる** (someone does it for me or my side).

| Sentence | Who acts |
| --- | --- |
| 友達と教え合う | Each teaches the other |
| 友達に教えてもらう | The friend teaches me |
| 友達に教えてあげる | I teach the friend |
| 友達が教えてくれる | The friend teaches me (for my benefit) |
| 母がお弁当を作ってくれる | My mother makes my packed lunch for me |

So 兄に教え合った does not work: に marks one person giving help, while 合う needs both sides acting.

## Common confusions

- **切れない is not "do not cut".** In these compounds it is a potential negative: "cannot do completely".
- **出す after a stem is not always "start".** 降り出す is "start to rain", but 持ち出す and 逃げ出す are about
  moving out.
- **込む after a stem does not always mean "into".** 考え込む and 黙り込む do not enter anything.
- **上がる and 上げる follow the particles.** が with no object points to 上がる; を points to 上げる.
- **合う needs two sides.** With one person acting for another, use てもらう (giver に), てあげる (receiver に) or てくれる (giver が).
- **お互いに alone does not decide.** お互いに助けてあげる (doing favours for each other) is also possible, so look at who
  is in the subject and who receives the action.

## Levels

JLPT levels in this article are editorial estimates. There is no official JLPT grammar list; the levels follow
Bunpro's placement as the primary reference. On that basis 切る, 切れない, 込む (both senses), 上がる／上げる and 合う
are treated as N3. Bunpro does not list 通す as a separate point; it is placed at N3 here because other N3 grammar
references include it. Outward 出す, てもらう, てあげる and てくれる are N4 forms. Each exercise is labelled N3, even when its
answer is an N4 form, because every item asks you to choose against an N3 alternative.
