# Everyday feelings, speech and changing scenes

An onomatopoeic expression does not have one grammatical shape for every sense.
**とんとんたたく** means “tap repeatedly,” while **収支はとんとんだ**
（しゅうし） means “income and expenditure break even.” The same spelling
participates in different constructions.

This companion to [Onomatopoeia in sentences](onomatopoeia-in-sentences.md)
practises selected everyday uses of twenty-three existing words. Learn the
complete phrase, not a suffix recipe inferred from an onomatopoeia tag.

## Read the verb, not only the sound shape

[しくしく](../words/しくしく.md) has two different everyday uses:

- しくしく泣く（なく） — sob softly
- お腹がしくしく痛む（おなか・いたむ） — have a lingering dull stomach ache
- お腹がしくしくする — have that dull aching feeling

と is also possible in these selected frames, including しくしくとする for
pain. This does not turn every crying use into the same する predicate.
The stomach is not literally crying, and the pain description is not a diagnosis.
The separate same-spelling dictionary identity is not merged into this practice.

[ぺらぺら](../words/ぺらぺら.md) also changes with its surroundings:

| Frame | Selected meaning |
| --- | --- |
| スペイン語がぺらぺらだ | Be fluent in Spanish |
| ぺらぺらしゃべる | Chatter freely or glibly |
| ページをぺらぺらめくる | Flip through pages |
| ぺらぺらの紙（かみ） | Thin, flimsy paper |

Before the speech or page-turning verbs, と can also fit. For material,
ぺらぺらな and ぺらぺらした modifiers are documented too; の is not the only
possible connector. The practice here uses copular material descriptions rather
than pretending to grade every modifier alternative. Fluency can be praise,
while unguarded chatter can be criticism. Neither guarantees accuracy.

## Speech, secrecy and noise

[ひそひそ](../words/ひそひそ.md) focuses on low-voiced speech.
[こそこそ](../words/こそこそ.md) focuses on avoiding notice and can describe
nonverbal behavior as well.

- 図書館でひそひそ話した。（としょかん・はなした）  
  We spoke in whispers in the library.
- こそこそと裏口から出た。（うらぐち・でた）  
  Someone slipped out the back entrance furtively.

Whispering can be considerate rather than secretive. Conversely, someone can
act secretly without speaking at all. こそこそする describes furtive behavior,
but does not prove wrongdoing: a surprise can have a kind purpose.

[がやがや](../words/がやがや.md) describes a mixture of people's voices:
がやがや話す or 部屋ががやがやしている（へや）, “a room is full of chatter.”
The と versions are also possible. This is not a general label for any machinery
noise, nor proof that people are arguing. The dictionary's archaic laughing
sense belongs to the reading かやかや, not automatically to modern がやがや.

[ぶつぶつ](../words/ぶつぶつ.md) needs special care:

- ぶつぶつ文句を言う（もんく・いう） — grumble complaints
- 腕にぶつぶつができる（うで） — develop small bumps on the arm

In the second example, **ぶつぶつ is a noun**. It names what appears and can
also be an object: 腕のぶつぶつを医師に見せる（いし・みせる）, “show a doctor
the bumps on one's arm.” The bumps do not make a muttering sound.

There are other senses too. ぶつぶつと切る（きる） describes cutting into pieces.
The same cutting-result use also permits **ぶつぶつに切る**; do not reject に
merely because と is the model adverbial form. The dictionary also records
ぶつぶつ煮える（にえる）, “simmer,” but simmering is not separately graded here.

## Movement can be noisy, busy or successive

[どたどた](../words/どたどた.md) evokes heavy, noisy footsteps. It does not
necessarily identify speed, anger or a person's weight.
[ばたばた](../words/ばたばた.md) has a wider selected range:

- 羽をばたばたさせる（はね） — flap one's wings
- 準備でばたばたする（じゅんび） — rush around with preparations
- 看板がばたばた倒れる（かんばん・たおれる） — signs topple one after another

In the first, を marks what is made to move. In the second, nobody needs to
be literally flapping. In the third, 倒れる selects the succession of falls.
The batch does not include the separate colloquial vehicle noun.

## Taps, progress and bursts

[とんとん](../words/とんとん.md) can describe tapping, smooth progress, or an
even result. [こんこん](../words/こんこん.md) can depict knocking, coughing or
steadily falling snow. [どんどん](../words/どんどん.md) can depict pounding,
rapid progress or events occurring one after another.

The contrasts in tapping or pounding are tendencies, not a fixed loudness scale.
Each word still needs the relevant action:

雪がこんこんと降っている。（ゆき・ふっている）  
Snow is falling thickly.

This mimetic こんこん is not the separate 懇々 “earnestly,” 昏々 “deeply asleep,”
or 滾々 “flowing copiously.” Shared kana do not merge dictionary identities.

For change, **どんどん減る**（へる）, “decrease rapidly,” is as meaningful as an
increase: the verb supplies the direction. どんどん質問してください
（しつもん）, “go ahead and ask plenty of questions,” is an invitation,
not proof that questions have already been asked.

[どっと](../words/どっと.md) instead presents a burst, surge or sudden
development:

仕事が終わると、疲れがどっと出た。（しごと・おわる・つかれ・でた）  
Once work ended, fatigue hit me all at once.

A crowd is not required. Nor does どっと itself mean “laugh”: 観客がどっと笑う
（かんきゃく・わらう） supplies the audience and the laughter verb.

**Final と already belongs to どっと. Do not append another と.**
The loanword ドット, “dot,” is a different word. Compare this with the repeated
shape **うとうと**, which can take an additional particle: うとうとと.
Spelling alone is not a universal test for whether と can be added.

## Sleep, attention and rest

[うとうと](../words/うとうと.md) describes light dozing or nodding off.
[すやすや](../words/すやすや.md) presents quiet, peaceful sleep.
The meanings can overlap: light dozing can look peaceful, and うとうと does not
require visible nodding or repeated waking. The peaceful-choice scenes explicitly
exclude **light dozing itself**: one describes an observed sleeper, and another
wishes for settled, peaceful sleep. These premises distinguish the choices;
peacefulness alone does not make the categories mutually exclusive.

- 電車でうとうとしていた。（でんしゃ）  
  I was dozing on the train.
- 父はすやすや寝ていた。（ちち・ねていた）  
  My father was sleeping peacefully.

うとうとする is an ordinary predicate. For すやすや, retain a sleep verb such
as 寝る or 眠る（ねむる）. Adults, not only babies and animals, can sleep
すやすや. “Sleepy” alone does not necessarily assert actual dozing.
In 今夜はすやすや眠れますように（こんや・ねむれます）, “may you sleep peacefully
tonight,” the speaker expresses a wish rather than reporting an observed sleeper.

[ぼんやり](../words/ぼんやり.md) can describe an indistinct object or an
absentminded person. A mountain can look ぼんやり even while the observer is
concentrating; someone can ぼんやりしている while fully awake.
ぼんやりした記憶（きおく） is “a vague memory.”

[のんびり](../words/のんびり.md) emphasizes being relaxed and unhurried.
のんびりする means “take it easy,” while のんびりした休日（きゅうじつ） means
“an unhurried day off.” Simply moving slowly while tense is not the same thing.

The two modifier scaffolds in this set explicitly ask for **する → した**.
They do not ban other descriptions. In particular,
のんびりと休日を過ごした（すごした）, “spent a day off leisurely,” is grammatical:
the adverb reaches 過ごした across the noun phrase. It does not perform the
specific noun-modifier transformation requested in that scaffold.

## Exhaustion, vitality and clearer states

[ぐったり](../words/ぐったり.md) highlights limpness or listlessness:
ぐったりする, ぐったり横になる（よこになる）, “lie down limply.”
[くたくた](../words/くたくた.md) can describe an exhausted person, a worn and
limp material, or food cooked until very soft.

- もうくたくたです。  
  I am completely worn out now.
- シャツがくたくたになった。  
  The shirt became worn and limp.
- キャベツをくたくたになるまで煮た。（にた）  
  I cooked the cabbage until very soft.

With なる, **に** presents the result in the ordinary model. **と + なる**
can also express the result and is retained in these bounded tasks; it is
not the same function as manner と before an action verb. Soft cabbage need
not be a failed dish, and a worn shirt need not be torn.

[ぴんぴん](../words/ぴんぴん.md) can describe someone full of life with する,
or vigorous movement with 跳ねる（はねる）, “jump / flop.” It is not a medical
guarantee of perfect health.

[すっきり](../words/すっきり.md) can describe feeling refreshed, looking
uncluttered, or becoming satisfactorily resolved. Compare すっきりする with
疑問がすっきり解けない（ぎもん・とけない）, “my doubt is not satisfactorily
resolved.” Do not translate every use as bodily refreshment.

## Stillness, smiles and unexpected events

[じっと](../words/じっと.md) can describe stillness, sustained attention or
patient restraint. じっとする means “stay still”; じっと見る（みる） means
“look intently”; じっと耐える（たえる） means “endure patiently.”
Its final と is already part of the word. Do not add a second one.

じっとしていてください, “please stay still,” also permits the conversational
contraction **じっとしててください**. Contracted speech can retain a polite
request ending. This is not a request for literal silence in every possible use.

[にっこり](../words/にっこり.md) describes a warm or broad smile. にっこり笑う
and にっこりする do not require laughter aloud. 私ににっこりした means
“gave me a warm smile”; に marks its recipient. The smile's appearance does
not prove every aspect of the person's feelings.

[ばったり](../words/ばったり.md) is selected by its verb:

- ベッドにばったり倒れる（たおれる） — flop onto the bed
- 友達にばったり会う（ともだち・あう） — unexpectedly meet a friend
- 連絡がばったり途絶える（れんらく・とだえる） — contact abruptly stops

と is also possible with these verbs. Neither the encounter nor the cessation
requires an audible thud or a physical collision.

## Practice scope

Read each task's response contract. A word-plus-particle task keeps its printed
verb and excludes added verbs or phrases. A supplied-ending task keeps that
ending without repeating it. The copular-state tasks practise direct attachment
to the printed copula, not an added explanatory のだ／のです phrase.
Formation scaffolds request an exact grammatical operation; choose-and-copy
tasks request one of the displayed words.

The spelling requested here is **hiragana**. Fill only the complete blank, with
no added punctuation. Accepted alternatives remain valid even when only the
model choice is displayed. If two correct buttons appear, choose either one.
The two じっと attachment drills explicitly preserve the exact supplied shape:
documented elongations such as じいっと are outside that bounded operation,
not universally incorrect. The separate stillness scaffold retains its stated
してて contraction.
The app uses authored string membership, not automatic kana folding or free
paraphrase grading.

These tasks have no new JLPT labels or hard gates. Use **All** or **Unclassified**
for new Grammar material and check the separate browser filter as needed.
Existing word-card levels, including the N2 additions, do not classify these
Grammar tasks. See the [pilot guide](onomatopoeia-in-sentences.md) for controls.
Practice completion is not independent proof of transfer or learning benefit.
