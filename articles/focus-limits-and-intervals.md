# Focus, limits and intervals: こそ, なんか／なんて, に限る, だけしか, おきに and それぞれ

**Is this the one that truly counts, or something not worth worrying about? Is a sentence giving advice or
stating a rule? Did only one person come, or did only one person stay away? Does something happen every day or
every other day?** These short words answer such questions. They are small, but each one changes what the
sentence claims.

| Expression | Reading | Job here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| こそ | こそ | Single out one thing as the one that truly counts |
| なんか／なんて | なんか／なんて | Play something down, or present news as hard to believe |
| に限る | にかぎる | Recommend the best course, or state who or what qualifies |
| だけしか～ない | だけしか～ない | Only X, and nothing more |
| おきに | おきに | At intervals of; every other |
| それぞれ | それぞれ | Each one, respectively |

## 1. こそ: the one that truly counts

**こそ** usually follows a noun (sometimes a particle phrase such as 君にこそ) and singles it out: *this* one,
and no other, is the one that matters. It usually replaces は, が or を; こそが is also used in the subject
position.

| Pattern | Example | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reply + こそ | こちらこそ | No, it is I who should (thank you, apologize) |
| Time + こそ | 今度こそ | This time, for sure |
| Demonstrative + こそ | これこそ | This, precisely |
| Noun phrase + こそ | 続けることこそ | Continuing is what really counts |

> 「ありがとう。」「いいえ、こちらこそありがとう。」  
> 「ありがとう。」「いいえ、こちらこそ ありがとう。」  
> "Thank you." "No, thank *you*."
>
> 今度こそ合格したいです。  
> こんどこそ ごうかくしたいです。  
> This time, for sure, I want to pass.
>
> これこそずっと探していた本だ。  
> これこそ ずっと さがしていた ほんだ。  
> This is exactly the book I have been looking for all along.

こそ implies a contrast: something else might have been expected, or the other person said something about
themselves. In 「私こそまだまだです」, the speaker answers someone who has just said that *they* are still lacking.
After praise alone, that contrast is missing, and なんか／なんて is the humble choice (section 2).

こそ does not attach directly to a plain-form verb such as 辞める or 見える. Fixed reason patterns such as
からこそ belong to a separate topic and are not practised here.

## 2. なんか and なんて: playing something down

**なんか** and **なんて** present something as unimportant, unwelcome or surprising. Both follow nouns. After a
plain-form clause or quoted words, なんて is the usual form.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Noun | なんか or なんて | 私なんか／私なんて, someone like me |
| Plain-form clause | なんて | 辞めるなんて, the idea that (someone) quits |
| Quoted words | なんて (takes the place of と) | 無理だなんて言わないで |

Common uses:

- **Humble about oneself**: 「私なんかまだまだです。」 Someone like me still has a long way to go.
- **Dismissive about something else**: 「あんな犬なんか全然怖くないよ。」 A dog like that doesn't scare me at all.
- **Surprise or disbelief**: 「田中さんが辞めるなんて、信じられません。」 Tanaka quitting? I can't believe it.
- **Unexpected good news**: 「こんなにきれいな海が見えるなんて、思わなかった。」 I never thought we could see
  such a beautiful sea.

> もう無理だなんて言わないで。  
> もう むりだなんて いわないで。  
> Don't say things like "it's impossible".

**Not every なんか is dismissive.** In conversation なんか also means "something like" in a neutral
suggestion (お茶なんかどう？, how about some tea or something?), and it can be a filler. These uses are not tested
in this set; the exercises are built so that the dismissive or humble reading is the one the context needs. For the
listing side of なんか and など, see [Japanese listing and examples](japanese-listing-and-examples.md).

## 3. に限る: nothing beats it, or limited to

**に限る** has two senses. Read the sentence as a whole to decide which one it uses.

| Sense | Typical sentence | Meaning | Clue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| おすすめ: nothing beats this | 寒い夜は温かい鍋（なべ）に限る。 | On a cold night, nothing beats hot pot. | Advice for a situation |
| 限定（げんてい）: limited to | 割引（わりびき）は学生に限ります。 | The discount is limited to students. | A rule about who or what qualifies |

Other advice situations work the same way: when you are tired, on a hot day, and so on. The polite form **に限ります** is used in both senses, so politeness is not a clue.

For advice, に限る follows a dictionary-form verb, a ない-form verb or a noun:

| Formation | Example | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dictionary form + に限る | 疲れた時は早く寝るに限る。 | When you're tired, nothing beats going to bed early. |
| ない form + に限る | 知らない人からのメールは開けないに限る。 | It's best not to open email from strangers. |
| Noun + に限る | 夏は冷たいそばに限る。 | In summer, nothing beats cold soba. |

> 風邪をひいた時は、ゆっくり休むに限ります。  
> かぜを ひいた ときは、ゆっくり やすむに かぎります。  
> When you have a cold, nothing beats a good rest.
>
> 申し込みは、一人一回に限ります。  
> もうしこみは、ひとり いっかいに かぎります。  
> Applications are limited to one per person.

Do not confuse に限る with **とは限らない**, "not necessarily": 高い物がいいとは限らない means that expensive things
are not always good. That pattern is practised in another set.

## 4. だけしか～ない: only, and nothing more

**だけしか** combines だけ (only) with しか (nothing but) for extra emphasis. Like しか, it needs a negative
predicate. The order is always だけしか; しかだけ is not used. しか alone usually gives the same meaning with
less emphasis.

The important contrast is with **だけ followed by a negative**. With だけ, the negative applies to the item that
だけ marks:

| Sentence | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 一人だけしか来ませんでした。 | Only one person came (nobody else). |
| 一人だけ来ませんでした。 | Only one person did not come (everyone else came). |
| 一人だけ来ました。 | Only one person came (neutral だけ with an affirmative). |

> 試験まであと一週間だけしかありません。  
> しけんまで あと いっしゅうかんだけしか ありません。  
> There is only one week left until the exam.
>
> 宿題はほとんど終わりましたが、数学の宿題だけまだ終わっていません。  
> しゅくだいは ほとんど おわりましたが、すうがくの しゅくだいだけ まだ おわっていません。  
> I have almost finished my homework, but the math homework alone isn't finished yet.

だけしか with an affirmative predicate (×山田さんだけしか反対しました) does not work; use だけ there. For the
basics of だけ and しか, see [Only, even, and unexpected inclusion](only-and-unexpected-inclusion.md).

## 5. おきに, ずつ and それぞれ: intervals, steps and each

### おきに: at intervals of

**おきに** follows a number and a counter. With days and longer units, it counts the units *skipped*:
**一日おきに** means every other day (Monday, Wednesday, Friday…). With short units and distances, it gives the
spacing and is usually translated "every":

> この薬は、一日おきに飲んでください。  
> この くすりは、いちにち おきに のんでください。  
> Please take this medicine every other day.
>
> この道には、五十メートルおきに桜の木が植えてあります。  
> この みちには、ごじゅうめーとる おきに さくらの きが うえてあります。  
> Along this road, cherry trees are planted every fifty metres.

**Caution about ごとに.** ごとに counts each unit: 一日ごとに is "every day", while 一日おきに is "every other
day". For distances and short time units the two often describe the same spacing. With larger numbers of days or
with hours, speakers do not always agree on how to count おきに, so when an exact schedule matters, state it
directly. The exercises use only 一日おきに, a distance and 一行おきに.

### ずつ: per unit, one at a time

**ずつ** follows a quantity: 一行ずつ (line by line), 二つずつ (two each). ずつ gives the amount per step and
does not itself say whether anything is left between steps; spacing is おきに's job. A full treatment is in
[Quantity, ranges, frequency and scope](quantity-ranges-and-frequency.md#7-distribution-and-increments).

> ノートには、一行ずつ書きましょう。  
> のーとには、いちぎょうずつ かきましょう。  
> In your notebook, write line by line.
>
> ノートには、一行おきに書きましょう。  
> のーとには、いちぎょう おきに かきましょう。  
> In your notebook, write on every other line.

### それぞれ: each one, respectively

**それぞれ** says that something applies to each member separately.

| Position | Example | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before the predicate | 兄と弟はそれぞれ違う仕事をしています。 | My brothers each do a different job. |
| Before a quantity | 子供たちにみかんをそれぞれ二つあげました。 | I gave the children two mandarins each. |
| それぞれの + noun | どの公園にも、それぞれのよさがあります。 | Every park has its own good points. |
| Noun + それぞれ | 過ごし方は人それぞれです。 | It differs from person to person. |

Compare the position of the two distributing words: **みかんをそれぞれ二つ** and **みかんを二つずつ** describe
the same sharing. それぞれ comes before the quantity; ずつ follows it.

## What the exercises practise

- **Focus**: choose こそ or なんか／なんて from a short dialogue or context line.
- **に限る**: read a sentence as おすすめ or 限定, and build advice with the dictionary form or the ない form of a
  supplied verb.
- **Only**: choose だけしか or だけ; the context tells you whether the item is the only one that is so or the
  only one that is not.
- **Intervals and allocation**: choose おきに, ずつ or それぞれ.

the primary reference: こそ, なんか／なんて, に限る, だけしか, おきに, それぞれ and ずつ are N3 entries there, and
だけ is N5.
