# Kaeru: change, replace, exchange, and return

Several Japanese verbs are pronounced **かえる (kaeru)**, but they are not all interchangeable spellings of one word. Start with the action and grammar, then choose a spelling:

- **[変える](../words/変える.md)**: change a state, property, plan, or content.
- **[替える](../words/替える.md), [換える](../words/換える.md), 代える**: replace, exchange, or substitute, with overlapping spelling preferences.
- **[帰る](../words/帰る.md), 還る, 返る**: someone or something returns; these do not conjugate like 変える.
- **孵る**: hatch from an egg, a separate verb.

One reading trap belongs nearby: **交える is normally まじえる (majieru)**, not かえる. It belongs to a neighboring vocabulary of mixing and interaction, discussed below.

## First separate the grammar

The change/replacement verbs are **transitive**: someone changes or replaces something, and that object normally takes **を**. They are **ichidan verbs**, whose endings attach to かえ-.

The return and hatch verbs are **intransitive**: the subject returns or hatches, rather than acting on a direct object. They are **godan verbs**, even though their dictionary forms also end in える.

| Verb group | Polite form | Negative | て-form |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 変える・替える・換える・代える | かえます | かえない | かえて |
| 帰る・還る・返る・孵る | かえります | かえらない | かえって |

Compare **予定を変えた（よていをかえた）**, "changed the plan," with **家に帰った（いえにかえった）**, "went home." The contrast is not just a kanji choice: **かえた** and **かえった** are different conjugations.

## 変える: make something different

**変える** foregrounds alteration: the state, appearance, arrangement, content, or choice is different from before.

> 予定を変えました。（よていをかえました。）  
> I changed the plan.

> 髪型を変えました。（かみがたをかえました。）  
> I changed my hairstyle.

The kanji **[変](../kanji/変.md)** is the useful landmark for change. The verb does not necessarily mean improvement, and the thing changed need not be a physical object.

Its intransitive partner is **[変わる](../words/変わる.md)（かわる）**:

| Someone changes it | It changes |
| --- | --- |
| 予定を変える | 予定が変わる |
| Change the plan. | The plan changes. |

**変わる is かわる, not かえる.** Similar-looking related forms must be learned with their own readings and grammar.

## 替える: put another item in its place

**替える** is a useful starting point for replacing an item or switching to another one, often a fresh item of the same kind.

> 電池を替えました。（でんちをかえました。）  
> I replaced the battery.

> 古いタオルを新しいものに替えてください。（ふるいタオルをあたらしいものにかえてください。）  
> Please replace the old towel with a new one.

The point is that another item takes its place, not simply that the original item's appearance changes. However, "same kind" and "new" are learning shortcuts, not conditions that every use must satisfy.

**[取り替える](../words/取り替える.md)（とりかえる）** also describes replacement or swapping. It can describe replacing batteries or exchanging seats with a friend; the context tells us which.

The related **[替わる](../words/代わる.md)（かわる）** describes something being replaced or another item taking its place. Do not use the transitive 替える pattern automatically when there is no direct object.

## 換える: exchange, convert, or renew

**換える** foregrounds exchange or conversion, and can also describe renewal.

> 円をドルに換えました。（えんをドルにかえました。）  
> I exchanged yen for dollars.

> 部屋の空気を換えましょう。（へやのくうきをかえましょう。）  
> Let's replace the air in the room with fresh air.

The air example is not a trade between two people. Likewise, exchanging yen for dollars does not require both sides to be the same kind of money.

**AをBに換える** can mean "exchange or convert A into B." The same を/に pattern also occurs with the other transitive かえる verbs, so particles alone do not decide the spelling.

## 代える: let another person or thing fulfill a role

**代える** highlights substitution in a role. The substitute can be a person, but it need not be.

> 投手を代える。（とうしゅをかえる。）  
> Substitute another pitcher.

> 書面をもって挨拶に代える。（しょめんをもってあいさつにかえる。）  
> Use a written statement in place of a greeting.

The second is a formal expression: **書面をもって** means "by means of a written statement," and that statement serves in place of the greeting. It is not simply a request to rewrite the greeting.

The kanji **[代](../kanji/代.md)** also appears in **[代わる](../words/代わる.md)（かわる）**, "take someone's place," and **代わり（かわり）**, "substitute; replacement." Compare **父に代わって（ちちにかわって）**, "on behalf of my father," with changing the father himself.

## These spelling preferences overlap

A practical map is:

| Spelling | Useful question | Typical starting point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 変える | Is a state, content, or choice being altered? | 予定を変える — change the plan |
| 替える | Is another item being put in its place? | 電池を替える — replace the battery |
| 換える | Is exchange, conversion, or renewal the focus? | 円をドルに換える — exchange yen for dollars |
| 代える | Is another person or thing fulfilling its role? | 投手を代える — substitute a pitcher |

These are **not four exclusive semantic boxes**. Replacing something changes it; exchanging something can replace it; replacing a person can also be a substitution in a role.

JMdict groups **替える・換える・代える** in one entry with spelling tendencies, while listing **変える** separately. The Culture Council's spelling guidance also explicitly permits overlap: for forms such as **入れ替える／入れ換える（いれかえる）**, 替 emphasizes putting something different in place, while 換 can emphasize exchange.

Thus, learn conventional expressions and the writer's emphasis rather than declaring every other spelling wrong. The kanji help distinguish interpretations, but the noun and the situation still matter.

## 帰る: return home or to one's base

**帰る** is the everyday verb for going or coming home, or returning to a place one belongs. It can also describe a guest leaving to go home.

> 今日は早く家に帰ります。（きょうははやくいえにかえります。）  
> I will go home early today.

**[帰](../kanji/帰.md)** points toward homeward return. It does not decide whether English should use "go" or "come"; that depends on viewpoint.

The destination takes **に** or **へ**. In **家に帰る**, 家 is the destination, not an object being returned. Remember the godan forms **帰ります・帰らない・帰って**.

A familiar homecoming exchange makes this verb easy to remember:

| Speaker | Japanese | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Returning person | ただいま。 | I'm home. |
| Person welcoming them | お帰りなさい。（おかえりなさい。） | Welcome home. |

Here, **帰り（かえり）** is the **ます-stem** of godan **帰る**, as in **帰ります（かえります）**. Contrast ichidan **変える**, whose polite form is **変えます（かえます）**. The whole **お帰りなさい** expression is a conventional greeting, not an instruction to go home despite its **なさい** ending.

**[戻る](../words/戻る.md)（もどる）**, with **[戻](../kanji/戻.md)**, is a broader "go back; return": the place need not be home, and the verb can also describe a return to an earlier state. **帰る and 戻る can both describe going home**; this is not an absolute home/non-home split.

## 返る: something comes back or a state returns

**[返る](../words/返る.md)** describes something returning to its owner, a response coming back, or a return to an earlier state.

> 貸した本が返ってきました。（かしたほんがかえってきました。）  
> The book I lent out came back.

> 我に返った。（われにかえった。）  
> I came to my senses.

This is not a strict "people use 帰る, objects use 返る" rule: **我に返る** describes a person's mental state, not their trip home.

The kanji **[返](../kanji/返.md)** also appears in **[返す](../words/返す.md)（かえす）**, the transitive verb "give or put something back":

| Returning subject | Someone returns an object |
| --- | --- |
| 本が返ってくる（ほんがかえってくる） | 本を返す（ほんをかえす） |
| The book comes back. | Return the book. |

返る can also concern turning over or reversing orientation. Dictionaries recognize **反る（かえる）** for this sense, but **[反](../kanji/反.md)** also has the different verb **反る（そる）**, "warp; arch; bend backward." Do not assume that every 反る is pronounced かえる, or use 反る as the everyday spelling for going home.

## 還る: a more marked spelling of return

**還る（かえる）** is a documented spelling grouped with 帰る in dictionaries, not a new ichidan verb. It still conjugates **還ります・還らない・還って**.

Writers can choose it to emphasize returning to an origin or source, as in **土に還る（つちにかえる）**, "return to the earth." This makes it worth recognizing in writing, but it is not a rule that every return to an origin must use 還 or that a literal round trip is required.

For ordinary "go home," **帰る** is the practical default. Treat 還る as a spelling choice with contextual emphasis, not as an unrelated verb meaning only spiritual return.

## Another kaeru: 孵る, "hatch"

**孵る（かえる）** is a separate intransitive godan verb: an egg hatches or a young animal emerges from an egg.

> 卵が孵った。（たまごがかえった。）  
> The egg hatched.

It is not another spelling of "change" or "return." Its transitive counterpart is **孵す（かえす）**, "hatch an egg," not 変える.

## Why 交 belongs nearby, but not in the kaeru list

**[交](../kanji/交.md)** appears in **交える（まじえる）**, "mix in; include; cross; exchange," depending on the construction.

> 冗談を交えて話す。（じょうだんをまじえてはなす。）  
> Speak with jokes mixed in.

Other words include **交わる（まじわる）**, "intersect; associate," and **交わす（かわす）**, "exchange," as in **言葉を交わす（ことばをかわす）**, "exchange words." The English idea of exchange connects them with 換える, but their readings and constructions differ.

Similarly, **交代（こうたい）**, "taking turns; replacement," is a compound with an on-reading. Its inclusion of 交 does not make 交える a verb pronounced かえる.

## Two more homophones, not alternate spellings

**蛙（かえる）**, often written カエル, is the noun "frog." It is not a verb in this family.

**買える（かえる）**, "can buy," is the potential form of **[買う](../words/買う.md)（かう）**, not the dictionary form of another change/return verb.
