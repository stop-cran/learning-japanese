# Light movement, effort and progress

The same action can be described from different angles: how it moves, how it began, or how
much effort it took. These examples are not a promise that every similar-looking word can
replace another. For visible form families, see [sound patterns](onomatopoeia-sound-patterns.md).

The practice cohorts mix themes. Find [precise fit, density and disorder](onomatopoeia-fit-and-disorder.md),
[behaviour and reactions](onomatopoeia-behaviour-and-reactions.md),
[bodies and surfaces](onomatopoeia-body-and-texture.md), or
[food, wetness and loose fit](onomatopoeia-food-and-water.md) in the other guides.
This page covers movement, progress and swarms.

## A brief movement or a repeated one

猫（ねこ）がふわりと飛（と）び降（お）りた。  
*The cat jumped down lightly.*

[ふわり](../words/ふわり.md) and [ふわっと](../words/ふわっと.md) both have light,
soft movement uses; [ふわふわ](../words/ふわふわ.md) can describe floating or a fluffy
state. These uses overlap: do not make a strict duration rule from the endings.
The final と of ふわっと is part of the word; ふわりと uses a separate particle.

[ころり](../words/ころり.md) can describe a light roll or fall; compare
[ころころ](../words/ころころ.md), which can describe an object rolling along.
[ちらっと](../words/ちらっと.md) can describe a brief glance, while
[ちらちら](../words/ちらちら.md) has intermittent or flickering uses. Compare the
actual scenes rather than assuming that repetition always counts separate actions.

週末（しゅうまつ）にふらっと旅（たび）に出（で）た。  
*I went on an unplanned trip over the weekend.*

[ふらっと](../words/ふらっと.md) can depict an unplanned or casual action.
[ふらふら](../words/ふらふら.md) can describe unsteady movement, among other uses;
similar form alone does not force the same interpretation.
[ひょっこり](../words/ひょっこり.md) can describe an unexpected appearance or meeting,
while [ひょこひょこ](../words/ひょこひょこ.md) can depict bobbing or unsteady steps.
Existing [ひょいと](../words/ひょいと.md) can describe an easy lift or an unexpected
appearance. [つかつか](../words/つかつか.md) describes purposeful, brisk walking.

## Paths, water and movement in groups

[うねうね](../words/うねうね.md) and [くねくね](../words/くねくね.md) can
describe winding paths; くねくね also describes wriggling.
[にょろにょろ](../words/にょろにょろ.md) can describe slithering.
[ゆさゆさ](../words/ゆさゆさ.md) can depict large, slow swaying or shaking.
[ぷかぷか](../words/ぷかぷか.md) describes buoyant floating; it does not by itself
say what substance supports the object.

[ちょろちょろ](../words/ちょろちょろ.md) can describe water trickling or a child
darting about; [たらたら](../words/たらたら.md) can describe dripping or a trickle.
[ぞろぞろ](../words/ぞろぞろ.md) describes people moving in succession or a crowd.
[うじゃうじゃ](../words/うじゃうじゃ.md) can describe a swarm or cluster; neither
word supplies an exact headcount.
[ぶんぶん](../words/ぶんぶん.md) can depict buzzing or vigorous swinging.
[すうすう](../words/すうすう.md) can evoke air coming through a gap.
[ひっそり](../words/ひっそり.md) can describe a quiet, inconspicuous place or life;
it does not require the absolute absence of sound.

## Working steadily or moving ahead

彼（かれ）はこつこつ模型（もけい）を作（つく）った。  
*He patiently built a model.*

[こつこつ](../words/こつこつ.md) foregrounds steady effort;
[じっくり](../words/じっくり.md) can describe taking time to consider something
carefully. Compare [みっちり](../words/みっちり.md), which can describe intensive work.
[ぼちぼち](../words/ぼちぼち.md) can suggest gradual progress or that it is about time
to do something. [どしどし](../words/どしどし.md) can describe arrivals in succession
or encourage people to act without hesitation.

[ぐんぐん](../words/ぐんぐん.md) can describe vigorous or rapid progress;
[すらすら](../words/すらすら.md) can describe answering or reading smoothly.
Compare [すいすい](../words/すいすい.md), which also has smooth-progress uses.
Similar-looking [すらり](../words/すらり.md) can instead describe a slender figure;
see [body and texture](onomatopoeia-body-and-texture.md).
[じんわり](../words/じんわり.md) can describe warmth gradually spreading, and
[ほんのり](../words/ほんのり.md) a faint scent or color: gradual development and
slight degree are distinct questions.

The drills practise selected adverb-before-verb frames, sometimes allowing と.
For forms already ending in と, such as ちらっと and ふらっと, do not add a second
と. Learn each construction from its card and exercise, not from its English translation.
