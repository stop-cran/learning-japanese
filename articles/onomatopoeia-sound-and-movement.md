# Rain, rolling, walking, and similar-looking words

The [onomatopoeia overview](japanese-onomatopoeia.md) introduces the family.
These comparisons show why an English gloss or a small spelling difference is not enough
to choose the right word.

## Rain is not just "rain noise"

| Word | What the scene highlights | Example phrase |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [しとしと](../words/しとしと.md) | Quiet, gentle, continuing rain | 雨がしとしと降る（あめがしとしとふる） |
| [ざあざあ](../words/ざあざあ.md) | Strong, noisy pouring rain | 雨がざあざあ降る |
| [ぱらぱら](../words/ぱらぱら.md) | Scattered light drops, among other small scattered things | 雨がぱらぱら降る |
| [ぽつぽつ](../words/ぽつぽつ.md) | Separate, intermittent drops or occurrences | 雨がぽつぽつ降り始めた（ふりはじめた） |

The last example means "Rain began falling in separate drops." ぱらぱら and ぽつぽつ
can describe overlapping real situations; they are not calibrated rainfall measurements.
しとしと emphasizes a quiet sustained manner, not merely a smaller volume than ざあざあ.
Script variants such as ザーザー do not turn the rain into a different phenomenon.

[ごろごろ](../words/ごろごろ.md) can describe rumbling thunder, but it is not a rain word alone:
雷がごろごろ鳴っている（かみなりがごろごろなっている） means "Thunder is rumbling."

## ころころ and ごろごろ: rolling is only one use

- 小さなボールがころころ転がった。（ちいさなボールがころころころがった。） —
  A small ball rolled along.
- 大きな石がごろごろ転がり落ちた。（おおきないしがごろごろころがりおちた。） —
  Large stones rumbled down.
- 休日は家でごろごろしていた。（きゅうじつはいえでごろごろしていた。） —
  I lazed around at home on my day off.

[ころころ](../words/ころころ.md) versus ごろごろ supports a light/heavy comparison in the
rolling scenes. The third sentence illustrates a separate established sense, not a person
necessarily rolling across the floor. ころころ also describes frequent changes:
意見がころころ変わる（いけんがころころかわる）, "opinions keep changing."

For [くるくる](../words/くるくる.md) and [ぐるぐる](../words/ぐるぐる.md), think spinning or
going around rather than treating them as alternate spellings of ころころ and ごろごろ.
See [sound-pattern tendencies](onomatopoeia-sound-patterns.md).

## How someone walks

| Word | Typical impression | Not simply |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [すたすた](../words/すたすた.md) | Briskly, without hesitation | Any fast movement, including running |
| [とぼとぼ](../words/とぼとぼ.md) | Trudging, often dejected or tired | A neutral speed measurement |
| [のろのろ](../words/のろのろ.md) | Sluggishly, frustratingly slowly | Necessarily sadness |
| [よちよち](../words/よちよち.md) | Unsteady small steps, especially a toddler | Deliberately walking slowly |
| [ふらふら](../words/ふらふら.md) | Unsteadiness or aimless movement | Specifically a toddler's gait |

彼は何も言わずにすたすた歩いていった。（かれはなにもいわずにすたすたあるいていった。） —
He walked briskly away without saying anything.

疲れて、とぼとぼ家に帰った。（つかれて、とぼとぼいえにかえった。） —
Tired, I trudged home.

Do not infer an emotion solely from speed. A clip could show gait more clearly than a still
image; a future image quiz should avoid claiming an unambiguous walking manner from a vague pose.

## Similar-looking forms are not necessarily synonyms

| Word | Useful starting sense | Remember a construction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [ぺらぺら](../words/ぺらぺら.md) | Speaking fluently; also chatter or flimsy material | 日本語がぺらぺらだ（にほんごがぺらぺらだ） — be fluent in Japanese |
| [ぺこぺこ](../words/ぺこぺこ.md) | Very hungry; also repeated bowing | おなかがぺこぺこだ — be starving |
| [ぺたぺた](../words/ぺたぺた.md) | Repeated light contact or sticking | シールをぺたぺた貼る（はる） — stick stickers on repeatedly |
| [べたべた](../words/べたべた.md) | Sticky; also clingy | 手がべたべたする（てがべたべたする） — hands feel sticky |

This is a **sound/spelling comparison**, not a list of four interchangeable words.
ぺたぺた/べたべた also merits a semantic comparison in
[the texture guide](onomatopoeia-texture-and-warmth.md).

Likewise, [どきどき](../words/どきどき.md) is a pounding heart or nervous/excited feeling,
whereas [わくわく](../words/わくわく.md) normally involves eager anticipation.
Both can fit a happy situation. English "excited" alone cannot make them fair opposing
answers in every quiz.
