# Advice, certainty and partial denial

**Is something worth doing, wrong to do, or simply unnecessary? Is the speaker sure, only guessing, or
ruling something out?** This article covers sentence endings that answer those questions. Most of them
attach to a plain verb or adjective, so the hard part is choosing the one that matches the speaker's reasoning.

| Expression | Attaches to | Job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ～ばいい | ば-conditional | All you need to do is…; it would be good if… |
| ～ばよかった | ば-conditional | I should have…; if only… (about the past) |
| ～ことだ | Dictionary or ない-form | The thing to do is… (advice) |
| ～べきではない | Dictionary form (する → する／す) | Should not; it would be wrong to… |
| ～ことはない | Dictionary form | There is no need to… |
| ～に違いない | Plain form; nouns and な-adjectives directly | I'm sure that…; must be… |
| ～とは限らない | Plain form | Not necessarily; not always |
| ～わけがない | Plain form; な-adjective + な, noun + の／である | There is no way that… |
| ～ないことはない | ない-form | It is not that one can't…; it is possible, but… |
| ～そうもない／そうにない | ます-stem | Very unlikely to…; doesn't look as if… |

The set also uses three N4 forms as contrasts: **はずだ** (expected by an arrangement or known facts),
**かもしれない** (only a possibility) and appearance **そうだ** (looks likely to).

JLPT levels in this series are editorial estimates. Bunpro's placement is the primary reference, and there is
no official JLPT grammar list, so other resources may place these patterns differently.

## 1. ばいい looks ahead; ばよかった looks back

**ばいい** gives a soft piece of advice, says one step is enough, or expresses a hope about something that has not
happened yet. Form the ば-conditional first: 聞く → 聞けば, 押す → 押せば, 晴れる → 晴れれば, する → すれば.

> 受付で聞けばいいよ。  
> うけつけで きけばいいよ。  
> You can just ask at the reception desk.
>
> あとは、このボタンを押せばいい。  
> あとは、このボタンを おせばいい。  
> All that's left is to press this button.
>
> 明日、晴れればいいなあ。  
> あした、はれればいいなあ。  
> I hope it's sunny tomorrow.

The question **どうすればいい？** ("What should I do?") is one of the most common uses.
**たらいい** is very close and often interchangeable in advice: 受付で聞いたらいいよ is also natural.
いい may be written 良い or よい in writing.

**ばよかった** is the past form used for regret: the chance has already passed.

> 今朝、天気予報を確認すればよかった。  
> けさ、てんきよほうを かくにんすればよかった。  
> I should have checked the weather forecast this morning.
>
> 晴れればよかったのに。  
> はれればよかったのに。  
> If only it had been sunny.

The choice depends on time: if the chance is still ahead, use ばいい; if it is gone, use ばよかった.
For negative regret forms such as 言わなければよかった, see
[Necessity, advice and instructions](necessity-advice-and-instructions.md).

## 2. ことだ, べきではない and ことはない

These three can follow the same dictionary-form verb, but they say very different things.

### ことだ: "the thing to do is…"

**ことだ** after a dictionary form (or ない-form) gives advice, often from experience. It frequently follows a
condition such as ～なら or ～たら that names the listener's goal or problem.

> 日本語が上手になりたいなら、毎日少しでも使うことだ。  
> にほんごが じょうずになりたいなら、まいにち すこしでも つかうことだ。  
> If you want to get good at Japanese, the thing to do is use it every day, even a little.
>
> 道に迷ったら、近くの人に聞くことだ。  
> みちに まよったら、ちかくの ひとに きくことだ。  
> If you get lost, the thing to do is ask someone nearby.

Because it sounds like a senior giving advice, ことだ is used with friends, juniors or in general advice,
not towards a superior. This advice ことだ is different from the nominalising こと in 泳ぐことが好きだ.

### べきではない: "should not"

**べきではない** says an action would be wrong or improper. べきでない and the casual べきじゃない mean the same.
With する, both するべきではない and すべきではない are used.

> 図書館の本に線を引くべきではない。  
> としょかんの ほんに せんを ひくべきではない。  
> You should not draw lines in library books.
>
> どんなに急いでいても、運転中に携帯電話を見るべきではない。  
> どんなに いそいでいても、うんてんちゅうに けいたいでんわを みるべきではない。  
> However much of a hurry you are in, you should not look at your phone while driving.

**ものではない** also states a social norm against something (人の悪口を言うものではない), and the positive
**べきだ** says what one should do. The existing practice in
[Koto, wake and formal nouns](koto-wake-and-formal-nouns.md) covers those.

### ことはない: "there is no need to…"

**ことはない** says an action is unnecessary. It does not say the action is wrong. It often appears with
**わざわざ** ("go to the trouble of") or after a reason that removes the need. こともない is a softer variant.

> その本なら図書館にあるから、わざわざ買うことはないよ。  
> そのほんなら としょかんに あるから、わざわざ かうことはないよ。  
> If it's that book, the library has it, so there's no need to buy it.
>
> 雨はもうやんだから、傘を持っていくことはないよ。  
> あめは もう やんだから、かさを もっていくことはないよ。  
> The rain has stopped, so there's no need to take an umbrella.

A rough guide: if the reason only shows that the action is not needed, you are looking at **ことはない**; if the
reason shows harm to someone, **べきではない** is the usual choice. This is a tendency, not a rule. ことはない can
also follow a harm reason as a milder rebuke ("there's no reason to…"), and with a general statement such as
運転中に携帯電話を見ることはない it can simply mean "I never look". The practice therefore offers only ことだ and
べきではない in the items that give a harm reason.

## 3. に違いない, はずだ and かもしれない

**に違いない** states the speaker's firm conclusion from evidence: "I'm sure that…". Nouns and な-adjectives attach
directly, without だ: 母の字に違いない, 静かに違いない.

> 部屋の電気がついている。弟はもう帰っているに違いない。  
> へやの でんきが ついている。おとうとは もう かえっているに ちがいない。  
> The light is on in his room. My brother must already be home.
>
> 毎日長い列ができている。きっと料理がおいしいに違いない。  
> まいにち ながい れつが できている。きっと りょうりが おいしいに ちがいない。  
> There's a long queue every day. The food must be good.

It often goes with **きっと** or **絶対に**. It is a little written or emphatic; in conversation, だろう or
きっと～と思う are also common.

**かもしれない** presents only a possibility. It goes with **もしかしたら** or with weak evidence:

> もしかしたら、午後は雨が降るかもしれない。  
> もしかしたら、ごごは あめが ふるかもしれない。  
> It might rain this afternoon.

もしかしたら, まさか and 一体 as adverbs that set up their ending are practised in
[Attitude, conjecture and manner adverbs](attitude-and-conjecture-adverbs.md#2-adverbs-that-set-up-an-ending-もしかしたら-まさか-一体).

**はずだ** says what should be true according to an arrangement, a plan or known facts. When the expectation
fails, it is はずだ that sounds natural, not に違いない:

> 約束では三時に来るはずだけど、まだ来ない。  
> やくそくでは さんじに くるはずだけど、まだ こない。  
> According to our arrangement, he's supposed to come at three, but he isn't here yet.
>
> 今日は映画を見に行くはずだったが、中止になった。  
> きょうは えいがを みに いくはずだったが、ちゅうしに なった。  
> I was supposed to go and see a film today, but it was cancelled.

はず and に違いない overlap when a speaker reasons from strong evidence (弟は帰っているはずだ is also natural). The
practice therefore offers only one of them in each item where they could both fit. For more on はず and
かもしれない, see [Thinking, possibility and expectation](thinking-possibility-and-expectation.md).

## 4. とは限らない and わけがない

**とは限らない** denies a guarantee: something is not necessarily so. It does not say the idea is false, only
that it cannot be counted on. It often follows **必ずしも**, **いつも** or a general statement.

> 人気がある映画が、自分にとっても面白いとは限らない。  
> にんきが ある えいがが、じぶんにとっても おもしろいとは かぎらない。  
> A popular film isn't necessarily interesting for you.
>
> 今日行っても、すぐ座れるとは限らない。  
> きょう いっても、すぐ すわれるとは かぎらない。  
> Even if you go today, you won't necessarily get a seat right away.

**わけがない** rules the idea out completely: "there is no way that…". わけはない and the kanji 訳 are also used.

> 今日は日曜日だから、あのパン屋が開いているわけがない。  
> きょうは にちようびだから、あの パンやが あいている わけがない。  
> Today is Sunday, so there's no way that bakery is open.

Ask whether the facts leave any chance. If they do, use とは限らない; if they leave none, use わけがない.
**わけではない** ("it doesn't mean that…") is close to とは限らない when it corrects an assumption; it is
practised in [Koto, wake and formal nouns](koto-wake-and-formal-nouns.md).

## 5. ないことはない: a reluctant or qualified yes

**ないことはない** is a double negative: "it is not that I can't". It attaches to the ない-form, very often of a
potential verb: 行けない → 行けないことはない. It says yes, but with a reservation, a condition or little
enthusiasm, so it is usually followed by けど or a qualification. ないこともない is a softer variant.

> 辛い料理は、食べられないことはないけど、自分からはあまり注文しない。  
> からい りょうりは、たべられないことはないけど、じぶんからは あまり ちゅうもんしない。  
> I can eat spicy food, but I don't often order it myself.
>
> 歩いて行けないことはないけど、三十分ぐらいかかるよ。  
> あるいて いけないことはないけど、さんじゅっぷんぐらい かかるよ。  
> We could walk, but it takes about thirty minutes.

Do not confuse it with **ことはない** ("no need"): 行くことはない means "you don't need to go", but
行けないことはない means "I could go".

## 6. そうもない and そうだ

Appearance **そうだ** after a ます-stem says something looks likely to happen. **そうもない** (or **そうにない**)
says there is no sign that it will happen or can be done.

> 西の空が明るくなってきた。雨はもうすぐやみそうだ。  
> にしの そらが あかるく なってきた。あめは もうすぐ やみそうだ。  
> The western sky is getting brighter. It looks like the rain will stop soon.
>
> 雨が強いままだ。しばらくやみそうもない。  
> あめが つよい ままだ。しばらく やみそうもない。  
> The rain is still heavy. It doesn't look like stopping for a while.
>
> 仕事がまだたくさん残っていて、今夜は行けそうもない。  
> しごとが まだ たくさん のこっていて、こんやは いけそうもない。  
> I still have a lot of work left, so it doesn't look like I can make it tonight.

そうもない is often used to decline politely, as in the last example. ないだろう is close in meaning, but it is a
prediction rather than a judgment from what the speaker can see. For more appearance forms, see
[Appearance, resemblance and impressions](appearance-resemblance-and-impressions.md).

## 7. Recognition only: んじゃない and 考えられない

**んじゃない** after a dictionary form is a blunt, spoken prohibition, typically from a parent to a child or in
anger: 走るんじゃない! ("Don't run!"). With a rising tone, the same sound can instead be a question
(～んじゃない？ "Isn't it…?"), so listen to the intonation.

**考えられない** is the negative potential of 考える, used as "unthinkable" or "I can't think of":
ほかの理由は考えられない ("I can't think of any other reason"). It is not practised as a separate target here.

## Common confusions

- **ことはない vs べきではない**: "no need" is not the same as "wrong". ことはない usually follows a reason that
  removes the need, and べきではない a reason that shows harm, but ことはない can also give a milder "there's no
  reason to…" after a harm reason.
- **ことはない vs ないことはない**: the extra ない turns "no need to do" into "it is possible to do".
- **に違いない vs かもしれない**: きっと and 絶対に go with に違いない; もしかしたら goes with かもしれない.
- **に違いない vs はずだ**: an unmet arrangement or plan (約束では, 予定では, はずだったが) takes はず.
- **とは限らない vs わけがない**: "not guaranteed; it may or may not be so" is とは限らない; "impossible" is
  わけがない.
- **そうもない vs そうだ**: for a verb stem, the negative outlook is そうもない or そうにない; avoid
  *やみそうではない* for "doesn't look like stopping".
- **ないことはない vs そうもない**: 行けないことはない says the speaker can go, though with reluctance or a
  limitation; 行けそうもない says it looks unlikely that the speaker can go.
