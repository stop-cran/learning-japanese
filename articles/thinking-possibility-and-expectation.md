# Thinking, possibility and expectation

**来ると思う**（くるとおもう） presents a thought that someone will come.
**来るかもしれない** leaves that arrival possible.
**来るはずだ** presents an expectation supported by reasons.
These are different ways of presenting a judgment, not fixed percentages on a confidence scale.

Keep three questions separate:

- What proposition is being considered: coming, not coming, being quiet, or having already left?
- How does the speaker present it: a thought, a possibility, or a reason-based expectation?
- When is the thinking or expectation located, and when is the event located?

Several formulations can fit one situation. The exercises name a formation operation or ask you to
interpret a supplied statement; they do not invent an exclusive modal choice from a vague English translation.

## 1. とおもう: present the content of a thought

**思う（おもう）** means think or believe here. **と** marks the content of that thinking:

> この部屋は静かだと思う。  
> I think this room is quiet.

**部屋（へや）** means room; **静か（しずか）** means quiet.
The thought is that the room is quiet. The sentence does not independently prove that it is quiet.

Use a plain predicate before と in the basic pattern:

| Predicate type | Nonpast thought content | Past thought content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Noun | 店は休みだと思う。 | 店は休みだったと思う。 |
| な-adjective | この部屋は静かだと思う。 | この部屋は静かだったと思う。 |
| い-adjective | この修理は高いと思う。 | この修理は高かったと思う。 |
| Verb | 友達が来ると思う。 | 友達が来たと思う。 |

**店（みせ）** is shop; **休み（やすみ）** is a day off or closure; **修理（しゅうり）** is repair work;
**高い（たかい）** means expensive in this example; **友達（ともだち）** is friend.
The left column has thoughts about a nonpast situation; the right has current thoughts about a past situation.
For example, **来たと思う** means "I think the friend came," not "I thought the friend would come."

The ordinary full nominal predicate has **だ**, not な, before と. Verbs and い-adjectives do not add it.
Casual thought expressions can omit nominal だ; these full-predicate conversion drills explicitly request
that required copulas not be omitted. An expanded copula such as である is outside the requested short forms,
not generally ungrammatical. For basic quotation, see [explanations, quotations and endings](explanations-quotations-and-endings.md).

### The time of thinking is a separate layer

| Statement | Thinking/held belief | Content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| この部屋は静かだと思う。 | Presented now | The room is quiet |
| この部屋は静かだと思った。 | An earlier thought | The room was judged quiet from that viewpoint |
| 電車はもう出たと思っている。 | Currently held belief | The train has already left |
| 電車はもう出たと思っていた。 | Previously held belief | The train had already left from that viewpoint |

**電車（でんしゃ）** is train; **出た（でた）** means departed here; **もう** means already.
In the last two examples, the departure is inside the thought. **思っている／思っていた** locate
the held belief; they do not simply repeat the departure's time.

思っている often presents a held view or belief; it does not mean "permanent" or guarantee that it is correct.
An earlier belief may later prove right or wrong. For example:

> 電車はもう出たと思っていた。でも、まだホームにいた。  
> I had thought the train had already left. But it was still at the platform.

The later sentence supplies the correction; past 思った／思っていた does not by itself state a correction.
Unmarked 思う commonly presents the speaker's own thought. **母もそう思っている**,
"My mother thinks so too," can attribute a held view to someone else.
Context, questions and quotation also matter: do not turn this learning preference into a universal
first-/third-person ban.

## 2. かもしれない: leave a proposition possible

**かもしれない** means may or might. It works with present, future or past content:

> 店は休みかもしれない。  
> The shop may be closed for a day off.
>
> 昨日、友達が来たかもしれない。  
> The friend may have come yesterday.

**昨日（きのう）** means yesterday. The second sentence is a present possibility judgment about a past event,
not a prediction that the friend will come in the future.

| Base predicate | Before かもしれない |
| --- | --- |
| 休みだ | 休みかもしれない |
| 静かだ | 静かかもしれない |
| 高い | 高いかもしれない |
| 来る | 来るかもしれない |
| 休みだった／静かだった | 休みだったかもしれない／静かだったかもしれない |
| 高かった／来た | 高かったかもしれない／来たかもしれない |

Drop the standalone **だ** from an affirmative nonpast noun/な-adjective before this ending.
Do not delete the past **だった**, and do not add だ after a verb or い-adjective.
**かもしれません** is an ordinary polite counterpart; conversational **かも** is also common.
**かも知れない** is a recognized spelling, not a different degree of probability.
These endings do not assign a numerical probability or belong exclusively to one gender.

### The proposition can itself be negative

Compare:

> 店は休みではないかもしれない。  
> The shop may not be closed for a day off.
>
> 昨日、友達が来なかったかもしれない。  
> The friend may not have come yesterday.

The negative proposition appears **before** かもしれない: **休みではない** or **来なかった**（こなかった）.
The **ない inside かもしれない** does not itself make every proposition negative.
Neither positive nor negative possibility confirms what actually happened.

For conjecture versus an unqualified assertion, reuse the
[N5 conjectural-ending practice](explanations-quotations-and-endings.md#でしょう-and-だろう-conjecture-is-not-a-numerical-probability).
An assertion need not be true either; presentation and truth are different questions.

## 3. はずだ: an expectation supported by reasons

**はずだ** presents something as expected given the speaker's information or reasoning:

> 休みの予定は変わっていない。今日は店が休みのはずだ。  
> The closure schedule has not changed. The shop should be closed today.

**予定（よてい）** means schedule or plan; **変わっていない（かわっていない）** means has not changed.
This is an expectation, not advice that the shop ought to close, and not independent confirmation that it has.
The information or reasoning can still be mistaken.

| Predicate type | Positive expectation | Negative expectation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Noun | 休みのはずだ | 休みではないはずだ |
| な-adjective | 静かなはずだ | 静かではないはずだ |
| い-adjective | 高いはずだ | 高くないはずだ |
| Verb | 来るはずだ | 来ないはずだ |

The affirmative noun takes **の**; the affirmative nonpast な-adjective takes **な**.
Plain verbs and い-adjectives attach directly. Negative predicates such as **静かではない**
also attach directly: do not add another な or の after them.
Past predicates likewise attach directly: **休みだったはずだ**, **高かったはずだ**, **来たはずだ**.

The new attachment drills supply a reason-framed expectation and test the predicate connection,
not independent discovery of the speaker's evidence. Negative nominal drills request full uncontracted
forms retaining は. **じゃない** and forms without that は can be natural outside this specified operation.

The existing [expectation-time explanation and practice](koto-wake-and-formal-nouns.md#はず-past-expectations-past-events-and-the-actual-result)
separate **来るはずだった**, an earlier expectation, from **来たはずだ**, a current inference about a past arrival.
Their twelve time/polarity tasks retain their original identities.
Past はずだった often suggests an unfulfilled expectation, but an appropriate recollection context can
report an earlier expectation that was fulfilled. Neither event time nor actual outcome can be read from
the last copula alone.

## 4. ないはずだ versus はずがない: where does negation act?

Both can lead toward a negative conclusion. Their structure and stance differ:

| Statement | What is presented |
| --- | --- |
| 友達は来ないはずだ。 | I expect that the friend will not come. |
| 友達は来るはずがない。 | The friend cannot be coming / there is no way the friend will come. |
| これは水ではないはずだ。 | I expect that this is not water. |
| これは水のはずがない。 | This cannot be water. |

**水（みず）** means water. In **来ないはずだ**, the expected proposition is already negative:
"the friend does not come." In **来るはずがない**, the proposition is positive, but the speaker
rules it out as incompatible with what they take to be true.
The same contrast works with a noun: **水ではない** versus **水のはずがない**.

For example, an absence reply can support **来ないはずだ**.
If the speaker takes the friend's being far away with no way to arrive in time as decisive,
**来るはずがない** can express that stronger rejection.
These contexts illustrate stances; they do not make every alternative ungrammatical.
The speaker can be wrong in either case. "Cannot" here is a judgment, not a proof of objective impossibility,
and neither construction is automatically a prohibition.

The scope tasks use **内側（うちがわ）**, "inside," for a negative proposition before はず,
and **外側（そとがわ）**, "outside," for the rejection supplied by はずがない.
These are local labels for the displayed contrast, not fixed probability bands or an exhaustive theory of negation.

Do not automatically replace every negative-looking ending with another.
**はずではない／はずじゃない** can deny an expectation or express that something is not supposed to be so;
its discourse use requires context. It is not the keyed はずがない operation in these scope tasks.

## Practice, reuse and follow-ups

The new set has **36 N4 exercises in four small groups**:
24 predicate-attachment conversions and 12 supplied-statement interpretations.
The interpretation labels are shown before answering in a fixed order:
**今／前** for the time of thinking, **肯定（こうてい）／否定（ひてい）** for the proposition's polarity,
and **内側／外側** for the displayed negative scope. Kana answers are accepted.
The modal frames are printed in the attachment tasks; learners transform the supplied polite predicate,
not copy a printed missing answer. Quietness also accepts the recognized spelling **閑か（しずか）**,
without implying that it is as frequent as 静か.

Six shared context keys deliberately connect repeated propositions across tense, modality and scope.
They are not 36 independent conversations or a measure of mastery.
The original expectation-time, expectation-polarity and mixed-routine groups remain in their original files;
only two of the mixed group's six tasks are expectation inferences.
Optional recommendations also reuse N5 quoted-predicate and conjectural-interpretation groups.
There is no new hard prerequisite or forced confidence-ranking exercise.

For overlapping evidence-based impressions, see [looks, likeness and informed impressions](appearance-resemblance-and-impressions.md).
The existing [formal-noun comparison](koto-wake-and-formal-nouns.md#intention-expectation-and-what-ought-to-happen)
also discusses normative べき and intended-role つもり. Those harder comparisons are optional,
not required N4 distractors here.
For the separate reporting family, see [embedded questions, reports and noticing](embedded-questions-and-reports.md).
It distinguishes outer messages and reporting perspectives, reusing this thought group without expanding its assessed scope.

The exact JMdict **はずがない** entry supports its ruling-out meaning; dictionary spelling/reading
support is kept separate from claims about frequency or discourse.

The [source and coverage ledger](https://github.com/stop-cran/learning-japanese/blob/main/docs/grammar-judgment-expectation-sources.json) records qualifications:
we do not adopt fixed certainty percentages, a permanence rule for 思っている,
a universal person restriction, a weak "not necessarily" paraphrase for はずがない,
or the claim that negative expectations must always use はずがない.
The existing expectation article retains its evidence for fulfilled past expectations.
These are sourced editorial curriculum estimates, not an official JLPT syllabus,
native-speaker certification or an exhaustive inventory of possible paraphrases.
