# Pointing and asking questions

Japanese distinguishes **standing in for a thing**, **modifying a noun**, and **naming a place**.
Then it distinguishes asking *which/who/where* from referring to *someone/something/somewhere*.
This is a beginner guide to those jobs, not a catalogue of every use of a question word.

## Three pointing families

| Ordinary physical scene | A thing, without a following noun | Before a noun | A place |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Near the speaker / on the speaker's side | [これ](../words/これ.md) | [この](../words/この.md) + noun | [ここ](../words/ここ.md) |
| Near the listener / on the listener's side | [それ](../words/それ.md) | [その](../words/その.md) + noun | [そこ](../words/そこ.md) |
| Away from both, presented as over there | [あれ](../words/あれ.md) | あの + noun | [あそこ](../words/あそこ.md) |
| Ask for the identity or location | [どれ](../words/どれ.md) | [どの](../words/どの.md) + noun | [どこ](../words/どこ.md) |

Suppose you hold a book while your friend stands across the room:

> これは私の本です。  
> これは わたしの ほんです。  
> This is my book.
>
> この本を見てください。  
> このほんを みてください。  
> Please look at this book.

これ replaces the thing; この belongs with 本. A particle follows the **whole noun phrase**:
これを, この本を. Do not make ordinary “this book” by putting これ straight before 本.
Nor does この need another の: use この本, not このの本.
An adjective can intervene: この赤い本（このあかいほん）, “this red book.”
See [whole noun phrases and their particles](japanese-nouns-and-basic-particles.md#noun-phrase--particle-then-a-predicate).

If your friend refers to the book you are holding, **their** ordinary choice is それ / その本.
The object has not moved; the speaker has changed. A tower well away from both of you can be あれ / あの塔
（あのとう, that tower over there）. A location at that tower can be あそこ.
The hesitation [あの, “um”](../words/あの.md), is a different use from あの before a noun.

These are perspectives, **not distance measurements**. When you stand together, how you divide the surrounding
space can affect そこ versus あそこ. Do not infer an invisible room layout from an English “that.”
The exercises state whose side an object is on, or explicitly frame it as distant from both.

Pointing also works in conversation. それ / その can retrieve something just mentioned; あれ / あの can
refer to something both people recognize from shared experience. The physical table is a starting point,
not a ban on those uses. The exercises here principally practise the stated physical scenes.
Ordinary writing uses kana; recognizing a dictionary kanji spelling does not make it the recommended model.

## Asking which one, which noun, or where

Suppose three bags are on display:

> あなたのかばんはどれですか。  
> あなたのかばんは どれですか。  
> Which one is your bag?
>
> どのかばんがあなたのですか。  
> どのかばんが あなたのですか。  
> Which bag is yours?

どれ stands alone; どの keeps the noun. These examples deliberately give several choices.
For an ordinary choice between two, どちら / どっち is a useful onward expression; this page does not
make the number of objects a universal test for every possible conversation.
どんな asks “what kind of,” rather than merely selecting an item. The
[どんな family](https://bunpro.jp/grammar_points/そんな-こんな-あんな-どんな) is an optional N4 next step
under this repository's grammar-level policy, not a required contrast in these N5 exercises.

Place words keep the particle appropriate to their role:

> ここに受付があります。  
> ここに うけつけが あります。  
> There is a reception desk here.
>
> そこで待ってください。  
> そこで まってください。  
> Please wait there.
>
> どこで昼ご飯を食べますか。  
> どこで ひるごはんを たべますか。  
> Where will you eat lunch?

に locates the reception desk; で locates the waiting or eating activity. どこ is not a substitute for
every location *plus its particle*. See [existence versus activity locations](japanese-nouns-and-basic-particles.md#に-and-で-being-somewhere-versus-doing-something-there).

## Make an ordinary polite question with か

For the polite patterns here, add か after the finished predicate:

| Statement | Question |
| --- | --- |
| 先生です（せんせいです）, is a teacher | 先生ですか（せんせいですか）, is ... a teacher? |
| 明日行きます（あしたいきます）, will go tomorrow | 明日行きますか（あしたいきますか）, will ... go tomorrow? |
| 近いです（ちかいです）, is near | 近いですか（ちかいですか）, is ... near? |

Japanese does not require English-style inversion of subject and verb. Understood participants can stay
unspoken: 行きますか can ask whether **you** will go when the conversation supplies that person.
Both 。 and ？ occur in written questions; the punctuation is not the grammatical operation.

This is **not** a rule to attach か mechanically to every plain statement. In particular, ordinary polite
questions use 先生ですか, not a conversion of plain 先生だ into 先生だか.
Casual questions can instead rely on intonation and other endings; plain-final か has its own tone and uses.
Start with the [polite/plain predicate foundation](japanese-adjective-foundations.md#a-noun-predicate-bridge).

## Ask for the missing information

| Wanted information | Useful expression | What an answer supplies |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A person | [誰](../words/誰.md)（だれ） | A person's identity |
| A location | どこ | A place |
| A selected item | どれ / どの + noun | The item |
| A reason | なぜ / どうして / なんで | Why the event or choice happened |
| A method or means | どうやって | How to do it or what means to use |

> 誰が通訳しますか。  
> だれが つうやくしますか。  
> Who will interpret?
>
> 誰に聞きましたか。  
> だれに ききましたか。  
> Whom did you ask?

Keep the person's role: 誰が asks for the subject; 誰に asks for the person approached.
どなた is a more respectful way to ask who; it keeps the same case role, as in どなたが or どなたに.
Do not automatically replace が with は merely because English starts with “who.”
See [answering who or which](wa-ga-mo-topic-and-inclusion.md#answering-who-or-which).

> なぜ日本語を勉強しますか。  
> なぜ にほんごを べんきょうしますか。  
> Why do you study Japanese?
>
> 病院までどうやって行きますか。  
> びょういんまで どうやって いきますか。  
> How will you get to the hospital?

なぜ tends to sound more formal or analytical; どうして is broadly usable; なんで is common in casual
conversation. These are tendencies, not three mutually exclusive meanings or automatic politeness guarantees.
The reason questions here accept all three when the stated conversation permits them.

**Why versus how is not a one-word partition.** どうして can also ask how, and なんで can mean
“by what means”; なにで makes that latter reading clearer. どうやって is a useful explicit method question.
The context must say whether the speaker wants a reason, transport, a tool, or a procedure.
Do not mark a fitting どうして or なんで wrong merely to preserve a neat English glossary.
The exercises use なぜ as the unambiguous reason button when contrasting it with method.

## Something, someone, somewhere: か inside a phrase

| Unspecified referent | Example of the negative “none” pattern |
| --- | --- |
| [何か](../words/何か.md)（なにか）, something / anything in a question | 何も食べません（なにもたべません）, eat nothing |
| [誰か](../words/誰か.md)（だれか）, someone / anyone in a question | 誰も来ません（だれもきません）, nobody comes |
| [どこか](../words/どこか.md), somewhere / anywhere in a question | どこにも行きません（どこにもいきません）, go nowhere |

The か in 誰か makes the person unspecified; it does **not** by itself make a sentence a question.
In 誰か来ましたか（だれかきましたか）, “Did anyone come?”, the two instances of か do different jobs.
The question does not assert that someone actually came.

Keep any needed case role in the whole phrase:

| Role | Unspecified participant or place | No participant or place in the negative statement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Person approached | 誰かに聞きます（だれかにききます）, ask someone | 誰にも聞きません（だれにもききません）, ask no one |
| Companion / conversation partner | 誰かと話します（だれかとはなします）, talk with someone | 誰とも話しません（だれともはなしません）, talk with no one |
| Destination | どこかに行きます, go somewhere | どこにも行きません, go nowhere |
| Direction of travel | どこかへ行きます, head somewhere | どこへも行きません, do not head anywhere |

Here か comes **before** the retained に / と / へ; も comes **after** it.
More respectful person wording keeps that order too: どなたかに / どなたにも, or どなたかと / どなたとも.
Those forms are accepted in the person-phrase practice; choosing them does not by itself make every surrounding expression honorific.
In the ordinary subject/object “none” patterns, use 誰も来ません or 何も買いません
（なにもかいません, buy nothing）, not 誰がも or 何をも as a mechanical beginner template.
For an unspecified object, both 何か買います and 何かを買います are possible; do not derive a ban on を
from the different 何も pattern. The [basic case-role guide](japanese-nouns-and-basic-particles.md#a-small-map)
explains the underlying に / と / を relationships.

Do not choose か or も just by spotting a negative ending:

> 何か忘れていませんか。  
> なにか わすれていませんか。  
> Haven't you forgotten something?

This checks for an unspecified forgotten thing; it does not say that nothing was forgotten.
By contrast, 何も忘れていません（なにもわすれていません）says “I haven't forgotten anything.”
The meaning belongs to the **whole construction**. Likewise, the negative 誰も pattern is not a claim
that も always means “nobody/nothing”; ordinary 私も行きます（わたしもいきます）means “I will go too.”
Other question-word patterns can be affirmative as well. Do not predict their meaning from も alone.
See [ordinary inclusion and its scope](wa-ga-mo-topic-and-inclusion.md#も-add-the-right-thing-to-the-comparison);
the wider [restriction and inclusion discussion](only-and-unexpected-inclusion.md) is optional onward reading.

## What this practice does

The groups separate pointing scenes, noun-phrase formation, polite endings, requested information, and
indefinite/negative phrases. Interpretive tasks ask what a whole question or negative statement does,
not just which English word matches an isolated Japanese token.
Formation tasks explicitly request the family, noun retention, ending, or case marker being practised.
Scene and meaning tasks remain scaffolded by English context; they are not a claim of spontaneous conversational mastery.
Discourse pointing, the どんな family, embedded questions and the full range of affirmative question-word patterns
are not independently practised here.
