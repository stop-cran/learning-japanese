# Numbers, counters, time and dates

**Choose the unit, write the whole expression, then learn its reading.** These are
different skills. Knowing that age uses 歳 does not yet mean you can read 二十歳.
The N5 foundation has an N4-oriented extension. These are practical teaching
targets, not an official exhaustive JLPT checklist.

## Three kinds of practice

The original counter questions leave a suffix blank in a sentence: 三（ ） for
three pens needs 本. Their typing mode also accepts the suffix alone.
**Write the expression** instead gives a meaning and number: "20 years old"
needs 二十歳, 20歳, 二十才 or 20才, not just 歳. Arabic digits are ordinary Japanese
writing here; you do not have to convert them to kanji.

**Read the expression** shows the Japanese expression and its context. Type its
complete kana reading, not its English meaning or just the unit. Hiragana and
katakana are accepted; small っ, long vowels and the number still matter.
An accepted alternative unit is not an alternative reading: 八個 can be はっこ or
はちこ, but やっつ reads 八つ. Likewise, 四時三十分 expresses the same time as 四時半,
but only よじはん reads the displayed 四時半.

Some expressions are already kana, such as いくつ. They are explained or practised
in the original context questions, not turned into copy-the-answer reading drills.

## Everyday counts: objects, age and floors

Use 本 for long narrow things, 匹 for small animals, 枚 for flat things, 人 for
people, 個 for individual objects, 冊 for bound books, 台 for machines, 杯 for
cupfuls and 回 for occurrences. The native general counting series ends at
**十（とお）**, without a final つ: not 十つ.

Learn sound changes with the whole number: 八本 can be はっぽん or はちほん;
十本 is じゅっぽん or じっぽん; 十杯 is じゅっぱい or じっぱい; 八回 can be
はっかい or はちかい. The じゅっ / じっ alternatives are also used in compounds
such as 三十歳. The answer screen lists the spellings and readings accepted for
that exact expression.

**歳 / 才** counts age. 歳 is the usual full spelling and 才 a common simpler one.
[二十歳（はたち）](../words/二十歳.md) has a special everyday reading; the regular
にじゅっさい / にじっさい readings are also possible. 何歳（なんさい） asks age.
おいくつ is another polite age question when it refers to a person; it is not a
reading of 何歳. With objects, いくつ can instead ask how many.

**階** counts building floors; **回** counts how many times. 三階 can be さんがい
or さんかい. Japanese **1F is the ground-level floor**. The floor prompts use
the Japanese building label, avoiding the different international meanings of
"first floor". 何階 asks which floor.

## Time & dates: keep the sense visible

| Meaning | Written expression | Reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Four o'clock | 四時 | よじ |
| Four hours | 四時間 | よじかん |
| Half past four | 四時半 | よじはん |
| January | 一月 | いちがつ |
| One month of duration | 一か月 | いっかげつ |
| First day of a month | 一日 | ついたち |
| One day of duration | 一日 | いちにち |

Clock hours use 時: pay particular attention to 四時（よじ）, 七時（しちじ） and
九時（くじ）. A duration in hours needs [時間](../words/時間.md). 半 adds half an
hour: 一時間半 is an hour and a half, whereas 一時半 is half past one.
分 counts minutes in both clock times and durations, with forms such as 一分
（いっぷん）, 三分（さんぷん）, 四分（よんぷん） and 六分（ろっぷん）.

Calendar months use がつ, including 四月（しがつ）, 七月（しちがつ） and
九月（くがつ）. なながつ and the clock reading ななじ can also help distinguish seven
clearly in speech; the exercises retain しち as the teaching default.
Duration uses か月 / ヶ月 / 箇月 (also カ月 / ヵ月 / ケ月), read かげつ with number changes:
一か月（いっかげつ）, 六か月（ろっかげつ）. These are spelling alternatives,
not different durations. Native ひと月 and ふた月 also express one and two months;
the month-duration prompts explicitly request the か月 form without final 間.

Dates 1–10 have special forms, as do 十四日（じゅうよっか）,
[二十日（はつか）](../words/二十日.md) and 二十四日（にじゅうよっか）.
Do not extend よっか to every number ending in four. Documented regular alternatives
such as じゅうよんにち, にじゅうよんにち and にじゅうにち are also accepted for the
corresponding written expressions; learn the conventional defaults first.
[一日](../words/一日.md) needs context: ついたち for the date, いちにち for the duration.
Many other day counts share their date and duration readings. Adding 間 can make
a duration explicit: 二日間 is ふつかかん, not a reading of the shorter 二日.

Weeks use [週間](../words/週間.md), beginning with 一週間（いっしゅうかん）.
Years use 年, with 四年（よねん）; 年間 makes a duration explicit.
Age still uses 歳, not 年. Interrogatives include 何時, 何時間, 何分, 何月,
何か月, 何日, 何週間 and 何年; the context tells you which quantity is requested.

## Beyond ten: build the number, then keep the counter

十一 is 10 + 1, 二十三 is 2 × 10 + 3, and 三十 is 3 × 10.
The final part still controls important counter sounds: **十一本（じゅういっぽん）**
is eleven long objects; **二十三本（にじゅうさんぼん）** is twenty-three.
三十本 is さんじゅっぽん / さんじっぽん. In contrast, 三十枚 is
さんじゅうまい and 三十台 is さんじゅうだい: do not contract every counter.

For people, 十一人 is じゅういちにん, not a compound ending in ひとり.
十四人 ends in よにん. 名 is a formal person-count alternative, while 回 and 度
can both count ordinary repetitions. A written alternative does not supply
another reading of the displayed counter.

The compound-form drills sometimes specify a counter or notation. They practise
the **complete expression and its reading**, not independent counter selection.
Contextual suffix questions train that separate choice. Use the requested unit
instead of converting to an equivalent amount in another unit.

## Prices, measurements and temperatures

Japanese large numbers group around **万（まん）= 10,000**:
一万円（いちまんえん）is ten thousand yen; 十万円（じゅうまんえん）is
a hundred thousand; 百万円（ひゃくまんえん）is a million.
Zeros can disappear from the spoken structure: 一万百円
（いちまんひゃくえん）is 10,100 yen, not 11,000.
Practise 三百（さんびゃく）, 六百（ろっぴゃく）, 八百（はっぴゃく）,
三千（さんぜん）and 八千（はっせん）as whole numbers.

メートル / キロメートル measure length; グラム / キログラム measure weight.
キロ alone can abbreviate the larger unit when the context makes it clear.
The symbol is not read as an English letter: 3m is さんメートル.
The decimal point is てん; 0.5キログラム is れいてんごキログラム or
ゼロてんごキログラム. 半キロ expresses the same weight, but is not the
reading of that displayed decimal.

パーセント (%) gives a proportion out of 100. A rate of 25% is
二十五パーセント（にじゅうごパーセント）. A discount rate and the
percentage left to pay are different quantities; these unit drills do not
replace Grammar's interpretation of those sentences.

度（ど）can measure temperature. マイナス五度（マイナスごど）is minus
five degrees; omitting the negative part changes the value. The prompts specify
Celsius; ℃ and °C make that scale explicit.
In the temperature expression **三十八度五分（さんじゅうはちどごぶ）**,
分 is a tenth of a degree: the temperature is 38.5°C. This is **ぶ**, not the
minute reading ふん / ぷん. The same temperature can be written as the decimal 38.5°C.

## Complete dates, timetables and durations

Read calendar components in their own senses:
四月一日（しがつついたち）is April 1, not four months and one day.
二千二十四年（にせんにじゅうよねん）is calendar year 2024;
the final 四年 still uses よねん.

A 24-hour timetable keeps its hour value: 十四時（じゅうよじ）is 14:00,
not fourteen hours of duration. 十九時三十分
（じゅうくじさんじゅっぷん / じゅうくじさんじっぷん）is 19:30.
Use 時間 for elapsed hours instead: 一時間二十分 is an hour and twenty
minutes. 二時間半 is two and a half hours, not half past two.

一年六か月（いちねんろっかげつ）combines a year and six months.
二か月半（にかげつはん）is two and a half months; it is not automatically
a fixed number of days because calendar months have different lengths.
Some written drills specify Arabic digits or a particular unit spelling to keep
the accepted forms clear. For dates and timetables, follow the prompt's Japanese
unit notation rather than substituting slashes, a colon or zero-padded fields.

## Labels and positions are not quantities

二号室（にごうしつ）labels room number 2; it does not say that there are two
rooms. 二番線（にばんせん）identifies railway platform/track number 2.
三番乗り場（さんばんのりば）identifies boarding point number 3.

**目（め）** can mark a position in a sequence: 三番目（さんばんめ）is third,
二回目（にかいめ）is the second time, and 二人目（ふたりめ）is the second
person. Compare 三回, “three times,” with 三回目, “the third time.”
何番目 asks which position; 何番 asks a number. 番 can also express rank in
context, so “番 is always just a label” would be too strong. The drills state
whether they request a label, a particular ordinal construction or a quantity.

## More everyday counters: pairs, clothes, houses and people

The N4-oriented extension adds these practical families. The level is a learning
allocation, not proof that every use belongs to an official N4 list.

| Counter | What is being counted here | Useful model |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 足（そく） | Complete pairs of footwear | 三足（さんぞく／さんそく）— three pairs |
| 着（ちゃく） | Garments | 六着（ろくちゃく）— six garments |
| 軒（けん） | Houses and shop establishments | 三軒（さんげん／さんけん）— three houses/shops |
| 名（めい） | People, especially in formal headcounts | 七名（ななめい／しちめい）— seven people |
| 度（ど） | Occurrences or experiences | 二度（にど）— twice |
| 泊（はく） | Nights spent at lodging | 二泊（にはく）— two nights |

**A pair is not one loose piece.** 左右そろったくつした一足 means “one
complete left-right pair of socks.” A single sock without its partner can be
くつした一枚（いちまい）, “one individual sock.” 組（くみ）can also describe
the matched set. Do not use 足 for every kind of pair: a pair of earrings is
not footwear. Keep 六足 as ろくそく, not a guessed contraction.

**Clothing counters overlap.** シャツ三着（さんちゃく） and シャツ三枚
（さんまい）can both count three shirts. 着 is useful for coats, dresses,
jackets and other garments; 枚 also counts many individual clothing items.
A matched multi-piece outfit and its separate pieces need not have the same
count. In a race, 一着 means “first place,” not one garment: context decides.
Six garments are 六着（ろくちゃく）; eight are 八着（はっちゃく）.

**Count the house or shop, not its rooms or floors.** 軒 is useful for homes,
cafes and small shops. Other units can also fit: 戸（こ） counts housing units,
棟（とう） buildings, and 店（てん）／店舗（てんぽ） shops. A building can
contain several housing units or shops, so these counts are not always
interchangeable. The drills state what is being counted and accept the fitting
alternatives. 八軒 can be はちけん or はっけん; 何軒 can be なんげん or
なんけん.

**名 changes the register, not the number of people.** 参加者六名
（さんかしゃろくめい）, “six participants,” is a formal headcount; 六人 is
also natural. A restaurant host may ask 何名様（なんめいさま）でしょうか,
“How many people are in your party?” When giving your own party's count, say
二名です or 二人です without adding 様 to yourselves. Read every displayed
part: 何名様 includes さま.

**Occurrences are not temperatures.** 三度（さんど） can mean “three
times” or “three degrees”; the surrounding situation supplies the sense.
回 is a neutral occurrence counter and overlaps with 度 in the past-experience
examples. 遍（へん） is another possible occurrence counter, especially with
small numbers. These are not blanket substitutions inside fixed expressions.
The numeric drills use Sino-Japanese readings such as 一度（いちど） and
二度（にど）; native expressions such as 二度（ふたたび）, “again,” have
their own usage and are not the target of those prompts.

## Overnight stays and itinerary days

泊 counts nights at lodging, not buildings and not exact 24-hour blocks.
For example, arriving Saturday afternoon and leaving Sunday morning is
一泊二日（いっぱくふつか）, “one night over two calendar days.”
Arriving Friday afternoon and leaving Sunday morning is
二泊三日（にはくみっか）, “two nights over three calendar days.”
Neither expression promises 48 or 72 elapsed hours.

Keep **二泊 as にはく**, not にぱく. Other useful forms are 三泊（さんぱく）,
四泊（よんぱく／よんはく）, 六泊（ろっぱく／ろくはく）,
八泊（はっぱく／はちはく） and 何泊（なんぱく）.
The itinerary drills explicitly ask for the compact nights-then-days notation;
the other overnight drills ask only for the number of stays.

## Intermediate quantities: grouping, fractions and uncertain amounts

The N3-oriented extension adds practical forms and distinctions. As with the
earlier material, this is a study allocation, not an official JLPT inventory.

### Four-digit groups and place value

**億（おく）is 100,000,000; 兆（ちょう）is 1,000,000,000,000.**
Japanese large-number units group by four digits. For example,
1億2300万（いちおくにせんさんびゃくまん）is 123,000,000.
一億三万円（いちおくさんまんえん）is 100,030,000 yen: empty positions
between the 億 and 万 groups are not pronounced as extra zeros.
Some writing prompts request Arabic digits with Japanese unit characters,
while others allow kanji numerals. Follow the visible notation request.

十億（じゅうおく）is one billion; 百億（ひゃくおく）is ten billion.
In contrast, 兆 causes contraction in **一兆（いっちょう）** and
**十兆（じゅっちょう／じっちょう）**. 一兆二千億
（いっちょうにせんおく）is 1,200,000,000,000: keep both four-digit groups.
These are reading and notation drills, not financial arithmetic.

The **place** of a digit is its [位（くらい）](../words/位.md).
In 123, 百の位（ひゃくのくらい）, “the hundreds place,” contains 1.
The number has three [桁（けた）](../words/桁.md), “digits.”
Place value, digit count, approximate くらい and ranking 位（い）are
different uses. A code read digit by digit is not automatically a quantity:
do not insert 万 or 億 into an identifier merely because it has many digits.

### Fractions and half

For a fraction, put the **denominator before 分の**, then the numerator:
三分の二（さんぶんのに）is two thirds. Think “two of three equal parts.”
分 is **ぶん** here, not the minute reading ふん／ぷん.
This does not change the reading of a time such as 三分（さんぷん）.

For a mixed number, the modern connector is **と**:
一と二分の一（いちとにぶんのいち）is one and a half.
The older mixed-number connector 箇（か）exists, but the drills request と.
**半分（はんぶん）**, “half,” refers to half of the stated whole, not
necessarily half a price or half an hour. Keep the requested quantity and unit.

### Proportions, discounts and increases

One **割（わり）** is a tenth. 二割引（にわりびき）, also written 二割引き,
means 20% off the regular price, not “pay 20%.” With no other charges, 80%
remains to pay. For the distinction between the amount removed and the amount
left, use [quantity interpretation](quantity-ranges-and-frequency.md#9-a-part-and-the-amount-left).
The expression drills practise the form; they do not replace that decision.

七割（ななわり）is 70%; 十割（じゅうわり）is the whole.
**半額（はんがく）** means half the established monetary amount.
**一割増し（いちわりまし）**, also written 一割増, adds 10% of the base:
the final amount is 110%, not 10%. 増し and 引 describe opposite changes.

In the combined **割・分** scale, 四割五分（よんわりごぶ）is 45%:
four tenths plus five hundredths. The value of 分 depends on its scale;
do not transfer this hundredth-of-the-whole value to every use of 分.
It is **ぶ** here, not fractional **ぶん** or minute **ふん／ぷん**.
The extension does not require the historical long tail of fractional units.

Decimal digits after the point are read separately. For example, 12.05%
（じゅうにてんれいごパーセント／じゅうにてんゼロごパーセント）retains the
zero: it is not 12.5%. Either れい or ゼロ can read this decimal zero.
Already established negative temperatures and decimal measurements remain
part of the easier foundation rather than being relabelled N3.

### Ratios and multipliers

**二対一（にたいいち）** is two to one; reversing it to 一対二 reverses
the order of the quantities. The cue must establish which side is which.
[対（たい）](../words/対.md) also connects opponents and scores; it is not
the pair-counter 対（つい）in 一対（いっつい）.

**二倍（にばい）** is a final quantity twice the base, not an instruction
to add twice the base. **1.5倍（いってんごばい）** gives one and a half times
the base; **0.5倍（れいてんごばい／ゼロてんごばい）** gives half.
倍 is not restricted to increases. State the actual comparison: a photo's
width multiplier does not by itself specify its area multiplier.
**何倍（なんばい）** asks for the factor relative to that base.

### Unspecified and approximate quantities

**数十人（すうじゅうにん）** means an unspecified number of people in the
tens. **十数人（じゅうすうにん）** instead places the uncertain part after ten.
Do not swap their order or turn either into a particular exact headcount.
The prefix **数（すう）** does not have a universal exact “two to ten” limit
in every context. Compare the noun [数（かず）](../words/数.md).

Useful forms include 数百枚（すうひゃくまい）, “several hundred sheets,”
数千人（すうせんにん）, “several thousand people,” and
数日（すうじつ）, “several days.” 数分（すうふん）is several minutes:
keep the actual unit and its reading.

In a statement, **何十人（なんじゅうにん）** can evoke dozens and
**何百個（なんびゃっこ）** hundreds of individual items.
Compare 何千（なんぜん）with 数千（すうせん）.
Context distinguishes these unspecified magnitudes from a direct “how many?”
question. Neither supplies a particular exact count. Sentence interpretation
belongs to Grammar; the expression drills explicitly identify the intended use.

**約（やく）** and **およそ** precede an estimate; **程度（ていど）** can
follow the amount: 約二週間（やくにしゅうかん）, “approximately two weeks,”
and 一時間程度（いちじかんていど）, “around an hour.”
These estimates are not exact mathematical minimums or maximums.

Adjacent-number estimates retain important counter-specific sounds:

| Rough amount | Reading |
| --- | --- |
| Two or three hundred yen: 二、三百円 | にさんびゃくえん |
| Three or four thousand yen: 三、四千円 | さんよんせんえん |
| Three or four hours: 三、四時間 | さんよじかん |
| Two or three days: 二、三日 | にさんにち |
| Two or three people: 二、三人 | にさんにん |
| Eight or nine times: 八、九回 | はちきゅうかい／はっきゅうかい |

These are conventional approximations, not a rule that every neighboring
pair of numbers can be joined in the same way. With explicit **か**, repeat
the complete quantity on each side and keep its ordinary reading:
一人か二人（ひとりかふたり）, “one or two people.”
Some writing drills request kanji numerals and specify the permitted comma
form; this is a notation exercise, not a ban on other everyday typography.

## Intermediate units: animals, documents and boxes

**頭（とう）** commonly counts large animals and trained working animals:
一頭（いっとう）, 六頭（ろくとう）, 八頭（はっとう／はちとう）.
For example, 牛一頭（うしいっとう）is one cow, while
盲導犬八頭（もうどうけんはっとう／もうどうけんはちとう）is eight guide dogs.
頭 and 匹 overlap; neither an animal's size nor its job establishes an
absolute grammatical boundary. Read the actual displayed counter, not a
different accepted writing alternative.

**羽（わ）** counts birds: 三羽（さんわ／さんば）,
六羽（ろくわ／ろっぱ）, 八羽（はちわ／はっぱ）and
十羽（じゅうわ／じゅっぱ／じっぱ）. 一羽 remains いちわ, including in
十一羽（じゅういちわ）. 何羽 can be なんわ or なんば.
These counter readings are not the separate noun 羽（はね）, “feather/wing.”
The ordinary whole-bird unit also works for a whole roast chicken; it does not
count each small cut piece. Generic animal/object alternatives depend on the
actual context, not a rigid rule that every bird must take only 羽.

**部（ぶ）** counts complete copies of documents or publications.
資料一部（しりょういちぶ）is one complete copy of the handout/materials;
it can contain several sheets. **一部 is いちぶ**, not いっぷ.
If a complete form is only one sheet, both 部 and 枚 can fit.
Copies of the same bound book can also be counted with 部 or 冊; 部 is not
restricted to loose paper. Count the requested entity, not just its material.

**通（つう）** counts letters, messages and documents as complete
communications. 一通（いっつう）, 六通（ろくつう）, 八通（はっつう）,
十通（じゅっつう／じっつう）and 何通（なんつう）have different sound patterns;
do not contract every number in the same way.
A letter on three sheets is one letter but three sheets. Conversely, one
postcard can be counted both as a message with 通 and as a sheet with 枚.
件 can count email entries, and 本 can count communications.
For a sealed letter, 封 is another unit. Those overlaps do not make a message,
a sheet, a complete copy and a bound volume universally interchangeable.

**箱（はこ）** counts boxes or boxfuls. 一箱（ひとはこ）is one box;
the alternative いっぱこ is also recognized in the expression practice.
One box containing six cookies is still one box, not six boxes.
An empty assembled box can also take 個; flattened cardboard boxes may take
枚. There is no universal package size hidden inside the counter.

## Packages, dishes and collections

The counted entity can be a container, its contents, a whole set or one
member of that set. A preferred counter is not always the only fitting one.
The Japanese question must establish the physical state before asking you
to choose; an explanation shown afterward cannot supply a missing condition.

**袋（ふくろ）** counts bags or bagfuls: 一袋（ひとふくろ）,
二袋（ふたふくろ）, 十袋（じゅっぷくろ／じっぷくろ）and
十四袋（じゅうよんふくろ）. The reading たい is also used, especially
in trade: 一袋（いったい）, 二袋（にたい）.
These readings do not establish the package weight. Two bags containing
two kilograms each are two bags, not two kilograms. A flat empty bag can
take 枚; filled bags also allow ordinary individual-object counts.

**皿（さら）** counts dishes or platefuls of food:
一皿（ひとさら）, 二皿（ふたさら）, 八皿（はちさら／はっさら）.
Empty flat plates are commonly counted with 枚.
The plate count is not automatically the number of people served: one
large salad can be enough for two people. Counts of dishes, menu items
and person-portions can overlap, but are not universal conversion units.

**組（くみ）** counts complete sets or groups:
一組（ひとくみ）, 二組（ふたくみ）and
十組（じゅっくみ／じっくみ）.
A cup and its matching saucer can be one set even though they are two
physical pieces. 組み is also used for sets. In contrast, the numbered
school labels 一組 and 二組 are **いちくみ** and **にくみ**.
The label identifies a class; it does not count how many classes exist.

**人組** describes membership size:
二人組（ふたりぐみ／ににんぐみ）, 三人組（さんにんぐみ）and
四人組（よにんぐみ）. Two three-person groups are 二組 of 三人組;
neither expression alone is the total headcount.
The exercise states whether it wants a group count, membership label or
school-class identifier rather than expecting you to guess the sense.

**束（たば）** counts bundles:
一束（ひとたば）, 二束（ふたたば）and
十束（じゅったば／じったば）.
A flower bundle and a tied stack of paper need not contain the same number
of pieces. Read the actual stated contents. 束 also has readings such as
そく and つか with different dictionary senses and context-specific unit
conventions; **束 does not universally mean 100 pieces**. In particular,
traditional paper quantities are not a rule for every modern tied package.

**通り（とおり）** counts alternatives or ways:
一通り（ひととおり）, 二通り（ふたとおり／にとおり）,
三通り（さんとおり／みとおり）and 何通り（なんとおり）.
Two possible readings of a word are 二通り, not 二通（につう）letters.
Numerical 一通り means one way in an explicitly numerical context; in
other sentences it can mean generally or roughly all the way through.
The practice models さんとおり while recognizing the separately attested
みとおり; acceptance does not imply equal frequency.

## Small objects, cases and housing units

**粒（つぶ）** counts small grain-like objects, including seeds and small
round candies: 一粒（ひとつぶ）and 二粒（ふたつぶ）are the practice
models. Count the loose objects, not their wrappers or bags. Dictionaries
also record the counter reading りゅう; recognizing that reading does not
make it equally frequent with the native-number model.

**錠（じょう）** counts physical tablets:
一錠（いちじょう）, 六錠（ろくじょう）and 十錠（じゅうじょう）.
Do not automatically contract every counter after 六 or 十.
Small round tablets can also be counted with 粒 or 丸（がん）, depending on
their physical form. A package label of 十錠 describes ten tablets, not ten
administrations. The practice uses inventory and original-contents labels,
never a recommendation about how much medicine to take.

**件（けん）** counts cases such as inquiries, bookings and reported
software problems. Compare 三件（さんけん）, three cases, with
三軒（さんげん）, three houses or establishments. Six cases are
六件（ろっけん）; eight can be 八件（はちけん／はっけん）.
Several bookings at one hotel do not become several hotel buildings.
An inquiry email can also be counted as a message with 通（つう）:
the preferred case counter does not make every other fitting unit wrong.

**品（ひん／しな）** can count dishes of food or selected merchandise:
一品（いっぴん／ひとしな）, 二品（にひん／ふたしな）and
三品（さんぴん／みしな／さんしな）.
Learn the numerical compound, not just the separate noun's reading.
There is no absolute rule assigning one reading only to restaurants and the
other only to shops. 品 and 点（てん）can count items with different
individual shape counters together; 種類（しゅるい）counts kinds instead.
Two different products can be both two items and two kinds, whereas two
identical products need not be two kinds.

**戸（こ）** counts dwelling units; **棟（とう／むね）** counts whole
buildings. One apartment building can contain several independent homes,
and each home can contain several rooms.
一戸（いっこ）and 六戸（ろっこ）count the homes, not the number of
families currently living there. 一棟（いっとう／ひとむね）and
二棟（にとう／ふたむね）count the buildings.
軒 can also count homes or buildings in fitting contexts; it is not
universally excluded by 戸 or 棟. Likewise, むね versus とう is not an
absolute small-building versus large-building rule. In a housing-count
context, 八戸 is はちこ／はっこ, not the place name はちのへ.

## Portions, pieces, kinds and rooms

**人前（にんまえ）** counts servings:
一人前（いちにんまえ）and 二人前（ににんまえ）.
Two diners can order three servings; a serving count does not establish the
number of diners or a universal portion size.
**人分（にんぶん）** describes a quantity intended for people:
一人分（ひとりぶん）, 二人分（ふたりぶん）and
三人分（さんにんぶん）.
It can describe recipe yield or materials prepared for a group.
Do not transfer the native one/two readings of 人分 to 人前.

**点（てん）** counts merchandise items or score points:
一点（いってん）, 六点（ろくてん）and
八点（はってん／はちてん）.
One notebook and two identical towels make three merchandise items, even
though 冊 and 枚 are their individual shape counters.
**種類（しゅるい）** and **種（しゅ）** instead count kinds:
一種類（いっしゅるい）and 二種類（にしゅるい）.
Repeated identical items do not create new kinds. A score of 八点 is another
meaning of 点, not a count of merchandise or students.

**切れ（きれ）** counts cut pieces or slices:
一切れ（ひときれ）, 二切れ（ふたきれ）,
三切れ（さんきれ／みきれ）and 六切れ（ろっきれ）.
A slice cut from a cake is not another whole cake.
**房（ふさ）** can count a connected fruit cluster, such as a bunch of
grapes, or an inner segment of citrus:
一房（ひとふさ）, 二房（ふたふさ）and
三房（さんふさ／みふさ）.
A mandarin's two segments are not two bunches of whole oranges.
Generic 個 or つ can also be natural when the scene establishes the pieces
or groupings being counted.

**[株（かぶ）](../words/株.md)** can count company shares or plants with their bases or roots:
一株（ひとかぶ）, 二株（ふたかぶ）and 六株（ろっかぶ）.
A share count does not give its price, and a rooted plant is not simply a
cut stem. The practice asks for stated quantities, not investment decisions.

**丁（ちょう）** counts whole tofu blocks:
一丁（いっちょう）, 二丁（にちょう）and 八丁（はっちょう）.
A retail package can hold one block, more than one block or smaller portions;
the actual product determines the relationship.
Cutting one block into eight small cubes does not make eight whole blocks.
Those pieces can be counted with 個, つ or 切れ as appropriate.
丁 alone specifies neither a universal mass nor a universal package size.

**部屋（へや）** counts rooms:
一部屋（ひとへや）and 二部屋（ふたへや）.
Room 307 is an identifier, 307号室, not a
count of 307 rooms. Several rooms can belong to one dwelling inside one
building. 室 can also count rooms in fitting contexts; the exercise cues
explicitly request 部屋 rather than treating it as the only possible unit.

## Identifiers, ranks and text quantities

**課（か）**, **章（しょう）** and **節（せつ）** can name lessons,
chapters and sections. Follow the divisions actually used by the document:
these labels are not a universal hierarchy for every book.
一課（いっか）and 六課（ろっか）show the contracted lesson-count forms.
Finishing only Lesson 6 is still finishing one lesson, not six.
In structured text, 節 is せつ, distinct from the ふし word used for a tune
or a node.

**第三章（だいさんしょう）** and **第三話（だいさんわ）** identify
Chapter 3 and Episode 3. They do not report how much someone has read or
watched. A contextual short label such as 五話（ごわ）can also identify
Episode 5 without 第; the practice specifies the requested notation.

**巻（かん）** can identify a volume of a work:
一巻（いっかん）and 八巻（はちかん／はっかん）.
Three physical copies of Volume 1 are 三冊（さんさつ）, all bearing the
same 一巻 label. This contrasts the volume identifier with the copy count,
not a universal ban on overlap between 巻 and 冊.

**位（い）** expresses a placing, such as 一位（いちい）or
三位（さんい）. **級（きゅう）** can label a grade:
一級（いっきゅう）and 二級（にきゅう）.
The relevant examination or ranking system supplies the scale; these forms
do not define one hierarchy shared by every system. Modern JLPT labels are
N1-N5, not these 級 expressions.
**段（だん）** can count physical tiers: 一段（いちだん）is one tier,
whereas 三段目（さんだんめ）identifies the third tier.
For a shelf, also establish whether the counting starts at the top or bottom.

**桁（けた）** counts digit positions:
一桁（ひとけた）and 二桁（ふたけた）.
The printed label `07` has two digit positions even though its numerical
value is seven. **行（ぎょう）** counts lines of text:
一行（いちぎょう）and 六行（ろくぎょう）.
Do not substitute the separate group reading いっこう or the bank-counter
reading こう in this text-line context.

**字（じ）** and **文字（もじ）** can both count written characters.
花火 contains 二文字（ふたもじ）, not two words.
二十文字以内（にじゅうもじいない）sets an inclusive maximum of twenty
characters: twenty is allowed. 五十字以内（ごじゅうじいない）similarly
includes fifty. A particular form may define how it counts spaces or other
symbols; there is no universal rule that only 字 can count punctuation.

**便（びん）** can identify a numbered flight or count scheduled services.
三便（さんびん）on a timetable can label Flight 3; 二便（にびん）
can instead count two departures. Neither determines how many different
aircraft operate them. These short practice forms do not prescribe every
airline's multi-digit announcement or radio-call convention.

## Intermediate labels and short durations

**第三課（だいさんか）** identifies lesson 3. It does not say that someone
completed three lessons. A count of three completed lessons can instead be
三課（さんか）. Bare 三課 can also serve as a lesson label, so read the
context; 第 is not mandatory for every label. The writing prompts explicitly
state which meaning and notation they request.

**秒（びょう）** counts seconds: 一秒（いちびょう）is one second.
A minute-second duration such as 一分三十秒
（いっぷんさんじゅうびょう）has two components: one minute and thirty seconds.
It is not a clock hour, and 三十秒 does not contract to さんじゅっびょう.
Adding final 間 can make a duration explicit, but do not pronounce かん
when the displayed expression does not contain 間.

## Bands, decades and centuries

**三十代（さんじゅうだい）** means someone's thirties, not exactly age
thirty. **二千円台（にせんえんだい）** describes a price from 2,000 yen
to below 3,000 yen, rather than an exact price. 台 is also attested with
age bands: the exercises' requested 代 or 台 spelling is a stated
convention, not an absolute partition of their meanings.

**二千十年代（にせんじゅうねんだい）** labels the 2010s in a decade
context. Compare the individual year 二千十年, 2010. 年代 can describe
other divisions of years too; the context establishes the intended band.
[世紀](../words/世紀.md) is a century:
一世紀（いっせいき）and 二十一世紀（にじゅういっせいき）.
The twenty-first century covers 2001-2100. A numbered historical century
is not interchangeable with any arbitrary hundred-year span.

## Practical measurements: retain the stated unit

[リットル](../words/リットル.md) measures volume, not mass.
二リットル（にリットル）and 百五十ミリリットル
（ひゃくごじゅうミリリットル）retain their different units.
0.5リットル is れいてんごリットル or ゼロてんごリットル.
Do not assume that a litre of every substance weighs a kilogram.
In the specified metric sense, one [トン](../words/トン.md) is
1,000 kilograms; 三トン（さんトン）is a mass, not a vehicle count.

[時速](../words/時速.md)（じそく）expresses distance per hour;
秒速（びょうそく）expresses distance per second.
時速五キロメートル（じそくごキロメートル）is five kilometres per hour.
秒速三メートル（びょうそくさんメートル）is three metres per second.
The stated speed does not establish how far someone has already travelled.
Keep the full distance unit in these drills instead of relying on the
abbreviations used in some everyday reports.

[面積](../words/面積.md)（めんせき）is measured area:
二十平方メートル（にじゅうへいほうメートル）is twenty square metres,
not a twenty-metre length. A room can be described as
六畳（ろくじょう）in area; the counter reading じょう differs from the
standalone mat noun [畳](../words/畳.md)（たたみ）.
坪（つぼ）is another area unit: 五坪（ごつぼ）is five tsubo.
These labels do not count rooms or identify a floor.
Physical tatami dimensions vary; do not infer one universal exact mat
size from a 六畳 label. The drills request stated units, not surveying
or traditional-to-metric conversion.

### Rice and recipe measures

For uncooked rice, **一合（いちごう）** and **二合（にごう）** are
volume measures. The Japanese cooking rice cup convention is 180 mL
for one 合, unlike an ordinary 200 mL cooking cup. This is practical
cooking guidance, not a claim of an exact statutory conversion,
fixed rice mass or guaranteed cooked yield.

**大さじ（おおさじ）** is the Japanese tablespoon measure, 15 mL;
**小さじ（こさじ）** is the teaspoon measure, 5 mL.
大匙 and 小匙 are alternative spellings.
大さじ一杯（おおさじいっぱい）is one tablespoonful;
小さじ二杯（こさじにはい）is two teaspoonfuls.
These are calibrated recipe measures, not arbitrary household spoons.
Ingredient mass depends on what fills the volume.

Compact recipes often omit 杯: 大さじ1 or 大さじ1/2.
With the fraction written out, 大さじ二分の一
（おおさじにぶんのいち）is half a tablespoon, and 小さじ二分の一
（こさじにぶんのいち）is half a teaspoon. Keep the named spoon
measure and read 分の denominator-first. A writing prompt may request
杯 or a particular fraction notation; do not add unprinted words when
reading its displayed answer.

### A gap before repetition

**一年ぶり（いちねんぶり）** describes a year since the previous
occurrence. **六か月ぶり（ろっかげつぶり）** describes a six-month gap.
For a visit or repeated event, this gap is not the duration of the event
itself. The expression drills explicitly supply that meaning and request
年ぶり or か月ぶり; producing the form is not an independent
Grammar interpretation exercise.
