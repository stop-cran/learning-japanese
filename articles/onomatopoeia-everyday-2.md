# Sensation, handling and balance

These exercises connect twenty existing onomatopoeia cards with selected bodily
sensations, handling actions and movements. They continue the approach in
[Onomatopoeia in sentences](onomatopoeia-in-sentences.md): choose the sense and
the whole construction together.

Descriptions of pain or shaking are **language practice, not diagnostic categories**.
Neither a bodily mimetic nor a social description establishes an unstated cause,
personality or intention.

## A sensation with する, or a detail with another verb

Compare:

- 足がずきずきする。（あし） — My foot throbs.
- 奥歯がずきずき痛む。（おくば／いたむ） — My back tooth throbs with pain.

[ずきずき](../words/ずきずき.md) supplies the pulsing quality. In the first,
する forms the sensation predicate; in the second, 痛む already supplies the pain
verb. Do not insert another finite predicate before it.

The following are useful starting impressions, not a clinical classification:

| Word | Selected sensation |
| --- | --- |
| [ちくちく](../words/ちくちく.md) | Small repeated pricks or a scratchy sting |
| [ひりひり](../words/ひりひり.md) | Raw, smarting or burning sting |
| [きりきり](../words/きりきり.md) | Sharp, gripping pain |
| [じんじん](../words/じんじん.md) | Lingering tingling, throbbing or numbness |
| [ぴりぴり](../words/ぴりぴり.md) | A sharp sting or tingle |

There is overlap. A description does not prove that an actual needle is present,
that a particular disease is involved, or that one person's experience is objectively
more intense than another's.

**胃がきりきりと痛む**（い）, “my stomach hurts sharply,” is a useful whole phrase.
The pain sense allows と even though the pinned JMdict entry's POS label is simply
“adverb.” A dictionary's tag list is not a complete list of surface constructions.
The rotation, grinding and brisk-action senses of きりきり are outside these tasks.

In the selected sensation frames, the tasks retain supported bare and と alternatives.
If する, した or している is already printed, do not repeat it in the blank.
Elsewhere, an ordinary contraction such as してる may be possible; a printed ending
is not an invitation to rewrite the rest of the sentence.

## Physical and social uses must stay separate

### ちくちく and ぴりぴり

- セーターで首がちくちくする。（くび）
  — The sweater makes my neck feel prickly.
- 嫌味をちくちく言う。（いやみ／いう）
  — Make repeated needling remarks.

The second is a documented figurative speech use, not a claim that someone physically
pricked the listener. It does not have to be forced into the bodily-sensation frame.

- 舌がぴりぴりする。（した） — My tongue tingles.
- 職場の空気がぴりぴりしている。（しょくば／くうき）
  — The atmosphere at work is tense.

Here 空気 means the mood. The workplace sentence does not imply a literal skin sting,
a shrill noise, deliberate intimidation or hostility. Other tearing and sound senses
of ぴりぴり exist, but are not automatically present in an on-edge person.

### むずむず and ぞくぞく

[むずむず](../words/むずむず.md) can describe an itchy, tickly bodily feeling or
an eager, restless urge:

- 鼻がむずむずする。（はな） — My nose tickles.
- 走りたくてむずむずしている。（はしりたくて）
  — I am itching to run.

The desired action is explicit in the second example. Literal skin itching is not required.
The first does not identify an allergy or any other medical cause.

[ぞくぞく](../words/ぞくぞく.md) can be chilly, fearful or thrilling:

- 冷たい風でぞくぞくした。 — The cold wind made me feel shivery.
- 好きな歌手の登場が近づき、ぞくぞくしている。
  （かしゅ／とうじょう／ちかづき）
  — My favorite singer's appearance is approaching, and I feel a shivery thrill.

Anticipatory pleasure is supplied by the second scene; it is not mandatory in every
ぞくぞく sentence. This is also not **続々**（ぞくぞく）, the separate expression
for things happening one after another.

## Movement, sound and the body's experience

[ぶるぶる](../words/ぶるぶる.md) emphasizes trembling or shaking:
体がぶるぶる震える（からだ／ふるえる）, “the body trembles.”
Cold, fear and other causes are possible. A trembling hand alone does not establish
habitual timidity.

[がんがん](../words/がんがん.md) changes interpretation with its subject and verb:

| Frame | Interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| 音楽ががんがん鳴る（おんがく／なる） | Music blasts loudly |
| 頭ががんがんする（あたま） | A pounding sensation in the head |
| がんがん働く（はたらく） | Work flat out, in casual speech |

A headache does not need to be an audible sound inside the head. Working hard does
not require literal banging.

[げほげほ](../words/げほげほ.md) depicts forceful repeated coughing:
げほげほせきこむ, “break into a heavy cough,” or げほげほせきをする,
“cough heavily.” These sound impressions do not identify a disease.

## Actions and their results are not interchangeable

[ごしごし](../words/ごしごし.md) describes forceful rubbing or scrubbing.
It can modify an unsuccessful action or a negative request:

- ごしごしこすったが、汚れは落ちなかった。（よごれ／おちなかった）
  — I scrubbed hard, but the stain did not come off.
- ごしごしこすらないでください。 — Please do not rub hard.

The first does not contradict the mimetic: effort need not succeed.
The second does not assert that the action occurred.

[ぐしゃぐしゃ](../words/ぐしゃぐしゃ.md) can describe crumpling, disorder or a sodden mess:

- 紙をぐしゃぐしゃに丸めた。（かみ／まるめた）
  — I crumpled the paper into a ball.
- 髪がぐしゃぐしゃだ。（かみ） — My hair is a mess.
- 雨でノートがぐしゃぐしゃになった。
  — The rain turned the notebook into a soggy mess.

紙 and 髪 share the reading かみ but are different words. Messy hair need not be wet;
crumpled paper need not be torn. The action example also permits a bare or と
description of crumpling manner. に presents the resulting disordered shape.

[びりびり](../words/びりびり.md) has three selected scenes:

- 封筒をびりびり破く。（ふうとう／やぶく） — Rip an envelope apart.
- 窓がびりびり震える。（まど／ふるえる） — A window rattles.
- 手がびりびりする。（て） — A hand has an electric-like tingling feeling.

The tearing task accepts と for manner and に for a torn-up result. The bodily
sensation does not by itself prove actual electrical injury, and the window does
not experience bodily pain.

## Crowding, bulging and flatness

[ぎゅうぎゅう](../words/ぎゅうぎゅう.md) highlights pressure and crowding;
[ぱんぱん](../words/ぱんぱん.md) often highlights fullness and bulging.
They overlap in tightly filled containers, so these tasks supply the intended word
rather than pretending that every packed bag admits only one description.

- 服をぎゅうぎゅう詰める。（ふく／つめる） — Cram clothes in tightly.
- 電車は人でぎゅうぎゅうだ。（でんしゃ） — The train is packed with people.
- かばんがぱんぱんだ。 — The bag is bulging.

For the packing action, **ぎゅうぎゅう**, **ぎゅうぎゅうと** and
**ぎゅうぎゅうに** all fit the selected frame. That does not license と directly
before the state copula だ. “Full to bursting” is not a prediction that a bag must break.

The other selected ぱんぱん use is sound: 手をぱんぱんたたく, “clap the hands sharply.”
Clapping dust off the hands need not express approval or applause.

[ぺちゃんこ](../words/ぺちゃんこ.md) foregrounds flatness:

- 空き缶をぺちゃんこにつぶす。（あきかん） — Crush an empty can flat.
- パンはぺちゃんこだ。 — The bread is squashed flat.

An action's resulting condition uses に here; the state takes the printed copula.
Flat things can remain intact. “Messy,” “torn,” “wet” and “flat” are not equivalent.

## Turning, wobbling and swaying

[くるり](../words/くるり.md) presents a compact turn:
くるりと振り返る（ふりかえる）, “quickly turn to look back.”
It is not a measurement demanding precisely 360 degrees.

[ぐらぐら](../words/ぐらぐら.md) can describe unstable support:
椅子の脚がぐらぐらする（いす／あし）, “the chair leg wobbles.”
But **湯がぐらぐら煮立つ**（ゆ／にたつ） describes water boiling vigorously.
The subject is the water, not an unstable cooking pot.

[ゆらゆら](../words/ゆらゆら.md) describes repeated swaying or wavering:
炎がゆらゆら揺れる（ほのお／ゆれる）, “a flame wavers.”
A boat rocking ゆらゆら is not necessarily damaged or about to capsize.

## Response scope and sources

Keep the supplied word in hiragana or katakana, without changing its shape.
Fill only the whole blank; leave printed words and endings untouched.
Some screens offer two valid choices: choose either one. Interpretation tasks instead
ask for exactly **はい** or **いいえ**, under the definitions printed in that task.

The answer lists are bounded authored strings, not free sentence grading. These
exercises add no Grammar JLPT labels, gates or vocabulary-mastery requirements.
The pilot guide explains scope controls. Studying these scenes is not verified
transfer, a medical assessment or evidence of learning effectiveness.

The cards link the pinned
[JMdict](https://www.edrdg.org/wiki/index.php/JMdict-EDICT_Dictionary_Project)
entries. Consequential construction checks also used Digital Daijisen's actual
[ちくちく](https://www.weblio.jp/content/ちくちく),
[きりきり](https://www.weblio.jp/content/きりきり),
[ぴりぴり](https://www.weblio.jp/content/ぴりぴり),
[ぎゅうぎゅう](https://www.weblio.jp/content/ぎゅうぎゅう) and
[ぱんぱん](https://www.weblio.jp/content/ぱんぱん) entries, and the
[むずむず entry in Seisenban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten](https://kotobank.jp/word/むずむず-641744).
Historical examples document uses, not equal modern frequency.
