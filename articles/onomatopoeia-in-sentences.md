# Onomatopoeia in sentences

Knowing that [ぐっすり](../words/ぐっすり.md) concerns sound sleep does not yet tell you how
to put it into a sentence. Compare **ぐっすり眠る**（ねむる）, "sleep soundly," with
**[へとへと](../words/へとへと.md)になる**, "become exhausted." Both describe a vivid
experience, but they do not use the same construction.

The twelve-word core set practises **a familiar word, one selected sense, and a sentence frame
together**. The focused sets below extend that approach across the existing 203-word
[onomatopoeia collection](japanese-onomatopoeia.md). Neither the core nor the expansion is a
suffix chart that works for every word or every dictionary sense.

## Before a verb: keep the action

Some words describe how an action happens:

- 音楽を止めたら、子供はぐっすり眠った。
  （おんがくをとめたら、こどもはぐっすりねむった。）
  - After we stopped the music, the child slept soundly.
- 風船のひもを[しっかり](../words/しっかり.md)持っていてね。
  （ふうせんのひもをしっかりもっていてね。）
  - Keep a firm hold on the balloon's string.

The sleep or holding verb remains in the sentence. ぐっすり does not replace 眠る, and it
does not mean "quickly." The adverb itself does not change for past time or a wish:
ぐっすり眠った and ぐっすり眠りたい retain the same ぐっすり.

In these combinations, と is optional: **ぐっすりと眠る**, **しっかりと持つ**.
[にこにこ](../words/にこにこ.md)笑う（わらう）, "smile cheerfully," and
[きらきら](../words/きらきら.md)光る（ひかる）, "glitter," also allow と with these verbs.
にこにこ笑う need not involve laughter aloud.

But **[そっと](../words/そっと.md)** already ends in と as part of the supplied word:
そっと触れる（ふれる）, "touch gently." Do not add another と. Its emphatic forms,
such as そうっと, are real forms, but this pilot asks you to keep the supplied word shape.
Neither the spelling of a word nor the tag "onomatopoeia" makes と universally optional.

## With する: the reaction is the predicate

These selected reactions commonly form predicates with する:

| Word and selected sense | Example | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [わくわく](../words/わくわく.md): eager anticipation | わくわくする／わくわくしている | Feel excited in anticipation / be experiencing that excitement |
| [どきどき](../words/どきどき.md): pounding heart | 胸がどきどきする（むね） | My heart pounds |
| [いらいら](../words/いらいら.md): irritation | いらいらしている | Be irritated or impatient |

The polite nonpast form of する is **します**, not です: わくわくする → わくわくします.
This is a formation exercise, not a claim that a conversational exclamation such as
わくわくです can never be heard. With the ongoing ending already printed, insert the
word without repeating the verb: わくわく + しています → わくわくしています.

The と versions are also accepted for these reaction frames, for example
いらいらとしています. Check this use separately from と before an ordinary action verb.
It is not the unrelated **volitional + とする**, as in 出ようとする（でようとする）,
"try to leave / be about to leave."

The whole scene still matters. Running can cause どきどき without pleasant excitement.
いらいらしている describes the person experiencing irritation, not deliberately irritating
someone else. None of these ordinary expressions diagnoses a medical condition.

## Before a noun: more than one fitting form

A characteristic can be attached to a noun, but the connector depends on the word and sense:

| Selected use | Useful models | Important qualification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A dependable person | しっかりした人／しっかりしている人 | The shorter した description need not be past-only. |
| Fluffy texture | [ふわふわ](../words/ふわふわ.md)の毛布／ふわふわな毛布／ふわふわした毛布（もうふ） | の and な can both fit; texture is not floating or lightheadedness. |
| Oversized fit | [ぶかぶか](../words/ぶかぶか.md)の靴／ぶかぶかな靴／ぶかぶかした靴（くつ） | Judge the fit for the wearer, not softness or fashion. |

The な in these texture and fit descriptions is not a template for every mimetic word.
For an ordinary dependable-person description, use **しっかりした人** or **しっかりしている人**,
not **しっかりな人**.

**しっかりした人** can describe someone who is dependable **now**. Likewise,
ふわふわした毛布 can describe a blanket that is fluffy now. Do not read every descriptive
した as "used to be this way, but no longer is." Read the whole sentence and characteristic.

For example:

友達はしっかりした人なので、安心して相談できる。
（ともだちはしっかりしたひとなので、あんしんしてそうだんできる。）
- My friend is dependable, so I can consult them with confidence.

The contextual modifier tasks accept fitting verbal descriptions as well as the short models:
した, している, and ordinary contracted してる, with と where it fits. The texture and
oversized-fit tasks also accept fitting する modifiers and formal である. These are alternatives
within a bounded response, not a demand to memorize every variant at once.

The one explicitly named **した formation drill** is narrower: it asks for that transformation.
Other grammatically possible descriptions are not wrong everywhere; they are outside that
particular operation. In the contextual tasks, do not invent a longer simile or change the
selected sense just to make a different answer work.

Product names and headlines can use compact expressions such as a bare mimetic word joined
to a noun. This pilot practises ordinary sentence modifiers instead. It does not claim to
grade every possible compound, quotation or creative expression.

## A state, and a change into that state

For the selected exhaustion, hunger and oversized-fit descriptions, learn these frames:

| State | Change |
| --- | --- |
| へとへとだ／へとへとです - be exhausted | へとへとになる - become exhausted |
| お腹が[ぺこぺこ](../words/ぺこぺこ.md)だ／ぺこぺこです（おなか） - be very hungry | お腹がぺこぺこになる - become very hungry |
| 私にはぶかぶかです（わたし） - it is too big on me | The pilot practises the state and noun modifiers for this word, not all possible changes. |

Before a supplied **です**, the word goes directly into the blank. Do not insert な or に
between へとへと and です. With **なる**, に is the ordinary resulting-state connector.
The change tasks also accept the more formal と + なる form, without presenting it as the
default everyday model or confusing it with manner と.

Learn senses separately. ぺこぺこ can also describe repeated bowing or a flexing surface.
Those uses often have する. Even for hunger, a historical dictionary records a する example:
the useful modern model お腹がぺこぺこです is **not** proof that every hunger-plus-する
expression is impossible. A wrong option in a quiz is rejected in its **printed sentence**,
not as a universal ban on the word.

## Finding and using the practice

Open **Grammar**, select **All** or **Unclassified** for new material, and find
**Onomatopoeia in sentences** in the chapter browser. The core set's groups cover everyday actions
and reactions, people and things, mixed scenes, and reading descriptions; the focused sets add
thematic groups to the same chapter. Targeted practice
uses the existing Grammar controls. These tasks have no new hard prerequisite gates.

The browser has a separate scope filter. If the topic is hidden, use **Clear search and
browse filters**, or open **Filters and next topic** and choose **All** or **Unclassified**
there too. **Accessible list** provides a list instead of the diagram. **Chapters** collects the
core and focused groups together; **Article sets** offers their individual sets. Opening the
same exercise through either route does not create separate progress.

The tasks deliberately have **no JLPT label**: a word card's level does not classify a
complete Grammar exercise. Newly unseen unclassified tasks will not enter an N5/N4/N3-only
new-material queue. Already-started Grammar reviews retain the app's normal scope behavior.
Vocabulary-card progress does not automatically introduce or complete Grammar tasks.

For the core completion tasks, retain the supplied word in hiragana or katakana and type only the
missing fragment, without extra punctuation or content words. If a connector is optional,
the blank includes the word too, so you never need to submit an empty answer.

Some choice appearances show two valid answers: **choose either one**, not both. All authored
typed alternatives remain accepted even when the screen displays only the model choice.
Typing uses an exact accepted-answer list with whitespace trimming and NFKC normalization;
it is not unrestricted sentence grading, automatic kana folding or typo correction.
The supported hiragana/katakana strings are explicitly listed.

On the answer screen, compare your response, the model sentence and the explanation.
A valid typed alternative may differ from the model sentence without being an error.
Optional vocabulary help supplies the meaning/reading of words around the blank; consulting
a card or this guide is study support, not an unassisted trial result.

This is supplementary practice. Familiarity with these fixed items is not proof of transfer
to a new situation. Learning effectiveness remains unverified for both the core and the expansion.
