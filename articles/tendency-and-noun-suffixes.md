# Tendency, state and noun-forming suffixes

**Is it a habit, a slight sign, or a character? Is it covered, left, felt or measured?** Japanese answers many of
these questions with a short ending attached to a noun, an adjective or a verb stem. This article covers thirteen
such endings and the familiar forms they are usually confused with. The useful habit is to look at what the
ending attaches to and at what the sentence is about before choosing.

| Ending | Attaches to | Works like | Job |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| がち | Noun; verb stem | な-adjective (がちだ, がちな) | Tends to happen; prone to (often unwelcome) |
| 気味（ぎみ） | Noun; verb stem | Noun (気味だ, 気味の) | A slight sign of a state, now |
| っぽい | Noun; verb stem; い-adjective stem | い-adjective (っぽく, っぽくて) | -ish; having the character of |
| だらけ | Noun | Noun (だらけだ, だらけの) | Covered in, full of (unwelcome) |
| っぱなし | Verb stem | Noun (っぱなしだ, っぱなしにする) | Left as it is; kept going |
| み | い-adjective stem (some) | Noun | A felt quality, or a thing that has it |
| さ | い/な-adjective stem | Noun | A quality as a degree you can measure |
| 的（てき） | Noun (mostly kanji compounds) | な-adjective (的な, 的に) | -ic, -al, typical of |
| 型（がた） | Noun | Noun (型の) | Type, model, size class |
| 同士（どうし） | Noun naming the shared relation (often a person: 恋人; also 隣) | Noun | People in the same relation to each other |
| たち | Noun for people | Noun | Plural or group |
| 当たり（あたり） | Number + counter | Suffix on a counter, before the amount | Per unit |
| 辺り（あたり） | Place or time word (including dates such as 五月) | Noun | Around, roughly |
| 向き（むき） | Noun | Noun (向きの, 向きだ) | Facing; naturally suited to |
| 向け（むけ） | Noun | Noun (向けの, 向けに) | Made or intended for; bound for |
| がたい | Verb stem | い-adjective | Nearly impossible, as the speaker feels it; written style, fixed phrases |
| にくい | Verb stem | い-adjective | Hard or awkward to do; not apt to happen |

さ, たち and にくい are earlier-level forms that appear here as contrasts. The humble plural ending ども (私ども) is mentioned below
for reading only.

JLPT levels in this series are editorial estimates. Bunpro's placement is the primary reference, and there is
no official JLPT grammar list, so other resources may place these patterns differently.

## 1. Tendency: がち, 気味 and っぽい

**がち** says something tends to happen again and again, usually something unwelcome. It attaches to a noun or a
verb stem and behaves like a な-adjective: 忘れがちだ, しがちな間違い. Words such as よく, 〜と (whenever) and
general statements are good clues.

> 冬は、運動不足になりがちだ。  
> ふゆは、うんどうぶそくに なりがちだ。  
> In winter, people tend to get too little exercise.
>
> 子どものころは病気がちで、よく学校を休んだ。  
> こどものころは びょうきがちで、よく がっこうを やすんだ。  
> As a child I was often sick and was often absent from school.

**気味（ぎみ）** reports a slight sign of a state at one time: "a touch of", "a bit". It behaves like a noun:
風邪気味だ, 緊張気味の顔. Clues are 少し, 今日は, 今朝から and a single occasion.

> 今朝からのどが痛い。風邪気味なので、早く寝よう。  
> けさから のどが いたい。かぜぎみなので、はやく ねよう。  
> My throat has hurt since this morning. I have a touch of a cold, so I'll go to bed early.
>
> 初めての発表で、彼は少し緊張気味だった。  
> はじめての はっぴょうで、かれは すこし きんちょうぎみだった。  
> At his first presentation he was a little nervous.

**っぽい** means "-ish", "like", "having the character of". It makes an い-adjective, so it is followed by a noun
directly, by 〜く before a verb, or by 〜くて. It can also describe a person's habit (怒りっぽい, quick to anger;
忘れっぽい, forgetful).

> もう大学生なのに、弟の話し方はまだ子どもっぽい。  
> もう だいがくせいなのに、おとうとの はなしかたは まだ こどもっぽい。  
> He is already a university student, but my brother still talks in a childish way.
>
> 遠くから見ると、その建物は白っぽく見えた。  
> とおくから みると、その たてものは しろっぽく みえた。  
> From far away, the building looked whitish.

**Common confusion.** 風邪がち (often catching colds) is a habit; 風邪気味 (a touch of a cold) is today's state;
風邪っぽい (feels like a cold) is a casual impression. Ask whether the sentence describes something that keeps
happening (がち), a slight state now (気味) or a character that is like something (っぽい). In the exercises, each
option includes the ending the slot needs: がちだ, がちな, 気味だ, 気味の, っぽい, っぽく, っぽくて.

## 2. Covered or left: だらけ and っぱなし

**だらけ** follows a noun: covered in or full of something, usually unwelcome (泥, ほこり, 間違い, 傷).

> 雨の中でサッカーをして、服が泥だらけになった。  
> あめの なかで さっかーを して、ふくが どろだらけに なった。  
> I played soccer in the rain and my clothes got covered in mud.
>
> 急いで書いたので、作文は間違いだらけだった。  
> いそいで かいたので、さくぶんは まちがいだらけだった。  
> I wrote it in a hurry, so the essay was full of mistakes.

**っぱなし（っ放し）** follows a verb stem. It means something was left in a state that should have changed
(つけっぱなし, left on), or that an action kept going (立ちっぱなし, standing the whole time).

> テレビをつけっぱなしにして寝てしまった。  
> てれびを つけっぱなしに して ねて しまった。  
> I fell asleep with the TV left on.
>
> 電車が混んでいて、一時間ずっと立ちっぱなしだった。  
> でんしゃが こんでいて、いちじかん ずっと たちっぱなしだった。  
> The train was crowded, so I stood the whole hour.

**Common confusion.** The two endings attach to different parts of speech, so first ask what the base word is: a
noun (泥, 傷) takes だらけ, a verb stem (つけ, 立ち, やられ) takes っぱなし. Some words, such as 間違い, are both a
noun and a verb stem; then the meaning decides. An essay full of mistakes is 間違いだらけ.

## 3. み and さ

Both turn an adjective into a noun. **さ** names a quality as a degree, often measured or compared: 高さ (height),
深さ (depth), 大きさ (size). **み** names a quality as something felt or seen, or a thing that has it. It works
with a limited set of adjectives: 楽しみ (something to look forward to), 痛み (pain), 強み (strong point), 赤み
(a tinge of red), 深み (depth felt in flavour or character, or a deep spot in water), 重み (felt weight,
importance).

> 来月の旅行が今から楽しみです。  
> らいげつの りょこうが いまから たのしみです。  
> I'm already looking forward to next month's trip.
>
> けがをしたところに、少し赤みが出てきた。  
> けがを した ところに、すこし あかみが でてきた。  
> A little redness appeared where I was hurt.
>
> この湖の深さは約三十メートルだ。  
> この みずうみの ふかさは やく さんじゅうめーとるだ。  
> This lake is about thirty metres deep.

**Common confusion.** A number in metres or kilograms, or a verb such as 量る（はかる, weigh）, points to さ. A set phrase such as 〜が楽しみだ, 痛み止め
(painkiller) or 〜みが出る points to み.

## 4. 的 and 型

**的（てき）** turns a noun into a な-adjective: 個人的な意見 (a personal opinion), 平和的に (peacefully), 現実的では
ない (not realistic). It can take とても and other degree words.

> これは私の個人的な意見です。  
> これは わたしの こじんてきな いけんです。  
> This is my personal opinion.
>
> 話し合いで、問題を平和的に解決したい。  
> はなしあいで、もんだいを へいわてきに かいけつしたい。  
> I want to solve the problem peacefully, through discussion.

**型（がた）** names a type, model or size class and stays a noun: 大型（おおがた）の台風 (a large typhoon), 最新型
(the newest model), 夜型 (a night person), 血液型（けつえきがた） (blood type). It links to another noun with の.

> 大型の台風が近づいている。  
> おおがたの たいふうが ちかづいている。  
> A large typhoon is approaching.
>
> 私は夜型なので、朝早く起きるのが苦手だ。  
> わたしは よるがたなので、あさ はやく おきるのが にがてだ。  
> I'm a night owl, so I'm bad at getting up early.

**Common confusion.** な, に or とても before or after the slot suggests 的; の before a noun, or a noun used as
subject or object, suggests 型.

## 5. 同士 and たち

**同士（どうし）** names people who stand in the same relation to each other: 隣同士 (next to each other), 恋人同士
(a couple), 敵同士 (enemies of each other). **たち** simply makes a plural or group: 子どもたち, 弟たち. A humble
plural, **ども**, appears in 私ども（わたくしども, we, used politely to customers）; this set does not practise it.

> 私と田中さんは席が隣同士だ。  
> わたしと たなかさんは せきが となりどうしだ。  
> Tanaka and I sit next to each other.
>
> 子どもたち、そろそろ帰る時間ですよ。  
> こどもたち、そろそろ かえる じかんですよ。  
> Children, it's about time to go home.

## 6. あたり: per unit or around

**当たり（あたり）** sits on a counter and is followed by an amount for each unit: 一人あたり三千円 (3,000 yen per
person), 一日あたり三時間 (three hours a day). **辺り（あたり）** follows a place or a point in time and means "around":
駅のあたり, 来週あたり, 五月あたり. Both are often written in kana. A date such as 五月 contains a number but is a
point in time, so look at what follows: an amount per unit means "per"; no such amount means "around".

> 四人で一万二千円だったから、一人あたり三千円だ。  
> よにんで いちまんにせんえんだったから、ひとりあたり さんぜんえんだ。  
> It was 12,000 yen for four, so it's 3,000 yen per person.
>
> 来週あたり、また寒くなるらしい。  
> らいしゅうあたり、また さむく なるらしい。  
> It seems it will get cold again around next week.
>
> 毎年、五月あたりから急に暑くなる。  
> まいとし、ごがつあたりから きゅうに あつく なる。  
> Every year it suddenly gets hot from around May.

## 7. 向き and 向け

**向き（むき）** means facing a direction (南向きの部屋), a forward-looking attitude (前向きに考える, to think
positively) or naturally suited to someone or something (先生向きの性格,
ピクニック向きの天気). **向け（むけ）** means deliberately made or intended for an audience (小学生向けの番組), or bound
for a destination (アメリカ向けの車).

> この部屋は南向きで、一日中明るい。  
> この へやは みなみむきで、いちにちじゅう あかるい。  
> This room faces south and is bright all day.
>
> これは小学生向けに作られた科学の番組だ。  
> これは しょうがくせいむけに つくられた かがくの ばんぐみだ。  
> This is a science programme made for primary school children.

**Common confusion.** Words such as 作られた, 字幕を付けて or 輸出される (someone made or sent it for a target) point to
向け. A direction, or a natural fit that nobody planned, points to 向き.

## 8. がたい and にくい

Both follow a verb stem and mean "hard to". **にくい** is the everyday form: something is physically awkward to do
(歩きにくい, hard to walk in) or is not apt to happen to an object (壊れにくい, hard to break). **がたい** expresses a
near-impossibility felt by the speaker: "can hardly be ...". It belongs mostly to written style and fixed
phrases, and some of those phrases do not work with にくい.

> 現場の写真があるので、彼がそこにいたことは動かしがたい事実だ。  
> げんばの しゃしんが あるので、かれが そこに いたことは うごかしがたい じじつだ。  
> There is a photo of the scene, so it is an undeniable fact that he was there.
>
> 弱い人をいじめるなんて、許しがたい行為だ。  
> よわい ひとを いじめるなんて、ゆるしがたい こういだ。  
> Bullying weak people is an unforgivable act.
>
> このコップはプラスチックなので、落としても壊れにくい。  
> この こっぷは ぷらすちっくなので、おとしても こわれにくい。  
> This cup is plastic, so it doesn't break easily even if you drop it.

**Common confusion.** With verbs of thinking or feeling, both are often possible: 信じがたい and 信じにくい
(hard to believe) both occur, and the exercises do not test such pairs. Learn がたい in its fixed phrases:
動かしがたい事実 (an undeniable fact), 許しがたい行為（こうい） (an unforgivable act). 動かしにくい would be about
something physically heavy, and 許しにくい reports that forgiving would be hard for someone; it is not the fixed phrase that condemns an act. For physical awkwardness or what
happens to an object, にくい is the natural choice. In writing, がたい is sometimes spelled 難い (許し難い).
