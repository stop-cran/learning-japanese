# Meals, flavor and material texture

These exercises connect twenty familiar onomatopoeia cards with selected sentence uses.
They extend the approach in [Onomatopoeia in sentences](onomatopoeia-in-sentences.md):
learn **a word, a sense and its sentence frame together**, rather than attaching the same
set of endings to every mimetic.

The scope is drinking and eating, selected flavor descriptions, and a few material
conditions. It is not every dictionary sense or an exhaustive conjugation chart.

## What the eater or drinker does

[ごくごく](../words/ごくごく.md), [がぶがぶ](../words/がぶがぶ.md) and
[ぐびぐび](../words/ぐびぐび.md) all work with 飲む（のむ）, “drink,” but draw attention
to somewhat different impressions. ごくごく presents substantial repeated gulps;
がぶがぶ suggests copious mouthfuls; ぐびぐび makes the gulping especially vivid.
These are overlapping descriptions, not measured mouthful sizes.

- 水をごくごく飲んだ。 — I drank water in big gulps.
- お茶をぐびぐびと飲む音が聞こえる。
  （おちゃ／おと／きこえる） — I can hear someone gulping tea.

In these drinking combinations, bare forms and forms with **と** both work.
ぐびぐび is associated with alcohol, but tea is possible too.
Drinking ごくごく is **not** the degree-adverb homophone in ごくごく普通（ふつう）,
“very ordinary.” A shared reading does not give the drinking word a general する predicate.

がぶがぶ has another selected use: **お腹ががぶがぶだ**（おなか） describes the
liquid-filled, sloshing feeling in the stomach. Compare that state with
水をがぶがぶ飲む, the action of guzzling. The stomach is not itself drinking.

[ちびちび](../words/ちびちび.md) focuses on small amounts at a time:

- お茶をちびちびと飲む。 — Drink tea in small sips.
- 残ったお金をちびちび使う。（のこった／おかね／つかう）
  — Use the remaining money a little at a time.

The second is not a description of slow hand movement. Context, not the word alone,
decides whether sparing use is approved of.

## Mouth movement is not always food

[もぐもぐ](../words/もぐもぐ.md) can describe chewing or indistinct speech.
Compare **もぐもぐかむ**, “chew with small repeated mouth movements,” and
**もぐもぐ言う**（いう）, “mumble.” A speech example does not automatically say
that there is food in the mouth.

[むしゃむしゃ](../words/むしゃむしゃ.md) foregrounds hearty munching.
[ぱくぱく](../words/ぱくぱく.md) can describe eating mouthful after mouthful, but
**口をぱくぱくさせる**（くち） describes repeated mouth opening and closing even
without an asserted meal. A goldfish can be described this way without identifying
what, if anything, it is eating.

[もりもり](../words/もりもり.md) also fits hearty eating. It does not mean that
the food is crunchy. Its energy image extends to **やる気がもりもり湧く**（やるき／わく）,
“motivation wells up.” The thing growing here is motivation, not a mouthful of food.

[ぽりぽり](../words/ぽりぽり.md) is useful for light crunching, but the verb can
take it into a different scene:

- きゅうりをぽりぽりかじる。 — Take crunching bites of cucumber.
- 頭をぽりぽりかく。（あたま） — Scratch one's head with small repeated movements.

The scratching does not by itself establish embarrassment or a medical cause.

## Repetition, a quick movement, and easy completion

[ぺろぺろ](../words/ぺろぺろ.md) highlights repeated licking in **ぺろぺろなめる**.
A cat grooming its paw is not necessarily eating. Likewise, an ongoing
アイスをぺろぺろなめている does not say that the ice cream has already been finished.

[ぺろり](../words/ぺろり.md) can present a quick tongue movement or easy food completion:

- 舌をぺろりと出した。（した／だした） — Briefly stuck out one's tongue.
- 大きなプリンをぺろりと食べた。 — Polished off a large pudding.

The second need not involve licking. The first does not itself tell us whether the
person was teasing, embarrassed or merely posing.

[ずるずる](../words/ずるずる.md) likewise needs its predicate:

| Frame | Selected reading |
| --- | --- |
| そばをずるずるすする | Slurp noodles |
| 袋をずるずる引きずる（ふくろ／ひきずる） | Drag a bag along |
| 会議がずるずる続く（かいぎ／つづく） | A meeting drags on |

These frames allow と. An unresolved meeting is not a slurping sound, and its
continuation alone does not establish that someone deliberately delayed it.

## Flavor descriptions and other senses

[あっさり](../words/あっさり.md) describes a light, uncomplicated flavor;
[こってり](../words/こってり.md) a rich, heavy one. Neither is automatically a
judgment that the food is good or bad.

- あっさりしたスープ — a light soup
- こってりとした料理（りょうり） — a rich, heavy dish

**した** in a characteristic description need not mean “used to be like this, but
isn't now.” Optional と is possible in these selected food descriptions.
An ongoing description can also be expressed as スープはこってりしている,
“the soup is rich and heavy.”

The あっさり scaffold explicitly practises short **した** modification. Its answer
scope is narrower than free composition: it does not declare every other verbal
modifier impossible. In ordinary prose, している and its conversational contraction
してる can also describe a current characteristic.

A separate restaurant request asks for food that **is not rich or heavy**. Here,
negation belongs inside the description of the food, not on the request:

- こってりしていないスープが好きです。 — I like soup that isn't rich and heavy.

For this food characteristic, **こってりしない** and **こってりしていない** both
work as negative modifiers; the latter also contracts to **こってりしてない** in
speech. Each permits と after こってり. A plain or contracted modifier can sit inside
a sentence ending politely in お願いします（おねがいします）, “please.”
The exercise requests these ordinary nonpast verbal negatives, not past descriptions,
changes of state, added focus particles or a quoted menu-label contrast. An affirmative
こってりしている modifier is grammatical but asks for the opposite food characteristic.
Do not extrapolate a universal の/な/する/した menu from these examples.

With another verb, the meaning changes:

- あっさり引き受ける（ひきうける） — readily agree to take something on
- バターをこってり塗る（ぬる） — spread butter thickly
- こってり叱られる（しかられる） — be thoroughly scolded

Flavor richness is not the same as a liquid's viscosity.

### Three useful さっぱり frames

[さっぱり](../words/さっぱり.md) requires particular care:

1. **シャワーを浴びて、さっぱりした。**（あびて）
   — I felt refreshed after taking a shower.
2. **さっぱりとした味が好きだ。**（あじ／すき）
   — I like a light, refreshing taste.
3. **さっぱり分からない。**（わからない）
   — I do not understand at all.

Refreshment and light flavor can use と with する. The ordinary negative-degree
model in the third example uses bare さっぱり. Its exercise asks you to interpret
the supplied sentence, not generate every possible wording. Do not carry the
refreshment meaning into that negative sentence. Nor does a light taste mean that there is no flavor.
The dictionary also has a poor-results さっぱりだ; that is not the after-shower
model taught here.

[ぼそぼそ](../words/ぼそぼそ.md) has a similar sense boundary:
**ぼそぼそ話す**（はなす） is subdued, indistinct speech;
**パンがぼそぼそしている** describes dry, unappealing bread.
The documented modifier ぼそぼそなパン belongs to the food sense. It does not
make な an all-purpose attachment to a speech verb. Mumbling alone does not prove secrecy.

## Material state versus sound or movement

[かちかち](../words/かちかち.md) and [がちがち](../words/がちがち.md) can both
describe hard, rigid material. Voicing does not provide an exact hardness scale.

- パンはかちかちだ。 — The bread is rock-hard.
- 粘土はがちがちだ。（ねんど） — The clay is very hard.
- 時計がかちかち鳴る。（とけい／なる） — A clock ticks.
- 歯ががちがち鳴る。（は） — Teeth chatter.

The state examples do not assert a sound. Conversely, the sound examples are not
merely saying that clocks or teeth are hard. These tasks do not cover the separate
rigid-outlook or nervous-person uses.

[ぼろぼろ](../words/ぼろぼろ.md) can describe a worn condition, crumbling, or falling drops:

- かばんはぼろぼろです。 — The bag is tattered.
- クッキーがぼろぼろと崩れた。（くずれた） — The cookie crumbled.
- 涙がぼろぼろこぼれた。（なみだ） — Tears spilled in drops.

ぼろぼろに崩れた also presents the resulting broken-up condition. It is not
necessary to reject に merely because the model uses manner と.

[ぐにゃぐにゃ](../words/ぐにゃぐにゃ.md) concerns pliability or limpness:
ゴムのおもちゃがぐにゃぐにゃ曲がる（まがる）, “the rubber toy bends floppily.”
With 曲がる, bare/と manner descriptions and a に description of the resulting
distorted shape can fit. Limp noodles are not automatically pleasantly chewy, sticky or liquid.

## Using the exercises

Keep the supplied word shape, in hiragana or katakana, and fill the **whole blank only**.
Do not repeat a verb or ending that is already printed. Answer lists explicitly retain
the supported alternatives; the app does not infer a new phrase from its meaning.
Some choice appearances offer two valid completions: choose either one.

For interpretation questions, copy exactly **はい** or **いいえ**, as defined in the
task. They ask whether a claim follows from the actual description, not whether it
could happen in some imagined situation.

These tasks have no new Grammar JLPT labels. The pilot guide explains the separate
new-material and browser scope controls. Vocabulary help supplies local readings and
glosses; it is not proof of unassisted performance. This bounded practice has not
established learning benefit or transfer to new situations.
