# Moisture, food, weather and sound in sentences

This companion to [Onomatopoeia in sentences](onomatopoeia-in-sentences.md) practices 24 existing words in sensory scenes. Follow the noun, particles and printed verb: the same sound-shaped word may describe a condition, an action, a named thing, or a figurative use.

## Condition, change, action and named thing

| Construction in its scene | Meaning | What to notice |
|---|---|---|
| 部屋が[じめじめ](../words/じめじめ.md)している。 | The room is damp and muggy. | The subject itself has this condition. |
| 服が[びしょびしょ](../words/びしょびしょ.md)になった。 | The clothes became soaking wet. | `に` marks the condition reached. |
| 雨が[しとしと](../words/しとしと.md)降っている。 | Gentle rain is falling. | The mimetic describes the printed action. |
| 猫の[かりかり](../words/かりかり.md)を器（うつわ）に入れた。 | I put the cat's kibble in a bowl. | The word itself names the food. |
| 紙に赤い[ぽつぽつ](../words/ぽつぽつ.md)がある。 | There are red spots on the paper. | Here it is a noun, not an adverb. |

Do not add `する` to every mimetic. **びしょびしょです** and **のどがからからだ** are basic copular descriptions. Before a printed `です` or `だ`, the requested state word needs no inserted `な` or `と`. Here `です` is polite and `だ` is plain; both can describe the state directly.

Conversely, do not treat `にする` as interchangeable with a subject's own condition. **シャツがじとじとしている** describes the shirt; changing it to a making/keeping construction changes the roles. The exercises explicitly distinguish these operations.

Many resulting-state examples also permit the more written **となる** alongside everyday **になる**. This acceptance does not mean the styles are identical, or that `に` and `と` can be exchanged freely before every verb.

## Dampness and dryness: the scene selects the meaning

[じめじめ](../words/じめじめ.md) describes unwelcome dampness and can also describe a gloomy mood. An indoor room can remain じめじめ after rain stops. A person's **じめじめしていたくない** concerns not wanting to stay gloomy, not their humidity; applying that judgment to someone else can sound critical.

[じとじと](../words/じとじと.md) foregrounds clammy, clinging wetness. It overlaps with じめじめ rather than defining a precise moisture threshold. **じとじとした服** is a useful characteristic modifier.

[びしょびしょ](../words/びしょびしょ.md) and [ぐっしょり](../words/ぐっしょり.md) both describe heavy soaking. Compare **びしょびしょになった**, **ぐっしょりぬれた／ぐっしょりとぬれた**, and **汗（あせ）でぐっしょりだ**. The surrounding sentence identifies the liquid; the mimetic alone does not.

[かさかさ](../words/かさかさ.md) can describe dry, rough hands with `する`, or rustling paper with **鳴る（なる）**. [からから](../words/からから.md) similarly changes with the scene: **のどがからからだ** is a parched throat; **缶（かん）がからから転がる（ころがる）** is a rattling can; **からからと笑う** is an open, loud laugh. The laugh need not be mockery or literal dryness.

[さらさら](../words/さらさら.md) selects different senses in **髪（かみ）がさらさらになる**, **水がさらさら流れる**, and **名前をさらさら書く**: silky hair, murmuring water, and fluent writing. The water use is not “dry water.” The unrelated negative-polarity **さらさら～ない** is not this sensory entry.

[どろどろ](../words/どろどろ.md) can describe thick liquid, a muddy coating, tangled relationships, or a rumbling sound. Compare **スープがどろどろになる**, **靴（くつ）が泥（どろ）でどろどろだ**, **関係（かんけい）がどろどろしている**, and **太鼓（たいこ）がどろどろ鳴る**. The last is a separate dictionary sound entry; neither relationships nor drums must contain literal mud.

[べちゃべちゃ](../words/べちゃべちゃ.md) contrasts unwanted soggy food with **べちゃべちゃしゃべる**, a usually disparaging description of continuing chatter. It is not a neutral substitute for pleasantly moist [しっとり](../words/しっとり.md).

## Crispness and activity are not universal food categories

[かりかり](../words/かりかり.md) often suggests firm crispness. With a person, **そんなにかりかりしないで** asks them not to get so worked up; with pet food, **カリカリを入れる** uses a noun.

[さくさく](../words/さくさく.md) can describe lightly crisp cookies, crunching steps on snow, or efficient progress: **編集（へんしゅう）がさくさく進む** means editing goes smoothly, not that the work makes a chewing sound.

[ぱりぱり](../words/ぱりぱり.md) often suggests brittle crispness in thin food or a sharp cracking sound. **氷（こおり）がぱりぱり割れる** does not involve eating. Thinness is a prototype, not an absolute restriction.

[こりこり](../words/こりこり.md) describes firmer resistance in a bite, but **首（くび）がこりこりする** describes stiffness. A sensory description is not a medical diagnosis. **こりこりした歯ごたえ（はごたえ）** uses `した` for a characteristic, not necessarily a past-only condition.

[しゃきしゃき](../words/しゃきしゃき.md) often suits a fresh vegetable crunch, while **しゃきしゃきと働く（はたらく）** describes brisk, precise work. Texture words are not objective food-safety tests, and their useful prototypes overlap.

## Cooking sounds and bubbles

With [ぐつぐつ](../words/ぐつぐつ.md), keep the verb's roles: **豆（まめ）をぐつぐつ煮る（にる）** means someone simmers the beans; **豆がぐつぐつ煮える（にえる）** describes the beans simmering. Noticeable bubbling does not specify an exact temperature.

[ことこと](../words/ことこと.md) often suggests a gentler simmer, but can also depict light rattling away from the stove. [じゅうじゅう](../words/じゅうじゅう.md) is sizzling, as in **肉がじゅうじゅう焼けている**. **じゅうじゅういっている** reports the sound; it does not guarantee that the center is cooked through. **重々（じゅうじゅう）** “fully/repeatedly” is unrelated, not a kanji spelling for sizzling.

[ぶくぶく](../words/ぶくぶく.md) can describe bubbles or swelling: **泡（あわ）がぶくぶく出る** versus **豆がぶくぶく膨らむ（ふくらむ）**. [ぷくぷく](../words/ぷくぷく.md) likewise has bubbling and rounded-shape uses. Neither requires boiling or fixes a bubble's size. An affectionate-sounding shape word does not automatically make comments on another person's body welcome.

## Rain, sound labels and distribution

[しとしと](../words/しとしと.md) describes quiet, gentle rain. [ざあざあ](../words/ざあざあ.md) suggests a sustained rushing sound in heavy rain or flowing water. It can also describe radio static: **ざあざあという雑音（ざつおん）** labels the sound before a noun. No water need be present.

The final `と` of **しとしと**, **ことこと**, or **じとじと** belongs to the word. Adding the optional particle produces **しとしとと降る**, **ことことと煮る**, or **じとじととしている**; do not delete part of the word to avoid the repeated と.

[ぱらぱら](../words/ぱらぱら.md) can describe flipping pages or loose grains: **雑誌（ざっし）をぱらぱらめくる** and **ご飯がぱらぱらになる** select different uses. Loose rice is not necessarily unpleasantly dry. The para para dance is a different dictionary identity.

[ぽつぽつ](../words/ぽつぽつ.md) covers spots as a noun, isolated drops, scattered houses, and intermittent speech. **ぽつぽつ話す** need not mean speaking quietly; **家がぽつぽつ見える** need not involve motion or sound. Separated raindrops do not guarantee a later downpour.

[ごろごろ](../words/ごろごろ.md) also changes with its verb: **雷（かみなり）がごろごろ鳴る**, **石がごろごろ転がる**, **石がごろごろある**, and **家でごろごろする** describe rumbling, rolling, things lying about, and lounging. **目がごろごろする** reports an uncomfortable foreign-body-like sensation, not its definite cause.

## Practice scope

Use the supplied word and fill only the complete blank. Ordinary hiragana/katakana spellings are accepted; the long-vowel words also accept forms such as **ジュージュー** and **ザーザー**. Do not substitute a different sound shape such as びちょびちょ when the task supplies びしょびしょ.

Most tasks apply a word to a scene. A few explicitly request a `した` modifier, a standard written `という` sound label, or direct/`と` adverbial attachment; those are bounded drills, not claims that no other description is possible. Choice mode requires **one** fitting answer, even when several are available. Interpretation questions use the fixed labels **言える** and **言えない** to separate supported readings from extra assumptions.

These are representative selected uses, not every dictionary sense, a frequency ranking, a new JLPT classification or demonstrated learning effectiveness. The cards retain their wider explanations and source distinctions.
