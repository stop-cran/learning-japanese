# Japanese verbs: forms and everyday meanings

**Read, do not read, read yesterday, and will read tomorrow** need different combinations of form and
context. Start with the verb's conjugation pattern, then ask what the complete sentence says.
The [basic-particle lesson](japanese-nouns-and-basic-particles.md) explains the noun phrases around it.

## Nonpast is not just "right now"

> 毎晩、本を読みます。  
> まいばん、ほんをよみます。  
> I read books every evening.
>
> 明日、この本を読みます。  
> あした、このほんをよみます。  
> I will read this book tomorrow.

Both use 読みます. The first describes a routine; the second places an action in the future.
The ordinary nonpast form does not by itself mean that reading is happening at this instant.
For that reading, compare 今、本を読んでいます（いま、ほんをよんでいます）,
"I am reading a book now," in [actions, states and time](japanese-actions-states-and-time.md).

Past 読みました（よみました） reports a past reading event. A past statement need not say that a
habit or situation has permanently ended. Time expressions, the predicate and the surrounding account matter.

## Three useful conjugation patterns

| Pattern | Examples | Basic operation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ichidan, often called Group 2 or "ru-verbs" | 食べる（たべる）, eat; 見る（みる）, see | Remove final る before endings such as ます or ない. |
| Godan, often Group 1 or "u-verbs" | 読む（よむ）, read; 書く（かく）, write | The final sound changes according to the ending. |
| The major irregular verbs | する, do; 来る（くる）, come | Learn their forms explicitly. |

**Not every verb ending in る is ichidan.** 帰る（かえる）, "return home," is godan:
帰ります／帰らない／帰った. Contrast 食べます／食べない／食べた.
Learn a doubtful verb's class from its dictionary entry; the last written character is not enough.
Other verbs have particular irregular forms too, notably ある becoming ない in the ordinary plain negative.

## Plain and polite: keep time and polarity separate

| Meaning/form | 読む: plain | 読む: polite ます-series | 食べる: plain | 食べる: polite ます-series |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Affirmative nonpast | 読む | 読みます | 食べる | 食べます |
| Negative nonpast | 読まない | 読みません | 食べない | 食べません |
| Affirmative past | 読んだ | 読みました | 食べた | 食べました |
| Negative past | 読まなかった | 読みませんでした | 食べなかった | 食べませんでした |

Plain forms are not automatically rude. Whether they suit a conversation depends on the relationship and
setting; they also occur inside clauses in otherwise polite sentences.
Conversational negatives such as 読まないです exist too. A drill explicitly requesting the **ます-series**
has a narrower task than "say this politely." Neither 読みますです nor 読むます is the ordinary pattern.

The **ます-stem** is the part before ます: 読みます gives 読み, 食べます gives 食べ.
To form it from a **godan dictionary form**, change the final u-row kana to the corresponding **i-row** kana:
書く → 書き, 買う → 買い, 待つ → 待ち, 話す → 話し, 読む → 読み.
Then add the requested ます-series ending: 待ちます, 待ちません, 待ちました, or 待ちませんでした.
For an **ichidan** verb, remove final る: 食べる → 食べ, 見る → 見.
The irregular する／来る stems are し／来（き）, as shown below.

## Make the negative carefully

For a godan verb, use the corresponding **a-row** sound before ない:
書く → 書かない, 読む → 読まない, 帰る → 帰らない.
Final **う becomes わない**, not あない: 買う（かう）, "buy," becomes 買わない.
Final つ gives たない: 待つ（まつ）, "wait," becomes 待たない.

For an ichidan verb, replace る: 食べる → 食べない, 見る → 見ない.
The negative past changes **ない to なかった**: 食べなかった, 帰らなかった.

| Verb | Polite nonpast | Plain negative | Plain past | て-form |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| する | します | しない | した | して |
| 来る（くる） | 来ます（きます） | 来ない（こない） | 来た（きた） | 来て（きて） |
| ある | あります | ない | あった | あって |

Notice the changing reading of 来. The ordinary negative of ある is ない, not あらない;
the polite negative is ありません.

## The て-form and the plain past share sound patterns

The て-form connects to another expression; **it is not itself a past tense**.
For the basic plain past, the following て／で endings correspond to た／だ:

| Dictionary ending | て-form | Plain past | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Godan う・つ・る | って | った | 買う → 買って／買った; 待つ → 待って／待った; 帰る → 帰って／帰った |
| Godan む・ぶ・ぬ | んで | んだ | 読む → 読んで／読んだ; 遊ぶ（あそぶ） → 遊んで／遊んだ; 死ぬ（しぬ） → 死んで／死んだ |
| Godan く | いて | いた | 書く → 書いて／書いた |
| Godan ぐ | いで | いだ | 泳ぐ（およぐ） → 泳いで／泳いだ |
| Godan す | して | した | 話す（はなす） → 話して／話した |
| Ichidan る | て | た | 食べる → 食べて／食べた |

**行く（いく）, go, is the important exception here: 行って／行った.**
Do not turn it into 行いて／行いた. The table's godan-る rule does not apply to ichidan 食べる.

> 駅まで歩いて、電車に乗りました。  
> えきまであるいて、でんしゃにのりました。  
> I walked to the station and took the train.

歩いて connects the first action to the second. The final predicate and context locate the sequence in
the past. Connections can express more than time order: see
[listing and linking](japanese-listing-and-examples.md#て-くて-and-で-connecting-is-not-always-listing-examples).

## Use the forms, not just the table

The practice separates requested formation, time/polarity interpretation and small contextual choices.
Ordinary kana and the taught kanji spellings are accepted where they fill the same blank.
A requested form is a visible exercise boundary, not a claim that other complete sentences are ungrammatical.

Next, use these forms in [actions and states](japanese-actions-states-and-time.md) and
[requests and rules](japanese-requests-permission-and-rules.md).
[Verb extensions](verb-extensions-direction-and-aspect.md) then shows why 読んでおく and 読み続ける
attach differently; those advanced constructions are not prerequisites here.
