# 気 expressions: attention, concern, liking, and feelings

The character **気 (き)** appears in many everyday expressions, but it does not have one English equivalent. It can evoke
mind, attention, feeling, energy, or atmosphere; the surrounding verb and particles make the expression specific.
Learn each phrase as a whole pattern, not as a formula for translating 気.

The [気 kanji card](../kanji/気.md) introduces the character, and the [気 word card](../words/気.md) covers the noun.
This guide compares common expressions that are easy to mix up and points to cards with fuller definitions.

## Start with the whole pattern

In the table, **X** is the thing that occupies your attention, that you mind, that you like, or that you notice.

| Expression | Common frame | A useful first meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Xが気になる**（きになる） | X **が** 気になる | X is on my mind; X interests or bothers me |
| **Xを気にする**（きにする） | X **を** 気にする | mind, worry about, or be concerned about X |
| **Xが気に入る**（きにいる） | X **が** 気に入る | take a liking to X; find X pleasing |
| **Xに気が付く／気づく**（きがつく／きづく） | X **に** 気が付く | notice or realize X |
| **Xに気を付ける**（きをつける） | X **に** 気を付ける | be careful about X; watch out for X |
| **…気がする**（きがする） | verb/い-adjective plain form, or な-adjective + な, then (ような)気がする; noun + **のような**気がする | have a feeling or impression that… |

These are constructions with their own grammar. The particle belongs to the whole frame: **間違いに気づく**
("notice a mistake") but **人の目を気にする** ("care what other people think"). The same English translation
does not guarantee the same Japanese pattern.

## 気になる and 気にする: attention and concern

You may see 気になる described as spontaneous and 気にする as deliberate. Both expressions can describe worry, so treat
that only as a rough memory aid, not a strict rule.
**気になる** presents something as occupying or attracting your attention. It may express worry, but it also has a
distinct use for simple interest or curiosity. **気にする** commonly means to mind, care about, or worry about something.

> 試験の結果が気になった。  
> しけんの けっかが きになった。  
> The exam result caught my attention / I was concerned about the result.

> 試験の結果を気にした。  
> しけんの けっかを きにした。  
> I worried about the exam result.

Both sentences can describe the same concern, with the same past time reference. **気になる** puts the result in the
position of something that occupies one's attention; **気にする** presents the speaker as minding or worrying about
it. Neither sentence proves whether the speaker chose to worry.

For simple curiosity, use **気になる**, not **気にする**:

> 彼女がどう答えるか気になる。  
> かのじょが どう こたえるか きになる。  
> I'm curious how she'll answer.

The whole embedded question **彼女がどう答えるか** is the thing that is on your mind. Another common pattern is
**人の目を気にする**, to worry about or be self-conscious about what other people think.

> 人の目を気にしすぎなくてもいいよ。  
> ひとの めを きにしすぎなくても いいよ。  
> You don't have to worry so much about what other people think.

**気にしないで** is a conventional reassurance: "Don't worry about it" or "Don't let it bother you." It uses
**気にする**; it does not imply that concern is always a conscious choice or easy to control.

**気になる** also follows a verb in dictionary form to mean "come to feel like doing":

> やっと勉強する気になった。  
> やっと べんきょうする きに なった。  
> I finally feel like studying.

Its potential negative, **気になれない**, means "can't bring myself to":

> 今日は勉強する気になれない。  
> きょうは べんきょうする きに なれない。  
> I can't bring myself to study today.

This is different from a verb or adjective followed by **気がする**, which reports a feeling or impression:
**雨が降る気がする**（あめがふるきがする）, "I have a feeling it will rain."

## 気に入る means liking, not just noticing

**気に入る** means that something suits one's taste or pleases one. The thing liked often takes **が**; **を** is
also possible, especially with **気に入っている**.

> このかばんが気に入りました。  
> この かばんが きにいりました。  
> I took a liking to this bag.

> この店が気に入っています。  
> この みせが きにいっています。  
> I like this shop; it is one I have taken a liking to.

Compare:

> この店が気になった。 — This shop caught my interest; I was curious about it.  
> この店が気に入った。 — I took a liking to this shop; it suited my taste.

The verb is **気に入る（きにいる）**, not *きにはいる*. See the [気に入る word card](../words/気に入る.md)
for its forms and additional usage notes. The thing liked can take **が** or **を**; **気に入っている** describes
a continuing liking, not a stronger degree of liking.

## 気づく, 気を付ける, and 気がする

These expressions can appear in the same conversation but do different jobs:

> 間違いに気づいた。  
> まちがいに きづいた。  
> I noticed the mistake.

**気づく** reports becoming aware of something. It is also written **気が付く**.
The [気付く card](../words/気付く.md) covers noticing a fact or situation, including patterns with こと.

> 足元に気を付けてください。  
> あしもとに きをつけてください。  
> Please watch your step.

**気を付ける** is a caution or care expression, not a synonym for noticing after the fact. The [気を付ける card](../words/気を付ける.md)
covers its common uses, including **気を付けて** as "take care."

For noticing a whole event or fact, a clause can be nominalized with **ことに**:

> 携帯を家に忘れたことに気づいた。  
> けいたいを いえに わすれた ことに きづいた。  
> I realized I had left my phone at home.

Here **携帯を家に忘れた** is the fact that came to the speaker's attention. The [気付く card](../words/気付く.md)
gives more on this pattern.

> 今日は雨が降る気がする。  
> きょうは あめが ふる きがする。  
> I have a feeling it will rain today.

**気がする** presents a subjective feeling or impression; it does not assert that the speaker has noticed proof.
The fuller form **ような気がする** is also common; ような is optional after a verb or adjective. After a noun, keep
**のような**: **学生のような気がする**, "I have a feeling they're a student." Because 気 is a noun, use **ような**
before it, not ように. For the forms that come before よう, see the table in
[Likeness and impressions](appearance-resemblance-and-impressions.md#3-read-the-evidence-and-the-intended-claim).

## A few more useful 気 expressions

Use these as additional whole phrases. Their components can suggest a memory image, but the complete expression
sets the meaning.

| Expression | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **気が散る**（きがちる） | get distracted; lose concentration |
| **気が向く**（きがむく） | feel like doing something; be in the mood |
| **気が乗らない**（きがのらない） | not feel enthusiastic or up for something |
| **気が済む**（きがすむ） | feel satisfied or feel that a matter is settled |
| **人と気が合う**（ひとと きが あう） | click with someone; get along |
| **人に気を遣う**（ひとにきをつかう） | be considerate of someone; be tactful |
| **気を配る**（きをくばる） | pay attention to details or to other people's needs |

These expressions are related but not identical. **人に気を遣う**（ひとにきをつかう） is to be considerate
or tactful toward someone. **安否を気遣う**（あんぴをきづかう） is to worry about someone's safety.
**人を気にかける**（ひとをきにかける） means to keep someone in mind and care about their welfare; it overlaps
with **気遣う**. Notice the readings: **気を遣う** is **きをつかう**, while **気遣う** is **きづかう**.
The dictionary entries for [気を遣う](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%B0%97%E3%82%92%E9%81%A3%E3%81%86-3227653),
[気遣う](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%B0%97%E9%81%A3%E3%81%86-474647), and
[気に掛ける](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%B0%97%E3%81%AB%E6%8E%9B%E3%81%91%E3%82%8B-471889) describe their
overlapping senses.

## Nouns and compounds: use the other kanji as a clue

The other kanji often helps narrow a word down. Think of its contribution as a helpful clue, not a
literal equation that will predict every compound.

| Word | Main idea | Contrast |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [元気](../words/元気.md)（げんき） | well-being, vitality, or lively energy | You can feel well but not feel like doing a particular task. |
| **やる気**（やるき） | motivation or willingness to act | A desire or readiness to do something. |
| **本気**（ほんき） | serious intent; genuinely meaning or doing something | **本気を出す** means to put in one's real effort or get serious about an activity. |
| [平気](../words/平気.md)（へいき） | being untroubled, unfazed, or able to cope | Says how little something bothers you; it does not mean you feel energetic. |

> 元気だけど、勉強する気になれない。  
> げんきだけど、べんきょうする きに なれない。  
> I feel fine, but I can't bring myself to study.

> 彼は本気で取り組んでいる。  
> かれは ほんきで とりくんでいる。  
> He's taking it seriously.

> 今から本気を出す。  
> いまから ほんきを だす。  
> I'm going to really try now.

**本気を出す** focuses on putting serious effort into an action. **本気になる** means to become serious.
You may also see the casual shortened form **本気出す**; the full expression is **本気を出す**.

Other compounds point to different areas: [天気](../words/天気.md) is weather; [空気](../words/空気.md) is air and,
figuratively, a social atmosphere; [雰囲気](../words/雰囲気.md) is an atmosphere or overall impression. Learn each
compound as its own word rather than expecting 気 always to mean "mood."

## Quick check

Choose a phrase to fit each situation. Suggested answers follow; they are examples, not the only possible wording.

1. A friend apologizes for a tiny typo. Reassure them: "Don't worry about it."
2. A new café has caught your interest. "I'm curious about the new café."
3. You saw the new bag and decided you like it. "I took a liking to this bag."
4. You spot dirt on your shirt: "I noticed the stain."
5. Tell someone walking on an icy path: "Please watch your step."

**Suggested answers**

1. 気にしないで。
2. 新しいカフェが気になる。
3. このかばんが気に入った。
4. シャツの汚れに気づいた。
5. 足元に気を付けてください。
