# Onomatopoeia for motion, voices, and everyday sounds

One scene can be described from several angles: **movement, sound, result, or someone's attitude**.
The [onomatopoeia index](japanese-onomatopoeia.md) contains all 200 cards.

## Movement does not automatically imply sound

- カーテンが[ゆらゆら](../words/ゆらゆら.md)揺（ゆ）れる。 — The curtain sways.
- 窓（まど）が[がたがた](../words/がたがた.md)鳴（な）る。 — The window rattles.
- 椅子（いす）の脚（あし）が[ぐらぐら](../words/ぐらぐら.md)する。 — The chair's leg wobbles.

ゆらゆら need not be audible; がたがた foregrounds sound in the second sentence;
ぐらぐら foregrounds instability in the third. These are not exclusive categories:
a loose object may both wobble and rattle.

Other useful movement groups:

| Group | Learn the distinction without making it absolute |
| --- | --- |
| [くるり](../words/くるり.md) / [くるくる](../words/くるくる.md) | A compact turn versus often repeated or continuing turning |
| [ぴょんぴょん](../words/ぴょんぴょん.md) / [ひょいと](../words/ひょいと.md) | Repeated light hops versus a nimble or unexpected action |
| [ひらひら](../words/ひらひら.md) / [ちらちら](../words/ちらちら.md) | Thin things fluttering versus intermittent little appearances or glances; falling things can overlap |
| [すいすい](../words/すいすい.md) / [するする](../words/するする.md) | Overlapping smooth, unobstructed movement |
| [ずんずん](../words/ずんずん.md) / [ぐいぐい](../words/ぐいぐい.md) | Vigorous progress versus often forceful pushing, pulling, or momentum |
| [てくてく](../words/てくてく.md) / [とぼとぼ](../words/とぼとぼ.md) | Steady walking versus often weary or downcast plodding |
| [ちょこちょこ](../words/ちょこちょこ.md) / [よちよち](../words/よちよち.md) | Small quick steps versus often a toddler's uncertain steps |

[じりじり](../words/じりじり.md) can make progress feel gradual or impatient.
It also describes a scorching sun; that does not mean the sun is literally inching along.

## Action, sound, and result are different questions

- 床（ゆか）を[ごしごし](../words/ごしごし.md)こする。 — Scrub the floor vigorously.
- 紙を[びりびり](../words/びりびり.md)破（やぶ）く。 — Rip the paper apart.
- 紙を[ぐしゃぐしゃ](../words/ぐしゃぐしゃ.md)に丸（まる）める。 — Crumple the paper into a ball.
- 空き缶（あきかん）を[ぺちゃんこ](../words/ぺちゃんこ.md)につぶす。 — Crush the empty can flat.

Scrubbing does not guarantee a clean result; crumpling does not necessarily tear; flattening does not
necessarily make pieces. For packing, [ぎゅうぎゅう](../words/ぎゅうぎゅう.md) often foregrounds pressure
and crowding, while [ぱんぱん](../words/ぱんぱん.md) foregrounds bulging fullness. Their uses overlap.

The sound families [かたかた](../words/かたかた.md), [がたがた](../words/がたがた.md),
[かちかち](../words/かちかち.md), and [がちゃがちゃ](../words/がちゃがちゃ.md) have
overlapping clicking/rattling impressions. Likewise, [ぱちぱち](../words/ぱちぱち.md),
[ばきばき](../words/ばきばき.md), and [ばりばり](../words/ばりばり.md) overlap in sharp sounds.
Voicing is a useful tendency, not a calibrated loudness meter or a guarantee that only one word fits.

## Water, wind, and fire

| Scene | Useful expressions |
| --- | --- |
| Separate drops | [ぽたぽた](../words/ぽたぽた.md); compare [ぽつぽつ](../words/ぽつぽつ.md) |
| Flowing or trickling | [だらだら](../words/だらだら.md), with a liquid subject |
| Repeated splashing | [じゃぶじゃぶ](../words/じゃぶじゃぶ.md), [ざぶざぶ](../words/ざぶざぶ.md), [ばしゃばしゃ](../words/ばしゃばしゃ.md) |
| A plop into water | [ぽちゃん](../words/ぽちゃん.md) |
| Gurgling liquid | [ごぼごぼ](../words/ごぼごぼ.md); heating is not required |
| Gentle breeze / howling wind | [そよそよ](../words/そよそよ.md) / [びゅうびゅう](../words/びゅうびゅう.md) |
| Visible flames / smoke | [めらめら](../words/めらめら.md) / [もくもく](../words/もくもく.md) |

The three repeated splashing words do not encode exact water depths or container sizes.
An image of washing can support several of them. A fire can simultaneously burn めらめら
and crackle ぱちぱち: visible motion and sound are different aspects of the same event.

The smoke word もくもく is not 黙々（もくもく）, the separate word in “work silently.”
Dictionary spelling and sense restrictions also matter: the camera-clicking sense grouped with
ばしゃばしゃ is restricted to **ぱしゃぱしゃ** in the inspected entry, not the voiced form taught here.

## Animal voices and breathing

| Word | Starting scene |
| --- | --- |
| [にゃあにゃあ](../words/にゃあにゃあ.md) | A cat meowing |
| [わんわん](../words/わんわん.md) | A dog barking; also a person bawling |
| [ぴよぴよ](../words/ぴよぴよ.md) / [ちゅんちゅん](../words/ちゅんちゅん.md) | Little bird cheeps/chirps; the associations overlap |
| [かあかあ](../words/かあかあ.md) | A crow cawing |
| [ぶうぶう](../words/ぶうぶう.md) | A pig oinking, a horn, or grumbling |
| [メーメー](../words/メーメー.md) | A sheep bleating |
| [こけこっこー](../words/こけこっこー.md) | A rooster crowing |
| [くんくん](../words/くんくん.md) | Sniffing or an animal whimpering |
| [はあはあ](../words/はあはあ.md) | Panting or gasping |
| [げほげほ](../words/げほげほ.md) | Heavy coughing |

Sound words often work before 鳴く（なく）, “make an animal call,” or as a quoted sound with と.
But the following action matters:

- 犬がくんくんにおいをかぐ。 — The dog sniffs.
- 犬がくんくん鳴く。 — The dog whimpers.

Some sound expressions also name animals in child-directed speech. That is not a license to replace
every neutral animal noun in adult writing. Katakana, as in メーメー, does not imply a foreign loanword.

## Looking and speaking: manner is not intention

[きょろきょろ](../words/きょろきょろ.md) describes looking around;
[じろじろ](../words/じろじろ.md) often describes an intrusive stare;
[ちらちら](../words/ちらちら.md) describes repeated brief looks.
None proves whether the observer is curious, dishonest, or worried.

For speech, distinguish delivery from content:

- [はきはき](../words/はきはき.md)答（こた）える — answer clearly and briskly.
- [ずけずけ](../words/ずけずけ.md)言う — speak bluntly, often tactlessly.
- [きっぱり](../words/きっぱり.md)断（ことわ）る — refuse decisively.
- [べらべら](../words/べらべら.md)話す — chatter without restraint, sometimes indiscreetly.

A clear voice can deliver a tactless comment, and a quiet voice can deliver a firm refusal.
[こっそり](../words/こっそり.md) emphasizes avoiding notice, not simply low volume.
[ぐずぐず](../words/ぐずぐず.md) can mean grumbling or dawdling; it is not a neutral label for every complaint.

[せっせと](../words/せっせと.md) foregrounds diligent effort, while
[きびきび](../words/きびきび.md) often foregrounds crisp, efficient movement.
The final と is already present in せっせと and ひょいと: do not double it.
In [しーん](../words/しーん.md)とする, by contrast, と follows the word to describe hushed silence.
[ざわざわ](../words/ざわざわ.md) can describe the crowd murmur that breaks that silence.

## Sources and limits

The linked cards identify inspected [JMdict](https://www.edrdg.org/wiki/index.php/JMdict-EDICT_Dictionary_Project)
entries, including homographs and reading restrictions. Examples are original teaching sentences.
These comparisons provide useful prototypes, not exhaustive substitution rules or proof that
every randomized text or image quiz has only one defensible answer.
