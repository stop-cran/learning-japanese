# Onomatopoeia for eating and drinking

The useful unit is a **word plus a scene and a construction**, not just an English adjective.
Compare the food with the eater:

- このパンは[もちもち](../words/もちもち.md)している。 — This bread is springy and chewy.
- 子どもがパンを[もぐもぐ](../words/もぐもぐ.md)食べている。 — The child is chewing the bread.

The first describes texture; the second describes mouth movement. The same bread could occur
in both sentences. Return to the [onomatopoeia index](japanese-onomatopoeia.md) for all 200 cards.

## Gulps and little sips

| Expression | Useful starting scene | Construction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [ごくごく](../words/ごくごく.md) | Repeated substantial gulps | 水をごくごく飲む（のむ） |
| [がぶがぶ](../words/がぶがぶ.md) | Copious gulping or guzzling | 水をがぶがぶ飲む |
| [ぐびぐび](../words/ぐびぐび.md) | Vivid, audible gulps, often of alcohol | お茶をぐびぐび飲む |
| [ぐいぐい](../words/ぐいぐい.md) | Vigorous drinking; also forceful movement elsewhere | 水をぐいぐい飲む |
| [ちびちび](../words/ちびちび.md) | Taking a little at a time | お茶をちびちび飲む |

The first four **overlap**. They are not a scientific scale of mouthful size, and ぐびぐび does
not require alcohol. A still image of someone holding a glass cannot reliably distinguish them.

- 運動のあと、水をごくごく飲んだ。 — After exercising, I drank water in big gulps.
- 熱いお茶をちびちび飲んだ。 — I drank the hot tea in little sips.

ちびちび can also concern slowly using up a supply: it is not the sound of one particular drink.
Likewise, [ずるずる](../words/ずるずる.md) in そばをずるずるすする describes slurping noodles,
not the heavy dragging that the same word describes with 引きずる（ひきずる）.

## Mouth movement, appetite, and completion

| Expression | What it often foregrounds |
| --- | --- |
| [もぐもぐ](../words/もぐもぐ.md) | Repeated chewing movements; also indistinct speech |
| [むしゃむしゃ](../words/むしゃむしゃ.md) | Hearty munching or gobbling |
| [ぱくぱく](../words/ぱくぱく.md) | Repeated mouth opening; also hearty eating |
| [もりもり](../words/もりもり.md) | Appetite or vigorous energy |
| [ぺろぺろ](../words/ぺろぺろ.md) | Repeated licking; also quickly eating something up |
| [ぺろり](../words/ぺろり.md) | A quick lick or tongue movement; easily polishing food off |

These are not compulsory stages of a meal. Several can truthfully describe the same person.
The following contrast changes the action, not merely the food:

- 猫が前足（まえあし）をぺろぺろなめている。 — The cat is licking its front paw.
- 大きなケーキをぺろりと食べた。 — I polished off the large piece of cake.

The second sentence does not say that I licked the cake. Similarly, a fish that
口をぱくぱくさせる, “opens and closes its mouth,” is not necessarily eating.

## Texture is not appetite

[ぽりぽり](../words/ぽりぽり.md), [かりかり](../words/かりかり.md),
[ぱりぱり](../words/ぱりぱり.md), and [さくさく](../words/さくさく.md) can overlap
in crisp-food descriptions. Prototypes help, but “crunchy” alone does not make a unique quiz answer.

- きゅうりをぽりぽり食べた。 — I ate the cucumber with little crunches.
- おなかがすいて、おにぎりをむしゃむしゃ食べた。 — I was hungry and gobbled the rice balls.

The first foregrounds crunching; the second foregrounds hearty eating without claiming that rice is crisp.
ぽりぽり also describes little scratches, so 頭（あたま）をぽりぽりかく means scratching one's head.

For undesirable softness or dryness, compare:

- [ぐにゃぐにゃ](../words/ぐにゃぐにゃ.md): limp and pliable, not necessarily pleasant chewiness.
- [ぼそぼそ](../words/ぼそぼそ.md): dry, unappealing food; also subdued mumbling.
- [ぱさぱさ](../words/ぱさぱさ.md): dry and lacking moisture.
- [ぼろぼろ](../words/ぼろぼろ.md): crumbling or falling apart; also worn-out objects or people.
- [かちかち](../words/かちかち.md) / [がちがち](../words/がちがち.md): hardness or stiffness,
  with separate clicking/chattering and nervous-tension uses.

See the earlier [texture and warmth guide](onomatopoeia-texture-and-warmth.md) for more comparisons.

## Light flavor, rich flavor, and a refreshed person

[あっさり](../words/あっさり.md) and [さっぱり](../words/さっぱり.md) can both describe
light food. [こってり](../words/こってり.md) often suggests a rich, heavy quality.
These are impressions of food, not numerical fat-content or calorie labels.

- あっさりしたスープが好きだ。 — I like light soup.
- このラーメンはこってりしている。 — This ramen is rich and heavy.
- シャワーを浴（あ）びて、さっぱりした。 — I felt refreshed after a shower.

Do not replace さっぱり with あっさり in the shower sentence just because the two overlap for soup.
Other senses also need their own grammar:

- あっさり断る（ことわる） — refuse outright.
- さっぱり分からない — not understand at all.
- こってり叱られる（しかられる） — receive a severe scolding.

The positive food contrast does not turn into a universal three-word substitution rule.

## Sources and limits

The linked cards cite the inspected [JMdict](https://www.edrdg.org/wiki/index.php/JMdict-EDICT_Dictionary_Project)
entries and distinguish primary scenes from other senses. These examples and explanations are original teaching
text, not quotations from recipes or corpus attestations. Typical impressions are not rigid semantic borders;
the curated quiz exclusions protect known overlaps, not every possible ambiguous choice.
