# Making, letting and being made to act

How can 読ませた（よませた） mean either "made someone read" or "let someone read"?
And who reads in 読ませられた（よませられた）?
Keep three questions separate: **how the verb is formed, who does the underlying action, and what the
surrounding situation tells us about permission or obligation**.

Start with [verb classes](japanese-verb-foundations.md) and then
[passive frames](passive-and-affected-participants.md).
The **causative**, 使役（しえき）, presents someone or something as causing or allowing an action.
The **causative-passive**, 使役受け身（しえきうけみ）, presents the person made to perform it.
Learn the causative first rather than treating all the long endings as one interchangeable pattern.

## 1. Build the causative

| Class | Operation | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Godan | Final u-row kana → a-row counterpart + せる; final う → わ | 読む → 読ませる（よませる）; 待つ → 待たせる（またせる）; 買う → 買わせる（かわせる） |
| Ichidan | Remove final る + させる | 食べる → 食べさせる（たべさせる）; 見る → 見させる（みさせる） |
| する | Replace with させる | 掃除する → 掃除させる（そうじさせる）, have someone clean |
| 来る（くる） | Use 来させる（こさせる） | 学校に来させる, have someone come to school |

Godan changes follow **う→わ, く→か, ぐ→が, す→さ, つ→た, ぬ→な, ぶ→ば, む→ま, る→ら**.
Thus 話す becomes 話させる（はなさせる）, not 話せる, which is its potential.
Check the class of a verb ending in る rather than assuming it is ichidan.

The **new causative inflects as ichidan**:

| Requested form | 読ませる |
| --- | --- |
| Plain affirmative past | 読ませた |
| Plain negative nonpast | 読ませない |
| Plain negative past | 読ませなかった |
| Polite affirmative | 読ませます／読ませました |
| Polite negative | 読ませません／読ませませんでした |

A negative causative does not automatically mean the underlying action never happened.
先生は学生に本を読ませなかった can deny the teacher's making/letting the student read;
the student might still have read independently. The context determines what is being denied.

## 2. Separate causer, actor and object

> 先生は学生に本を読ませた。  
> せんせいは がくせいに ほんを よませた。  
> The teacher had the student read the book.

- **Causer:** 先生, the teacher, who brings about or permits the reading.
- **Lexical actor:** 学生, the student, who actually reads.
- **Object:** 本, the book, which is read.

"Lexical" here means the action in the original verb 読む, before the causative ending.
Calling the teacher the grammatical subject does not make the teacher the reader.
Likewise, in 先生は学生に教室を掃除させた, the student cleans the classroom.

For these transitive frames, the object has を and the actor has に.
For an intransitive action, the actor can have を:

> 先生は学生を学校に来させた。  
> せんせいは がくせいを がっこうに こさせた。  
> The teacher had the student come to school.

学校に is the destination, not the actor. Intransitive causatives can also use に for the actor in
appropriate patterns. Particle choice depends on the verb, its other participants and the intended
presentation; it is **not a universal make/let switch**. The practice supplies these frames rather than
testing every possible に／を substitution.

The **topic is not automatically the causer**. An object can be made the topic, with は replacing を:

> 教室は、先生が学生に掃除させた。  
> きょうしつは、せんせいが がくせいに そうじさせた。  
> As for the classroom, the teacher had the student clean it.

Here the first named entity is the object; 先生が is the causer and 学生に the lexical actor.
Use the whole frame, not the first noun or は alone, to assign roles.

## 3. Making or allowing: read the stated situation

The same transitive frame can describe different situations:

> 子どもは「嫌だ」と言ったが、母は子どもに野菜を食べさせた。  
> こどもは「いやだ」と いったが、ははは こどもに やさいを たべさせた。  
> The child said no, but the mother made the child eat the vegetables.
>
> 子どもは「食べたい」と言った。母は「いいよ」と言って、子どもに野菜を食べさせた。  
> The child said they wanted to eat them. The mother agreed and let the child eat the vegetables.

The actor has **に in both**. The first situation supplies opposition; the second supplies a request
and consent. Do not infer those circumstances from に alone, or add an unstated emotion such as anger.

Permissive causatives can also have を:

> 子どもは「遊びたい」と言った。母は「いいよ」と言って、子どもを庭で遊ばせた。  
> こどもは「あそびたい」と いった。ははは「いいよ」と いって、こどもを にわで あそばせた。  
> The child asked to play. The mother agreed and let the child play in the garden.

を therefore does not automatically mean coercion. Without enough context, a causative may leave
the make/let distinction open. Causation also need not be deliberate: 笑わせる（わらわせる）,
"make someone laugh," need not describe an intentional attempt.

A wish is a different claim from causing or allowing an event.
[Wanting an outcome](desire-actions-and-outcomes.md) does not assert that it was brought about.
[Receiving an action as a favor](benefactive-actions.md) adds its own viewpoint; it is not merely a
polite replacement for every causative.

## 4. Add passive after causative

The order is **original verb → causative → passive**:

| Original | Causative | Full causative-passive |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 読む | 読ませる | 読ませられる（よませられる） |
| 待つ | 待たせる | 待たせられる（またせられる） |
| 食べる | 食べさせる | 食べさせられる（たべさせられる） |
| 掃除する | 掃除させる | 掃除させられる（そうじさせられる） |
| 来る | 来させる | 来させられる（こさせられる） |

Remove the causative's final る and add られる. Do not simply attach a causative ending after the
original verb's passive form. The resulting causative-passive also inflects as ichidan:

> 読ませられる → 読ませられた／読ませられない／読ませられなかった  
> Polite: 読ませられます／読ませられました／読ませられませんでした

Compare the perspective while keeping the event roles:

> 先生は学生に本を読ませた。  
> The teacher had the student read the book.
>
> 学生は先生に本を読ませられた。  
> がくせいは せんせいに ほんを よませられた。  
> The student was made to read the book by the teacher.

The student is still the reader, and the teacher is still the causer.
In the second frame, **先生に marks the causer**, not the reader.
The book remains the object. Neither changing the topic nor adding passive reverses the actual reading.

The object can also be the topic of a causative-passive report:

> 教室は、学生が先生に掃除させられた。  
> きょうしつは、がくせいが せんせいに そうじさせられた。  
> As for the classroom, the student was made to clean it by the teacher.

教室 is still the object, 学生が marks the student who cleans, and 先生に marks the causer.
Making the classroom the topic does not make it the person being made to act.

Ordinary causative-passive reports often suggest an unwelcome obligation. They do not, without context,
prove that the actor was angry, that force was physical, or that the action was harmful.
A negative such as 読ませられなかった denies being made to read; it does not prove that no reading occurred.

**A recognition limit:** the full ending can also coincide with the potential of a causative.
For example, in a suitable setting 食べさせられる can mean "can get someone to eat."
Our role questions explicitly ask for the causative-passive reading; they do not claim to classify
every uncontextualized させられる. Broader causative-potential comparisons are outside the scored scope.

## 5. Common shortened causative-passives

After learning the full form, recognize these common shorter forms:

| Full | Shorter | Plain past pair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 読ませられる | 読まされる（よまされる） | 読ませられた／読まされた |
| 待たせられる | 待たされる（またされる） | 待たせられた／待たされた |
| 買わせられる | 買わされる（かわされる） | 買わせられた／買わされた |

For the ordinary godan pattern shown here, the a-row base takes **される** instead of **せられる**.
This is **not** the potential ら-removal seen in 食べれる.
The shorter causative-passive still inflects as ichidan: 読まされない, 読まされた, 読まされません.

Do not extend this standard shortcut to every verb:

| Keep the full form in this pattern | Reason |
| --- | --- |
| 話させられる（はなさせられる） | Godan ending in す; not the shortcut 話さされる |
| 食べさせられる | Ichidan; not 食べさされる |
| させられる | する; not さされる |
| 来させられる（こさせられる） | 来る; not 来さされる |

These are the boundaries of the ordinary shortening taught here, not a declaration that no dialect
ever uses another form. Named shortening/expansion drills request one operation; the full form is
not generally wrong just because a shorter form is available.

**Separate recognition:** short *causatives* such as 読ます and 食べさす also occur, especially in
conversational or regional usage. They are not themselves passive, and they inflect as godan verbs
ending in す: 読ます → 読ました. Do not confuse that past with 読まされた.
This lesson explains the connection but does not independently drill every short causative or dialect.

## 6. A small permission-request bridge

> この写真を使わせてください。  
> このしゃしんを つかわせてください。  
> Please let me use this photograph.

使う（つかう） → 使わせる → 使わせて, then ください.
The speaker asks to be the user of the photo; compare 使ってください, which asks the listener to use it.
A request does not establish that permission was granted.
This is supporting reading, not another scored group.

For the receiving-side viewpoint behind later benefactive combinations, revisit
[giving and receiving](giving-receiving-and-viewpoint.md).
The full させてもらう／させていただく system is outside this N4 batch; it is not a required distractor.

## Practice route and limits

The six groups move from causative formation to roles and contextual make/let interpretation, then to
causative-passive formation, perspective and common shortening/expansion.
Role questions use fixed codes **第一（だいいち）**, **第二（だいに）**, **第三（だいさん）**
for the first, second and third named entities in the Japanese sentence, not shuffled button positions.
The task asks which entity causes, performs or receives the lexical action.

Several operations deliberately share the same reading, waiting, meal, cleaning and arrival scenes.
They make the differences visible without claiming independent conversational transfer from every item.
Ordinary typed forms and recognized same-sense spellings are accepted within the named operation;
recognition does not imply equal spelling frequency.
