# About, towards, and from someone's perspective

**について identifies what is being discussed; に対して can identify the target of a response or attitude;
にとって identifies a standpoint for evaluating something.** An English translation containing "about,"
"to," or "for" does not by itself choose among them.

Compare three ways a teacher can be involved:

> 先生について話しました。  
> せんせいに ついて はなしました。  
> We talked about the teacher.
>
> 先生に対して丁寧に話しました。  
> せんせいに たいして ていねいに はなしました。  
> I spoke politely to the teacher.
>
> 先生にとって、この本は大切です。  
> せんせいに とって、このほんは たいせつです。  
> This book is important to the teacher.

The first does not say the teacher was listening. The second concerns speech directed to the teacher.
The third evaluates the book in relation to the teacher; it does not say that anyone spoke about it.
These are different relations, not three levels of politeness.

This is part four of the noun/particle series. The [basic-particle lesson](japanese-nouns-and-basic-particles.md)
explains roles such as recipient and object; [は, が, and も](wa-ga-mo-topic-and-inclusion.md) explains topic,
contrast, and inclusion. The [restriction lesson](only-and-unexpected-inclusion.md) considers which alternatives
are included or excluded. Here we connect those ideas to [noun-based grammar](koto-wake-and-formal-nouns.md).

## A small map, not a translation replacement list

Here **N** means a noun or an appropriately built noun phrase.

| Pattern | Question it helps answer | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nについて | What is the subject matter? | 旅行について話す - talk about the trip |
| Nに対して | Towards whom/what is the action, response, or attitude directed? | 子供に対して親切だ - be kind to children |
| Nにとって | For whom/what is this valuable, difficult, necessary, etc.? | 学生にとって難しい - difficult for students |
| Nに関して | What matter does this concern? | 計画に関して話す - speak regarding the plan |
| Nのために | Whose benefit, or what purpose, motivates the action in this use? | 家族のために働く - work for one's family |
| Nの場合 | Which case or circumstances are being considered? | 雨の場合 - in the event of rain |

Readings: 旅行（りょこう）, 対して（たいして）, 親切（しんせつ）, 関して（かんして）,
計画（けいかく）, 場合（ばあい）.

The final two rows contain nouns. They overlap with some English renderings of the first rows,
but are not structurally interchangeable with them.

## について: subject matter, not necessarily the listener

> 友達と旅行について話しました。  
> ともだちと りょこうに ついて はなしました。  
> I talked with a friend about the trip.

友達と identifies the conversational partner; 旅行について identifies the subject matter.
A sentence can contain both. The about-phrase can also accompany finding out, investigating, writing,
or asking about something:

> 学校の歴史について調べました。  
> がっこうの れきしに ついて しらべました。  
> I investigated the school's history.

Do not insert の between the noun and this relation: **旅行について**, not 旅行のについて.
For a following noun, however, learn **Nについての + noun**:

> 旅行についての本を読みました。  
> りょこうに ついての ほんを よみました。  
> I read a book about travel.

The whole 旅行についての本 phrase is the object marked by を. This の connects the about-phrase to 本;
it does not mean that travel owns the book.

You can add は to the relation, as in **旅行については、後で話します**, "As for the trip, I will talk about
it later" (後で: あとで). The added は manages topic or contrast; について still supplies the subject-matter
relation. They are not rival labels for exactly the same grammatical job.

## に対して: a directed relation, not automatic hostility

The [対する](../words/対する.md) family is useful for the target of treatment, an attitude, or a response:

> 先生は子供に対して親切です。  
> せんせいは こどもに たいして しんせつです。  
> The teacher is kind to children.
>
> 質問に対する答えを読みました。  
> しつもんに たいする こたえを よみました。  
> I read the answer to the question.

Kindness and an ordinary answer are not opposition. Translations such as "against" fit some contexts,
not every occurrence of 対して or 対する.

Before a predicate, the common form here is **Nに対して**. Before a noun, **Nに対する + noun** is a useful
model. **Nに対しての + noun** also occurs:

| Predicate relation | Noun modification |
| --- | --- |
| 質問に対して答える - respond to a question | 質問に対する答え / 質問に対しての答え - an answer to a question |
| 子供に対して親切だ - be kind to children | 子供に対する態度 / 子供に対しての態度 - an attitude towards children |

態度 is たいど, "attitude." Do not mechanically use bare に対して directly before 答え or 態度.
The choice of form and surrounding sentence matters; the two noun-modifying realizations need not sound
equally concise in every style.

Simple に can be natural too: **子供に親切です** is not a mistake. The longer expression makes the directed
relation more explicit and often sounds more deliberate. It does not replace every に or を:
meeting a friend is ordinarily **友達に会う**, and reading a book is **本を読む**. A compound relation
does not abolish the verb's own argument pattern.

## にとって: the standpoint of an evaluation

> 学生にとって、この本は難しいです。  
> がくせいに とって、このほんは むずかしいです。  
> This book is difficult for students.
>
> 私にとって、家族は大切です。  
> わたしに とって、かぞくは たいせつです。  
> My family is important to me.

The evaluation may be favorable or unfavorable. It concerns the relevant person's needs, experience,
or circumstances, not necessarily an opinion that person has actually voiced.
The complement need not be a person: **植物にとって、水は必要です**, "Water is necessary for plants"
(植物: しょくぶつ; 必要: ひつよう), evaluates a requirement for plants.

Keep the noun directly before the relation: **私にとって**, not 私のにとって.
An adjective can then modify another noun in the usual way:

> 私にとって大切な本  
> わたしに とって たいせつな ほん  
> a book that is important to me

Here 大切な modifies 本. Do not assume every noun-modifying expression is built by adding の in the
same position; learn the actual construction you use.

にとっては and にとっても add topic/contrast or inclusion. They do not turn evaluation into a beneficiary
action. Nor should にとって replace every English "in my opinion": identifying who performs an action,
reports a fact, or holds a role may require a different construction.

## One useful neighbor: に関して and に関する

[関する](../words/関する.md) concerns what something relates to:

> 計画に関して話しました。  
> けいかくに かんして はなしました。  
> We talked regarding the plan.
>
> 学校の歴史に関する本を読みました。  
> がっこうの れきしに かんする ほんを よみました。  
> I read a book concerning the school's history.

For ordinary subject matter, について and に関して often overlap. に関して readily fits an organized
discussion, explanation, or document and tends to sound more formal than について. This is a tendency,
not a different truth value or a ban on using it in conversation.

The useful noun patterns are **Nに関する + noun** and **Nに関しての + noun**:
**歴史に関する本 / 歴史に関しての本 / 歴史についての本** can all describe a book about history.
The internal forms differ even when the same book could be described by all three.

Compare **質問に関する説明**, an explanation concerning a question, with **質問に対する答え**,
an answer responding to it. A document could satisfy both descriptions; "related matter" and "directed
response" are not permanently disjoint categories. Use the actual relation and context, not a rule that
one English noun always permits exactly one Japanese connector.

## The bridge to formal nouns

### について話す and のことを話す

> 先生について話しました。  
> せんせいに ついて はなしました。  
> We talked about the teacher.
>
> 先生のことを話しました。  
> せんせいの ことを はなしました。  
> We talked about the teacher / matters concerning the teacher.

These can describe the same conversation. The structures are different:

- **先生 + について** supplies a relation to the speaking.
- **先生 + の + [こと](../words/こと.md)** forms a noun phrase for matters concerning the teacher;
  **を** marks that phrase as the object of 話す.

のこと can encompass circumstances, experiences, or other matters connected with someone.
It is not automatically a claim of emotional closeness, and について is not automatically impersonal.
For a defined report topic, について or に関して often makes the relation straightforward;
のことを話す also fits many ordinary conversations. Do not demand an artificial unique winner when the
task only asks to talk about someone or a plan.

This overlap is not permission to replace について with のことを in every sentence.
The を belongs to an object construction: it does not form an evaluation such as **私にとって大切です**.
Likewise, **旅行についての本** is not made into the same noun phrase by substituting のことを.

### Standpoint is not the same as intended benefit

> 子供にとって、この本は大切です。  
> こどもに とって、このほんは たいせつです。  
> This book is important for the children.
>
> 子供のために、この本を作りました。  
> こどもの ために、このほんを つくりました。  
> We made this book for the children.
>
> 子供に対して親切に話しました。  
> こどもに たいして しんせつに はなしました。  
> I spoke kindly to the children.

The first evaluates importance; it does not assert who made the book or why.
The second presents the children as intended beneficiaries; that purpose does not prove the book actually
helped them. The third describes the target of the speaker's treatment.
The same project can involve all three relations, but one sentence does not automatically assert the others.

[ため](../words/ため.md) also has purpose and cause uses, developed in the
[formal-noun lesson](koto-wake-and-formal-nouns.md#ため-おかげ-and-せい-a-goal-a-cause-or-an-evaluation).
**働くために**, "in order to work," attaches a dictionary-form verb directly.
**雨のため**, "because of rain," has a causal reading, not a beneficiary.
Do not reduce every ため to a person's benefit.

### Attachment belongs to the construction

Compare these forms rather than memorizing "formal nouns always take の":

| Preceding expression | Actual pattern | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Noun + direct relation | 学生にとって | for students, as the relevant standpoint |
| Noun + noun-based case | 学生の場合 | in the case of students |
| Noun + noun-based benefit | 家族のために | for the family's sake |
| Na-adjective + case | 必要な場合 | if necessary |
| I-adjective + case | 難しい場合 | if it is difficult |
| Plain verb + case | 遅れる場合 | if one will be late |
| Plain verb + nominalizer | 日本で働くこと | working in Japan |
| Noun predicate + nominalizer | 学生であること | being a student |

遅れる is おくれる. [場合](../words/場合.md) names the case or circumstances to which something applies.
**私の場合は** can introduce facts about my own circumstances; **私にとって** selects a standpoint for an
evaluation. They sometimes fit related situations, but 私の場合は does not always evaluate, and にとって
does not replace the conditional meaning of 遅れる場合.

**学生のこと** means matters about a student or students. **学生であること** explicitly nominalizes
"being a student." A conversation about a student's circumstances might include their status, but that
possible topical overlap does not make the grammatical structures identical.

### A nominalized clause can be the complement

> 日本で働くことについて話しました。  
> にほんで はたらく ことに ついて はなしました。  
> We talked about working in Japan.

Read the layers as **[日本で働く + こと] + について**.
こと makes the clause noun-like; について relates that whole phrase to the discussion.
The sentence does not, by itself, say that anyone has already worked in Japan. The discussion may concern a plan.
Similarly, **日本で働くことに対する不安**, "anxiety about working in Japan" (不安: ふあん), directs
an attitude towards the nominalized activity.

This lesson practices the explicit こと pattern. Nominalizing の has its own distribution; it is not
a freely interchangeable substitute in every construction. See the
[こと／の comparison](koto-wake-and-formal-nouns.md#a-particularly-important-neighbor-の).

## に対して can also compare situations

> 兄は家で働くのに対して、妹は学校で働きます。  
> あには いえで はたらくのに たいして、いもうとは がっこうで はたらきます。  
> My older brother works at home, whereas my younger sister works at a school.

This compares workplaces. It is not an action directed at the brother, and it does not say the siblings
are in conflict. The relevant pattern here is **plain verb clause + の + に対して**:
the の packages the first situation, and に対して supplies the comparison.
Do not stop reading at のに and mistake the larger sequence for the separate concessive
[のに construction](noni-and-concessive-contrasts.md).

For recognition, na-adjective and noun predicates can have **静かなのに対して**, "whereas it is quiet,"
and **学生なのに対して**, "whereas someone is a student"; である formulations also occur.
These connectors are not the genitive の in 家族のため.
The exercises recognize the comparison and its layers, not every possible comparative attachment.

## What is being classified, and what is being practiced?

These expressions are often taught as **compound particles** or **compound relational expressions**.
That functional label does not make them all morphologically identical. 対して／対する and 関して／関する
show verb-form differences, while こと, ため, and 場合 are nouns in the constructions discussed here.
Nor does ending in に prove that an expression contains a formal noun.
The terminology and boundaries of "formal noun" vary; no common modern derivation is assumed for this whole set.

Practice distinguishes named-form building, defined interpretation labels, and bounded contextual choices.
In shared topic tasks, fitting について, に関して, and のことを formulations are accepted together.
An interpretation label outside a quoted sentence is not a connector to insert inside that sentence.
Comparative attachment beyond the demonstrated patterns, the full range of ため/場合, and further relations
such as において, として, によって, and をめぐって remain outside this lesson's practiced scope.

Kana **について**, **にとって**, and **こと** are the ordinary models here.
The recognized spellings **に就いて**, **に取って**, and **事** may occur; recognition is not equal frequency.
Practice also accepts kana readings of 対して／対する and 関して／関する. Rare historical spellings are not
new grammatical contrasts.
