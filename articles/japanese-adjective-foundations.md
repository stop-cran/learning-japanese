# Japanese adjectives: describing things and changing the ending

Compare **安いです**, "it is inexpensive," with **静かです**, "it is quiet."
Both describe something politely, but their negative and past forms are built differently.
The important distinction is the **whole adjective pattern**, not whether its spelling happens to end in い.

## Two basic patterns

| Use | い-adjective: 安い（やすい）, inexpensive | な-adjective: 静か（しずか）, quiet |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plain affirmative statement | 安い | 静かだ |
| Polite affirmative statement | 安いです | 静かです |
| Before a noun | 安い店（やすいみせ）, an inexpensive shop | 静かな部屋（しずかなへや）, a quiet room |

Do not add plain だ after ordinary 安い: **安いだ** is not the basic pattern.
Do not attach な to every adjective: **安いな店** is wrong for "an inexpensive shop."
Conversely, the basic modifier is 静かな部屋, not 静かい部屋.
Before a noun, the affirmative nonpast connector is な; it is not the sentence-final だ.

**きれい** ("clean; beautiful") and **嫌い（きらい）** ("disliked") use the な pattern despite their final い.
They are not formed by replacing that last い with くない or かった.
Some words have special or multiple patterns; this lesson is not a rule that every descriptive word fits one
simple spelling test.

## Time and negation

| Meaning | い pattern, plain | な pattern, plain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Is inexpensive / quiet | 安い | 静かだ |
| Is not inexpensive / quiet | 安くない | 静かではない／静かじゃない |
| Was inexpensive / quiet | 安かった | 静かだった |
| Was not inexpensive / quiet | 安くなかった | 静かではなかった／静かじゃなかった |

For the い pattern, replace final い with **くない**, **かった** or **くなかった**.
For the な pattern, keep 静か and change what follows it.
**じゃ** is the common contraction of **では**, not a different tense.

> 昨日は寒かったです。今日も寒いです。  
> きのうはさむかったです。きょうもさむいです。  
> It was cold yesterday. It is cold today too.

A past description does **not** logically guarantee that the property has disappeared now.
The two sentences are compatible. Likewise, 安かった does not by itself mean the shop is now expensive.

## Polite forms: not just でした after everything

| Meaning | い pattern, polite | な pattern, polite |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Affirmative nonpast | 安いです | 静かです |
| Negative nonpast | 安くないです／安くありません | 静かではないです／静かではありません |
| Affirmative past | 安かったです | 静かでした |
| Negative past | 安くなかったです／安くありませんでした | 静かではなかったです／静かではありませんでした |

じゃ variants are also possible in the な-pattern negatives.
The ordinary polite past of 安い is **安かったです**, not 安いでした.
Conversational forms and more formal alternatives can overlap; an exercise requesting a particular ending
must state that restriction before asking for an answer.

## いい changes to よ-

For **いい／良い**, "good," use the よ- forms when inflecting:

| Nonpast | Negative | Past | Negative past | Connecting form |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| いい／よい | よくない | よかった | よくなかった | よくて |

良い can represent いい or よい; 良かった is read よかった.
Do not form **いかった** or **いくない**.
This does not turn every word ending in いい into the same exception:
かわいい ("cute") gives かわいかった, whereas かっこいい ("cool") gives かっこよかった.

## A noun-predicate bridge

Noun predicates use endings resembling the な pattern, but **ordinary noun modification uses の, not な**:
学生の部屋（がくせいのへや）, "the student's room," is different from 静かな部屋, "a quiet room."

| Meaning | Plain | Ordinary polite |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Was a student | 学生だった | 学生でした |
| Was not a student | 学生ではなかった／学生じゃなかった | 学生ではありませんでした／学生じゃありませんでした |

学生ではなかったです／学生じゃなかったです are also ordinary polite past negatives.
The noun itself does not acquire an English-style case ending.
See [nouns and basic particles](japanese-nouns-and-basic-particles.md#when-the-predicate-is-a-noun-だ-and-です).

## Connecting descriptions

> この部屋は広くて、静かです。  
> このへやはひろくて、しずかです。  
> This room is spacious and quiet.
>
> この部屋は静かで、明るいです。  
> このへやはしずかで、あかるいです。  
> This room is quiet and bright.

Use **い → くて**, but **な-adjective + で**. Simultaneous qualities are not a sequence of events.
The existing [listing lesson](japanese-listing-and-examples.md#て-くて-and-で-connecting-is-not-always-listing-examples)
already practises these connections, including よくて.

For **becoming** quiet or inexpensive, see the short ～になる／～くなる bridge in
[retained and changed states](koto-wake-and-formal-nouns.md#まま-with-nouns-and-adjectives-staying-the-same-versus-becoming).
That article's comparison with まま is more advanced; it is not needed to learn this ending table.
