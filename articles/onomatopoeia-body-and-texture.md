# Bodies, appearance and surfaces

These descriptions select a sensation or appearance; they are not medical diagnoses or
objective judgments about a person's body. For food and liquid textures, see
[food, water and texture](onomatopoeia-food-and-water.md).

## Unsteadiness and physical reactions

回転遊具から降りると、頭がくらくらした。  
*After getting off the spinning ride, I felt dizzy.*

[くらくら](../words/くらくら.md) can describe dizziness.
[がくがく](../words/がくがく.md) can describe shaking knees or chattering teeth.
[よろよろ](../words/よろよろ.md) describes tottering movement, while
[ひょろひょろ](../words/ひょろひょろ.md) can describe a slender, frail appearance or
unsteady movement. [へなへな](../words/へなへな.md) can describe weakness or flimsiness.
Compare [ふらふら](../words/ふらふら.md) for unsteady movement and
[ぐったり](../words/ぐったり.md) for exhaustion or limpness. These are descriptions,
not mutually exclusive symptoms.

[ぞわぞわ](../words/ぞわぞわ.md) can describe a shivery or prickling sensation.
[ひくひく](../words/ひくひく.md) describes twitching: 頬がひくひくする describes
the cheek twitching, while 鼻をひくひくさせる presents the nose as what is moved.
The sentence does not establish a medical cause.

## Eyes and bodies

知らせを聞いて、彼は目をぱちくりさせた。  
*On hearing the news, he blinked in surprise.*

[ぱちくり](../words/ぱちくり.md) can describe blinking in surprise;
[ぱっちり](../words/ぱっちり.md) can describe wide-open, bright eyes. The
ぱっちりした目 noun phrase describes the eyes; it does not mean they have stopped being
bright. [ぽかん](../words/ぽかん.md)とする, discussed in
[behaviour and reactions](onomatopoeia-behaviour-and-reactions.md), can depict a blank
reaction without specifying its cause.

[すらり](../words/すらり.md) can describe a slender, well-proportioned figure:
the selected exercises use すらりとした before a noun. Similar-looking
[すらすら](../words/すらすら.md) can describe fluent reading or speech, not a figure.
[むちむち](../words/むちむち.md) can describe a plump or full-bodied appearance.
[ぶよぶよ](../words/ぶよぶよ.md) describes a soft, flabby or mushy condition.
Compare [ぷにぷに](../words/ぷにぷに.md), used for soft, yielding texture: such
descriptions can overlap, and tone depends on what is being described.

## Hair, edges and textures

風で髪がぼさぼさになった。  
*The wind made my hair messy.*

[ぼさぼさ](../words/ぼさぼさ.md) can describe untidy hair.
[もじゃもじゃ](../words/もじゃもじゃ.md) can describe shaggy hair or a beard;
[ふさふさ](../words/ふさふさ.md) can describe thick, luxuriant hair or fur.
Thickness alone does not establish whether something is neat.
[くしゃくしゃ](../words/くしゃくしゃ.md) can describe dishevelled hair or crumpled
paper; compare [ぐしゃぐしゃ](../words/ぐしゃぐしゃ.md) in
[fit and disorder](onomatopoeia-fit-and-disorder.md).

[ごわごわ](../words/ごわごわ.md) describes a stiff, coarse texture;
[がさがさ](../words/がさがさ.md) can describe rough, dry skin or a rustling sound.
[ぎざぎざ](../words/ぎざぎざ.md) describes a jagged or serrated edge, rather than
the surface feel of cloth. [くっきり](../words/くっきり.md) can describe an outline
standing out clearly; clarity of outline does not require a jagged edge.

The exercises use ごわごわする for a condition and ごわごわになる for a change.
They also use ぎざぎざの before a noun and ぎざぎざする as a predicate. These are
word-specific frames, not a universal rule for attaching particles.
