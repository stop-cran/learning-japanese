# Volition, plans and goals

**I intend to do it**, **it is planned**, **I hope it happens**, and **it has become possible**
can concern the same event without saying the same thing. This article connects those viewpoints.
The new N4 practice focuses on **plain volition**, **volitional thought**, and **予定（よてい）**.
Existing goal, change, maintained-practice and desire exercises keep their original homes.

Start with [wants, plans and invitations](wants-plans-and-invitations.md) for たい, つもり and
polite invitations. These are learning-reference levels, not an official examination syllabus.

## 1. Form the plain volitional

The volitional is a verb form useful for a resolve or proposal: depending on context,
**行こう（いこう）** can be "I'll go" or "let's go." Do not translate every occurrence as plural "let's."

| Verb class | Operation | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Godan | Change the final u-row sound to the corresponding o-row sound, then add う | 買う → 買おう（かおう）; 書く → 書こう（かこう） |
| Godan, continued | Keep the consonant, including its voicing | 泳ぐ → 泳ごう（およごう）; 話す → 話そう（はなそう） |
| Ichidan | Remove final る and add よう | 食べる → 食べよう（たべよう）; 見る → 見よう（みよう） |
| Irregular する | Use しよう | 宿題をする → 宿題をしよう（しゅくだいをしよう） |
| Irregular 来る | Use 来よう, read **こよう** | 明日も来る → 明日も来よう（あしたもこよう） |

The godan ending changes are **う→おう、く→こう、ぐ→ごう、す→そう、つ→とう、
ぬ→のう、ぶ→ぼう、む→もう、る→ろう**. For example, 待つ（まつ）, "wait,"
becomes 待とう; 読む（よむ）, "read," becomes 読もう.

Ending in る does not identify an ichidan verb. **帰る（かえる）**, "return home," is godan:
**帰ろう**, not 帰よう. **見る** is ichidan: **見よう**, not 見ろう.
**話す** is a whole godan verb, not the irregular word する attached to a stem.
For other finite forms, see [verb foundations](japanese-verb-foundations.md).

> 家に帰ろう。  
> いえにかえろう。  
> I'll head home. / Let's head home.

Said to oneself, this can present a resolve. Addressed to companions in a shared plan, it can
propose going home together. The verb ending alone does not establish the participants.
Compare polite **帰りましょう（かえりましょう）**: plain volition does not take **です**
as a shortcut to that polite form.

**Verb-ending よう is not the noun-based よう** in ように／ようだ. 食べよう is one
conjugated verb; 食べるように contains the nonpast verb followed by a separate construction.
The matching sound is not a reason to apply the same attachment rule.

## 2. Put the intended action inside a thought

Use **volitional + と + 思う（おもう）** to present an intention:

> 今夜、友達に手紙を書こうと思います。  
> こんや、ともだちにてがみをかこうとおもいます。  
> I'm thinking of writing a letter to my friend tonight.

The volitional remains plain before と; the final **思います** makes the whole statement polite.
This is not an invitation to the listener merely because 書こう occurs inside it.

> 昼は会社で食べようと思っています。  
> ひるはかいしゃでたべようとおもっています。  
> I am planning to eat at work at lunchtime.

**思っている／思っています** can present an intention someone holds. It does not encode
a minimum number of days, a commitment percentage, or a guarantee that the action happens.
It is useful when reporting another person's intention too:

> 姉は日本で働こうと思っています。  
> あねはにほんではたらこうとおもっています。  
> My older sister intends to work in Japan.

That reports her intention; it does not prove that she works there already. Ordinary standalone
思う statements often present the speaker's perspective, but questions, quotations and reports
need their actual participants. Do not turn that tendency into an unconditional person ban.

### An intention is not just a prediction with a stronger confidence score

| Presented thought | Example | Useful reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Intention to take an action | 私は今夜、手紙を書こうと思います。 | I intend/am thinking of writing a letter tonight. |
| Expectation about an event | 私は今夜、手紙を書くと思います。 | I think I will write a letter tonight. |
| Someone else's held intention | 姉は日本で働こうと思っています。 | My older sister intends to work in Japan. |
| The speaker's expectation about her | 私は姉が日本で働くと思っています。 | I think my older sister will work in Japan. |

Readings: 私（わたし）, 今夜（こんや）, 手紙（てがみ）, 書く（かく）,
姉（あね）, 日本（にほん）, 働く（はたらく）.
The first-person expectation can coexist with an intention; it does not deny one.
In the last row, the speaker holds the expectation and the sister is the prospective worker.
The new interpretations classify the **explicit presentation**, not hidden motives or certainty.
Compare [ordinary thoughts and expectations](thinking-possibility-and-expectation.md).

## 3. Present an activity as planned: 予定

**予定（よてい）** is a plan or scheduled arrangement. For these ordinary affirmative patterns:

| What comes before 予定 | Connection | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Verb | Plain nonpast verb directly before 予定 | 博物館へ行く予定です。 |
| Event noun | Noun + の + 予定 | 明日は会議の予定です。 |

> 来週は博物館へ行く予定です。  
> らいしゅうははくぶつかんへいくよていです。  
> I plan/am scheduled to go to the museum next week.

> 明日は会議の予定です。  
> あしたはかいぎのよていです。  
> A meeting is planned for tomorrow.

For the verb connection, change **行きます** to **行く** before 予定; keep polite **です**
after 予定. For the separate noun modifier, use **会議の予定**, not **会議だ予定**.
**会議をする予定です**, "we plan to have a meeting," is another way to describe the event;
the noun-conversion exercises expressly retain the noun rather than recasting it as an action.

The planned event can be presented without identifying who made the plan. A 予定 need not
have a booking number or a date stated in the same sentence, and plans can change.
It is not a promise that the event will happen.

For a past plan, change the final copula: **去年の夏休みは旅行の予定でした**
（きょねんのなつやすみはりょこうのよていでした）, "A trip was planned for last summer."
**予定でした** reports the earlier plan; it does not by itself prove either fulfillment or cancellation.

### つもり and 予定 can overlap

> 来週は博物館へ行くつもりです。  
> らいしゅうははくぶつかんへいくつもりです。  
> I intend to go to the museum next week.

Compared with **行く予定です**, this foregrounds the speaker's intention.
The two can describe the same visit: a personal intention and a planned activity are not mutually exclusive.
Do not turn them into a universal "unbooked versus booked" test or a scale of fixed commitment.
Likewise, an intention need not be something the person particularly wants to do.

The existing [intention discussion](wants-plans-and-invitations.md) covers wanting versus intending,
positive and negative plans, and polite proposals. The new tasks interpret the **given wording**,
not choose the only supposedly possible ending for an underspecified future event.

For reported descriptions, distinguish the relevant quoted message from a neighboring statement
by the narrator. [Quotations and endings](explanations-quotations-and-endings.md#と-and-って-mark-quoted-content)
explains how 「」 and と mark the quoted content; reporting someone else's intention does not make it your own.

## 4. A goal, an observed change, or a maintained practice

These are already practised in the [goal and change sections](koto-wake-and-formal-nouns.md).
Keep the original tasks rather than learning another copy of them:

| Viewpoint | Example | What it presents |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Intended enabling result | 字が読めるように、眼鏡をかけます。 | I put on glasses so that I can read the writing. |
| Changed possibility | 眼鏡をかけて、字が読めるようになりました。 | With glasses on, I became able to read the writing. |
| Changed routine | 最近は毎日、本を読むようになりました。 | Recently I have started reading every day. |
| Maintained effort | 毎日、本を読むようにしています。 | I make a point of reading every day. |
| Maintained avoidance | 夜は仕事のメールを読まないようにしています。 | I make a point of not reading work emails at night. |

Readings: 字（じ）, 読める（よめる）, 眼鏡（めがね）, 最近（さいきん）,
毎日（まいにち）, 本（ほん）, 読む（よむ）, 夜（よる）, 仕事（しごと）.

A goal does not prove its achievement. A changed possibility need not be a newly learned skill:
glasses can change access to the writing. A changed habit can use an ordinary dictionary-form verb,
not only a potential verb. Someone who could already read can start reading daily.

A change can be deliberately brought about. Maintained effort can also produce a change, and
making a point of something can admit occasional lapses. **読まないようにしている**
expresses care **not to read**, not an absence of effort.
These viewpoints can coexist; なる versus する is not a universal accidental/deliberate detector.

The original exercises supply their frames and request a form or bounded interpretation.
Their shared scenes are useful repetition, not independent conversations or proof of unrestricted mastery.

## 5. Wishes and desired outcomes

The existing [desire and outcome article](desire-actions-and-outcomes.md) distinguishes
**辞書がほしい** ("I want a dictionary"), **本を読みたい** ("I want to read a book"),
and **雨が降らないでほしい** ("I hope it does not rain").
An event being wanted does not make it a plan, instruction or accomplished result.
Keep that article's participant and negation practice in its original groups.

### といい: a hope here, not a guarantee

> 明日は晴れるといいですね。  
> あしたははれるといいですね。  
> I hope it is sunny tomorrow.

> 次の部屋は静かだといいですね。  
> つぎのへやはしずかだといいですね。  
> I hope the next room is quiet.

The ordinary nonpast attachment is **verb / i-adjective + といい**,
but **noun / na-adjective + だといい**. Here **静かだ** retains だ.
The wished condition is not guaranteed to happen.

Keep the desired situation's polarity **before といい**:

| Supplied polite predicate | Affirmative hope | Negative hope |
|---|---|---|
| 晴れます（はれます, be sunny） | 晴れるといい | 晴れないといい |
| 暖かいです（あたたかいです, be warm） | 暖かいといい | 暖かくないといい |
| 静かです（しずかです, be quiet） | 静かだといい | 静かではないといい |
| 休みです（やすみです, be a day off） | 休みだといい | 休みではないといい |

The negative column means hoping that the predicate **does not hold**: the last example
hopes it is **not** a day off. Negating the hoped-for situation is not the same operation
as saying that the situation would **not be good**.
The copular negatives **でない／ではない／じゃない／じゃあない** can all precede といい;
the contractions are conversational. **とよい** is another realization of the same pattern.
The formation practice requests short plain predicates, not the more formal copula **である**,
and fixes the lexical predicate rather than asking for every synonymous wish.

といい also appears in advice: **分からない言葉は辞書で調べるといいですよ**
（わからないことばはじしょでしらべるといいですよ） means "For words you don't understand,
it's a good idea to look them up in a dictionary." Do not call every occurrence a wish.
Final **な** and **ね** can shape how a wish is expressed, but do not mechanically select
"for myself" versus "for someone else" in every conversation.

### ように祈る and final wishes

> 明日は晴れるように祈っています。  
> あしたははれるようにいのっています。  
> I am hoping/praying for fine weather tomorrow.

> 試験に合格しますように。  
> しけんにごうかくしますように。  
> May I pass the exam.

In these wish uses, a desired nonpast positive or negative situation precedes ように.
祈る（いのる） can express praying or hoping; an explicit religious setting is not required.
**願う（ねがう, wish/hope）** can also introduce the wish content:

> 台風が来ないように願っています。  
> たいふうがこないようにねがっています。  
> I am hoping that the typhoon does not come.

The final wish commonly uses a polite predicate, as in **合格しますように**.
A negative polite wish can use **ませんように**:

> 会議に遅れませんように。  
> かいぎにおくれませんように。  
> May I not be late for the meeting.

Plain content before a prayer verb and polite sentence-final wishes are useful patterns,
not a ban on polite embedded wishes or plain final ones.
The drills explicitly request one form. They retain **に**; **よう** without に is also found
before verbs such as 願う, with a somewhat more formal feel, rather than being simply an error.
An optional quotative **と** can follow ように before a prayer/wish verb:
**成功するようにと祈っています**（せいこうするようにといのっています, "I am hoping/praying
for success"） is accepted alongside **成功するように祈っています**.
Wish content can contain different verb types: do not import the purpose pattern's
restrictions as a universal rule about what people can wish for.
This is not the same attachment task as the plain-verb purpose exercises.
Neither a wish nor a prayer establishes the result.

Keep the person wishing separate from the person in the desired event:

> 祖母は「孫が無事に帰れますように」と祈りました。  
> そぼは「まごがぶじにかえれますように」といのりました。  
> Grandmother prayed, "May my grandchild be able to return safely."

Grandmother is the person who prayed; the grandchild is the person returning in the wish.
This sentence alone does not report the grandchild's eventual return.

Read the full exchange: a sentence ending in ように can also leave an instruction or purpose
implicit. It is not automatically a prayer, and this is not the only Japanese construction
that can leave material unspoken.

### Manner or purpose plus desire: ように〜てほしい

This is a **recognition bridge**, not another scored desire set:

> 先生のように、ゆっくり話してほしいです。  
> せんせいのように、ゆっくりはなしてほしいです。  
> I want [you/someone] to speak slowly, like the teacher.

> 後ろの人にも聞こえるように、もう少し大きな声で話してほしいです。  
> うしろのひとにもきこえるように、もうすこしおおきなこえではなしてほしいです。  
> I want [you/someone] to speak a little louder so that people at the back can hear too.

The first **ように** supplies a model or manner. The second supplies an intended result
for the desired speaking action. In both, **話してほしい** expresses the desired action.
Compare ordinary **雨が降らないでほしい**, where the desired event is simply not raining,
and a final **晴れますように**, "may it be sunny," which is a wish rather than this combined frame.
Do not replace に and が mechanically to avoid repeating a particle; their roles still matter.

## Practice boundaries and further steps

The original plans/goals exercises contain seventeen form conversions and eight interpretations in three groups.
The other required areas use the original goals, changes, maintained practices and desire groups.
The separate hopes/wishes set now adds fourteen bounded formation tasks and six interpretations
in three groups: positive/negative hopes, embedded/final wish forms, and desired content,
certainty and participants. Choices recognize the requested form; typing requires its formation,
so those modes do not provide identical production evidence. Interpretation pairs share their
scenario keys and are not independent conversations.
Advice with といい and manner/purpose-plus-desire remain supporting recognition, not newly
scored targets. Shortened casual volition and negative volition are not new targets.

Optional **ことにする／ことになる**, purpose **ために**, and occasional nonpast **ことがある**
remain N3 follow-ups in the [formal-noun article](koto-wake-and-formal-nouns.md).
Past-experience ことがある is a different N5 use. The harder past/completion-belief つもり
tasks retain their existing N2 classification; no extra construction is an N4 prerequisite here.
