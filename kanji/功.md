# 功 — merit; achievement

**Strokes:** 5 · **Key (radical):** 力 (no. 19), on the right · **JLPT:** N1 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

功 concerns merit or achievement: something successfully accomplished or a valued contribution. [功績](../words/功績.md) こうせき highlights a meritorious achievement; [成功](../words/成功.md) せいこう means success in achieving a result.

功 can also refer to the effect produced by accumulated work or practice. It does not mean every effort or every neutral result.

**Modern-shape mnemonic, not etymology:** Picture [工](工.md)-shaped work beside [力](力.md)-shaped strength producing an accomplishment. This is a memory aid for the modern shape, not a historical or phonetic explanation.

| いさお | kun | 功 (いさお) — achievement; meritorious deed. Dated noun; recognition only. |

Reading questions select only コウ. ク is a valid reading in religious compounds, not another reading of 功績 or 成功. The native reading いさお is a documented dated noun, not merely a personal-name reading; it is retained for lookup rather than reading questions.

## Notes

- 功績 foregrounds a valued achievement or contribution; 成功 foregrounds success. Their meanings overlap, but a successful task need not be described as a distinguished contribution.
- [績](績.md), "achievements; results," shares the achievement meaning. This is a useful comparison, not a reliably wrong meaning choice.
- Compare [加](加.md), "add," and [幼](幼.md), "early childhood; immature." Like 功, both have five strokes and contain 力, but the other components and meanings differ.
- Distinguish the right-hand 力 in 功 from [刀](刀.md), "sword; blade": in 力 the slanting stroke crosses the upper stroke; in 刀 it starts inside the hooked outline.
