# 鵬 — peng; giant mythical bird

**Strokes:** 19 · **Key (radical):** 鳥 (no. 196) · **JLPT:** N1 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

鵬 names the **peng**, an enormous imaginary bird in Chinese mythology.
Learn it in [鵬](../words/鵬.md) (ほう). In the story associated with *Zhuangzi*,
the bird transforms from a fish. This is a mythical creature, not a living species.
It is distinct from the fenghuang, the [鳳凰](../words/鳳凰.md) (ほうおう)
often translated as "Chinese phoenix." An English "phoenix" gloss alone
does not establish that these birds or their stories are interchangeable.

**Modern-shape mnemonic, not etymology:** Find two [月](月.md)-like shapes
on the left and [鳥](鳥.md) on the right. Picture an enormous bird beside
two moons to remember its scale. This is only a memory aid for the current
shape, not a claim that the character historically means "two moons and a bird."

## Notes

- [鳴](鳴.md), "animal call; ring," has 口 beside 鳥 rather than the paired
  shapes on the left. [島](島.md), "island," has 山 below its bird-like upper
  part. 月 alone means "moon, month," not the mythical bird.
- おおとり also belongs to a wider dictionary family of bird spellings.
  Meaning and context matter: in this mythical-bird use, 鵬 identifies the peng, while 鳳 can identify
  the fenghuang. Do not use 鵬 for 大トリ (おおとり), the last or key performer.
- JMdict also records **ぼう** for the standalone word 鵬. That word variant
  does not change this card's frozen sole on-reading ホウ.
- Grade 9 is the source's name-use classification, not a claim that every
  occurrence is a name. Personal-name readings are outside this lesson.
