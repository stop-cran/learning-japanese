# 鮎 — sweetfish; ayu

**Strokes:** 16 · **Key (radical):** 魚 (no. 195), on the left · **JLPT:** N1 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

In Japanese, 鮎 normally names **ayu**, also called **sweetfish**, a fish associated with rivers and eaten as food. Learn this specific animal name rather than treating it as a general word for trout or smelt.

**Modern-shape mnemonic, not etymology:** Find [魚](魚.md), fish, on the left and [占](占.md), divine fortunes/occupy, on the right. Imagine a fortune-teller watching a fish. This is only a memory aid for the visible parts, not a historical explanation.

The source's **grade-9** classification concerns name-use kanji, not a ninth school grade. This lesson teaches a non-name animal noun.

Practice **あゆ**. All four sourced readings remain in the metadata, but only あゆ is selected for questions. The character dictionary records a **catfish** sense as well as the Japanese ayu sense; it does not make the two animals interchangeable. The [Seisenban entry](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E9%AE%8E-427693) explicitly distinguishes the Chinese catfish use from Japanese ayu.

## Notes

- **あゆ** and **アユ** also write this native animal name. JMdict labels it usually written in kana; katakana does not make it a foreign loanword.
- [魚](魚.md), fish, is the broader category, not an unambiguous wrong meaning choice for 鮎. [鯉](鯉.md) こい is carp, a different fish.
- [占](占.md) is the right-hand shape without the fish component; [店](店.md), shop, puts that shape under 广; [点](点.md), point/dot, has it above four bottom dots.
- Longer fish names must be learned as whole words. A spelling containing 鮎 does not by itself establish either the species or this character's reading.
