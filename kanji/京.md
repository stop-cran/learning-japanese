# 京 — capital

**Strokes:** 8 · **Key (radical):** 亠 (なべぶた, no. 8) · **Phonetic:** none · **JLPT:** N4 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

京 is commonly explained as a picture of a tall building on a mound or hill, which suggests a prominent place and hence a capital city.
The old form is debated and 亠 is only the dictionary key, so take the picture as the usual explanation rather than a certain fact.
It means "capital" (a national capital) and, in numbers, 10^16 (ケイ).

## Common words

- [東京](../words/東京.md) とうきょう — Tokyo
- 京都 きょうと — Kyoto
- 上京 じょうきょう — going to Tokyo (from the provinces)

## Notes

- 京 is read キョウ in both 東京 ("eastern capital") and 京都 ("capital city"); both names contain 京 but they are different cities. (For a true reversal, compare 田中 and 中田.)
- 上京 じょうきょう is "going up to the capital" (上 as in 上る "go up", toward the capital); today it usually means going to Tokyo. The same two characters are also place names where 上 means "upper": 上京区 かみぎょうく, the northern "upper capital" ward of Kyoto (opposite 下京区 しもぎょうく), and 上京 Shàngjīng, the "upper capital" of several medieval northeast Asian states (Balhae, Liao, Jin). The reading differs: じょうきょう for the verb, かみぎょう for the Kyoto ward.
- **Look-alikes:** 京 vs [高](高.md) (high): 高 has a 口 on top, then a 冂 frame containing another 口, while 京 has a single 口 above 小; compare also 景 (scenery), which adds 日.
