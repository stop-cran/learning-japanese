# 一 — one

**Strokes:** 1 · **Key (radical):** 一 (no. 1) · **Phonetic:** none · **JLPT:** N5 (unofficial list)

## Meaning and origin

一 is the simplest indicative character: a single horizontal line stands for the number one. [二](二.md) and [三](三.md) continue the pattern with two and three lines.
It is itself radical no. 1 and appears as a component in many kanji (三, 万, 上, 下).

Besides the plain number, 一 means "a single", "whole" (一日中 いちにちじゅう, all day) and "first" (一番 いちばん, number one, the most).

## Common words

- [一人](../words/一人.md) ひとり — one person; alone
- [一月](../words/一月.md) いちがつ — January
- [一万円](../words/一万円.md) いちまんえん — 10,000 yen
- 一つ ひとつ — one (thing)

## Notes

Before k, s, t, p sounds, イチ often contracts to イッ with a doubled consonant: 一回 いっかい, 一冊 いっさつ, 一本 いっぽん, 一分 いっぷん.
Do not confuse 一 with the long-vowel mark ー or the hyphen; 一 is a kanji, ー a katakana mark. In vertical or handwritten text 一 is also easy to mistake for 二 or 三 if lines are run together.
In formal documents (contracts, cheques) the number is written 壱 so that it cannot be altered.
