# 足 — foot, leg

**Strokes:** 7 · **Key (radical):** 足 (no. 157; written ⻊ at the left of characters) · **JLPT:** N4 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

足 is commonly explained as a pictograph: a round shape (口, the knee or lower leg) above 止, a foot with toes. The reading of the upper part is debated.
It means "foot" and "leg", and also "to be enough" (足りる), and it is a counter for pairs of footwear.
As a key it appears at the left as ⻊ in characters about feet and walking: 跳 (jump), 路 (path), 跡 (trace), 踏 (step).

## Notes

The counter 足 counts pairs of shoes and socks, with sound changes: 一足 いっそく, 二足 にそく, 三足 さんぞく, 何足 なんぞく.

Japanese separates 足 (foot and leg, from ankle or thigh down) from 脚 きゃく (leg, as of a table or a limb in formal usage); 足 is the ordinary word. Use 足す for adding numbers.

Look-alikes: 走 (run) has 土 over a 止-like base; 足 has 口 over 止.
