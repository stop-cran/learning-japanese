# 督 — supervise; urge

**Strokes:** 13 · **Key (radical):** 目 (no. 109), at the bottom · **JLPT:** N1 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

督 concerns supervising or directing people and urging action.
[監督](../words/監督.md) かんとく is supervision, or a director,
supervisor or coach. In 督促 とくそく, the emphasis is pressing
someone to carry out an action rather than letting it be delayed.
督励 とくれい is encouragement or urging.

Daijisen separates watching/regulating from pressing for timely action.
KANJIDIC2's coach, command, urge, lead and supervise glosses belong to
this range; they do not make every 督 compound a sports term.
総督 そうとく, governor-general or governor, illustrates an
administrative title.

**Modern-shape mnemonic, not etymology:** find the eight-stroke
叔-shaped group above [目](目.md). Imagine a supervisor keeping an
eye on what is happening. This is a memory aid, not a verified historical
derivation or claim about a sound component.

## Readings

| Reading | Type | Typical words |
| --- | --- | --- |
| トク | on | [監督](../words/監督.md) かんとく - supervision; director; coach |

Practice selects トク, also the Joyo reading. In 監督, [監](監.md)
supplies カン and 督 supplies トク. The two characters reinforce
related supervision ideas; they do not divide every use into separate
English job titles.

Daijisen also lists かみ as a kun reading, outside the KANJIDIC2
reading list used here. It is not selected for practice, and the cited
character entry gives no example for it. The difficult
spelling 基督 キリスト, Christ, is a complete-word reading:
JMdict marks the kanji spelling rare and phonetic, with kana usual.
It does not establish a general reading スト for 督.

## Common words

- [監督](../words/監督.md) かんとく - supervision; director; coach
- 督促 とくそく - urging; demand for action
- 督励 とくれい - encouragement; urging
- 総督 そうとく - governor-general; governor

## Notes

- [目](目.md) is only the lower eye; [眠](眠.md), sleep/sleepy,
  puts 目 on the left. [且](且.md), moreover/also, has an extended
  bottom bar and no upper 叔 group.
- Supervision and control overlap [監](監.md), [管](管.md),
  [御](御.md), [治](治.md), [制](制.md), [統](統.md) and
  [握](握.md). The regulatory branch also connects with [憲](憲.md).
  [理](理.md) also has Daijisen's おさめる (arrange/manage) sense.
  [経](経.md), [知](知.md) and [総](総.md) have management or
  governing branches too. [支](支.md) includes planning or arranging
  affairs, [処](処.md) managing or dealing with them, and [扱](扱.md)
  handling work. These are conservative management/oversight comparisons,
  not claims that arranging or handling something always means
  supervising people.
- Watching connects with [見](見.md), see, [観](観.md), observe/view,
  [察](察.md), observe/inspect, and [看](看.md), watch over/see.
  [番](番.md) includes keeping watch, [望](望.md) looking into the
  distance, and [偵](偵.md) seeking out another person's situation.
  Watching or surveillance need not be open, and is not always supervision.
  Guard/watch uses also connect with [守](守.md), guard/obey,
  and [警](警.md), warn/be on guard. [保](保.md) includes minding
  or caring for someone; it is kept apart conservatively without
  equating supervision with every kind of protection or preservation.
- [査](査.md), investigate/inspect, and [調](調.md), investigate/adjust,
  are kept apart conservatively for English checking/oversight
  comparisons, not because investigating is identical to supervising.
  [営](営.md), conduct business/manage, and [係](係.md),
  connection/person in charge, are kept apart for their management
  and responsible-person senses, not their unrelated title senses.
  KANJIDIC2 also lists "the one in charge" for [員](員.md),
  member/employee; it is kept apart conservatively, not because every
  member or employee supervises.
- The English coach gloss can suggest teaching or instruction:
  compare [教](教.md), teach/instruct, [師](師.md), teacher/specialist,
  [訓](訓.md), instruction/kun reading, and [授](授.md), impart/teach.
  [練](練.md) also includes training or drilling, and [育](育.md)
  has a training/teaching use in 育てる そだてる, alongside raising
  and nurturing. [養](養.md), nourish/raise, also has Daijisen's
  sense of cultivating someone's character or abilities, as in
  養成 ようせい, training. These conservative
  English coaching comparisons do not mean every supervisor teaches,
  every specialist coaches, or every kind of practice is coaching.
- Leading or commanding overlaps [導](導.md), [将](将.md),
  [率](率.md), [命](命.md) and [令](令.md). Role or authority
  uses connect with [領](領.md), [長](長.md), [主](主.md) and
  [官](官.md); actual job titles and ranks still differ.
  [頭](頭.md) and [首](首.md) also name leaders, and [権](権.md)
  concerns authority or power: possessing authority is not the same as
  exercising supervision. [指](指.md), pointing/indicating, and
  [引](引.md), which also includes taking someone along, are kept
  apart for loose English directing/leading comparisons, not as
  general Japanese synonyms for overseeing. [連](連.md),
  link/take along, is kept apart for the same reason: KANJIDIC2
  lists "lead," and Daijisen records leading someone along, as in
  連行 れんこう, taking someone away.
  [勅](勅.md), imperial order, retains its imperial restriction;
  [召](召.md), summon/respectful actions, specifies calling someone.
  Their command-related answers are kept apart, not treated as general
  replacements for supervising.
- Urging as encouragement overlaps [勧](勧.md) and [励](励.md).
  [激](激.md) can stir people or feelings, as in 激励,
  encouragement or spurring on. KANJIDIC2 also lists "encourage" for
  [勉](勉.md), while Daijisen emphasizes making one's own effort.
  These encouragement comparisons do not make every strong feeling or
  act of studying an act of urging.
- Urging as pressing or hurrying overlaps [促](促.md) and the
  prompting sense of [催](催.md). [急](急.md) also appears in
  急かす せかす, to hurry or press someone; it is not limited to
  one's own haste. [追](追.md) has a driving use in 追う おう and
  追い立てる おいたてる, to drive on or urge forward. Not every chase
  or expulsion is an act of urging. [進](進.md) can carry work
  forward or hasten it in 進める すすめる. This loose English
  progress/prompting comparison does not mean that advancing something
  always means urging a person. [責](責.md) usually concerns blame,
  but 責める せめる also has a separately recorded urge/press/pester
  sense. Urging someone does not necessarily mean blaming them.
- Urging by persuasion connects with [説](説.md), whose persuasion
  branch means explaining to win compliance. This is not the same as
  every theory or explanation.
- [詰](詰.md), pack tightly/press, includes demanding an explanation;
  [圧](圧.md), pressure/oppress, includes pressure on people.
  [押](押.md), push, is kept apart because English can also speak of
  pushing someone to act. [強](強.md) includes forcing or compelling.
  Not all pressure is physical, and not every act of urging is coercion,
  oppression or interrogation.
- A demand for action overlaps the requesting in [求](求.md) and
  [頼](頼.md). [需](需.md) also displays English "demand," but
  economic demand or need is not the same activity as urging someone.
  [願](願.md), wish/petition, includes requests, and [募](募.md),
  recruit/solicit, includes seeking contributions or participants.
  These request-related answers are kept apart without equating a
  private wish, a recruitment drive and a demand for timely action.
  [要](要.md) has a requesting sense as well as necessity/essentials;
  [招](招.md) summons or invites, and [呼](呼.md) calls to someone.
  These conservative request/call comparisons do not make every
  invitation or call a demand.
- [禁](禁.md), prohibit/ban, also concerns restricting conduct.
  Regulating may enforce a restriction; it does not mean that every
  instruction or request is a prohibition.
  [規](規.md) also includes bringing something within rules.
  [政](政.md) concerns governing public affairs. Administrative-role
  comparisons with [公](公.md), public/official, and [府](府.md),
  urban prefecture/administration, are conservative: an official,
  an administrative institution and the activity of overseeing differ.
- English "direct" can mean supervise, whereas [直](直.md)
  concerns directness or putting something right. These comparisons are
  excluded from meaning choices without equating the Japanese senses
  or licensing arbitrary substitution between complete words.
