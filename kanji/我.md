# 我 — self; I; ego

**Strokes:** 7 · **Key (radical):** 戈 (no. 62), at the right · **JLPT:** N1 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

我 is the self: the speaker as "I" or "we," and the ego that insists on its own way. [我々](../words/我々.md) われわれ means we or our group, and [我慢](../words/我慢.md) がまん is endurance or self-restraint. [怪我](../words/怪我.md) けが, an injury, also writes 我, but its characters spell the sound of the word rather than its meaning (see Readings). JMdict glosses the pronoun 我 われ "I; me" and the noun 我 が "obstinacy."

The Daijisen character entry gives three senses for ガ. The first is the self, as in 我田引水 がでんいんすい, "seeking one's own interests; straining (the interpretation or argument) to suit one's own interests; drawing water for one's own field," 自我 じが, "ego," 彼我 ひが, "self and others; this and that; both sides," 忘我 ぼうが, "trance; ecstasy; enthusiasm," and 没我 ぼつが, "selflessness." The second is putting oneself first, as in 我意 がい, "self-will; obstinacy," 我執 がしゅう, "egotism; obstinacy," and 我流 がりゅう, "self-taught method; one's own way; one's own style." The third is the essential self in Indian philosophy, the atman, as in 大我 たいが, "absolute ego."

**Modern-shape mnemonic, not etymology:** at the left, find a shape like 扌, the side form of [手](手.md), "hand," topped by a short slanting stroke, and at the right find 戈, the key, which has no card yet; the long horizontal stroke runs through both parts. Imagine a hand gripping a halberd and saying "this one is me." This is a memory aid only: it makes no claim that 手 or 戈 contributes meaning or sound. Historical formation is not established here.

In 我慢, 我 supplies ガ and 慢 supplies マン. In 我々, 我 carries われ and 々, a repetition mark, repeats it, giving われわれ. 怪我 is different: JMdict marks the spelling as ateji, kanji chosen to write the native word けが for their sounds, so the whole word is read けが and it gives 我 no reading of its own; the Daijisen character entry lists it among its hard-to-read words. In 我が, 我 writes わ and が is okurigana, the kana ending that follows the kanji; 我が国 adds 国, read くに. In 我輩, 我 alone writes わが and 輩 supplies ハイ. The trailing dash in わ.が- and わが- marks a prefix use, one that comes before another element; it is not pronounced.

The official Joyo table, the list of standard-use readings, shows ガ, with 我流, 彼我 and 自我 as examples; われ, with 我, 我々 and 我ら; and わ, with 我が国. It has no separate rows for わ.が- and わが-. Practice selects ガ and われ, the readings with linked word articles; わ, わ.が- and わが- are retained from KANJIDIC2 and illustrated with dictionary words. Being outside the practice set is not proof that a reading is invalid.

## Notes

- The other kanji in the common words are [慢](慢.md), "arrogance; slackness," in 我慢 and [怪](怪.md), "suspicious; uncanny," in 怪我, where both characters only spell the sound of けが. 我々 is 我 followed by the repetition mark 々, which is not a kanji with a card.
- [義](義.md), "righteousness; duty; meaning," has 我 at the bottom; [成](成.md), "become," has the same key 戈; and [画](画.md), "picture, brushstroke," is also read ガ. All are different kanji.
- [私](私.md), "I; private," [自](自.md), "self, oneself," and [身](身.md), "one's body; oneself," overlap the senses "I" and "self" that 我 and its words carry; 私 is the everyday word for "I," and 自 is the first kanji of 自我, "ego." They are kept out of this card's quiz options. Do not treat "I" or "self" as exclusive to any one character.
