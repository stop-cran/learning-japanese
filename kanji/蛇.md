# 蛇 — snake; serpent

**Strokes:** 11 · **Key (radical):** 虫 (no. 142), at the left · **JLPT:** N1 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

蛇 means **snake or serpent**. It also appears in words involving a
snake-like shape or course, such as [蛇行](../words/蛇行.md) だこう,
meandering. Learn each compound rather than translating every 蛇 literally.

**Modern-shape mnemonic, not etymology:** see [虫](虫.md) beside the
roof-and-curled-lower shape of 它. Imagine a snake sheltering under that
roof. This is a memory aid for the present shape, not a historical or
phonetic analysis.

## Readings

The full reading list is retained from KANJIDIC2. Reading questions
practise ジャ, ダ and へび; the other on-readings are explained below
but are not quizzed.

| Reading | Type | Typical words |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ジャ | on | [蛇口](../words/蛇口.md) じゃぐち — faucet; tap; 蛇 supplies ジャ, while 口 uses native ぐち |
| ダ | on | [蛇行](../words/蛇行.md) だこう — meandering; snaking; じゃこう is also a documented whole-word reading |
| イ | on | 委蛇 いい / いだ — winding; an obsolete word in JMdict, included for recognition |
| ヤ | on | KANJIDIC2 source-only information: no verified word example here; not quizzed |
| へび | kun | [蛇](../words/蛇.md) へび — snake; the native animal noun |

Kanjipedia explicitly lists 委蛇 (イイ), supporting 蛇's イ reading.
委蛇 is not the everyday adjective いい, "good." Its dictionary construction
uses と adverbially and たる before a noun, with classical たり forms;
it is not taught here as a な-adjective. Kanjipedia lists タ where this
KANJIDIC2 reading list used here includes ヤ. That source difference does not
license replacing or dropping a reading.

## Notes

[虫](虫.md), insect/bug, is the left component, not the meaning of 蛇
as a whole. [蚊](蚊.md), mosquito, has 文 on the right instead of 它;
[蛍](蛍.md), firefly, has 虫 below its upper cover rather than at the left.
Those insect labels do not mean "snake."

The source gloss "hard drinker" has a separate dictionary anchor:
the noun 蛇 read **じゃ** can mean a heavy drinker, shortened from
蛇之助 じゃのすけ. This does not make ordinary animal **へび**
a neutral label for a drunk person. Drinking habit, present intoxication
and alcohol-use disorder are not interchangeable concepts.
