# 逮 — arrest; chase

**Strokes:** 11 · **Key (radical):** 辵 (as 辶, no. 162) · **JLPT:** N1 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

KANJIDIC2 glosses 逮 as apprehend and chase. In [逮捕](../words/逮捕.md) たいほ, the whole word means arrest or taking someone into custody.

**Modern-shape mnemonic, not etymology:** imagine following the right-hand stack along the sweeping 辶 until someone is caught. This is a visual memory aid, not a claim about historical formation.

## Notes

- Separate character sources list ダイ and テイ as non-Joyo on-readings and およぶ as a kun reading. These source-specific readings do not replace the pinned metadata or the selected practice reading.
- [捕](捕.md) means capture, while [追](追.md) means chase or pursue. An arrest and a chase are not the same event. [循](循.md) includes following a course or sequence; following after someone and following a course overlap as English labels without making the Japanese words interchangeable.
- A character source also gives reaching: [届](届.md), [至](至.md), [到](到.md), [達](達.md), and [着](着.md) include reaching or arriving. Their whole words differ in use.
- [留](留.md) includes detaining or keeping in custody; an arrest and subsequent detention are related but not identical.
- English "arrest" can mean stop or slow a process, overlapping the labels of [止](止.md) and [停](停.md); [収](収.md) can include suppressing or settling and [凌](凌.md) can include staving off a difficulty. This is an English-label precaution, not a general Japanese stopping sense for 逮 or 逮捕.
- [取](取.md) includes taking or catching; [握](握.md) includes gripping and seizing power, while [持](持.md) includes holding. [得](得.md) includes acquiring or attaining. These are limited comparisons with apprehension or reaching, not claims that all of their uses mean arrest.
- [接](接.md) and [触](触.md) include touch or contact. A character source describes reaching an opponent and capturing them; the precaution does not establish a general meaning of touching, joining, or social contact for 逮.
- [休](休.md), [人](人.md), and [本](本.md) are loose visual contrasts rather than the closest shapes. Their distinct on-readings also provide choices in the starter pool.
