# 峠 — mountain pass; peak; climax

**Strokes:** 9 · **Key (radical):** 山 (no. 46), on the left · **JLPT:** N1 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

峠 is a mountain pass: the highest point of a road over a mountain, where the climb ends and the descent begins. By extension it is the peak or the worst point of something, the moment after which things ease. On its own, [峠](../words/峠.md) とうげ is the word for a pass; JMdict glosses it "(mountain) pass; highest point on a mountain road; ridge" and, in a second sense, "peak (e.g. of summer); worst (e.g. of an illness); crisis; critical point; most difficult part."

峠 is a 国字 こくじ, a kanji created in Japan; JMdict glosses 国字 "kanji created in Japan (as opposed to China); Japanese-made kanji." It has no on reading. The Daijisen entry for the word 峠 says the word is a sound change of たむけ, 手向け, "offering to a deity or someone's spirit," because travellers made offerings at the top of a pass to 道祖神 どうそじん, the "traveler's guardian deity." It gives two senses: the point where a mountain road stops climbing and starts down, with the example 峠道 とうげみち, "road over a mountain pass"; and the height of something's force, as in 病気は今夜が峠だ, "tonight is the crisis of the illness."

**Modern-shape mnemonic, not etymology:** find 山, "mountain," on the left, and on the right [上](上.md), "up, above," standing on [下](下.md), "down, below." On a mountain pass the road goes up, then down. This is a memory aid only: it makes no claim about how the character was formed or that 山, 上 or 下 contributes meaning. Historical formation is not established here.

## Readings

| Reading | Type | Typical words |
| --- | --- | --- |
| とうげ | kun | [峠](../words/峠.md) とうげ — mountain pass; critical peak |

In the word 峠, the kanji alone is read とうげ, with no okurigana, the kana ending that follows a kanji. In 峠越え とうげごえ, "crossing a mountain pass," the こ of こえ becomes ご.

The official Joyo table, the list of standard-use readings, shows とうげ, with 峠 and 峠道 as examples. KANJIDIC2 gives no on reading. Practice selects とうげ, the reading with a linked word article.

## Notes

- 峠 is used on its own as the word とうげ, so it has no other kanji in its common word.
- The set phrases 峠を越す とうげをこす and 峠を越える とうげをこえる mean "to pass the peak (of something); to get through the most difficult part; to be over the worst (e.g. an illness)."
- [岸](岸.md), "shore; riverbank," and [島](島.md), "island," also contain 山, at the top of 岸 and at the bottom of 島. [上](上.md), "up, above," is the top right part of 峠. All are different kanji.
- [頂](頂.md), "summit; receive," [山](山.md), "mountain," [旬](旬.md), "ten days; peak season," [通](通.md), "pass through; commute," and [経](経.md), "pass through; sutra," overlap the senses "pass," "peak" and "mountain" that 峠 and its word carry. They are kept out of this card's quiz options. Do not treat "pass" or "peak" as exclusive to any one character.
