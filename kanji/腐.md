# 腐 — rot; decay; spoil

**Strokes:** 14 · **Key (radical):** 肉 (no. 130) · **JLPT:** N1 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

腐 describes something breaking down or going bad: food can spoil, and wood or other material can decay or corrode. The verb 腐る (くさる) is intransitive—the thing that deteriorates is what rots. The character also appears in compounds for something stale or no longer useful, such as 陳腐 (ちんぷ), “stale” or “hackneyed.”

**Modern-shape mnemonic, not etymology:** [府](府.md), "urban prefecture; administration," covers the top, with its roof stroke running down the left side, and [肉](肉.md), "meat; flesh," sits beneath that roof. Imagine meat forgotten in a government office until it rots. This is a memory aid only: it makes no claim that 府 or 肉 contributes meaning or sound. Historical formation is not established here.

## Notes

The Jōyō table and Digital Daijisen list フ, くさる, くされる, and くさらす. KANJIDIC2 also records the suffix reading -くさ.る, the noun form くさ.れ (from くされる), and くさ.す; Kanjipedia marks くさす as outside the Jōyō readings. The source-only forms are kept distinct from the ordinary intransitive verb. For example, 腐らす (くさらす) is transitive, while 腐す (くさす) is a separate verb meaning “speak ill of.”

JMdict glosses 腐る as “to rot; to go bad; to decay; to spoil; to fester; to decompose; to turn sour (e.g. milk),” and KANJIDIC2 lists “sour” for 腐. That overlaps with the displayed title of 酸 (“acid; sour”), so 酸 is excluded from this card's meaning quiz.

The meaning-quiz distractors are 焼 (“bake; burn”), 洗 (“wash”), and 煮 (“boil; simmer; stew”); none describes rotting or decay.
