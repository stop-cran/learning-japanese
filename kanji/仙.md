# 仙 — hermit; immortal

**Strokes:** 5 · **Key (radical):** 人 (no. 9) · **JLPT:** N1 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

仙 is a person who trained in the mountains and mastered the arts of never growing old and never dying: a hermit or immortal. It can also mean a person free from worldly ties, or a person of rare talent who has mastered an art. It is also used to write cent, the American money unit. KANJIDIC2 lists “hermit”, “wizard” and “cent”. The word [仙人](../words/仙人.md) せんにん first means “immortal mountain wizard (in Taoism); mountain man (esp. a hermit)”.

**Modern-shape mnemonic, not etymology:** 亻 stands on the left, a variant of [人](人.md), "person," and [山](山.md), "mountain," stands on the right. Picture a person who has stood beside the same mountain for so long that the mountain has become home. This is a memory aid only: it makes no claim that 亻 or 山 contributes meaning or sound. Historical formation is not established here.

## Notes

- Joyo lists セン only, with 仙骨, 仙人 and 酒仙. Kanjipedia and Digital Daijisen also give only the on reading セン.
- KANJIDIC2 records the name readings そま and のり, and Digital Daijisen gives のり and ひさ; these are name uses, not quiz readings.

These three cards are excluded from meaning choices:

- [哲](哲.md) (“philosophy; wise; sage”): 仙 can also mean a person of rare talent who has mastered an art.
- [神](神.md) (“deity”): an immortal is pictured as a godlike being.
- [僧](僧.md) (“Buddhist monk; priest”): 仙 names a person who lives apart in the mountains for spiritual practice.

The meaning-quiz distractors are 父 (“father”), 供 (“accompany, offer”), and 生 (“life, birth”); none means a hermit or an immortal.
