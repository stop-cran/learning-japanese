# 女 — woman, female

**Strokes:** 3 · **Key (radical):** 女 (no. 38) · **JLPT:** N5 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

女 is a pictograph of a kneeling figure with crossed hands. As a key it appears in many kanji about women and family: 母, 姉, 妹, 好, 姫, 嫁.
In 好 (like), 女 and 子 (child) are commonly explained as mother and child. Component meanings are a historical layer, not a description of the word today.

## Notes

Opposite: [男](男.md). 女 can read おんな or め; 女の子 is the everyday word for "girl".

**Stroke-order mnemonic: くノ一.** The three strokes of 女, in order, look like the characters く, ノ and 一. Japanese fiction uses this as the word くノ一 (くのいち, "kunoichi"), now popularly "female ninja". The word is a playful spelling of 女 itself; it was a slang term for "woman" in the Edo period, and its "female ninja" meaning was popularised by novelist Yamada Fūtarō in the 1960s. Historians have found no records of female ninja working exactly like the men, so treat the image as a memory aid and pop-culture word, not as history. The first stroke (く) has the bend; then the long diagonal (ノ); the horizontal (一) comes last and crosses both.
