# 括 — tie together; enclose

**Strokes:** 9 · **Key (radical):** 手 (as 扌, no. 64) · **JLPT:** N1 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

括 concerns tying things together or enclosing them. [括弧](../words/括弧.md) かっこ, "brackets; parentheses," encloses whatever is written between its two marks. In 一括 いっかつ, "lumping together; bundle; lump; batch," and 総括 そうかつ, "summarization; summary; generalization," it means gathering separate things into one. The Daijisen character entry also gives a sense of drawing an opening shut, as in 括約筋 かつやくきん, "sphincter (muscle)." The verb 括る くくる covers tying up, bundling and summarizing.

**Modern-shape mnemonic, not etymology:** find 扌, the side form of [手](手.md), "hand," at the left and 舌, "tongue," at the right. Imagine a hand pulling a bundle shut with a cord. This is a memory aid only: it makes no claim that 舌 contributes meaning or sound here. Historical formation is not established here.

## Readings

| Reading | Type | Typical words |
| --- | --- | --- |
| カツ | on | [括弧](../words/括弧.md) かっこ — brackets; parentheses |
| くく.る | kun | 括る くくる - to tie up; to tie together; to bind; to bundle; usually written in kana; not selected for questions here |

In 括弧, 括 supplies カツ and 弧 supplies コ; the つ of カツ becomes a small っ before コ, giving かっこ. In 括る, 括 writes くく and る is okurigana. Practice selects カツ. くく.る is retained from KANJIDIC2 and illustrated with 括る, but that word has no linked article yet, so the reading is not selected for questions. This is a teaching selection, not a claim that くく.る is invalid, obsolete or rare. The Daijisen entry also lists くびれる as a kun reading; KANJIDIC2 does not, so it is not part of the metadata here.

## Notes

- [弧](弧.md), "arc; curved line," is the other kanji in 括弧.
- [活](活.md), "lively," and [話](話.md), "talk, speak," also have 舌 at the right, but 活 has 氵 at the left and 話 has 言. [抜](抜.md), "extract," shares 扌 at the left but not 舌.
- [結](結.md), "tie; join; conclude," and [束](束.md), "bundle," overlap with 括 in the ideas of tying or binding together. Learn the whole word rather than treating these as interchangeable.
