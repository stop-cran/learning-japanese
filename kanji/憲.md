# 憲 — constitution; law

**Strokes:** 16 · **Key (radical):** 心 (no. 61), at the bottom · **JLPT:** N1 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

憲 concerns fundamental rules or a constitution. Start with
[憲法](../words/憲法.md) けんぽう, a country's constitution.
The character's broader rule or law meaning does not make every school
rule a 憲法.

Daijisen also records regulating and officials. 憲兵 けんぺい,
military police, illustrates that branch rather than a kind of
constitution. The complete word decides which sense applies.

**Modern-shape mnemonic, not etymology:** find the roof 宀 at the top,
a 罒-shaped block in the middle, and [心](心.md) at the bottom.
Imagine keeping fundamental rules at the heart of a household.
This is a memory picture, not a claim about historical formation.
The indexing key is 心, not the upper roof.

## Readings

| Reading | Type | Typical words |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ケン | on | [憲法](../words/憲法.md) けんぽう - constitution |

Practice selects ケン, also the Joyo reading. Learn the complete word
けんぽう, including ぽう for [法](法.md) in this compound.
JMdict separately marks けんぼう as an obsolete reading of 憲法;
it is not the reading taught by the linked article.

Daijisen additionally lists のり as a kun reading. It is outside this
card's KANJIDIC2 reading list and is not selected for practice;
nonselection does not make it invalid or exclusively a name reading.

## Notes

- [寄](寄.md), draw near/stop by, [害](害.md), harm, and
  [寒](寒.md), cold weather, share a roof-like top but have different
  lower parts. None has 憲's complete middle-and-heart arrangement.
- Law and rule meanings overlap [法](法.md), [律](律.md),
  [規](規.md), [則](則.md), [制](制.md), [典](典.md) and
  [準](準.md). Their compounds are not interchangeable: a legal
  constitution differs from a method, measuring standard or ceremony.
- The regulatory or official branch overlaps [官](官.md),
  [監](監.md) and [督](督.md). [公](公.md) also displays English
  "official," but its public/official adjective differs from a person
  holding office. These comparisons are excluded from meaning choices;
  they do not turn every official into a constitution.
- JMdict also gives 立憲 an abbreviated political-party sense. The
  constitutionalism gloss is not a translation for every occurrence.
- English "constitution" can describe physical makeup or health.
  That is not the governmental meaning taught here.
