# 憲 — constitution; law

**Strokes:** 16 · **Key (radical):** 心 (no. 61), at the bottom · **JLPT:** N1 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

憲 concerns fundamental rules or a constitution. Start with
[憲法](../words/憲法.md) けんぽう, a country's constitution.
The character's broader rule or law meaning does not make every school
rule a 憲法.

Daijisen also records regulating and officials. 憲兵 けんぺい,
military police, illustrates that branch rather than a kind of
constitution. The complete word decides which sense applies.

**Modern-shape mnemonic, not etymology:** find the roof 宀 at the top,
a 罒-shaped block in the middle, and [心](心.md) at the bottom.
Imagine keeping fundamental rules at the heart of a household.
This is a memory picture, not a claim about historical formation.
The indexing key is 心, not the upper roof.

## Readings

| Reading | Type | Typical words |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ケン | on | [憲法](../words/憲法.md) けんぽう - constitution |

Practice selects ケン, also the Joyo reading. Learn the complete word
けんぽう, including ぽう for [法](法.md) in this compound.
JMdict separately marks けんぼう as an obsolete reading of 憲法;
it is not the reading taught by the linked article.

Daijisen additionally lists のり as a kun reading. KANJIDIC2 records
it as a name reading, not in its on/kun list. It is not selected for
practice; nonselection does not make it invalid or exclusively a name
reading across dictionaries.

## Notes

- [寄](寄.md), draw near/stop by, [害](害.md), harm, and
  [寒](寒.md), cold weather, share a roof-like top but have different
  lower parts. None has 憲's complete middle-and-heart arrangement.
- Law and rule meanings overlap [法](法.md), [律](律.md),
  [規](規.md), [則](則.md), [制](制.md), [典](典.md) and
  [準](準.md). Their compounds are not interchangeable: a legal
  constitution differs from a method, measuring standard or ceremony.
- Rule, standard or legal-text uses also occur in [例](例.md),
  [式](式.md), [格](格.md), [度](度.md), [程](程.md),
  [定](定.md) and [章](章.md). The legal uses of 式 and 格 include
  historical codes; this does not replace their familiar ceremony,
  formula, rank or character senses, or add practice readings.
  Agreements in [約](約.md) and precepts or directions in [訓](訓.md)
  are also kept apart conservatively. Not every promise or instruction
  is a law.
- [令](令.md), command/decree, has its own rule or law sense.
  [勅](勅.md), imperial order, and [命](命.md), life/command,
  are kept apart for related prescriptive answers, not their unrelated
  senses. An imperial order, an ordinary command and a constitution
  are not the same kind of enactment.
- [禁](禁.md), prohibit/ban, also has a rule restricting conduct
  sense. A constitution is not simply a ban; this direct comparison is
  kept out of meaning choices rather than treating the words as synonyms.
- The regulatory or official branch overlaps [官](官.md),
  [監](監.md) and [督](督.md). [僚](僚.md) is titled colleague, but
  Daijisen also gives it an official sense, as in 官僚 かんりょう,
  bureaucrat; a coworker is not thereby a regulator. [公](公.md) also
  displays English "official," but its public/official adjective
  differs from a person holding office. These comparisons are excluded
  from meaning choices; they do not turn every official into a
  constitution.
- [管](管.md) also has a regulating sense. Governing, managing or
  coordinating uses of [治](治.md), [御](御.md), [統](統.md),
  [理](理.md) and [政](政.md) are kept apart as related control
  comparisons, not general synonyms for law. [警](警.md) can abbreviate
  police or a police officer, and [守](守.md) occurs in historical
  local-official titles. Warning, guarding and holding a particular
  office are still distinct activities or roles.
- JMdict also gives 立憲 an abbreviated political-party sense. The
  constitutionalism gloss is not a translation for every occurrence.
- English "constitution" can describe physical makeup or health.
  That is not the governmental meaning taught here. [体](体.md),
  body/form, [質](質.md), quality/substance, [性](性.md), nature/gender,
  and [構](構.md), build/stance, are kept out of meaning choices for
  this English makeup/structure ambiguity, not because 憲 has those
  Japanese senses.
