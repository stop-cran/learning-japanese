# 剣 — sword; double-edged blade

**Strokes:** 10 · **Key (radical):** 刀 (no. 18), appearing as 刂 on the right · **JLPT:** N1 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

剣 concerns a sword or blade, especially a double-edged sword. The word [剣](../words/剣.md) つるぎ can also name swords more broadly; do not treat "double-edged" as an exceptionless definition.

**Modern-shape mnemonic, not etymology:** Picture a sword beside a pointed roof, a horizontal line and a [口](口.md)-shaped box, with spreading strokes below. The sword-shaped part is the right-hand 刂. This invented scene helps distinguish the modern shape; it is not a historical or phonetic explanation.

## Notes

- The indexing radical is [刀](刀.md), visible here as 刂. [剛](剛.md), "sturdy; unyielding strength," [剖](剖.md), "dissect; split open," and [則](則.md), "rule; follow a model," share right-hand 刂 but have different left blocks and meanings.
- [刀](../words/刀.md) かたな especially suggests a Japanese single-edged sword; 剣 especially suggests a double-edged one. Broader sword uses overlap, so this is not a strict division of all swords.
- [刃](../words/刃.md) は focuses on the blade or cutting edge, rather than naming the whole sword.
- 真剣 usually describes earnest engagement in the examples on its word card. Do not translate every occurrence literally as a weapon, or use it for every English sense of "serious."
