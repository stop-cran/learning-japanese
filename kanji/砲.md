# 砲 — cannon; gun

**Strokes:** 10 · **Key (radical):** 石 (no. 112), on the left · **JLPT:** N1 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

砲 names a gun, cannon or artillery. [鉄砲](../words/鉄砲.md) てっぽう
means a gun or firearm, while 大砲 たいほう is a cannon.
The character is not restricted to one modern weapon model.

There are established extensions, not just military uses. Daijisen
also gives 砲 a baseball heavy-hitter or home-run sense. JMdict records
sumo practice-pole and colloquial pufferfish senses for the whole word
鉄砲; those are not free translations of every bare 砲.

**Modern-shape mnemonic, not etymology:** find [石](石.md) beside
[包](包.md). Imagine a stone-like round object enclosed in a display
case beside an old cannon. This is a shape-memory scene, not a
historical account of weapons or of the character's components.

## Readings

| Reading | Type | Typical words |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ホウ | on | [鉄砲](../words/鉄砲.md) てっぽう - gun; テツ + ホウ changes in this compound |

Practice selects ホウ, also the Joyo reading. 鉄砲 has a small っ and
ぽう; do not create independent metadata readings テッ or ポウ.
JMdict also recognizes an archaic つつ family with 銃 and 砲 spellings.
That recognition example does not change this frozen reading inventory
or make つつ an alternative reading of every 砲 compound.

## Common words

- [鉄砲](../words/鉄砲.md) てっぽう - gun; firearm
- 大砲 たいほう - cannon; also a baseball long-ball hitter
- 砲撃 ほうげき - bombardment; shelling
- 砲台 ほうだい - gun emplacement; artillery battery

## Notes

- [包](包.md), wrap/enclose, is the right-hand shape.
  [砂](砂.md), sand, and [硬](硬.md), hard/stiff, share 石 but have
  different right sides and meanings.
- [射](射.md) and [撃](撃.md) concern shooting or striking, rather
  than simply naming the gun. [兵](兵.md) and [軍](軍.md) concern
  troops; [武](武.md) concerns military or martial matters. Sharing
  this subject does not make all of their labels synonyms for 砲.
- The separate name reading づつ in KANJIDIC2 is not a lexical quiz
  reading. Historical spelling 鉄炮 is not the spelling practiced here.
