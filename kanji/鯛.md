# 鯛 — sea bream

**Strokes:** 19 · **Key (radical):** 魚 (no. 195), on the left · **JLPT:** N1 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

鯛 names **sea bream**, especially the red sea bream called 真鯛 (まだい). It is not a general word for every red fish or every fish sold as "snapper" in English.

**Modern-shape mnemonic, not etymology:** Find [魚](魚.md), fish, on the left and [周](周.md), circuit/circumference, on the right. Imagine a sea bream swimming a circuit. This is a memory aid for the visible parts, not a historical explanation.

The source's **grade-9** classification concerns name-use kanji, not a ninth school grade. This lesson teaches a non-name animal noun.

## Notes

- **たい** and **タイ** also write this native animal name. JMdict labels it usually written in kana; katakana does not make it a foreign loanword.
- In 真鯛 (まだい), the second part is pronounced **だい**, a voiced form of たい. Learn the whole word; this does not make だい the standalone noun's reading.
- [魚](魚.md), fish, is the broader category. [鯉](鯉.md) こい, carp, and [鮎](鮎.md) あゆ, ayu, are different fish, not synonyms.
- [周](周.md) is the right-hand shape alone. [調](調.md), investigate/tune, adds 言 on the left; [週](週.md), week, has the movement-shaped outer part. Their meanings are not sea bream.
- A longer name containing 鯛 need not name a sea bream: 金目鯛 (きんめだい) is splendid alfonsino. Likewise, たい焼き names a shaped sweet, not grilled fish.
