# 脈 — pulse; vein

**Strokes:** 10 · **Key (radical):** 肉 (no. 130) · **JLPT:** N1 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

脈 first names a blood vessel and the pulse felt there. It can also describe a connected line, such as a mountain range, or the flow that connects ideas. In 文脈 (ぶんみゃく), it means the context or line of thought that helps a passage make sense; it does not mean a physical pulse there. A further figurative sense is a prospect or hope.

## Notes

The Jōyō table and Digital Daijisen list ミャク; Kanjipedia also records バク and the non-Jōyō kun reading すじ. Digital Daijisen lists 水脈 as a difficult whole-word reading, みお; that compound reading is not a standalone reading of 脈 and does not establish a quiz answer. The source-only readings are retained in the table for completeness, not presented as ordinary or selected readings.

JMdict gives “hope” and “thread (of an argument)” as senses of 脈, and Digital Daijisen describes the word 脈 as a future prospect or hope (先の望み・見込み). Kanjipedia gives すじ (line; sinew) and すじみち (line of reasoning) among its meanings. These overlap with the displayed titles of 望 (“hope”), 希 (“hope; rare”), 筋 (“muscle; sinew; line; plot”), and 糸 (“thread; yarn”), so those cards are excluded from meaning choices.

The meaning-quiz distractors are 目 (“eye”), 手 (“hand”), and 足 (“foot, leg”); none means a pulse, vein, or connected line.
