# 行 — go

**Strokes:** 6 · **Key (radical):** 行 (no. 144) · **Phonetic:** none · **JLPT:** N5 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

行 is commonly explained as a pictograph of a crossroads: the oldest forms show two roads crossing. The left and right halves later
became independent components, 彳 (a step, as in 待 and 径) and 亍. From "road" the meanings extend to "go", "travel" and "carry out"
(おこなう), and then to "a line or row" (as in a line of text, 一行 いちぎょう) and "a business" (as in 銀行, bank).
Because 行 is its own radical, it is both a key and a character.

## Notes

- The kun reading is い.く in everyday speech; ゆく is literary or fixed (行方 ゆくえ, 行く末 ゆくすえ). 行 plus 〜き gives 行き (bound for): 東京行き とうきょうゆき／とうきょういき.
- 行く is irregular in the て／た form: 行って, 行った (not 行いて).
- Special on readings: コウ is the usual one in compounds (旅行, 銀行), ギョウ is for "line/event" (行事), アン for a few Buddhist or old words.
- Related: [来](来.md) (come, the opposite direction of travel in deixis) and [出](出.md) (go out).
