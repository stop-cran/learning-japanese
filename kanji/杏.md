# 杏 — apricot

**Strokes:** 7 · **Key (radical):** 木 (no. 75) · **JLPT:** N1 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

杏 writes **apricot**, [杏](../words/杏.md) あんず, the tree and fruit. The plant is *Prunus armeniaca*, distinct from [梅](../words/梅.md) うめ, which is also called Japanese apricot in English.

**Modern-shape mnemonic, not etymology:** Notice [木](木.md) above [口](口.md). Imagine an apricot tree above an open mouth ready for its fruit. This is a memory aid based on the visible shapes, not a historical explanation.

## Notes

- This card asks the standalone plant noun あんず. KANJIDIC2 lists it as kun, but the word comes from Chinese-derived readings of 杏子; a character's kun classification does not by itself establish a word's native Japanese origin. キョウ and アン are retained for compound recognition, including both dictionary readings of 杏仁豆腐; they are not substitutes for あんず in the standalone fruit noun. コウ remains source-only, not a question.
- 杏仁 refers to the kernel, not the fruit's flesh. Recognizing its use in a prepared dessert is not advice to eat raw apricot kernels.
- 銀杏 has the whole-word readings いちょう for the ginkgo tree and ぎんなん for its nut. Neither supplies a new standalone reading of 杏.
- Compare [李](李.md), Japanese plum: it has 子 below 木, whereas 杏 has 口. [古](古.md), old, has 十 above 口; [村](村.md), village, places 寸 beside 木, and [相](相.md), mutual or aspect, places 目 beside 木.
