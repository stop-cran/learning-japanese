# 麟 — qilin; mythical animal

**Strokes:** 24 · **Key (radical):** 鹿 (no. 198) · **JLPT:** N1 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

麟 is associated with the **qilin**, an imaginary animal in Chinese tradition.
Learn it in [麒麟](../words/麒麟.md) (きりん), which also names the living
giraffe. Context distinguishes the mythical creature from the real animal.
The character can also represent an exceptionally gifted person; this is
figurative praise, not a claim that the person is an animal.

**Modern-shape mnemonic, not etymology:** Find [鹿](鹿.md), "deer," on the
left and the dense component 粦 (リン) on the right. Notice the
[米](米.md)-like top of 粦 and the paired lower shapes; picture a decorated
deer-like creature. This is a memory aid for the current shape, not a
historical account or a definition of every compound.

This card practises **リン**, the second part of キ + リン in 麒麟.
The frozen KANJIDIC2 metadata has no kun-reading. Kanjipedia additionally
lists きりん as a kun-reading of 麟; that dictionary difference is retained
as reference information, not added as a kun question here. It does not
change the compound's reading きりん.

## Common words

- [麒麟](../words/麒麟.md) (きりん) — giraffe, usually written キリン for
  the living animal; also the mythical qilin
- 麒麟児 (きりんじ) — child prodigy; a gifted young person with promise

The second item is a recognition expression, not a requirement to use it
as casual praise. The word article also explains the gifted-person sense
of bare 麒麟, which is not restricted to children by that compound's 児.

## Notes

- [鹿](鹿.md) alone means "deer," not giraffe or qilin. 米 means "rice
  grain"; [舞](舞.md), "dance," has somewhat similar spreading lower
  shapes but a different upper part. These are shape comparisons, not synonyms.
- This card uses the pinned **24-stroke** form. KANJIDIC2 also records
  23 strokes; Kanjipedia separately labels a 23-stroke old form. Do not
  omit a stroke from the displayed form to force it to match that alternate count.
- Grade 9 is the source's name-use classification, not a claim that every
  occurrence is a personal name. Name readings are outside this lesson.
