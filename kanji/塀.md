# 塀 — boundary wall; fence

**Strokes:** 12 · **Key (radical):** 土 (no. 32) · **JLPT:** N1 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

[塀](../words/塀.md) へい names a boundary wall or fence, such as one around
a house or garden. The noun does not prescribe one material: earth, boards,
stone and concrete can all occur. Contrast an enclosing property boundary
with a room's wall; this is a useful distinction, not a universal material rule.

**Modern-shape mnemonic, not etymology:** imagine 土 as the ground beside
a 尸-shaped cover over a 并-shaped pair of fence panels. These are visual
landmarks in the present outline, not the historical meaning of those parts.

## Notes

- JMdict also records 屏 as a spelling of the へい noun. That is a
  word-specific spelling relationship, not permission to replace every
  screen or wall character with 塀.
- 塀の中 へいのなか can refer to being inside prison. This is a separate
  phrase-level metonym; it does not make every 塀 a prison.
- [囲](囲.md) includes an enclosure or fence. [境](境.md), [界](界.md),
  [辺](辺.md) and [畔](畔.md) concern boundaries or edges; [関](関.md)
  and [障](障.md) include barriers. Direct quiz guards protect these
  broad labels without equating a field ridge, a checkpoint and a wall.
  [包](包.md) and [括](括.md) include enclosing; [標](標.md) has a
  separately recorded cordoning-off noun. An act, sign and physical
  fence need not be interchangeable.
- [古](古.md), old, [右](右.md), right, and [石](石.md), stone, do not
  name the boundary structure. A wall being old or made of stone does
  not turn its name into either property or material.
- Digital Daijisen and KANJIDIC2 classify 塀 as a Japanese-created
  character; 字通 gives a phonosemantic account involving historical
  forms. Some glyphs in the captured explanation are images missing
  from extracted text. The discrepancy is retained, not resolved by
  guessing the glyphs or promoting the mnemonic into etymology.
