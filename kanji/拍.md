# 拍 — clap; musical beat

**Strokes:** 8 · **Key (radical):** 手 (no. 64) · **JLPT:** N1 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

拍 concerns striking or clapping and keeping a beat. In
[拍手](../words/拍手.md) はくしゅ it contributes the clapping action,
while 手 identifies the hands. The noun 拍 はく can name a musical beat
and, in linguistics, a mora. A mora is a timing unit, not necessarily
the same unit as a syllable.

**Modern-shape mnemonic, not etymology:** imagine the hand-shaped 扌
beside 白 keeping a visible beat. The indexing radical is 手; 扌 is its
left-side form. This invented picture is not an account of the early character.

## Readings

| ヒョウ | on | 拍子 ひょうし, musical time or rhythm. This verified whole-word example has no linked support article here, so ヒョウ is recognition material. |

The full ハク・ヒョウ inventory and empty kun list remain unchanged.
字通 separately records うつ・たたく・はやし, and Weblio's Wiktionary
section includes ヒャク. These source-positive supplemental forms are
not additional selected practice readings.

## Common words

- [拍手](../words/拍手.md) はくしゅ - applause; clapping, also ritual
  hand-clapping in the appropriate context.
- 拍子 ひょうし - musical time, rhythm or beat; its moment/chance senses
  belong to the whole word.
- 心拍 しんぱく - heartbeat. The contextual ぱく does not replace ハク
  in the full character-reading inventory.

## Notes

- JMdict places 拍つ うつ in the complete 打つ/拍つ/搏つ/撲つ/擣つ
  family and flags the 拍つ spelling as rarely used. That family also
  contains sending, marking, injecting, throwing, paying, making,
  performing, visiting and other senses without an explicit allocation
  to every spelling. They remain source evidence, not a recommendation
  to replace ordinary 打つ with 拍つ in all of them. Conservative direct
  guards protect the unresolved broad choices; they do not assert that
  bare 拍 ordinarily means each action.
- [記](記.md), [識](識.md) and [筆](筆.md) also receive direct
  precautions for that family's unallocated writing/marking senses.
  識 has a real secondary writing sense, not only knowledge, and 筆
  names writing as well as the implement. These guards do not establish
  ordinary bare 拍 as a replacement for writing verbs.
- 字通 also gives keeping time, cheering and wooden clappers. Complete
  comparison families record wooden clappers under [木](木.md), and
  under search-only 析 in the 柝 family. [板](板.md), [盤](盤.md) and
  the broad music/time labels receive precautions too. A clapper is
  not all wood, and a beat is not a fixed number of seconds.
- [打](打.md), [撃](撃.md) and [当](当.md) include striking.
  [突](突.md) also has 突く つく, to hit or strike, beyond thrusting
  and suddenness; its direct guard does not equate every thrust with
  clapping. [鳴](鳴.md)'s 鳴らす ならす includes beating or snapping
  fingers. [響](響.md) instead concerns sound carrying or resonating,
  and an effect reaching someone: neither defines the clapping action
  or a musical pulse merely because a clap can resound.
  [律](律.md), [調](調.md) and [整](整.md) retain rhythm or metre
  senses even when their first displayed meaning concerns something
  else. A first English label alone cannot settle quiz safety.
- 字通's independent cheering sense motivates precautions with
  [勉](勉.md), whose character meanings include encouraging, and
  [勇](勇.md), which includes cheering up or being in high spirits.
  Exerting effort, being courageous and applauding are not universally
  interchangeable. [乗](乗.md) has 乗り のり, rhythm or getting into
  the spirit, with a kana-preferred qualification in that sense; its
  direct guard does not depend on the unrelated boarding meaning.
- The complete 拍手 かしわで entry has a rarely used 拍手 spelling beside
  柏手. 拍板 びんざさら and 拍板 はくはん are separate instrument
  entries. Neither whole-word reading establishes 拍 as かしわ or びん.
- 字通's old-dictionary quotations preserve オフス, a ヒムササラ
  instrument-name context, ナヅ and ニシル. Their precise historical
  spelling/sense allocation is not resolved here. They are retained as
  qualified lookup evidence, not declared invalid or ordinary modern
  pronunciations.
- [古](古.md), old, [右](右.md), right, and [川](川.md), river, have
  independently different lexical ranges, including their secondary
  nouns and counters; they are not selected merely for different titles.
