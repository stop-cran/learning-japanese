# 午 — noon

**Strokes:** 4 · **Key (radical):** 十 (no. 24) · **JLPT:** N5 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

午 is commonly explained as a pictograph of a pestle or mallet (a long stick with a crossbar). In Chinese it was borrowed as the seventh of the twelve
earthly branches (子丑寅卯辰巳午…), associated with the horse (the reading うま) and with the hours around midday. From there it means "noon".
Whether it was originally a pestle is uncertain; the zodiac and noon meanings are certain.

## Common words

- [午前](../words/午前.md) ごぜん — a.m., morning
- [午後](../words/午後.md) ごご — p.m., afternoon
- 正午 しょうご — noon

## Notes

- 午 appears almost only in 午前 and 午後 in daily use: before noon / after noon.
- **Look-alikes:** 午 vs 牛 (cow, うし): both have a slanted first stroke, two bars and a long vertical; in 午 the vertical starts at the upper bar, but in 牛 it also rises above the upper bar. 午 vs [千](千.md) (thousand): 千 has a slanted stroke on top, a horizontal bar and a vertical stroke, one bar fewer. 午 vs [年](年.md): 年 has 6 strokes.
- 午 is a zodiac character: the ordinary word for horse is 馬 (うま).
