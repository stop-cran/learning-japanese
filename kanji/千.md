# 千 — thousand

**Strokes:** 3 · **Key (radical):** 十 (no. 24) · **Phonetic:** 人 (commonly explained) · **JLPT:** N5 (unofficial list)

## Meaning and origin

千 is commonly explained as 人 (person) with a bar on it, the 人 giving the sound (Old Chinese sounds were close) and the line marking the number thousand.
The modern form looks like [十](十.md) with a tilted top stroke. Explanations differ, so treat any "ten times a hundred" story as a mnemonic.

## Common words

- [千円](../words/千円.md) せんえん — 1,000 yen
- [三千](../words/三千.md) さんぜん — 3,000
- 千人 せんにん — a thousand people
- 千葉 ちば — Chiba (place name; ち + ば, with 葉 は voiced by rendaku)

## Notes

Sound changes: 三千 さんぜん (セン → ゼン), 八千 はっせん. Plain 1,000 is written 千 (千円 せんえん; 一千円 is unusual), unlike 一万円 where 一 is kept.
Look-alikes: [十](十.md) (ten), 干 (dry) and [午](午.md) (noon). To tell them apart: 千 has a slanted stroke on top of a cross; 午 has a slanted stroke plus two bars and a long vertical; 干 has a flat top bar with no slanted stroke.
