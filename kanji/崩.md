# 崩 — crumble; collapse; demolish

**Strokes:** 11 · **Key (radical):** 山 (no. 46), at the top · **JLPT:** N1 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

崩 is crumbling: something solid falling apart, or someone breaking it down. [崩れる](../words/崩れる.md) くずれる is the intransitive verb, something collapses or loses its shape, and [崩す](../words/崩す.md) くずす is the transitive verb, someone demolishes or disturbs it. JMdict glosses 崩れる "to collapse; to crumble" and, in other senses, "to get out of shape; to lose one's shape," "to turn bad (e.g. weather)" and "to break money into small change." It glosses 崩す "to destroy; to demolish; to pull down; to tear down; to level," "to disturb; to put into disorder" and "to break (a bill); to change; to make change."

The Daijisen character entry gives two senses for ホウ. Crumbling or falling down, as in 崩壊 ほうかい, "collapse; crumbling; breaking down; caving in," 崩落 ほうらく, "collapse; break; cave-in; crash," 潰崩 かいほう, which JMdict marks archaic and lists after 壊崩, and the four-character idiom 土崩瓦解 どほうがかい, "complete collapse; (something) going to pieces (breaking up beyond repair)"; and the death of an emperor, as in 崩御 ほうぎょ, which JMdict marks honorific, "death of a sovereign (emperor, empress, king, queen, etc.); demise." It gives the kun readings くずれる and くずす. It also lists 雪崩 なだれ, "avalanche; snowslide," as a hard-to-read word; this is a reading of the whole word, not of either kanji.

**Modern-shape mnemonic, not etymology:** find 山, "mountain," at the top, and under it two [月](月.md), "moon, month," side by side. Imagine a mountain crumbling down onto two moons. This is a memory aid only: it makes no claim that 山 or 月 contributes meaning or sound. Historical formation is not established here.

In 崩れる, 崩 writes くず and れる is okurigana, the kana ending that follows the kanji; 崩す uses the same くず with す. The noun 崩れ くずれ, "crumbling; collapse; ruin," can follow another word, as in 山崩れ, where 山 supplies やま and くずれ keeps its く; 総崩れ そうくずれ, "rout; collapse," and 値崩れ ねくずれ, "price collapse," work the same way. In 崩壊, 崩 supplies ホウ and 壊 supplies カイ. 雪崩 is read なだれ as a whole word, so neither kanji supplies a separate part of that reading.

The official Joyo table, the list of standard-use readings, shows ホウ with 崩壊, くずれる with 崩れる and 山崩れ, and くずす with 崩す, and lists 雪崩 なだれ as a special reading. Practice selects くず.れる and くず.す, the readings with linked word articles, in KANJIDIC2 order; ホウ and the suffix -くず.れ are retained from KANJIDIC2 and illustrated with dictionary words. Being outside the practice set is not proof that a reading is invalid.

## Notes

- 崩れる and 崩す are this kanji with okurigana, so they have no other kanji.
- [岩](岩.md), "rock; boulder," also has 山 at the top, and [島](島.md), "island," has 山 at the bottom. [明](明.md), "bright, clear," has 月 on the right. All are different kanji.
- [壊](壊.md), "break; destroy; demolish," [倒](倒.md), "topple; collapse," [破](破.md), "tear; break," [落](落.md), "fall down," [亡](亡.md), "deceased," [死](死.md), "death," [乱](乱.md), "disorder; disturbance," [替](替.md), "replace; substitute," [換](換.md), "exchange; replace," and [均](均.md), "level; average," overlap the senses of 崩 and its words: collapse and demolition, an emperor's death in 崩御, disorder in 崩す, changing money in 崩れる and 崩す, and levelling in 崩す; 壊 appears with 崩 in 崩壊. They are kept out of this card's quiz options. Do not treat "collapse" or "break" as exclusive to any one character.
