# 伯 — uncle or aunt; eldest; count (noble)

**Strokes:** 7 · **Key (radical):** 人 (no. 9) · **JLPT:** N1 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

伯 means an older brother or sister of a parent, the eldest of brothers and sisters, a chief or leader, a title of respect for a master of an art, and the noble rank of count; in a few words it can also name a god. KANJIDIC2 lists “chief”, “count”, “earl”, “uncle” and also “Brazil”.

**Modern-shape mnemonic, not etymology:** 亻, a variant of [人](人.md), "person," stands on the left, and [白](白.md), "white," stands on the right, with [日](日.md), "sun, day," at its bottom. Picture a white-haired uncle, the eldest of the family, resting in the sun. This is a memory aid only: it makes no claim that 亻 or 白 contributes meaning or sound. Historical formation is not established here.

The official Joyo table lists 伯父 おじ and 伯母 おば as special readings; each is read as a whole word. JMdict entry 1607070 おじ, “uncle”, is written 叔父 or 伯父, with the note “伯父 or 叔父 respectively can mean older or younger than one's parent”, and entry 1607100 おば, “aunt”, is written 叔母 or 伯母. The existing article [伯母さん](../words/伯母さん.md) おばさん uses this spelling. Digital Daijisen also lists 伯父 read はくふ and 伯母 read はくぼ, where 伯 reads ハク; within those JMdict entries, はくふ belongs only to 伯父 and はくぼ only to 伯母.

## Notes

- Joyo lists ハク only, with 伯仲 and 画伯. Kanjipedia lists ハ and the kun はたがしら, かしら and おさ outside the table (外); Digital Daijisen gives the kun かみ.
- KANJIDIC2 records six name readings (いき, えき, か, き, は, ひろ), and Digital Daijisen gives eight (お, おさ, たか, たけ, とも, のり, はか, みち); these are name uses, not quiz readings.

These six cards are excluded from meaning choices:

- [長](長.md) (“long, chief”), [主](主.md) (“master, principal”) and [頭](頭.md) (“head”): 伯 can mean a chief or leader.
- [兄](兄.md) (“older brother”) and [姉](姉.md) (“older sister”): 伯 can mean the eldest of brothers and sisters, or a parent's older brother or sister.
- [神](神.md) (“deity”): in a few words, 伯 can name a god.

The meaning-quiz distractors are 自 (“self, oneself”), 物 (“thing, object”), and 毎 (“every, each”); they name oneself, a thing, and every or each; none means an uncle or aunt, the eldest or a count.
