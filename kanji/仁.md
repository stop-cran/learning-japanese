# 仁 — benevolence; humaneness

**Strokes:** 4 · **Key (radical):** 人 (no. 9) · **JLPT:** N1 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

仁 is kindness and loving care for others, and in Confucian teaching it is the highest virtue. It can also mean a person, and the kernel or seed of a fruit. KANJIDIC2 lists “humanity”, “virtue”, “benevolence”, “charity”, “man” and “kernel”. The word [仁](../words/仁.md) じん first means “benevolence (esp. as a virtue of Confucianism); consideration; compassion”.

**Modern-shape mnemonic, not etymology:** 亻 stands on the left, a variant of [人](人.md), "person," and [二](二.md), "two," stands on the right. Picture one person holding out a kind hand to a second person, so that two people stand together. This is a memory aid only: it makes no claim that 亻 or 二 contributes meaning or sound. Historical formation is not established here.

## Notes

- Joyo lists ジン, with 仁義 and 仁術, and ニ, with 仁王. Kanjipedia gives ニン outside the table (外).
- KANJIDIC2 records fourteen name readings, such as ひと, ひとし and よし, and Digital Daijisen gives twenty, such as めぐみ and やすし; these are name uses, not quiz readings.

These nine cards are excluded from meaning choices:

- [徳](徳.md) (“virtue; benevolence”) and [善](善.md) (“good; virtue; handle well”): 仁 is a virtue, a kind of moral goodness.
- [愛](愛.md) (“love”): 仁 includes loving care for others.
- [恵](恵.md) (“blessing; favor; grace”) and [恩](恩.md) (“favor; debt of gratitude”): 仁 means kindness and charity toward others.
- [優](優.md) (“gentle; superior”): 仁 is kind, gentle care for others.
- [人](人.md) (“person”) and [者](者.md) (“person in a role”): 仁 can also mean a person.
- [種](種.md) (“seed; kind”): 仁 can mean the kernel or seed of a fruit, as in 杏仁 きょうにん, “apricot seed; apricot kernel”.

The meaning-quiz distractors are 円 (“yen, circle”), 万 (“ten thousand”), and 前 (“front, before”); they name a currency or shape, a number and a position; none means benevolence.
