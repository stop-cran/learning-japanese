# 笹 — bamboo grass

**Strokes:** 11 · **Key (radical):** 竹 (no. 118; compressed at the top) · **JLPT:** N1 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

笹 writes **bamboo grass**, [笹](../words/笹.md) ささ. Think of the generally smaller bamboos called sasa, not ordinary lawn grass. [竹](../words/竹.md) たけ can name bamboo more broadly; the distinction is not simply "short versus tall."

The character dictionary identifies 笹 as a **kokuji**, a character created in Japan. Its detailed origin is not settled in the cited 字通 entry.

**Modern-shape mnemonic, not etymology:** Notice the compressed [竹](竹.md) top above a [世](世.md)-shaped lower part. Imagine bamboo leaves spreading across your world. This is a memory aid, not an explanation of why the character was created.

- 熊笹 くまざさ - kuma bamboo grass (*Sasa veitchii*); also written 隈笹

## Notes

- There is no on-reading in the frozen metadata. This lesson asks the native plant reading ささ, not a personal-name reading.
- In 熊笹, the second part is voiced as ざさ. Learn the whole word くまざさ; this does not add a separate metadata reading.
- [苗](苗.md) means seedling or young plant, not bamboo grass specifically. [荷](荷.md) concerns a load or burden and has a different top and lower part. [世](世.md) alone concerns the world or a generation.
- Dictionaries also record an old court-language use of ささ for [酒](../words/酒.md), alcoholic drink. That is not the ordinary plant meaning or a recommendation to order drinks with 笹.
