# 催 — host an event; sponsor

**Strokes:** 13 · **Key (radical):** 人 (as 亻, no. 9) · **JLPT:** N1 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

催 concerns holding or organizing an event, and more broadly bringing something about or prompting it. [催し](../words/催し.md) もよおし is an event that someone organizes, such as an exhibition or a social gathering, and [催促](../words/催促.md) さいそく is pressing someone to act. In 開催 かいさい, "holding (a conference, exhibition, etc.); opening; hosting," and 主催 しゅさい, "sponsorship; promotion; organizing; hosting," 催 supplies the event sense. In 催眠 さいみん, "hypnosis," and 催涙 さいるい, "tear-inducing," it describes inducing a state. The verb 催す もよおす covers both "to hold (an event)" and "to feel (sensation, emotion, call of nature, etc.); to show signs of." Which sense applies depends on the word.

**Modern-shape mnemonic, not etymology:** find 亻, the side form of [人](人.md), "person," at the left and [山](山.md), "mountain," above 隹 at the right. Imagine a person putting an event together until the crowd piles up like a mountain. This is a memory aid only: it makes no claim that 山 or 隹 contributes meaning or sound. Historical formation is not established here.

In 催促, 催 supplies サイ and 促 supplies ソク. In 催し, 催 writes もよお and し is okurigana: the word is the noun formed from the verb 催す もよおす. Practice selects サイ and もよお.す; the full source list also retains もよう.す. The absence of a linked もよう.す example here does not establish that the reading is invalid, obsolete or rare.

## Notes

- [促](促.md), "urge; prompt," is the other kanji in 催促. Both characters carry a prompting sense there, and the Daijisen character entry lists うながす, "to urge," as the first sense of 催, so 促 is kept out of this card's quiz options. Do not assume 催 means "urge" everywhere: in 開催 and 主催 it concerns events.
- [雇](雇.md), "employ; hire," and [集](集.md), "gather," also contain 隹, but 催 adds 亻 at the left and 山 above 隹. 集 is about people or things coming together; 催 is about organizing or prompting an occasion.
- [傾](傾.md), "tilt; incline," also has 亻 at the left, but its right side is a different shape.
