# 俳 — haiku; actor

**Strokes:** 10 · **Key (radical):** 人 (as 亻, no. 9) · **JLPT:** N1 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

俳 connects two useful vocabulary families: haiku and related verse in
[俳句](../words/俳句.md) はいく, and actors in [俳優](../words/俳優.md) はいゆう.
The whole word tells you which family matters; an actor need not be a poet.

The visible parts are 亻, the side form of [人](人.md), and [非](非.md).
**Modern-shape mnemonic:** imagine a performer standing beside the two rows of
strokes in 非 as though beside a stage prop. This is a visual memory aid, not
an account of the character's history.

## Notes

- The right-hand 非 does not make these words mean "not a poem" or "not an actor."
  A visible component is not a word-by-word translation rule.
- [悲](悲.md) has 非 above 心, rather than a person-shaped component on the left.
  [借](借.md) shares 亻 but has 昔 on the right, not 非.
- In 俳優, [優](優.md) also has a performer-related use; translating it as
  "gentle" does not explain the occupation.
- The traditional Japanese haiku pattern concerns Japanese sound units, not
  seventeen English syllables. The linked 俳句 article explains the form and
  its qualifications.
