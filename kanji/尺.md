# 尺 — length measure; shaku

**Strokes:** 4 · **Key (radical):** 尸 (no. 44) · **JLPT:** N1 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

尺 is a traditional unit of length. On the ordinary carpenter’s scale, one 尺 is about 30.3 centimeters. 尺 can also suggest measuring more broadly, as in 尺度 (しゃくど), a yardstick or criterion.

**Shape mnemonic, not etymology:** Trace the four strokes; the long stroke toward the lower right is easy to spot. Look for the same whole shape on the right of more complex characters.

## Notes

[訳](訳.md), [沢](沢.md) and [駅](駅.md) all have 尺 on the right, with speech, water and horse elements on the left respectively. [寸](寸.md) is a different traditional unit: ten 寸 make one 尺. [測](測.md) and [計](計.md) also concern measuring; neither is the name of this unit.
