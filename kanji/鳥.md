# 鳥 — bird

**Strokes:** 11 · **Key (radical):** 鳥 (no. 196) · **JLPT:** N4 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

鳥 is **bird**, the broad animal category. In food contexts, とり can also refer to chicken meat;
the more specific spelling 鶏（にわとり, chicken） helps distinguish chicken from birds in general.
鶏肉（とりにく） means chicken meat.

**Modern-shape mnemonic:** imagine the upper box as a head with an eye, the bent middle as a body
and wing, and the lower marks as moving feet. This is a visual aid, not a verified account of early forms.

## Notes

In 白鳥, [白](白.md) means white, but the usual whole-word meaning is the specific bird "swan,"
not simply any white bird.

[魚](魚.md) also has four lower marks; compare the upper bodies.
The 馬 component in [駅](駅.md) has a different upper shape from 鳥.

**Less common look-alike: 烏 からす (crow).** 烏 differs from 鳥 by a single stroke. In 鳥, the box at the top has
a horizontal line across its inside, like the middle line of 日: this is the fourth stroke, the "eye" of the mnemonic
above. 烏 leaves that box empty, so it has 10 strokes instead of 11. A traditional explanation is that the line was
left out because a crow's black body hides its eye. Treat this as a memorable story rather than settled history.

You will meet 烏 far less often than 鳥. It is not one of the jōyō (official everyday-use) kanji, and the bird is
usually written in kana: カラス or からす. The character does appear in 烏龍茶 ウーロンちゃ (oolong tea, also written
ウーロン茶; ウーロン is a sound borrowed from Chinese, not a usual reading of 烏) and in 烏賊 いか (squid). In 烏賊,
the two kanji together are read いか, a reading not built from the individual characters, and the word is usually
written イカ. 烏 and 鳥 are easy to mix up: JMdict, a major Japanese–English dictionary, even lists 鳥龍茶, with 鳥,
as a nonstandard spelling of oolong tea. The standard kanji spelling uses 烏, the one without the eye line.
