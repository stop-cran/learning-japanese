# 魔 — demon; magic

**Strokes:** 21 · **Key (radical):** 鬼 (no. 194), in the lower part · **JLPT:** N1 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

魔 concerns demons or malign supernatural influences, and magic.
[悪魔](../words/悪魔.md) あくま is a demon or devil;
魔法 まほう is magic or sorcery. The whole word and setting matter:
stage magic is not a claim that an evil spirit is present.

The character also occurs in words for a consuming obsession or a person
obsessed with an activity. It is not an appropriate neutral label for
someone simply having an interest.

**Modern-shape mnemonic, not etymology:** find the broad 麻 shape above
and around 鬼. Imagine a mysterious demon under that cover. This is a
memory aid for the present shape, not a historical explanation of 魔.

## Notes

- [妖](妖.md), bewitching/supernatural, [魅](魅.md), charm/bewitch, and
  [怪](怪.md), uncanny, overlap supernatural or enchanting uses.
  [悪](悪.md) and [凶](凶.md) overlap the evil branch, not every magic act.
- JMdict also records 魔 before の with the sense dreaded or terrible.
  That overlaps [怖](怖.md), frightening, so the pair is excluded from
  meaning choices; it does not make every fear supernatural.
- The English *charm* on [符](符.md) can suggest a spell, but its
  talisman/token sense is different from magic in general. Do not treat
  these comparisons as unrestricted Japanese substitutions.
- [摩](摩.md), rub/chafe, has 手 where 魔 has 鬼.
  [鹿](鹿.md), deer, has a similar broad enclosure but a different lower
  structure. [康](康.md), health/well-being, also has 广 with a different interior.
- The linked 悪魔 article distinguishes a fictional being from a
  figurative description. JMdict separately labels Christian and
  Buddhist senses of 悪魔; these are not a universal identity between
  religious figures.
