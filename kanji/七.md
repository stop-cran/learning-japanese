# 七 — seven

**Strokes:** 2 · **Key (radical):** 一 (no. 1) · **Phonetic:** none · **JLPT:** N5 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

The ancient form of 七 is a cross (+), the same shape that later became [十](十.md). It is commonly explained that the cross originally meant "to cut" and was borrowed for the number seven by sound; the modern shape then bent its vertical stroke to separate it from 十.
This explanation is a common one but not certain.

## Common words

- [七月](../words/七月.md) しちがつ — July
- [七日](../words/七日.md) なのか — the 7th; seven days
- 七つ ななつ — seven (things)
- 七時 しちじ — seven o'clock

## Notes

Both しち and なな are common. しち sounds close to いち (一) and し (四) so なな is often used in speech to avoid confusion (七十 ななじゅう, 七百 ななひゃく). Fixed words keep しち: 七月 しちがつ, 七時 しちじ.
Look-alikes: [十](十.md) (ten: its vertical line is straight and crosses the horizontal; in 七 the vertical curls right at the bottom), [九](九.md) and 力 (strength).
