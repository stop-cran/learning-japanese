# 唇 — lips

**Strokes:** 10 · **Key (radical):** 口 (no. 30), at the bottom · **JLPT:** N1 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

唇 concerns the lips around the opening of the mouth. The native word
[唇](../words/唇.md) くちびる can refer to one lip or both lips;
Japanese does not require an English-style plural ending.
Compare [口](口.md), the mouth more generally, rather than specifically
its lips.

**Modern-shape mnemonic, not etymology:** notice the [辰](辰.md)-shaped
upper part above the small 口. Imagine the lower box as a mouth with
prominent lips. This is an invented memory picture, not an account of the
character's historical formation or its sound component.

Practice selects くちびる, with its linked standalone word article.
シン remains in the full metadata and is illustrated by a dictionary
compound, but there is no linked 口唇 article here, so on questions are
not selected. This is not a claim that シン is invalid or rare.
JMdict also lists 口唇 as the whole-word reading くちびる; do not
divide that special reading into a new reading for each character.

## Notes

- [口](口.md), mouth, remains a broader guarded comparison, not another
  spelling to substitute for 唇 in the linked word.
- 脣 is another dictionary spelling of くちびる; JMdict marks that
  spelling as rare. The flag does not apply to ordinary 唇 or to the
  pronunciation くちびる.
- Digital Daijisen and Nihon Kokugo Daijiten also record a literary
  flower-petal sense of くちびる. [花](花.md) is therefore a guarded
  comparison, not a reliably wrong flower-related choice. This does not
  make a petal and an entire flower interchangeable in every context.
  The English [咲](咲.md) choice, "blossom; bloom," can also be read as
  a noun near this petal sense, so it is guarded directly. 咲く itself
  means "to bloom," not a word for a petal or for lips.
- Jitsu records おどろく, being startled or surprised, alongside the
  lip sense. Kanjipedia's historical account mentions trembling at the
  mouth. These source-specific senses motivate a direct [震](震.md)
  guard and conservative broad-choice guards for [動](動.md),
  [恐](恐.md) and [怖](怖.md). Moving, trembling, surprise and fear
  are not all synonyms. おどろく is supplemental lookup information,
  not a replacement pronunciation for the linked くちびる word.
- Jitsu also quotes the old-dictionary gloss ホトリ under the variant
  脣. Conservative direct guards for [側](側.md), [辺](辺.md),
  [際](際.md), [境](境.md), [界](界.md), [岸](岸.md) and [畔](畔.md)
  keep unresolved broad side, edge, boundary or waterside labels out of
  choices. This is not a claim that everyday 唇 means every kind of
  border or shore, or that ほとり is an ordinary reading of 唇.
  The quotation lacks context fixing its reach. [刃](刃.md), "blade;
  cutting edge," and [郊](郊.md), "outskirts; suburbs," are also guarded
  conservatively as qualified edge or outer-area choices. This prevents
  an unresolved broad-choice ambiguity, not a claim that lips are
  cutting tools or suburban areas.
- Nihon Kokugo Daijiten separately records the noun しん for a lip or
  a labial speech sound. The broad [音](音.md) label is conservatively
  guarded; a sound made with the lips is not the same thing as the lips.
- [古](古.md), old, [右](右.md), right, and [石](石.md), stone, also
  have a small lower box but much simpler upper strokes. Their displayed
  meanings do not describe lips or the qualified source senses above.
