# 紺 — navy blue

**Strokes:** 11 · **Key (radical):** 糸 (no. 120), compressed on the left · **JLPT:** N1 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

[紺](../words/紺.md) こん names a deep, dark blue, commonly translated
as navy blue. Daijisen describes a purplish cast. This is a color
description, not a promise of one exact digital color code.

The independent word is a noun: 紺の + noun describes something in
that color. Do not create an adjective by adding い.

**Modern-shape mnemonic, not etymology:** find [糸](糸.md) beside
[甘](甘.md). Imagine a navy-blue thread beside the little 甘-shaped
block. This is a modern visual association, not a historical dyeing
explanation or a claim that sweetness produces blue.

## Common words

- [紺](../words/紺.md) こん - navy blue
- 紺色 こんいろ - navy blue; deep blue
- 濃紺 のうこん - dark navy blue
- 紺青 こんじょう - a deep-blue color or pigment; dictionary translations vary

## Notes

- [紅](紅.md), crimson/deep red, [給](給.md), pay/bestow, and
  [結](結.md), tie/join/conclude, share 糸 but have different right sides.
- [青](青.md) covers blue more broadly. 紺 narrows the shade; it
  does not replace all blue or traditional green uses of 青.
- The English dark cue overlaps [濃](濃.md), concentrated/dark, and
  [暗](暗.md), dark. These choices are excluded while preserving
  the distinction: a deep color and a poorly lit room are not the
  same property, and neither other character specifically names navy blue.
- 紺青 has several blue-pigment and color glosses in the dictionary.
  Do not force them all into one invariant modern shade specification.
