# 番 — turn in a series

**Strokes:** 12 · **Key (radical):** 田 (no. 102) · **JLPT:** N3 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

番 marks a turn or a number within a series. [番号](../words/番号.md) ばんごう is an identifying number,
while 当番 とうばん concerns whose turn it is to be on duty.
This is not the same as counting how many objects there are.

**Modern-shape mnemonic:** picture the spreading upper strokes as people lining up above a square waiting area.
The lower 田 is a visible shape in this memory aid, not evidence of the character's historical meaning.

## Common words

- [番号](../words/番号.md) ばんごう — number; series of digits
- 一番 いちばん — number one; also "most" in comparisons
- 当番 とうばん — one's turn on duty; person on duty

## Notes

**Compared with [号](号.md) and ordinal [目](目.md):** 番 can mark either identification or order: 一番 (いちばん) can mean "number one" or "first," depending on context.
Adding 目 makes the sequence position explicit: 一番目 (いちばんめ), "first."
Ordinal 目 also follows other counting expressions, as in 三人目 (さんにんめ), "third person," and 三つ目 (みっつめ), "third item."
号 is used in conventional numbered designations such as 三号室 (さんごうしつ), "room number three."
That need not be 三番目の部屋 (さんばんめのへや), "the third room" in the order you encounter them.
号 can express order too, so learn the established expression rather than treating 番, 号, and 番目 as interchangeable.

次は私の番です。 (つぎはわたしのばんです。) — It is my turn next.

Do not stop recognizing the character at its lower 田: [留](留.md) has a different top,
and [男](男.md) places 田 above 力. [田](田.md) alone means a rice field, not a place in a queue.
