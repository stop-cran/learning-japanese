# 琴 — koto; zither

**Strokes:** 12 · **Key (radical):** 玉 (no. 96) · **JLPT:** N1 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

[琴](../words/琴.md) こと names the koto, a Japanese plucked zither,
not an upright Western harp. Older こと also covered string instruments
more broadly. 琴 きん names the Chinese seven-stringed qin or guqin.
Instrument names such as 風琴 ふうきん, organ, use 琴 in compounds;
they do not make every occurrence mean the modern Japanese koto.

**Modern-shape mnemonic, not etymology:** imagine two 王-shaped upper
groups as rows of strings above an 今-shaped stand. This invented picture
explains neither the character's history nor its sound. The indexing
radical is 玉, not a claim that the two upper groups are written 玉.

| キン | on | 琴 きん, qin; 琴線 きんせん, koto string or figurative heartstrings. Recognition only, without linked practice. |
| ゴン | on | 和琴 わごん, native Japanese zither. Learn the whole word; recognition only. |
| こと | kun | [琴](../words/琴.md) こと, koto; the existing article supports practice. |

The complete source inventory is retained above; only こと is selected
for questions. In the complete 琴/箏/筝 dictionary family, そう is
restricted to 箏/筝, not 琴. Weblio also records historical ゴム and キム;
字通 cites older dictionary forms ことのを and キノコト. These are
supplemental recognition evidence, not extra practice readings.

## Notes

- [古](古.md), old, [右](右.md), right, and [川](川.md), river,
  do not name a zither. An old instrument is not the meaning "old."
- 字通 also records a Chu-language use for 冢/塚, a burial mound.
  This is not taught as an unrestricted modern Japanese synonym.
  It nevertheless motivates direct precautions with [墓](墓.md), grave,
  and [丘](丘.md), hill or mound; not every hill is a tomb.
- [器](器.md), [械](械.md) and [具](具.md) have broad instrument/tool labels.
  [曲](曲.md), [楽](楽.md), [整](整.md) and [調](調.md) have
  music, melody or tuning senses. Their direct precautions protect
  against unresolved broad labels, not a claim that music, tuning
  and an instrument are universally interchangeable.
- [糸](糸.md) itself is recorded for stringed instruments such as the
  koto and shamisen, not only their strings. [緒](緒.md) receives a
  conservative precaution because 字通 cites 和名抄's old gloss
  ことのを for 琴, whose scope is unresolved, while 緒 includes
  instrument strings. This does not teach that 琴 means cord.
- Emotional meanings in 琴線 and marriage meanings in 琴瑟 belong
  to those whole words; do not automatically assign them to bare 琴.
