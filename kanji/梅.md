# 梅 — ume; Japanese apricot

**Strokes:** 10 · **Key (radical):** 木 (no. 75) · **JLPT:** N1 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

梅 writes **ume**, [梅](../words/梅.md) うめ, the tree and fruit also called Japanese apricot or Japanese plum. Those English names do not make it interchangeable with every plum or apricot.

**Modern-shape mnemonic, not etymology:** Notice [木](木.md) beside [毎](毎.md). Imagine checking an ume tree every day as its buds open. This is a memory aid, not an account of the character's historical construction.

## Notes

- 梅雨 has two dictionary readings. ばいう combines バイ + ウ; つゆ belongs to the whole written word, not to 梅 alone.
- In the conventional 松・竹・梅 ranking, 梅 names the lowest tier. That does not make the plant itself inferior or turn 梅 into a general word for anything bad.
- Compare [海](海.md), sea, with 氵 instead of 木; [毎](毎.md), every, has no left-hand tree component. [相](相.md), mutual or aspect, has 目 on the right.
- The dictionary also records むめ as obsolete kana usage for the word 梅. It is not an extra modern reading question in this card.
