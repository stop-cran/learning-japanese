# 丙 — third rank; third heavenly stem

**Strokes:** 5 · **Key (radical):** 一 (no. 1) · **JLPT:** N1 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

丙 names the third position in the label sequence 甲・乙・丙 (こう・おつ・へい),
comparable to A, B, C. It also names the third of the ten **heavenly stems**:
traditional calendar labels, not the twelve animal signs. This stem is associated
with fire in the traditional classification.

**Modern-shape mnemonic, not etymology:** picture a separate top [一](一.md) bar
above an [内](内.md)-like shape. The inner descending stroke reaches up to that
top bar. Use the extra bar to distinguish 丙 from 内.
Historical explanations differ; no settled origin is asserted here.

## Readings

| Reading | Type | Typical words |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ヘイ | on | [丙](../words/丙.md) へい — third rank; class C |
| ひのえ | kun | [丙午](../words/丙午.md) ひのえうま — Fire Horse, a calendar term combining 丙 ひのえ with 午 うま |

Use へい for the rank/class label. The native calendar name is ひのえ.
The stem can also be called へい; 丙午 has the documented alternate reading
へいご. That alternate does not replace the ひのえうま model in the kun row.

## Common words

These are label and calendar uses, not ordinary counting vocabulary.

- [丙](../words/丙.md) へい — the third rank or class in a 甲・乙・丙 sequence
- [丙午](../words/丙午.md) ひのえうま — Fire Horse, term 43 of the sixty-term calendar cycle

## Notes

- **Not the numeral three:** [三](三.md) さん counts a quantity. 丙 is a
  particular label; do not substitute it for 三 in ordinary counting.
- **Ten, twelve, sixty:** 十干 (じっかん) are the ten stems, and 十二支
  (じゅうにし) are the twelve branches associated with animals.
  Pairing the two sequences in order produces a cycle of sixty terms.
  丙 is a stem; [午](午.md) うま is the Horse branch.
- **Look-alikes:** [内](内.md) lacks the separate top bar; [肉](肉.md)
  has two stacked inner person-like marks instead. [病](病.md) has additional
  enclosing sickness-radical strokes around the 丙-shaped part. These are
  modern-shape comparisons, not claims about historical formation.
