# 少 — few, a little

**Strokes:** 4 · **Key (radical):** 小 (no. 42) · **Phonetic:** none · **JLPT:** N4 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

少 began as a variant of [小](小.md): in early forms both are small dots, and the two characters were later differentiated. 少 adds a stroke (ノ) to 小, which is often explained
as "taking a bit away" from something already small, hence "few, little". This is a plausible explanation but the history is uncertain, so treat it as a mnemonic.
少 refers to quantity ("few, a little"), while 小 refers to size.

## Notes

Its opposite is [多](多.md). Compare [小](小.md) (small), which has no extra stroke: 小さい ちいさい is size, 少ない is amount.
Okurigana (the kana after the kanji, like ない in 少ない): 少ない (すくない) and 少し (すこし); 少い is an old spelling, not standard today.
少し is often written in kana (すこし) in everyday text. Both 少 and 小 can be read しょう (少年 vs 小学生 しょうがくせい).
