# 攻 — attack; go on the offensive

**Strokes:** 7 · **Key (radical):** 攴 (as 攵, no. 66), at the right · **JLPT:** N1 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

攻 concerns going at something with force: moving against an opponent, or pressing deep into a subject. [攻撃](../words/攻撃.md) こうげき is an attack or offensive, and [攻める](../words/攻める.md) せめる is to attack, take on or act boldly. The Daijisen character entry gives a second sense of working deeply at something in order to master it, which explains [専攻](../words/専攻.md) せんこう, "major subject; special study." The same entry also lists おさめる as a kun reading; KANJIDIC2 does not, so it is not part of the metadata here.

**Modern-shape mnemonic, not etymology:** find [工](工.md), "craft, construction," at the left and 攵 at the right. Imagine a worker hammering away at a job until it gives way. This is a memory aid only: it makes no claim that 工 or 攵 contributes meaning or sound. Historical formation is not established here.

## Readings

| コウ | on | [攻撃](../words/攻撃.md) こうげき — attack; offensive; [専攻](../words/専攻.md) せんこう — academic major; specialization |

In 攻撃, 攻 supplies コウ and 撃 supplies ゲキ. In 専攻, 専 supplies セン and 攻 supplies コウ. In 攻める, 攻 writes せ and める is okurigana. Practice selects コウ and せ.める. [責める](../words/責める.md) せめる, "to blame; reproach," sounds the same and its kanji 責 also has the kun reading せ.める, but it is a different word with a different meaning, so match the kanji to the meaning.

## Notes

- [撃](撃.md), "shoot; strike," is the other kanji in 攻撃. Both characters concern hitting at a target, so their meanings overlap.
- [責](責.md), "blame; hold responsible," also has the kun reading せ.める, and 責める is a homophone of 攻める. 責める is to blame or reproach someone, whereas 攻める is to go on the offensive.
- [功](功.md), "merit; achievement," shares 工 and the reading コウ but has 力 at the right. [放](放.md), "release," and [敗](敗.md), "failure; defeat," share 攵 at the right but differ at the left.
- [戦](戦.md), "battle," also concerns fighting. 攻 is going on the offensive and 戦 is the fight itself, so do not treat "fighting" as exclusive to either character.
