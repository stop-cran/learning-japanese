# 監 — oversee; watch over

**Strokes:** 15 · **Key (radical):** 皿 (no. 108), at the bottom · **JLPT:** N1 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

監 concerns watching over or overseeing. [監督](../words/監督.md)
かんとく names supervision and can also name the person responsible,
such as a director or sports coach. 監視 かんし is monitoring or
surveillance, and 監査 かんさ is inspection or an audit.

Daijisen distinguishes watching/regulating, a supervisory role or
official, and a prison. It places 監禁 かんきん, confinement or
imprisonment, under the watching/regulating branch, not the prison-place
branch. 監獄 かんごく, prison, illustrates the place sense.
監禁 does not describe supervision in general. KANJIDIC2 also preserves
official, government-office, rule and administer glosses. Keep the
institution or activity in context rather than translating every 監
compound as "watch."

**Modern-shape mnemonic, not etymology:** find [臣](臣.md) at the
upper left, a short three-stroke group at the upper right, and
[皿](皿.md) below. Imagine an official checking a dish carefully.
This is a memory picture, not a verified origin or phonetic analysis.

## Readings

| Reading | Type | Typical words |
| --- | --- | --- |
| カン | on | [監督](../words/監督.md) かんとく - supervision; director; coach |

Practice selects カン, also the Joyo reading. In 監督, 監 supplies
カン and [督](督.md) supplies トク. Learn the complete word かんとく
and its context rather than assigning every English job title to either
character alone.

Daijisen also identifies ケン as a Go-on reading, an older layer of
Chinese-derived pronunciation, in official-title vocabulary such as
監物 けんもつ. It separately records 監 げん for old administrative
uses; JMdict marks that noun's district and Dazaifu-official senses
archaic. These are outside this card's カン-only KANJIDIC2 on-reading
list and practice selection, not invalid readings or alternatives to
substitute into 監督.
KANJIDIC2 separately records けん as a name reading, not an
additional on/kun practice reading.

## Common words

- [監督](../words/監督.md) かんとく - supervision; director; coach
- 監視 かんし - monitoring; surveillance
- 監査 かんさ - inspection; audit
- 監禁 かんきん - confinement; imprisonment

## Notes

- [白](白.md), white, has a compact box with an extra top stroke.
  [人](人.md), person, has two spreading strokes, and [小](小.md),
  small/little, has three strokes. None has 監's combined upper
  arrangement and lower dish. [皿](皿.md), dish/plate, is only that
  lower part, not the complete character.
- Seeing or watching overlaps [見](見.md), [観](観.md),
  [察](察.md) and [看](看.md); inspection overlaps [調](調.md) and
  [査](査.md), investigate/inspect,
  and guarding overlaps [守](守.md) and [警](警.md).
  [番](番.md) also has a keeping-watch sense; [望](望.md)
  includes looking into the distance, and [偵](偵.md) seeks out
  another person's situation. Surveillance need not be open, but
  not all observation or investigation is supervision.
  Watching something does not by itself establish its explanation or
  guarantee its safety. [保](保.md), protect/preserve, and
  [防](防.md), defend/ward off, are also kept apart for the
  protective English "watch over" comparison. Observation, care and
  preventing harm are not identical activities.
- Supervision and control overlap [督](督.md), [管](管.md),
  [御](御.md), [治](治.md), [制](制.md), [統](統.md) and
  [握](握.md). The regulatory branch also connects with [憲](憲.md).
  These are direct meaning comparisons, not interchangeable spellings.
  [理](理.md) also has Daijisen's おさめる (arrange/manage) sense.
  [経](経.md), [知](知.md) and [総](総.md) have management or
  governing branches too; [権](権.md) concerns authority or power.
  [支](支.md) includes planning or arranging affairs,
  [処](処.md) managing or dealing with them, and [扱](扱.md)
  handling work. These are conservative management/control comparisons,
  not claims that arranging or handling something always means
  supervising people, or that authority is the same as exercising it.
- KANJIDIC2's English "rule" can describe exercising control, not a
  rule to follow. [規](規.md), [則](則.md), [律](律.md),
  [典](典.md), [準](準.md), [法](法.md), law/method, and
  [儀](儀.md), ceremony/model, are kept apart conservatively for this
  loose English comparison; 監 does not thereby mean a statute,
  ceremony or measuring standard. KANJIDIC2 also lists "rule" for
  [王](王.md), king, in the sense of reigning. A monarch is not a
  supervisor, but this ruling comparison is kept apart conservatively.
- Directing or commanding comparisons include [導](導.md), lead/guide,
  [将](将.md), commander, [率](率.md), lead/rate, [命](命.md),
  life/command, and [令](令.md), command/decree. These meaning guards
  distinguish related leadership and control answers without claiming
  that every guide, commander or order is supervision.
- Official, administrative and supervisory-role comparisons include
  [官](官.md), [局](局.md), [府](府.md), [庁](庁.md),
  [署](署.md), [寮](寮.md), [領](領.md), [長](長.md) and
  [主](主.md). [相](相.md) has a minister sense and [僚](僚.md)
  an official sense; [役](役.md) can name a principal role or its
  holder. [頭](頭.md) and [首](首.md) also name a head or leader,
  not just body parts. KANJIDIC2 also lists "official" for [仕](仕.md);
  Daijisen describes becoming an official or serving, not the same
  activity as supervising. Supervising does not establish ownership or an
  identical rank in every institution. English "official" also appears on
  [公](公.md), where it describes public/official status rather than
  naming the same kind of officeholder.
- [省](省.md), ministry/omit, [政](政.md), politics, [営](営.md),
  conduct business/manage, and [係](係.md), connection/person in charge,
  are also excluded for their office, governing, management or
  person-in-charge senses. This does not mean every ministry is a 監
  or every person in charge is a supervisor; office names and
  responsibilities still differ.
  [職](職.md) also names a historical bureau; [部](部.md) and
  [課](課.md) can be organizational departments, and [院](院.md)
  includes public offices or institutions. KANJIDIC2 lists "office"
  for [社](社.md), company/shrine, which names associations and
  companies; a company office is not a government office. These
  office-related answers are kept apart without treating every
  institution as a supervisory office.
- [禁](禁.md), prohibit/ban, also has a confinement sense. Confining
  someone, a place of confinement and overseeing someone are not identical,
  but these related regulation/confinement answers are kept apart in practice.
  [留](留.md) includes detaining or keeping in place, and [閉](閉.md)
  closes access. These conservative confinement comparisons do not make
  every act of staying or shutting something an imprisonment.
- The archaic district sense overlaps [区](区.md), district/ward.
  [県](県.md) and [州](州.md) also name administrative divisions.
  Their ranks, periods and jurisdictions differ: a Nara-period 監 is
  not a modern prefecture, state or municipal ward. These conservative
  comparisons are not rules for renaming institutions.
