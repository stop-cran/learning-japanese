# 匁 — monme (3.75 g)

**Strokes:** 4 · **Key (radical):** 勹 (no. 20) · **JLPT:** N1 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

匁 names the **monme**, a traditional Japanese unit of mass equal to **3.75 grams**. It is still encountered in pearl trading; it is not simply an obsolete word. Historical texts also use it as a silver-money unit, a separate use from the mass conversion taught here.

**Modern-shape mnemonic, not etymology:** Picture a hooked outer outline around two crossing strokes. The canonical radical 勹 corresponds to the outer outline, although its shape differs from the standalone radical. This is a way to remember the present shape, not an account of its historical formation or a claim that the unit means "wrapping."

## Notes

- Learn 百匁 as the documented whole expression above. Do not assume that shortened め can freely replace もんめ after every number.
- Compare [久](久.md), "a long time," with three strokes, and [文](文.md), "sentence, writing," with four: similar outlines do not give 匁 their meanings. [包](包.md), "wrap; enclose," shares radical 勹 but is a different five-stroke character.
- Historical silver-money values depended on the period and exchange rate. The mass conversion is not a fixed conversion into modern money.
