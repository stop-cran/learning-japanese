# 李 — Japanese plum

**Strokes:** 7 · **Key (radical):** 木 (no. 75) · **JLPT:** N1 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

李 writes **Japanese plum**, [李](../words/李.md) すもも, the tree and fruit. This lesson's botanical anchor is *Prunus salicina*, not [梅](../words/梅.md) うめ, *Prunus mume*. Both can be called Japanese plum in English, so an English label alone does not settle the Japanese word.

**Modern-shape mnemonic, not etymology:** Notice [木](木.md) above [子](子.md), child. Imagine a child standing under a plum tree. This is a memory aid, not a claim about the character's origin.

## Notes

- This card asks すもも. リ remains available for recognizing compounds and the proverb, not as a replacement for the standalone native fruit noun. Personal-name readings require their own evidence and are not taught here.
- 李下 combines 李 with [下](下.md), below. The proverb literally says not to straighten your cap under a plum tree: raised hands could look like an attempt to take fruit. Its lesson is avoiding misleading appearances, not a general ban on adjusting hats.
- すもも can have a broader botanical scope, including European plums in qualified plant names. Do not infer that every occurrence of "plum" names *Prunus salicina*.
- Compare [杏](杏.md), apricot: its lower part is 口, not 子. [子](子.md) alone means child; [村](村.md), village, and [相](相.md), mutual or aspect, put a different component beside 木 rather than below it.
