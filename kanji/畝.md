# 畝 — raised ridge; land measure

**Strokes:** 10 · **Key (radical):** 田 (no. 102) · **JLPT:** N1 (community list)

## Meaning and origin

[畝](../words/畝.md) うね is a **raised row of earth for planting crops**. The word also describes ribs or ridges in fabric and other surfaces. Do not confuse the raised planting strip with the lower furrow between strips.

The same character also writes land-area units. Japanese 畝 せ and the Chinese measure called 畝 ほ in Japanese are different units, not alternative pronunciations with one fixed conversion.

**Modern-shape mnemonic, not etymology:** Notice the capped 田-shaped part on the left beside a [久](久.md)-shaped part on the right. Imagine a long raised row beside a field. This is a memory aid, not a claim about the character's history.

## Notes

- [畔](畔.md) あぜ emphasizes the raised boundary between rice fields; 畝 うね emphasizes a planting row. English "ridge" can translate both, so learn the agricultural context.
- In the Japanese area unit, せ does not mean a row count. The Chinese measure is a separate historical system whose size has varied; do not use the Japanese 99.174-square-metre value for it.
- The full on list is preserved in source order. The selected question teaches うね; an unselected reading is not thereby invalid, and source-only means unestablished as a word example in this bounded packet, not nonexistent.
- [留](留.md), retain or stay, and [畜](畜.md), livestock, have 田 at the bottom instead. 久 alone means a long time, not a planting ridge.
